# Hierarchy of Engagement

A three-level framework from [[source-sarah-tavel|Sarah Tavel]] (Benchmark GP; first PM at Pinterest) for diagnosing consumer-social product depth. An antidote to MAU-chasing and vanity metrics.

## Level 1: Core action

Every consumer product has one action that proves the user "got it" and predicts return. Pick the single action most correlated with retention.

- **Pinterest**: weekly active pinners/repinners (found via regression across like/follow/click/pin/time-on-site).
- **YouTube**: subscribing (shifted from "watching videos" per Shishir Mehrotra because it benefits both creators and viewers).
- **[[source-merci-grace|Slack]]**: 3 users × 50 messages in a team.
- **[[source-naomi-gleit|Facebook]]**: 7 friends in 10 days.

## Level 2: Accruing benefits + mounting loss

Product should **get better the more you use it**, AND you should **have more to lose by leaving**.

- **Accruing benefits**: Pinterest's "picked for you" algorithmic feed gets smarter per pin; Snapchat streaks accrue.
- **Mounting loss**: Your playlists, connections, history, social graph become sticky.

Anonymity destroys this; pseudo-anonymity preserves it.

## Level 3: Self-perpetuating via network effects + loops

The "kinetic energy" of each user action feeds back into the product.

- Clubhouse/Houseparty hit the ceiling because their only re-engagement loop was push notifications — eventually noise.
- Pinterest's social graph + algorithmic feed + user pins create both growth and engagement loops.
