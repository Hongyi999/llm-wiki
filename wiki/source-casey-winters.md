# Why most PMs are unprepared for the demands of a real startup | Casey Winters

**Guest**: Casey Winters (CPO, Eventbrite)
**Published**: 2023-04-14
**Duration**: 1:39:00
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WlRfyEpAKxw](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WlRfyEpAKxw)

## Executive summary

Casey Winters — CPO at Eventbrite, ex-Pinterest/GrubHub, and one of the most experienced growth strategists in tech — argues that most PMs under-communicate upward, under-prepare for big meetings, and plateau at a "strong executor" level because they never build the strategic muscle. He coins "perceived simplicity" as the honest goal of product scaling (vs. false promises of unbundling or progressive disclosure), introduces the kindle-vs-fire framing for growth investment, and champions product-led sales as the unified growth motion for B2B companies. The throughline: strategy, not execution, is the career bottleneck for senior PMs.

## Key ideas

1. **Upward communication as chapter-telling**: When briefing execs, don't start at chapter six (deep in the weeds) or at the beginning of time (re-explaining the company). Start at the last obvious point. Pre-meetings and role-play with your CPO de-risk big presentations.
2. **Perceived simplicity**: The honest goal of product scaling isn't unbundling or segmentation — it's making advanced features effortlessly discoverable when sought and invisible otherwise. WhatsApp is the gold standard.
3. **Product-led sales**: Unify self-service PLG with sales loops into one cross-functional engine. Sales picks up product-qualified leads who need more help; the product funnel feeds sales rather than competing with it.
4. **Kindle vs. Fire strategies**: Kindle strategies are non-scalable hacks that exist only to unlock fire strategies (content loops, virality, paid, sales). Don't hire a growth lead until you've unlocked a fire strategy.
5. **The Spectrum of Product People**: Left = crazy innovator (ideas, weak execution). Right = strong executor (ships, no strategy). Most companies need the middle. The senior-PM growth bottleneck is developing strategy on top of execution.
