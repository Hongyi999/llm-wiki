# Why most product managers are unprepared for the demands of a real startup | Casey Winters

**Guest**: Casey Winters  
**Published**: 2023-04-14  
**Duration**: 1:39  
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WlRfyEpAKxw](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WlRfyEpAKxw)

## Executive summary

Casey Winters, CPO of Eventbrite and veteran growth advisor to Pinterest, Airbnb, Tinder, and Canva, delivers a masterclass on the CPO role, executive communication, and growth strategy. He argues that the great filter for senior PMs is the inability to write strategy independently, and that the biggest bottleneck at scale is justifying non-sexy product investments (stability, performance, developer velocity) that are chronically underfunded because they're hard to measure. The episode also introduces his spectrum of product people framework, the concept of perceived simplicity for managing product complexity, and practical growth tactics including kindle vs. fire strategies and the rising importance of product-led sales and data network effects.

## Key ideas

1. **Executive communication is 'executives communication'**: Each executive has different concerns and communication styles. PMs must start their story at the right chapter -- not chapter six (skipping context) nor the beginning of time (re-explaining the obvious) -- and pre-meet with key stakeholders to de-risk important meetings.

2. **Perceived simplicity solves the product complexity lifecycle**: Rather than progressive disclosure, unbundling, or segmentation, Eventbrite pursues 'perceived simplicity' -- advanced features are easily discoverable when sought but effectively invisible otherwise. WhatsApp exemplifies this approach.

3. **The spectrum of product people and the strategy bottleneck**: PMs range from wild innovators (many ideas, poor execution) to strong executors (great at shipping, weak on strategy). Most companies need people in the middle, and the great filter for reaching director-plus levels is the ability to independently write a product strategy doc.

4. **Kindle strategies exist only to unlock fire strategies**: Early non-scalable growth hacks (kindle) should be explicitly designed to validate and unlock scalable growth loops (fire) -- content loops, viral loops, sales loops, or paid acquisition. Hire a dedicated growth person only once you've unlocked a fire strategy.

5. **Justifying non-sexy product investments requires coalition building**: For hard-to-measure work like performance, stability, and developer velocity, first align your engineering and design peers, then build custom metrics, run small proving experiments, and frame the investment as protecting existing value rather than only adding new value.

## Concepts covered

- **Perceived Simplicity** — A design philosophy where advanced features are easily discoverable when sought but effectively hidden from users who don't need them, avoiding the complexity trap.
- **Kindle and Fire Growth Strategies** — Kindle strategies are non-scalable early hacks that exist only to unlock fire strategies -- the scalable growth loops (content, viral, sales, paid) that drive millions of users.
- **Product-Led Sales** — Unifying self-service product loops with sales motions into one larger cross-functional engine, where product qualified leads feed the sales team.
- **Data Network Effects** — Leveraging product usage data to make the product increasingly valuable over time (e.g., better personalization, better ad targeting) -- distinct from selling data as a product.
- **The Spectrum of Product People** — PMs exist on a spectrum from wild innovators (many ideas, poor execution) to strong executors (great at shipping, weak on strategy), with the ideal in the middle.
- **Product Market Fit Erosion** — Product market fit degrades over time as user expectations rise and competitors improve; teams must proactively invest in maintenance or risk losing what they've built.
