# Source: Building minimum lovable products, stories from WeWork & Airbnb, and thriving as a PM | Jiaona Zhang

**Guest**: Jiaona Zhang
**Published**: 2023-07-02
**Duration**: 1:07:32
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gLFZNzeylCc

## Executive summary

Jiaona Zhang (JZ), SVP of Product at Webflow and Stanford lecturer, shares hard-won lessons from leading product at Dropbox, Airbnb, WeWork, and Webflow. She argues that the traditional minimum viable product is no longer sufficient in a competitive landscape and advocates for the 'minimum lovable product' — shipping fewer features but at a higher quality bar that creates genuine user delight. JZ dissects the Airbnb Plus failure as a case study in solution-first thinking and broken unit economics, offering a framework for when to push back on ambitious but unviable ideas. She also provides actionable advice on roadmap storytelling over spreadsheet prioritization, OKR ambition over sandbagging, and navigating the first 90 days as a new product leader. The episode closes with a cross-company meta-lesson: understand why users love your product and double down on that core strength rather than chasing adjacent competitors.

## Key ideas

1. **Minimum Lovable Product over MVP**: In a world of abundant alternatives, products must exceed a mere viability threshold. JZ argues teams should do five things exceptionally well rather than fifteen things at a mediocre level, scattering 'pixie dust' of delight in select areas to create genuine user love.
2. **Roadmap as story, not spreadsheet**: JZ pushes back on the RICE-framework-spreadsheet approach to prioritization, arguing that roadmaps should communicate themes and a narrative of why certain investments matter. This lets teams internalize the strategy and adapt when new learnings emerge, rather than rigidly following ranked project lists.
3. **Unit economics as a gating function for big bets**: Drawing on the Airbnb Plus failure, JZ emphasizes validating unit economics early rather than relying on 'magical thinking' that scale will fix fundamentals. She recommends time-boxed learning phases with explicit go/no-go milestones to avoid sunk-cost traps.
4. **Double down on your core love**: Across Dropbox, Airbnb, WeWork, and Webflow, JZ observes the same pattern — companies that chase competitors or spread into adjacencies lose ground, while those that deeply invest in the thing users already love them for compound their advantage.
5. **Trust as a bank account in leadership**: When joining a new company, JZ frames trust as a piggy bank — you must deposit (listen, build context, learn the product) before you withdraw (push for change). Pushing for change too fast without sufficient trust capital undermines your effectiveness.

## Concepts & frameworks

- [[minimum-lovable-product]] — An evolution of MVP that sets the quality bar at user delight rather than mere viability, arguing teams should do fewer things excellently rather than many things adequately.
- **Roadmap Storytelling** — The practice of framing roadmaps as thematic narratives that communicate why investments matter, rather than ranked spreadsheets of projects with impact/effort scores.
- **OKR Ambition Culture** — JZ's approach to OKRs emphasizing qualitative vision of 'what would crushing it look like' over safe numeric targets, preferring red/yellow OKRs with learning over green OKRs with sandbagging.
- **First 90 Days Framework** — A structured approach to onboarding as a product leader: build context through 40-50 cross-functional conversations, identify strategic gaps, establish go/no-go research plans, and build trust before pushing for change.
- **Trust as a Bank Account** — A leadership mental model where social capital must be deposited through listening and context-building before it can be withdrawn for pushing organizational change.
