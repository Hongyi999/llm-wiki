# Source: 35 years of product design wisdom ft. Bob Baxley

**Guest**: Bob Baxley (design leader at Apple, Pinterest, Yahoo, ThoughtSpot)
**Published**: 2025-06-12
**Duration**: ~1h 42m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X-83gvgVaWc

## Executive summary

Bob Baxley distills 35 years of design leadership across Apple, Pinterest, Yahoo, and ThoughtSpot into a philosophy: software is a medium (like film or music), and building great products is a moral obligation because every confusing interaction takes emotional energy from users. Introduces design tenets over design principles — tenets are decision-making tools that resolve recurring debates (e.g., "documentation is a failure state"), while principles are platitudes nobody argues against. Key insight: design-led ≠ designer-led, and design culture must exist in founding DNA — he's never seen it grafted on after the fact.

## Key ideas

1. **Design tenets over design principles** — Principles like "simple" or "clear" are unopposable platitudes. Tenets are opinionated decision-making tools that resolve recurring debates. Steve Jobs's Keynote tenets: difficult to make ugly presentations, cinematic transitions, innovation over PowerPoint compatibility. Baxley's ThoughtSpot tenets: documentation is a failure state; start simple, let users opt into complexity; the entire product should feel like it came from a single mind.
2. **Software is a medium** — Software evokes emotional responses (empowerment vs. confusion) like film or music. Silicon Valley's personal computing revolution started because a small group near Stanford saw software as a new form of media on par with movies, music, and books. Product teams should consciously design for the emotion they want to elicit.
3. **Moral obligation to build great products** — Hundreds of daily software interactions drain users' emotional energy through confusion and frustration. Product builders have an obligation to put that energy back. The Toast example: tonight you're at dinner with a grandmother — will your design make her a superhero or a fool?
4. **Design-led ≠ designer-led** — Design is a holistic mindset ("clear thinking made visible"), not just a team. Companies are design-led when design thinking permeates decisions. Design culture must exist at founding — Baxley has never seen it grafted on after the fact.
5. **Delay the primal mark** — The first visual artifact collapses possibilities. Wait as long as possible before drawing; stay in conceptual/conversational space to reach 2nd, 3rd, 4th ideas. Block-frame diagrams (even simpler than wireframes) keep teams discussing concepts rather than colors. High-res comps are the easy part; heavy lifting is deciding what to build.
6. **Small teams create breakthroughs** — 20 people on the original Mac patent, 24 on the iPhone. Brian Eno's "scenius" (collective genius) works in small groups. You get The Beatles with four people, not with 24. Scale people in after clarity of vision exists, not before.
