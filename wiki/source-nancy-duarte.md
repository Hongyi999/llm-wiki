# Source: Storytelling with Nancy Duarte

**Guest**: Nancy Duarte (CEO of Duarte Inc.; 250,000+ presentations including Al Gore's *Inconvenient Truth*)
**Published**: 2023-06-01
**Duration**: ~1h 17m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-kHkWgjGD7U

## Executive summary

Three imperatives: make the audience the hero, infuse talks with story structure (the "what is / what could be / new bliss" rise-and-fall), and help the audience literally see what you're saying. Empathy-first approach reframes the presenter as Obi-Wan-style mentor, not hero.

## Key ideas

1. **Audience is the hero; presenter is the mentor** — Power sits with the audience (they accept or reject the idea); the speaker's job is to come alongside with tools, like Obi-Wan giving Luke a lightsaber and inner resolve.
2. **"What is / what could be / new bliss" structure** — Presentations oscillate between current reality and better future, ending with a vivid new bliss. Duarte mapped this pattern by hand on quarter-inch graph paper across great speeches (MLK, Jobs's iPhone keynote).
3. **Story creates longing** — Structured contrast (rise and fall) mirrors ancient story architecture and activates shared brain-firing; it makes people long for a future they didn't previously want.
4. **The framework works at any scale** — Not just keynotes: used in meetings, phone calls, even getting a spouse to do chores — any "moment of influence."
5. **"Can they see what you're saying?"** — Use diagrams, sketches, star moments, grab-the-napkin visuals to align the room on a shared mental picture.

## B8 angle

"Audience is the hero, you are the mentor" is among the cleanest antidotes to founder/executive hero-narrative bloat. The "what is / what could be / new bliss" cadence is a portable scaffolding usable across pitch decks, product launches, internal memos, and board updates. Converges with Andy Raskin's strategic narrative template (name the shift → promise the future).
