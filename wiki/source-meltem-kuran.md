# Source: An inside look at Deel's unprecedented growth | Meltem Kuran Berkowitz

**Guest**: Meltem Kuran Berkowitz (Deel's Head of Growth since ~$1M ARR; employee #19-20)
**Published**: 2023-08-27
**Duration**: ~1h 11m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C1_sM0_ds2c

## Executive summary

How Deel became arguably the fastest-growing SaaS in history ($0 → $295M ARR in 3 years, EBITDA positive) by leading with cheap channels — Reddit/Quora question answering, intense SEO, VC and partnership plays — before layering paid on top. Philosophy: "build the skeleton before you put on the makeup" — don't run paid until your site is fast, indexed, and answers real questions.

## Key ideas

1. **Cheap channels = wherever people ask questions** — Set keyword alerts on Reddit/Quora/Twitter; respond as a human, not a brand. Drove 15% of the funnel early (now ~5% but larger absolute volume). "People don't want to be sold to, they want their problem solved."
2. **Is the Google search over? SEO mental model** — Don't shove keywords; write content that ends the user's search. Traffic-light framework: rank 700 keywords, triage green (buyer intent) / yellow / red, publish green highest-volume first.
3. **Build the skeleton before the makeup** — Order: (1) website fast + indexed; (2) findable content; (3) paid on top. "You can't run a successful paid ads program if you have a website that's loading in four plus seconds."
4. **Awareness is a trap for early B2B** — Most B2B exists because of active problems. Capture bottom-of-funnel intent for 6-8 months before running awareness. Notion's OOH ads worked because they came *after* people recognized the interface.
5. **Long-tail paid is where diversification happens** — Beyond big 4-5 platforms, 30% of lead flow comes from tiny review sites, newsletter/podcast ads. Same effort per channel; mandatory for lead-source diversification.
6. **Partner with who your audience goes to for this question** — VCs were Deel's breakthrough partner because founders ask VCs "what should we use for payroll?" Shared audience alone isn't enough; trust vector matters.
7. **Hire only revenue-committing growth leaders; little hands culture** — In interviews, if candidate commits only to lead KPIs, reject. "Little hands" (loosely from French): no matter how senior, do the nitty-gritty.
8. **Team structure: functional + regional, not fully regional** — Global functional teams (paid, content, brand) preserve craft expertise. Regional marketing managers pull from central teams.
