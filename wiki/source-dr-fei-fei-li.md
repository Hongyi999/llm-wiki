## Executive summary

Dr. Fei-Fei Li, whose ImageNet dataset and research sparked the deep learning revolution, explains the 30-year journey from AI winter to ChatGPT and argues the next frontier is spatial intelligence and world models. She founded World Labs to build generative 3D worlds that can be navigated and reasoned about — not just watched as videos — and launched Marble, which enables creators to prompt-to-worlds for VFX, game design, robotics, and therapy. Her core thesis: spatial understanding and embodied AI are as critical as language models but require different data and architectures and can't be solved by scaling alone. She advocates for human-centered AI development where ordinary people retain agency and dignity.

## Key ideas

1. ImageNet was the missing link: Before 2012, AI was data-starved; ImageNet provided 15 million labeled images on 22,000 concepts, enabling AlexNet to prove the GPU + big data + neural networks trio unlocked deep learning.
2. World models are fundamentally different from video generation: Spatial intelligence requires reasoning, interaction, and 3D structure, not just pixel prediction. World models let robots plan actions and creators explore navigable spaces.
3. Bitter lesson alone won't solve robotics: Unlike language where text input equals text output, robots must learn actions in 3D worlds from videos lacking action labels; scaling data alone fails.
4. The 20-year robotics journey mirrors self-driving cars: Self-driving cars are simpler robots (2D, don't touch things) yet have taken 20 years. Robots are harder (3D, must manipulate).
5. Everyone has a role in AI futures: Artists, farmers, nurses, accountants — human agency and dignity must be protected at every layer (development, deployment, governance).

## Concepts & frameworks

- [[spatial-intelligence|Spatial Intelligence and World Models]] — The ability to create, reason about, interact with, and understand 3D and 4D worlds — not just watch videos.
- **The Data-Architecture-Compute Trio** — Modern AI success requires massive labeled data, neural network architectures suited to the task, and compute.
- **Human-Centered AI Development** — AI governance and deployment must protect human dignity, agency, and choice.
- **Bitter Lesson Limitations for Physical Systems** — The scaling principle that works for language and vision fails for robotics because robots require embodied feedback and action labels.
