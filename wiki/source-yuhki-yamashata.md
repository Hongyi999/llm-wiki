# An inside look at how Figma builds product | Yuhki Yamashita (CPO of Figma)

**Guest**: Yuhki Yamashita  
**Published**: 2023-01-08  
**Duration**: 1:08:36  
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NepFo4zXyK4](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NepFo4zXyK4)

## Executive summary

Yuhki Yamashita, CPO of Figma, shares the philosophies and practices behind Figma's consistently high-quality product development. He argues that storytelling and synthesis are the most critical PM skills, and that PMs should own the 'why' behind product decisions rather than the 'what.' Yamashita details Figma's evolving approach to OKRs, moving from rigid metrics to headline-based commitments and back, revealing that even top product organizations are perpetually iterating on process. He explains how community-led growth, deep customer obsession (exemplified by CEO Dylan Field reading individual tweets), and dogfooding drive Figma's success as a product-led growth company.

## Key ideas

1. **Storytelling as the core PM superpower**: Yamashita believes that synthesis and memification are the most important skills for PMs. Great PMs distill complex information into memorable narratives that leaders can cite in meetings, effectively transferring knowledge and compelling action across the organization.

2. **PMs own the 'why,' not the 'what'**: While ideas can come from anywhere — customers, engineers, designers — the PM is uniquely responsible for articulating why the team is solving a particular problem. This clarity enables distributed decision-making at scale, as team members can make good local decisions when they understand the underlying rationale.

3. **OKRs as a perpetual work in progress**: Figma has cycled through multiple goal-setting approaches — from formal OKRs to headline-based commitments and back again. Yamashita identifies three qualities of good OKRs: legibility (people understand them), actionability (they inspire action), and authenticity (they reflect what the team actually does day-to-day).

4. **Customer obsession through community**: Figma's growth is community-led rather than purely product-led. CEO Dylan Field reads individual customer tweets (even those with zero likes) and has done so for a decade, creating a private 'Concerning Tweets' channel. The Friends of Figma program empowers passionate users to evangelize the product from within their organizations.

5. **Dogfooding as quality assurance**: Yamashita deliberately shifted Figma from a memo culture to a deck culture so that PMs would build presentations in Figma itself, encountering bugs and friction firsthand. He also uses FigJam for calibrations and performance reviews, ensuring the entire company has high personal accountability for product quality.

## Concepts covered

- **Memification** — The practice of distilling insights into memorable, quotable nuggets that leaders spontaneously cite in meetings, proving the insight has truly transferred.
- **Community-led growth** — Growth strategy where passionate internal champions within customer organizations evangelize a product, supported by programs like Friends of Figma.
- **Five whys** — Root cause analysis technique used in Figma engineering postmortems to trace problems back to underlying conditions rather than surface symptoms.
- **OKR legibility-actionability-authenticity** — Yamashita's three criteria for effective OKRs: people understand them, they inspire action, and they honestly reflect what teams actually prioritize day-to-day.
- **Dogfooding** — The practice of using your own product internally to discover quality issues, exemplified by Figma switching to a deck culture so PMs build presentations in Figma.
