# Source: Inside OpenAI | Logan Kilpatrick (head of developer relations)

## Executive summary

Logan Kilpatrick, head of developer relations at OpenAI, provides an insider's view of how OpenAI operates, ships products, and thinks about the developer ecosystem. He argues that the biggest opportunity for startups is building vertical, domain-specific AI applications rather than competing with ChatGPT on general-purpose use cases. Kilpatrick frames prompt engineering as fundamentally a human communication skill -- providing context is the key to getting better outputs from LLMs. He reveals that OpenAI's culture prizes two hiring traits above all else: high agency and high urgency, which enable their remarkably fast shipping cadence. The conversation also covers the GPT Store launch, the future of AI agents, and why GPT-5 should be seen as a better tool for existing problems rather than a world-changing paradigm shift.

## Key ideas

1. **Build vertical, not horizontal**: Startups should focus on domain-specific AI applications (like Harvey for legal) rather than competing with ChatGPT on general-purpose tasks. OpenAI will continue expanding general capabilities, but won't build verticalized products like AI sales agents, leaving massive opportunity for specialized builders.
2. **Context is all you need**: Prompt engineering is essentially a human communication skill -- the same techniques that help humans communicate effectively (providing background, specifying goals, giving examples) apply to LLMs. Models are eager to answer but lack context, so better inputs yield dramatically better outputs. Even small tricks like adding smiley faces can marginally improve performance.
3. **High agency and high urgency as hiring principles**: OpenAI's speed comes from hiring people who independently identify and solve problems without waiting for consensus from multiple departments. The Assistants API was born from engineers self-organizing around customer feedback, not top-down directives.
4. **GPTs as the gateway to AI adoption**: Custom GPTs solve the blank-slate onboarding problem of ChatGPT by packaging narrow, specific AI use cases that non-technical users can immediately understand and benefit from, potentially bringing the next hundreds of millions of users to AI.
5. **Plan for normalization, not revolution**: Each new model generation (including GPT-5) will feel revolutionary briefly, then quickly become normalized. Builders should plan for a world where people rapidly adapt to new capabilities, rather than assuming permanent disruption.
