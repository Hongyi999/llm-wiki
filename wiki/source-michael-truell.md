## Executive summary

Michael Truell, co-founder and CEO of Anysphere, describes Cursor's mission to invent a fundamentally new type of programming — a world 'after code' where engineers work as logic designers specifying intent rather than writing formal programming languages. Cursor hit $300M ARR just two years after launch through relentless product focus, dogfooding, and a counterintuitive decision to invest heavily in custom model development despite starting as a product company. Truell argues the future lies between two extremes: code remaining as-is and everything becoming a chatbot. Instead, he envisions a representation of software logic that's more like English pseudocode, where humans maintain full control while AI handles implementation. He sees the market resembling early search engines — winner-take-most, with continuous leapfrog potential — and believes taste and the ability to define what should be built will be the most valued skill.

## Key ideas

1. **A World After Code**: Truell envisions programming evolving away from formal languages (TypeScript, Python) toward a higher-level representation resembling pseudocode or English, where engineers specify intent and logic while AI handles implementation details. This is neither today's text editing nor a simple chatbot interface.
2. **Custom Models as a Counterintuitive Competitive Advantage**: Though Cursor started not expecting to do any model development, every 'magic moment' in the product now involves a custom model — from autocomplete (predicting multi-file edit sequences within 300ms) to code-base search (finding relevant code for foundation models) to output refinement (expanding high-level sketches into full diffs).
3. **Taste as the Key Future Skill**: As AI reduces the translation labor of turning ideas into software, 'taste' — having the right idea for what should be built and how it should work — becomes the dominant differentiator. This applies to both visual design and the logic of how software functions.
4. **Chopping Things Up vs. Going for Broke**: The most successful Cursor users break tasks into small, iterative chunks rather than trying to specify everything upfront. But Truell also encourages developers to experiment ambitiously in safe environments to discover the surprising limits of what models can handle.
5. **Hiring Too Slow as a Mistake**: Anysphere's biggest lesson was actually hiring too slowly despite being very intentional about quality. Their two-day work-test interview (a real project in their codebase) has been the most important hiring tool, persisting even at 60 employees.

## Concepts & frameworks

- [[ai-coding-tools|After Code]] — Truell's vision of a future where programming evolves from formal languages to a higher-level pseudocode-like representation that's more human-readable and editable.
- **Ensemble of Models** — The approach of combining frontier foundation models with custom-trained specialty models for different tasks (autocomplete, search, diff expansion) in a single product.
- [[taste-as-differentiator|Taste in Software]] — Having the right idea for what should be built — encompassing both visual design and the logic of how software works — increasingly valuable as implementation becomes automated.
- **Dogfooding** — The practice of using your own product intensely every day, which Truell credits as fundamental to maintaining realism about AI capabilities.
- **Background vs. Foreground AI** — The spectrum of AI coding from hands-on interactive editing (foreground) to spinning off autonomous tasks (background), with users wanting fluid movement between both.
