# Category Creation

The practice of inventing a new market category (name + language + budget line + analyst recognition) that a company can claim leadership of. Canonical treatment from [[source-barbra-gago|Barbra Gago]] (Pando/Miro/Greenhouse).

## The three validation inputs

A category only exists when all three are present:

1. **Budget** — Is there an actual line item customers will pay against? ("ATS" has budget; "recruiting optimization platform" didn't in 2018.)
2. **Language** — Are customers using consistent words for the pain? (Listen for it; don't invent it.)
3. **Competition** — Does another company also claim the category? Competition *validates*, not threatens. "You're not building a category until there is competition."

## Dual-track strategy

- **Sell into the existing category** where budget lives (ATS, performance management).
- **Build thought leadership for the new category** in parallel (employee progression, visual collaboration).
- **Bottom-up shift over time**, not a sudden switch.

## Canonical examples

- **Miro: "visual collaboration"** — Gago's success case. Replaced "whiteboard software" as the frame.
- **Greenhouse: "recruiting optimization platform"** — Gago's abandoned case. Customers and budgets stayed stuck on "ATS."
- **HubSpot: "inbound marketing"** — Classic category creation before the term existed.
- **Salesforce: "no software"** — Category creation via negative positioning.

## Tactics

- **Customer listening** — For language.
- **Analyst relationships** — G2/Gartner/Forrester as validators.
- **PR + thought leadership** — Educate buyers that a new budget line should exist.
- **Rename the problem**, not the product.

## When to abandon

When after 12-24 months:
- Customers still use the old name in conversations.
- Budget stays stuck in the old category.
- Analysts refuse to create a new Gartner quadrant.

Gago's advice: sell into the old category anyway. The failure mode is *trying to create a category without having the other two inputs.*
