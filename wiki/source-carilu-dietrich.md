# Source: How to achieve hypergrowth in your business and career | Carilu Dietrich (Atlassian)

**Guest**: Carilu Dietrich (former CMO/Head of Marketing at [[atlassian|Atlassian]] through IPO; advisor to Segment, Miro, 1Password, Bill.com, Sprout Social)
**Published**: 2023-04-30
**Duration**: ~1h 07m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Pm7QSWDIEUc

## Executive summary

Hypergrowth requires three elements: (1) an amazing product people love, (2) organic/viral word of mouth you can't buy, and (3) "riding the lightning" — hiring leaders 2–3 stages ahead because hypergrowth compresses 5–10 years into 1–2. Advertising amplifies but never creates demand. Atlassian's model — 2–3x more R&D spend than sales/marketing — proves PLG can scale without a traditional sales org, but demands the right product-market fit and buying behavior.

## Key ideas

1. **Advertising amplifies, doesn't create** — Oracle's front-page WSJ ads and Atlassian's HipChat campaign only work if the product is exceptional. Snowflake's sustained 101 billboard > Oracle's scattered millions. Consistency beats blitz.
2. **Atlassian PLG model** — 2–3x more spend on R&D vs. sales/marketing compared to peers. Sales only engages after product traction is demonstrated. No outbound cold calling; self-serve first.
3. **Hire 2x/3x leaders** — Hypergrowth compresses timelines so aggressively that you need leaders who've already seen the next stage. "Riding the lightning" means accepting that some hires will outgrow the role quickly.
4. **CMOs fail on trust gaps** — CMOs get fired when they can't speak revenue, board, or CEO language. Must master metrics, predict pipeline, think strategically about new markets, and endure downturns.
5. **Bundling kills PLG** — Single-product offers convert faster in self-serve; bundling requires evaluation and slows conversion. Bundling works for sales-led (discount incentive) but not PLG.
6. **Rule of 40 as company quality filter** — Carilu's post-it checklist: Rule of 40, investor caliber, NPS, net dollar retention, growth rate, burn rate, market position, Glassdoor ratings.
