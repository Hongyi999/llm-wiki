# Four Levels of PMF

A framework from [[todd-jackson|Todd Jackson]] at [[first-round-capital|First Round Capital]] that treats [[product-market-fit|PMF]] as a continuum rather than a binary, measured across **three dimensions**.

## The four levels

| Level | Name | What it feels like |
|---|---|---|
| **L1** | Nascent | Rumors of interest. The founder is pushing every rock uphill. |
| **L2** | Developing | A few users love it; usage is fragile and churn is real. |
| **L3** | Strong | Clear pull from the market. Retention and referrals both working. |
| **L4** | Extreme | Unambiguous, well-known PMF — the company is "blowing up." |

**60–70% of startups get stuck at L1 or L2**, usually because they mistake one strong dimension for PMF.

## The three dimensions

A PMF claim is only credible when it holds up across all three:

1. **Satisfaction** — do users love it? Measured via:
   - [[sean-ellis-test|Sean Ellis Test]] (≥40% "very disappointed")
   - NPS, qualitative "wow statements"
   - Retention curves (do they stick?)
2. **Demand** — is the market pulling? Measured via:
   - Organic growth rate
   - Referral / word-of-mouth share
   - Pipeline velocity
   - Inbound vs. outbound ratio
3. **Efficiency** — does the business work? Measured via:
   - Unit economics (LTV/CAC)
   - CAC payback period
   - Burn multiple
   - Gross margin trend

A product with strong **satisfaction** but broken **efficiency** is subsidy-driven and not yet PMF. A product with strong **demand** but weak **retention** is a press-cycle pump, not PMF. All three must move together.

## Benchmarks by level (qualitative)

- **L1 Nascent**: Sean Ellis score <20%. No repeatable acquisition channel. Founder is the sales team, and even they struggle.
- **L2 Developing**: Sean Ellis ~20–40%. Some organic pull. Efficiency is bad — CAC > LTV on most cohorts.
- **L3 Strong**: Sean Ellis ≥40%. Retention curves flatten. Multiple channels working. Unit economics turning positive on new cohorts.
- **L4 Extreme**: Sean Ellis ≥50%. Organic growth dominates. Customers self-serve. Efficient at scale.

## Required pairing: the [[four-ps-framework|Four Ps]]

Todd insists that measurement is only meaningful once the **Persona, Problem, Promise, Product** are explicitly written down. If the Four Ps are fuzzy, the numbers are measuring something uninterpretable.

## Key heuristics that go with the framework

- **[[dollar-driven-discovery|Dollar-driven discovery]]**: trust only real commitments.
- **Marginal customer lens**: look at customers 20–30, not 1–10.
- **Don't pivot on 10% signal, wait for 200% signal** (Jack Altman / Lattice).
- **"$100 vending machine"** metaphor (Brett Berson): efficient PMF should feel like a machine you can keep feeding.
- **4–6 year timeline** to L4 even for successful startups.
