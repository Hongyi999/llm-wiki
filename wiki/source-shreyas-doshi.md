## Executive summary

In this first-ever deep-dive interview, Shreyas Doshi unpacks five big ideas from his PM career spanning Yahoo, Google, Twitter, and Stripe. He introduces the pre-mortem ritual (tigers, paper tigers, elephants) for proactively surfacing project risks, the LNO framework for distinguishing leverage, neutral, and overhead tasks, and the three levels of product work (impact, execution, optics). He argues that most execution problems are actually strategy or culture problems, and that PMs should optimize for minimizing opportunity cost rather than maximizing ROI. A bonus sixth idea on high agency rounds out the episode.

## Key ideas

1. **Pre-mortems over post-mortems**: Instead of waiting for failure, teams should imagine a project has already failed and work backwards to identify tigers (real threats), paper tigers (false threats), and elephants (unacknowledged issues). This creates psychological safety and shared vocabulary that persists beyond the meeting.

2. **LNO framework for task prioritization**: Every PM task is either Leverage (10-100x return), Neutral (1x return), or Overhead (sub-1x return). The breakthrough is treating identical activities differently based on context -- the same bug report or set of meeting notes can be L, N, or O depending on strategic stakes.

3. **Three levels of product work -- impact, execution, optics**: Conflict between PMs and executives often stems from defaulting to different levels. Early-stage teams should optimize for execution, while recognizing that optics becomes the goal in organizations that over-reward status updates and presentations.

4. **Most execution problems are strategy or culture problems**: When teams are misaligned and bandaids keep falling off, the root cause is usually an absent strategy, a culture that punishes cross-team collaboration, or an interpersonal conflict between managers -- not a missing meeting cadence.

5. **Minimize opportunity cost, not maximize ROI**: ROI thinking drives teams toward quick wins because time is in the denominator. Opportunity cost thinking forces the question 'Is this the best use of my time?' -- a subtle but profound shift that Shreyas learned from observing Patrick Collison at Stripe.

## Concepts covered

- **Pre-mortem** — A team ritual where you imagine a project has already failed and work backwards to identify threats, using the taxonomy of tigers, paper tigers, and elephants.
- **LNO Framework** — A task classification system where L (leverage) tasks get perfectionist effort, N (neutral) tasks get adequate effort, and O (overhead) tasks get minimal effort.
- **Three Levels of Product Work** — Product work operates at impact, execution, and optics levels; misalignment on which level matters most causes friction between PMs and leadership.
- **Opportunity Cost over ROI** — In high-leverage roles, prioritize work that minimizes opportunity cost ('Is this the best use of my time?') rather than maximizing ROI, which biases toward quick wins.
- **High Agency** — The trait of finding ways to achieve goals without waiting for perfect conditions, combining ownership, creative execution, and resilience.
- **Placebo Productivity** — Intentionally doing neutral and overhead tasks for a day or two to build momentum before tackling a high-leverage task you have been procrastinating on.
