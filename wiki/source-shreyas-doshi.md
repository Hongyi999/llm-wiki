# The five big ideas of Shreyas Doshi

**Guest**: Shreyas Doshi

## Executive summary

Shreyas Doshi — the Twitter-famous PM philosopher with stints at Stripe, Twitter, Google, and Yahoo — delivers one of the highest-leverage episodes in B4: a deep tour through five of his most viral ideas. LNO as a prioritization framework, the "strong opinions weakly held" trap, pre-mortems as decision hygiene, the difference between high-agency and low-agency PMs, and the case for operating at a slightly higher altitude than your title. Shreyas's signature is ideas so compressed they feel obvious only in hindsight.

## Key ideas

1. **LNO framework (Leverage / Neutral / Overhead)**: Every task is L, N, or O. L tasks have 10x-100x return on quality; N tasks are linear; O tasks have zero upside past "done." Most PMs put A+ effort on O tasks (polishing status docs) and B- effort on L tasks (strategy memos). Flip that.
2. **"Strong opinions weakly held" is usually cargo-culted**: In practice it becomes "strong opinions loudly shared, rarely updated." Real version: hold calibrated beliefs, state uncertainty explicitly, and change your mind publicly when evidence shifts.
3. **Pre-mortems over postmortems**: Before launch, gather the team and ask "it's six months from now and this failed — why?" Pre-mortems surface risks while you can still mitigate them. Postmortems only surface what you missed.
4. **High-agency vs low-agency PMs**: High-agency PMs reframe "I can't because X" into "how can I, given X?" Low-agency PMs accept constraints as given. The difference compounds massively over a career.
5. **Operate one level above your title**: A senior PM who thinks like a director gets promoted to director. A senior PM who thinks like a senior PM gets stuck. Acting up-level is how you demonstrate readiness.
