# Source: Leading with empathy | Keith Yandell (DoorDash, Uber)

**Guest**: Keith Yandell (DoorDash; joined as Chief Legal Officer, later led HR, customer support, marketing, now BD/corp dev)
**Published**: 2023-02-09
**Duration**: ~55m 51s
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tlCK9TuALtk

## Executive summary

Yandell is the "generalist" poster child for Tony Xu's thesis that 10x outcomes come from non-specialists applying first-principles thinking to a function. The episode is a tour of DoorDash's distinctive culture (WeDash, "no politics / no assholes," founder humility at scale), Keith's operational habits (the "How to work with Keith" doc, helping reports find their next job, T3/B3 feedback), and hard-won lessons from near-death fundraising, IPO, and pandemic whiplash.

## Key ideas

1. **"How to work with Keith" manager doc** — Three sections: (1) expectations + traits of successful people at DoorDash; (2) Keith's own areas of growth / self-aware blind spots (he's a litigator and "argues to test conviction" even when he agrees); (3) commitments to the team (including "I will help you find your next job, even if it's not at DoorDash").
2. **"Are you an asshole?" hire story** — Keith called interviewee Ryan Sokol an asshole at a post-interview dinner. Ryan handled it gracefully, joined DoorDash, became VP Eng — and said the willingness to blackball him on culture grounds was why he joined.
3. **Generalists beat specialists for 10x outcomes** — Tony Xu's pitch to Keith (citing David Epstein's *Range*): specialists do things the way they've always been done, capping upside at incremental. Keith's formula: admit the knowledge gap in interviews, hire best-in-class subject-matter experts beneath you, add value on the sides, then get out of the way.
4. **Help your reports find their next job** — Keith forwards external recruiter pitches to his own direct reports. Second-order effect: blind references say "Keith puts you first," compounding over 5-10 years of hiring reputation.
5. **WeDash / customer obsession as hiring filter** — All employees do deliveries 4x/year. Story: his 8-year-old daughter called Tony Xu mid-delivery to complain about a bad route — Tony emailed the product org with a fix before they got home.
6. **Steel-man the other side + clarify the decision-maker** — Framework for unblocking C-suite deadlocks: ask each side to make the opposing case; clarify who the tiebreaker is; set a time horizon; move on.
7. **T3/B3 feedback ritual** — Three Tops, three Bottoms during reviews. Keith got chewed out by Travis Kalanick for only giving negatives — forced ritual creates safety for hard feedback in both directions.
8. **"Tough times make companies"** — DoorDash almost died pre-Series D. Scarcity benefits: mercenaries self-select out, unit-economics discipline, great hiring market, singularity of focus.

## B6 angle (hiring, management, leadership Part 2)

Yandell contributes several artifacts B5 voices don't have: (1) a literal, shareable "how to work with me" document — more concrete than [[source-ben-horowitz|Horowitz's]] aphorisms or [[source-alisa-cohn|Cohn's]] coaching framing; (2) the radical "help your reports leave" commitment, which extends [[source-jerry-colonna|Colonna]]-style compassion into a hiring-market strategy; (3) the generalist-leader thesis (non-expert leading a function of experts) — a useful counterpoint to [[source-heidi-helfand|Helfand]]/[[source-jonny-miller|Miller]]-style functional-expert career arcs. Pairs well with Horowitz on wartime CEO and with [[source-boz|Boz]]/[[source-rachel-lockett|Lockett]] on scaling people-management.
