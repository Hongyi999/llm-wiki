# Solution Deepening vs. Market Widening

The core roadmap tension [[rahul-vohra|Rahul Vohra]] surfaces in the [[superhuman-pmf-engine|Superhuman PMF Engine]]. Any feature-level decision can be classified into one of two buckets:

- **Solution deepening** — making your current lovers love the product more.
- **Market widening** — extending the product to people who don't love it yet.

The two are in tension because engineering time is finite, and optimizing for one class of user often degrades the experience for the other.

## The Superhuman rule

- Split the roadmap **roughly 50/50** between deepening and widening.
- **Bias toward deepening early.** Until the core persona is unambiguously locked in, widening dilutes the promise.
- Only widen once the "very disappointed" segment is large and stable enough to absorb the change.

## Why early-stage startups should deepen first

- **Love compounds.** Deeply loved products generate word of mouth, referrals, and tolerance for flaws. [[superhuman]]'s growth was primarily WOM.
- **Widening too early creates a "meh middle"** — a product that's fine for many and loved by none. This is the failure mode the [[sean-ellis-test|Sean Ellis Test]] catches.
- **The "very disappointed" segment is your compass.** Every deepening decision has a natural user to optimize for. Widening decisions don't — they're speculative by nature.

## How it plays with the Superhuman PMF Engine

1. Segment on "very disappointed" users → defines who to deepen for.
2. Tag "somewhat disappointed" reasons → defines what blockers to fix if you widen.
3. Roadmap allocation becomes a conscious decision rather than an accident of whoever shouted loudest.

## A related framing: the core vs. the edge

- **Core** = users who already love it. Deepening serves them.
- **Edge** = users who are curious but unconvinced. Widening serves them.
- Serving both requires explicit design decisions — which keyboard shortcuts to add vs. which onboarding hand-holding to add, for example.

## When to widen

- The core segment's retention is stable and their feature requests have plateaued.
- Further deepening shows diminishing returns.
- The blockers from the "somewhat disappointed" bucket are concentrated on a small number of issues that would unlock a known adjacent persona.
- Unit economics for the core are proven, and growth needs a larger TAM.

## When to keep deepening

- The core is still growing faster than you can serve them.
- Churn in the "very disappointed" segment exists at all — fix it first.
- You haven't yet reached 40% "very disappointed" on the [[sean-ellis-test|Sean Ellis Test]].
