# Good Strategy, Bad Strategy | Richard Rumelt

**Guest**: Richard Rumelt

## Executive summary

Richard Rumelt, a legendary strategy professor and author of Good Strategy Bad Strategy and The Crux, breaks down what strategy actually is and why most organizations get it wrong. He argues that strategy is fundamentally about solving problems — not setting goals, writing mission statements, or assembling laundry lists of priorities. A good strategy requires three elements (the 'kernel'): a diagnosis of the challenge, a guiding policy for addressing it, and coherent actions that implement the policy. Rumelt advocates reframing strategy as an 'action agenda' focused on the most important addressable challenge, powered by some source of asymmetric advantage. He emphasizes that the biggest barriers to good strategy are organizational dynamics, diffusion of effort, and leaders' reluctance to make hard choices about focus.

## Key ideas

1. **The Strategy Kernel (Diagnosis, Guiding Policy, Coherent Actions)**: Every real strategy must contain three elements — a diagnosis that explains the nature of the challenge, a guiding policy that sets the overall approach, and coherent actions that implement the policy without contradicting each other. If any element is missing, it is not a strategy.

2. **Strategy as Action Agenda, Not Goals**: Rumelt urges leaders to stop calling their wish lists 'strategy' and instead frame their work as an action agenda — a focused set of things they will actually do to address a specific, addressable challenge. Goals and ambitions are not strategies; they are starting points for identifying which challenges to tackle.

3. **The Crux — Focus on the Hardest Part**: Borrowed from rock climbing, the crux is the hardest move on a route. In strategy, identifying and solving the crux of the problem — the thing that makes the challenge hard — is where insight and breakthroughs come from. Great strategists immerse themselves in the difficulty until an insight emerges.

4. **Power and Asymmetry as Strategic Fuel**: Effective strategy requires a source of power — an asymmetry like proprietary knowledge, reputation, network effects, or a unique customer relationship — that tilts the odds in your favor. Without a source of power, you are making an even bet, which is not strategic.

5. **Organizational Dynamics as the Greatest Barrier**: The biggest obstacle to good strategy is not analytical — it is political. Diverse interests, fear of changing power structures, and the reluctance of leaders to make definitive choices cause organizations to produce incoherent, unfocused strategies. Someone must ultimately decide and direct.

## Entities discussed

- **Richard Rumelt** — Strategy professor emeritus at UCLA Anderson, author of Good Strategy Bad Strategy and The Crux, and strategy consultant.
- **UCLA Anderson School of Management** — Business school where Rumelt spent most of his academic career teaching strategy.
- **[[microsoft]]** — Referenced as an example of a company adapting its strategy to AI by investing in OpenAI and integrating AI into Bing.
- **IBM** — Used as a case study of leveraging customer relationships as a source of strategic power under Lou Gerstner, and later losing advantage in the cloud era.
- **Nokia** — Cited as an example of strategic failure due to diffused power in a matrix organization and replacement of engineering leadership with non-technical executives.
- **Clayton Christensen** — Author of The Innovator's Dilemma, recommended by Rumelt as essential reading on strategy.

## Concepts covered

- **[[strategy-choice-cascade|Strategy Kernel]]** — The three irreducible elements of a strategy: diagnosis, guiding policy, and coherent actions.
- **[[strategy-choice-cascade|Action Agenda]]** — Rumelt's preferred reframing of strategy as a concrete plan of what the organization will actually do about its most important challenge.
- **[[strategy-choice-cascade|The Crux]]** — The hardest part of a strategic challenge, borrowed from rock climbing — solving the crux is where design insight and strategic breakthroughs emerge.
- **[[strategy-choice-cascade|Strategic Power / Asymmetry]]** — A source of advantage (reputation, network effects, proprietary knowledge) that makes a strategy more than an even bet.
- **[[strategy-choice-cascade|Bad Strategy]]** — Strategy that substitutes goals for diagnosis, uses fluff language, contains contradictory actions, or lacks a clear understanding of the challenge.
- **Value Denial** — An exercise for identifying startup opportunities by asking what valuable things should exist but currently cannot be purchased.
