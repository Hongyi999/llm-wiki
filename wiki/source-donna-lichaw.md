# Source: How to discover your superpowers, own your story, and unlock personal growth | Donna Lichaw

**Guest**: Donna Lichaw (executive coach; author of *The Leader's Journey* and *The User's Journey*)
**Published**: 2024-02-25
**Duration**: ~1h 27m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7fA8DMMUNPI

## Executive summary

Counterintuitive case: effective leadership starts with the leader being the hero of their own story, not a user-centered focus on others. Stories we tell ourselves become self-fulfilling, so leaders should audit them with customer-research-style data (360s, peer interviews), lean into superpowers identified through peak-moment storytelling, and reframe "kryptonite" (imposter syndrome, ADHD, dyslexia) as functional rather than broken.

## Key ideas

1. **Be the hero of your own story** — Leadership energy must start inside-out; the user-centered "it's not about me" framing leaves leaders fragile and without fuel.
2. **Story-driven leadership with data** — Treat the stories you tell yourself like product hypotheses: validate them via 360s and conversations with colleagues; rewrite the ones that don't hold up.
3. **Superpowers emerge from peak-moment stories** — Forget StrengthsFinder lists; extract superpowers from concrete peak experiences across childhood, recent past, and your career — the patterns are more actionable than trait labels.
4. **Kryptonite is often functional** — Imposter syndrome, quietness, dyslexia, ADHD can signal growth edges or hidden strengths; ask "how does this serve me?" before eliminating it.
5. **Communicate your personal OS / README** — When the "story others tell about you" is the problem, explicitly share how you work (processing style, meeting preferences) to rewrite it.
