# This will make you a better decision maker | Annie Duke (Thinking In Bets, former pro poker player)

**Guest**: Annie Duke  
**Published**: 2024-05-02  
**Duration**: 1:21:01  
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=svQMODvIGAE](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=svQMODvIGAE)

## Executive summary

Annie Duke, former professional poker player and decision-making expert, shares battle-tested frameworks for improving organizational and individual decision quality. Her central thesis is that implicit intuitions must be made explicit to be examined and improved — a principle she has embedded at First Round Capital over five years, transforming their investment evaluation process with structured rubrics and forecasting. She argues there is no such thing as a long feedback loop (you choose its length by identifying correlated leading indicators) and that the purpose of meetings should be limited to discussion, with discovery and decisions happening asynchronously. Duke also covers pre-mortems with kill criteria, the 'nevertheless' leadership technique, and lessons from Daniel Kahneman on intellectual humility.

## Key ideas

1. **Discover-Discuss-Decide separation**: Meetings should only be for discussion. Discovery (gathering opinions) should happen asynchronously and independently to prevent cross-contamination, groupthink, and the loudest-voice-wins effect. Decisions should also be made outside meetings, ideally by a single decision-maker or via private voting.

2. **There are no long feedback loops**: Feedback loops are as long as you choose them to be. For any long-term outcome, there exist necessary-but-not-sufficient milestones and correlated signals that can be tracked much sooner. First Round now forecasts Series A probability at seed investment, shortening their feedback loop from 10 years to ~16 months.

3. **Make the implicit explicit**: Intuition is sometimes right and sometimes wrong. The only way to discover when it's wrong is to make judgments explicit through structured rubrics and scales. At First Round, this enabled tracking which partners are accurate on which dimensions (market, founder, product) and feeding that back for calibration.

4. **Pre-mortems need kill criteria**: Pre-mortems alone rarely change plans. Their real value is in establishing concrete kill criteria — observable signals paired with pre-committed actions (escalate, investigate, or kill). Without pre-committed actions, sunk cost bias will override rational stopping.

5. **Quitting is almost always too late**: Multiple biases (sunk cost, endowment, identity protection, desire for certainty) conspire to make people quit far later than they should. The Stewart Butterfield / Glitch-to-Slack story illustrates that quitting frees resources for opportunities that cannot even be seen while you're still committed to the failing path.

## Concepts covered

- **Nominal Group Technique** — Gathering opinions independently and asynchronously before group discussion to prevent cross-influence, conformity bias, and the loudest-voice-wins problem.
- **Kill Criteria** — Pre-committed observable signals paired with specific actions (escalate, investigate, or kill) established during pre-mortems to overcome sunk cost bias at decision time.
- **Feedback Loop Shortening** — The practice of identifying necessary-but-not-sufficient milestones and correlated signals to shorten any seemingly long feedback loop to months rather than years.
- **Nevertheless Leadership** — A leadership technique of fully hearing and reflecting back dissenting views, then proceeding with 'nevertheless, this is the decision' — balancing feeling heard with decisive action.
- **Resulting** — The error of judging decision quality by outcome quality; a good decision can have a bad outcome and vice versa due to luck and hidden information.
