# Source: What AI means for your product strategy | Paul Adams (CPO of Intercom)

**Guest**: Paul Adams
**Published**: 2023-10-26
**Duration**: 1:23:01
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R-Geamq9xc0

## Executive summary

Paul Adams, CPO of Intercom for over a decade, describes how his company ripped up its entire product strategy after ChatGPT launched and rebuilt from first principles around AI. He frames AI as a 'meteor coming towards you' that is bigger than mobile as a technology shift, and shares Intercom's journey building Fin, an AI-first chatbot that can answer 50-70% of customer support queries automatically. Adams offers a practical framework for assessing AI impact: map what your product does against what AI can do, then determine whether AI replaces or augments each capability. The episode is also rich with product frameworks -- differentiation vs. table stakes, the pendulum model, and product-market-story fit -- all drawn from hard-won lessons at Google, Facebook, and Intercom.

## Key ideas

1. **Map your product against AI capabilities**: Start with your product's core value proposition and ask 'Can AI do that?' For each capability, determine whether AI will fully replace it or augment it. Products involving workflows, multimedia, or text processing are directly in the path of disruption.
2. **Don't bolt AI on -- integrate it everywhere**: Drawing parallels to the mobile transition, Adams warns against creating isolated AI teams. Instead, every team member should learn about AI, similar to how mobile eventually became every product team's responsibility rather than a specialized function.
3. **Differentiation vs. table stakes roadmap allocation**: Products need both innovative differentiation (what attracts customers) and boring table stakes (what lets them actually adopt). Intercom has swung between 70/30 in both directions, learning that roughly 50/50 is the healthy balance for mature products.
4. **Product-market-story fit**: Having a great product in a great market isn't enough -- you need the right story. Many technically superior products (like Rdio vs. Spotify) fail because they can't clearly explain why they're better in ways customers care about.
5. **Adapt or die as operational mantra**: When Sam Altman publicly said customer service would be one of the first industries disrupted by AI, Intercom had already begun rebuilding. Their AI chatbot Fin now handles 50-70% of queries for some customers, with the biggest challenge being organizational change, not technology.

## Concepts & frameworks

- **Before/After Moments** — Adams' framework for identifying inflection points that fundamentally change the landscape, requiring companies to re-learn everything about their market.
- **Differentiation vs. Table Stakes** — A simplified Kano model for roadmap planning: differentiation attracts customers while table stakes let them actually adopt the product.
- **Swinging the Pendulum** — The pattern of over-correcting when fixing an undesirable state, applied to roadmap balance, hiring strategy, and AI investment.
- **Product-Market-Story Fit** — Extension of product-market fit adding story as an equally critical element -- the ability to explain why you're better in ways customers care about.
- **Four Forces of Jobs to be Done** — The push/pull framework for understanding product switching decisions, which Intercom's founders used to recruit Adams himself.
- [[product-velocity-ai-era|Ship Fast, Ship Early, Ship Often]] — Intercom's core product development principle embracing failure as a necessary byproduct of speed and learning.
- **Horizons Framework** — Strategic planning framework (H1/H2/H3) used at Intercom to debate whether current AI investments will be obsolete in 2 years.
