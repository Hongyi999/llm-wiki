# "I like being scared": Molly Graham's frameworks for rapid career growth | Molly Graham

**Guest**: Molly Graham
**Published**: 2026-01-04

## Executive summary

Molly Graham — founder of Glue Club, ex-Google/Facebook/Quip/CZI — has spent 18 years inside hypergrowth companies and distilled the experience into a compact set of durable frameworks for people and org scaling. Her thesis: the strategic problems of scaling aren't what break companies — the people and structural problems are. Leaders grow by repeatedly giving away what they've mastered, jumping into roles they're unqualified for, and resisting the gravity of holding on. "The cave you fear contains the treasure you seek."

## Key ideas

1. **Give away your Legos**: In a scaling company the Lego pile keeps getting bigger. Leaders who cling to the thing they just got good at get buried. Growth comes from repeatedly handing off your job and picking up the next unfamiliar pile — Molly "rehired herself every three weeks" at early Facebook.
2. **J-curve vs stairs careers**: Stair-step careers are predictable and ceiling-limited. J-curve careers require jumping off cliffs into roles you're unqualified for, falling for 6–9 months, and climbing to places stairs never reach. The fear to listen to is financial; the fear to ignore is "I'm scared I can't do this."
3. **The Waterline Model (snorkel before you scuba)**: When teams struggle, 80% of problems are structural (unclear goals, roles, success criteria) or team dynamics — not the people. Diagnose at the surface first. The manager's #1 job is clear roles and clear expectations.
4. **Six rules for goals**: No more than three company goals; one goal must win a fight; an intern on day one should understand them; "strategy should hurt" (make non-goals explicit); one goal, one owner; goals without a follow-up process are theater.
5. **Bob the monster and serve the business**: Externalize the "imposter" voice — name it Bob, let it pass, give it two weeks. When leading through emotional decisions (firings, reorgs, layering), always ask "what's right for the business?" — direct is kind.

## B5 angle

Near-complete playbook for the manager/leader side of B5. The six-rules-for-goals framework is immediately implementable. The J-curve model reframes career decisions for any hire asked to stretch. The Waterline Model prevents the common anti-pattern of firing a struggling IC when the real problem is structural.
