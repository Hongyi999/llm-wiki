# Source: Building Substack ft. Sachin Monga

**Guest**: Sachin Monga (Head of Product, Substack)
**Published**: 2022-10-30
**Duration**: ~1h 1m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zKP2HrMc23s

## Executive summary

Sachin Monga describes Substack's evolution from a single-player writing tool into a network-driven platform, anchored by a foundational principle: put writers and readers in control. The centerpiece is the Recommendations feature — a writer-curated, non-algorithmic discovery mechanism that has driven millions of new subscriptions and accounts for over a third of new subscriptions platform-wide. Sachin also reflects on the differences between building product at a high-growth startup versus a massive company like Facebook.

## Key ideas

1. **The Recommendations feature as a legendary growth lever** — Rather than building algorithmic "Substacks you might like" suggestions (the Facebook PYMK playbook), the team let writers manually choose which other newsletters to recommend. This preserved writer control while creating a viral goodwill loop: recommended writers see the inbound subscribers, prompting them to recommend others in return. The feature drives ~70% of Lenny's growth and millions of subscriptions across tens of thousands of writers.

2. **Writer control as a product principle** — Substack's founders start from a philosophical position: people should control their destiny on the internet, not be subject to ad-driven attention markets. This principle constrains product decisions — if a feature reduces writer or reader agency, it gets redesigned or shelved, even if a less principled version would ship faster.

3. **Build with writers, build with readers** — Before launching major features, the team runs a pilot with a small group of writers (now formalized as the "Product Lab," ~100 invite-only writers). The shipped version is often quite different from the original concept thanks to this feedback loop.

4. **Startup vs. big company product management** — At Facebook, prioritization is about permanent trade-offs (putting a Watch tab means no Marketplace tab). At a startup like Substack, prioritization is mostly about time-sequencing and unlocking future capabilities. The PM role at an early-stage company is more facilitator than decision-maker, especially when the founder has a strong product vision.

5. **Tool to network evolution** — Substack began as software for writers (single-player) and is evolving into an ecosystem with network effects: recommendations, community features, podcasting, guest posts, and a reader app. This mirrors the [[single-player to multiplayer]] transition discussed in other B10 sources.

6. **1,000 True Fans in practice** — Lenny reached ~$100K/year at 1,000 paid subscribers, validating Kevin Kelly's thesis. Sachin emphasizes that if you can find 1,000 paying fans, the network effects mean there are probably 2,000, 5,000, or 10,000. The key advice: just start, don't overthink whether people will pay, and don't underestimate your audience's willingness to support you.
