# Mastering product strategy and growing as a PM | Maggie Crowley (Toast, Drift, TripAdvisor)

**Guest**: Maggie Crowley
**Published**: 2023-11-05
**Duration**: 1:22:34
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4LjddcccYIo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4LjddcccYIo)

## Executive summary

Maggie Crowley (VP Product at Toast, formerly Drift and TripAdvisor) distills what actually separates great PMs from good ones and gives the most concrete strategy-doc template in the B4 batch. Her three traits — simplifying, following up on results, and carrying the water — cut across frameworks to describe observable behavior. She delivers practical warnings against "data-driven" dashboard worship, against mistaking online PM content for shipping reps, and for investing seriously in a personal brand once you have been in the game long enough to say something true.

## Key ideas

1. **Three traits of great PMs**: (a) Simplification — finding the one thing and staying with it; (b) Following up on results — instrumenting and broadcasting metrics weeks and months after launch; (c) Carrying the water — doing the boring, unglamorous work no one else will do.
2. **The strategy doc template**: Mission, landscape (competitors, SWOT, risks), current business goals, honest product assessment, technical hurdles, opportunities, challenges / what must be true, proposed solution (max 3 bullets), plan. Summary at the top (Minto Principle).
3. **"Data-driven" is a red flag**: Over-indexing on dashboards under-invests in qualitative research. Dashboards tell you what, never why. Great PMs combine quant with deep user understanding and judgment.
4. **Product content can be dangerous**: Frameworks shared online lose their nuance. Applying a framework is not the same as creating impact. Build intuition through shipping reps, not template collection.
5. **When in doubt, it's your job**: Unlike engineers (code) or designers (designs), a PM has no single deliverable — their deliverable is a business outcome, which means filling every gap.
