# Mastering product strategy and growing as a PM | Maggie Crowley (Toast, Drift, TripAdvisor)

**Guest**: Maggie Crowley

## Executive summary

Maggie Crowley, VP of Product at Toast and former Olympic speed skater, distills her hard-won lessons on what separates great product managers from average ones. She identifies three core traits -- simplifying relentlessly, following up on results, and carrying the water (doing the unglamorous work) -- as more predictive of PM success than the commonly cited strategy and communication skills. The episode also delivers a detailed walkthrough of how to write a product strategy document from scratch, a contrarian take on why 'data-driven' can be a red flag, and candid advice on how product content online can be both helpful and dangerous when applied without nuance.

## Key ideas

1. **The three traits of great PMs: simplify, follow up, carry the water**: The best PMs can find the single most important thing to work on and stick with it, proactively report results without being asked, and willingly do unglamorous work like QA, sales calls, and project management because they own outcomes, not just outputs.

2. **A bottom-up product strategy document template**: Crowley's strategy process starts with company mission, moves through a detailed landscape section (competitors, SWOT, risks, tech debt, customer feedback), identifies the top one or two opportunities, names the challenges, proposes a solution, and ends with a sequenced plan -- all shared openly for alignment.

3. **'Data-driven' as a red flag for product thinking**: Over-reliance on dashboards signals teams are neglecting qualitative research and user empathy. Talking to 10 users often yields more actionable insight than any dashboard, because data tells you what happened but not why.

4. **Product content can be dangerous when applied uncritically**: Frameworks from newsletters and podcasts are necessarily simplified and sanitized. The best PMs treat them as tools in a toolkit to deploy selectively, not as prescriptive formulas, and know when to throw them out entirely in favor of intuition.

5. **Building a personal brand accelerates PM careers**: Creating content forces reflection on your own work, which accelerates learning. It also opens networking, recruiting, and career opportunities -- but must be authentic and grounded in real experience to avoid being cringey.

## Concepts covered

- **Simplification as PM Superpower** — The ability to find the single most important thing to work on, communicate it clearly, and maintain focus over months -- the foundation of PM effectiveness.
- **Minto Pyramid Principle** — A communication framework where you lead with the conclusion first and support it with evidence -- critical for PM writing and executive communication.
- **Product Strategy Document** — Crowley's structured approach: mission, landscape analysis, honest product assessment, opportunity identification, challenges, proposed solution, and execution plan.
- **Carrying the Water** — The principle that PMs must do the hard, boring, unglamorous work (QA, sales calls, support) because they own outcomes and no one else will fill the gaps.
- **Data-Driven as Red Flag** — Crowley's contrarian view that teams proudly calling themselves 'data-driven' often over-index on quantitative metrics at the expense of user understanding and good judgment.
