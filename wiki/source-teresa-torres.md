# Build better products with continuous product discovery | Teresa Torres

**Guest**: Teresa Torres  
**Published**: 2022-10-06  
**Duration**: 48:18  
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9RFaz9ZBXpk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9RFaz9ZBXpk)

## Executive summary

Teresa Torres, author of the number-one most recommended book in Lenny's community (Continuous Discovery Habits), presents two interconnected frameworks for product teams: the Opportunity Solution Tree and continuous discovery. The Opportunity Solution Tree provides scaffolding for teams transitioning from output-focused to outcome-focused work, structuring the messy problem of 'start with an outcome and figure out what to build' into a visual tree of outcomes, opportunities (unmet needs/pain points/desires), solutions, and assumption tests. Continuous discovery means building feedback loops with customers every week — not through project-based research phases, but as an ongoing habit running in parallel with delivery. Teresa demonstrates her story-based interviewing technique live and argues that the product trio (PM, designer, engineer) should make decisions together through shared understanding rather than hierarchical authority.

## Key ideas

1. **Opportunity Solution Tree**: A tree visualization starting with an outcome, branching into the opportunity space (unmet needs, pain points, desires structured by experience map), then into solutions and assumption tests. Its power lies in helping teams decompose big evergreen problems into smaller, solvable opportunities while maintaining strategic coherence. 98% of people initially write opportunities as solutions — learning to stay in the problem space is the hardest skill.

2. **Continuous Discovery as Parallel Habit**: Discovery and delivery are not sequential phases — teams should always be doing both. Even one customer interview per week, done well, creates a continuous feedback loop. The goal is to make interviewing a recurring meeting that appears on your calendar automatically, using in-product opt-in intercepts or internal teams (sales, support) as recruiters.

3. **Story-Based Interviewing**: Instead of asking direct questions ('What do you like about Netflix?'), collect specific stories ('Tell me about the last time you watched something'). Stories reveal needs the customer is not even aware of, ground responses in actual behavior rather than abstract preferences, and produce dramatically richer insights. The sign of a good interview: the customer says 'When can we do this again?'

4. **The Product Trio Model**: PM, designer, and software engineer should decide together through shared understanding, not hierarchical authority. When trios disagree, they should seek a better option rather than defaulting to the PM as 'CEO of the product.' This requires unlearning siloed business culture, but teams that work this way have fundamentally fewer disagreements.

5. **Assumption Testing Over Whole-Idea Experiments**: Rather than running long, expensive experiments that test an entire idea, break ideas into underlying assumptions, prioritize the riskiest ones, and run small targeted tests. A well-functioning team can run half a dozen to a dozen assumption tests in a single week across three competing solutions.

## Concepts covered

- **[[continuous-discovery|Opportunity Solution Tree]]** — A tree visualization starting from an outcome, branching into opportunities (structured by experience map), then solutions and assumption tests — providing scaffolding for outcome-focused product work.
- **[[continuous-discovery|Continuous Discovery]]** — The practice of building ongoing customer feedback loops (at minimum one interview per week) that run in parallel with delivery, rather than doing research in project-based phases.
- **[[continuous-discovery|Story-Based Interviewing]]** — An interviewing technique focused on collecting specific past behavior stories rather than asking direct preference questions, revealing needs customers may not be consciously aware of.
- **Product Trio** — The model of PM, designer, and engineer making decisions together through shared understanding, eliminating the hierarchical 'PM as CEO of the product' anti-pattern.
- **Assumption Testing** — Breaking ideas into underlying assumptions, prioritizing the riskiest, and running small targeted tests — enabling teams to evaluate multiple solutions simultaneously in a continuous cadence.
- **Automated Interview Recruiting** — Using in-product intercepts or internal customer-facing teams to automatically schedule customer interviews, removing the recruiting burden from the product team.
