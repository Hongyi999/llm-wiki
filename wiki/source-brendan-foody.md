# Source: Why experts writing AI evals is creating the fastest-growing companies in history | Brendan Foody

**Guest**: Brendan Foody

## Executive summary

Brendan Foody built Mercor from $1M to $500M revenue run rate in just 17 months—the fastest ascent in history. The insight: frontier AI labs are bottlenecked not by compute or training data, but by evals—systematic ways to measure what success looks like for each model capability. Labs need expert humans (software engineers, investment bankers, doctors) to write evals and create post-training data. Foody established three core tenets (can-do attitude with ridiculous goals, ruthlessly high hiring bars, intense output-oriented culture) and argues that evals are the new PRD and sales collateral—showing what models can actually do.

## Key ideas

1. If the model is the product, the eval is the PRD: labs measure progress by running dozens of experiments against eval sets, making evals the bottleneck to improving model capabilities, not compute or data.
2. The bottleneck in AI is not job displacement but a new job category: experts writing evals and post-training data. The entire economy may become a reinforcement learning environment, but you still need humans to define what 'good' looks like.
3. Mercor went from $1 to $500M in 17 months by recognizing the market shift from low-skill crowdsourced labeling to high-skill expert vetting: matching experienced software engineers, bankers, and domain experts with labs that need capability measurement.
4. Set ridiculously ambitious goals (1.5M to 50M in run rate) and build org culture around hitting them; don't negotiate down based on external skepticism; intensity and high hiring standards compound fast.
5. Evals are becoming the new marketing for AI companies—they're the sales collateral showing researchers what's buildable and demonstrating efficacy to customers, not just internal measurement.
