# Product lessons from Waymo | Shweta Shrivastava (Waymo, Amazon, Cisco)

**Guest**: Shweta Shrivastava  
**Published**: 2023-04-09  
**Duration**: 0:42:16  
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VtNmAjNF3Tc](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VtNmAjNF3Tc)

## Executive summary

Shweta Shrivastava, Senior Director of Product Management at Waymo, discusses what it takes to build product for a fully autonomous driving system. She explains how Waymo uses deep learning trained on human driving data to create natural, trust-building vehicle behavior, and how their MVP bar is radically different from traditional software because safety is non-negotiable. The episode covers Waymo's dual-metric system spanning commercial KPIs (trips, active users, cost) and vehicle performance metrics (safety benchmarks, progress, stops-and-strands). Shweta also shares broader PM lessons from Amazon and Cisco, emphasizing working backwards from the customer problem, knowing what not to build, and the importance of listening and empathy as underrated PM skills.

## Key ideas

1. **Safety redefines MVP**: Unlike traditional SaaS products where you ship fast and iterate, Waymo's MVP bar for safety is extraordinarily high. You cannot cut corners on safety when building autonomous vehicles, which means the entire product philosophy of 'launch and learn' must be adapted to an environment where lives are at stake.

2. **Human-like driving through deep learning**: Waymo trains deep learning models on human driving data to create natural vehicle behavior, discarding bad driving patterns while preserving social norms like slowing on hills and nudging into lanes. The system must understand pedestrian intent, body language, road signs, and location-specific social norms to build rider trust.

3. **Dual metric system for autonomous vehicles**: Waymo tracks two categories of KPIs: commercial/operational metrics (trips per week, daily active users, operational cost) and system behavior metrics (safety performance benchmarked against human driving, stops-and-strands incidents, traffic impact). The goal is to be demonstrably safer than human drivers.

4. **Working backwards from the customer problem**: Drawing from her Amazon experience with the PR/FAQ process, Shweta emphasizes that all product management must start with the customer problem. She also stresses that knowing what you are NOT building is equally important to avoid the innovator's dilemma of incrementalism.

5. **Listening, empathy, and challenging assumptions**: Shweta identifies listening and empathy as the most underrated PM skills. She argues that if there is no conflict or contention in your team discussions, something is missing -- product leaders must proactively challenge their own assumptions and cultivate a beginner's mindset.

## Concepts covered

- **MVP in Safety-Critical Products** — The concept that minimum viable product standards must be dramatically elevated when human safety is a core requirement, as in autonomous driving.
- **[[working-backwards|Working Backwards]]** — Amazon's product development philosophy of writing a press release for the finished product before building it, forcing clarity on user value proposition.
- **Innovator's Dilemma** — Clayton Christensen's theory that large market leaders become incremental and vulnerable to disruptive upstarts, which Shweta argues leaders must proactively counter by disrupting themselves.
- **Autonomous Driving Levels (L2-L5)** — The SAE classification system for vehicle autonomy where L4 (Waymo's level) is fully autonomous without human driver intervention, while L5 covers any environment including off-road.
- **Trust-Building Through Naturalness** — The design philosophy of making autonomous vehicle behavior feel natural and human-like to build rider trust, including mimicking social driving norms.
