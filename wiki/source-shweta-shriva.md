# Product lessons from Waymo | Shweta Shrivastava (Waymo, Amazon, Cisco)

**Guest**: Shweta Shrivastava
**Published**: 2023-04-09
**Duration**: 42:16
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VtNmAjNF3Tc](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VtNmAjNF3Tc)

## Executive summary

Shweta Shrivastava, Senior Director of Product Management at Waymo (ex-AWS, Nauto, Cisco), describes what product management looks like when the MVP bar is "demonstrably safer than human drivers." She explains why Waymo committed to full autonomy from the start rather than building up from driver-assist, how trust is engineered by making the car behave with human-like "body language," and how dual metrics — commercial/operational alongside safety benchmarks — keep the team honest. She also offers cross-applicable PM lessons on working backwards (Amazon PR/FAQ), self-disruption, and the Rule of Seven for escalating from email to conversation.

## Key ideas

1. **MVP redefined for safety-critical products**: MVP still applies, but the minimum safety bar is enormously higher than in SaaS. You cannot cut corners on safety and iterate later.
2. **Trust through naturalness**: The car must feel human (slowing on downhill, merging "body language") for riders to trust it. Achieved by training deep models on human driving while filtering unsafe patterns.
3. **Dual metric categories**: Commercial/operational metrics (trips/week, DAU, cost) alongside system behavior (safety vs. human benchmarks per 100K miles, stops/strands, traffic impact).
4. **Long-term investor buy-in via commercial progress**: Show real commercial deployments, not just technical milestones. Investors see through short-term gimmicks.
5. **Disrupt yourself before someone else does**: Market leaders drift toward complacency. Product leaders must challenge their own assumptions proactively.

- **Trust through naturalness** — Engineering human-like behavior to build rider trust.
- **Dual-category metrics** — Commercial alongside system behavior as balancing constraints.
- **Working backwards (Amazon PR/FAQ)** — Start from the customer press release; see [[source-ian-mcallister]].
- **Innovator's dilemma / self-disruption** — Challenging your own assumptions proactively.

Demonstrates how core PM tenets (customer-centricity, MVP, working backwards, prioritization) apply to hardware+software products with non-negotiable safety requirements. The safety-first MVP framing is a valuable lens for any PM working on high-stakes products.
