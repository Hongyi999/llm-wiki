# Scripts for navigating difficult conversations | Alisa Cohn (executive coach)

**Guest**: Alisa Cohn
**Published**: 2025-01-05
**Duration**: 1:23:43
**YouTube**: [Lenny's Podcast — Alisa Cohn](https://www.lennyspodcast.com/)

## Executive summary

Alisa Cohn — executive coach to C-suite leaders at Venmo, Etsy, Wirecutter, DraftKings, Microsoft, Google, Pfizer, named a top-50 coach by Thinkers50 and #1 startup coach by Global Gurus — argues that managers avoid hard conversations because they confuse "making people happy" with leadership, and the avoidance kills careers, teams, and companies. Her antidote is rehearsed, neutral, observation-based scripts for performance feedback, denied promotions, firings, and defensiveness — plus the reframe that a leader's job is to create a *winning* culture, not a happy one. She also ships tactical artifacts (the founder prenup, personal operating manual, three end-of-meeting questions) that turn soft skills into reusable tools.

## Key ideas

1. **Scripts for difficult conversations**: Start with "what I'm hearing" or "what I've observed"; frame it as "we both know"; define a clear forward action. Lead with the bad news (don't bury it), use observable facts (not judgments), and pair with "hope for the future" — e.g., for a denied promotion: "I'm not going to promote you, but it's really important to me that you succeed in your career here."
2. **Your job isn't to make people happy**: Founders default to the loved-leader identity; the real job is driving results and building winning culture. Avoiding feedback isn't kindness — it leads to the employee's eventual firing with zero advance warning ("why didn't anyone tell me?").
3. **Pre-firing conversation + no-surprise culture**: Firing should never be a surprise. Have the "this is a deal breaker" conversation 30 days before termination: crystal-clear expectations, stakes, and a path — "I know you have it in you to change, but if you don't fix these things, we're not going to have a future together."
4. **Three questions to end every meeting**: (1) What did we decide here? (2) Who needs to do what by when? (3) Who else needs to know? Go around the room on question 1 — attendees often leave with six different interpretations of the same decision.
5. **The founder prenup and personal operating manual**: Per Noam Wasserstein, 65% of startups fail due to co-founder conflict. Align upfront on values, vision of success, conflict style, and decision-making when you disagree. Extend to the team via a Personal Operating Manual covering communication style, delegation preferences, gold-star behaviors, and pet peeves.
