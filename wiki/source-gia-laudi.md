# Source: Customer-led growth | Georgiana "Gia" Laudi (Forget The Funnel)

**Guest**: Georgiana "Gia" Laudi (ex-Unbounce, co-founder of Forget The Funnel)
**Published**: 2022-09-29
**Duration**: ~1h 7m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0FgZ1VVxEBo

## Executive summary

Funnels, pirate metrics, and MQL/SQL are antiquated because they center the business rather than the customer and ignore post-acquisition retention. Her consultancy's process — understand best customers via jobs-to-be-done research, map their value-centric journey with KPIs per milestone, then fix messaging/onboarding gaps — routinely doubles or triples conversion (SparkToro doubled trial-to-paid; one social tool saw 89% website + 40% trial-to-paid lift from messaging alone).

## Key ideas

1. **Funnels are gross; map the customer's value journey instead** — Inspired by Airbnb's "Project Snow White" storyboard (Brian Chesky → Disney biography → Pixar storyboard artist). ~6-milestone journey in three phases: struggle, evaluation, growth.
2. **Best customers = research panel** — Survey customers who are (a) getting value, (b) paying happily, (c) signed up 3-6 months ago. Ask about trigger moments, deal-breakers, must-haves.
3. **Prioritize one customer job, not personas** — Criteria: high willingness to pay, urgent, high retention/expansion, congregates in marketable segment, you have unfair advantage. SparkToro picked "marketers needing novel audience insights" over "data analysts."
4. **Proactive AND reactive onboarding** — For each milestone, fire in-app/email nudges plus win-back sequences. "70% of people log into a product once and never come back. The fact that so many companies don't have win-back always blows my mind."
5. **Messaging hierarchy from voice of customer, not founder intuition** — 5-7 page messaging guide becomes the guardrail. "You want to reflect back what they said they care about, not what you think is the coolest thing about your product."

## Notable quote

## B7 angle

Strongest counter-voice to pirate-metrics/funnel orthodoxy ([[source-sean-ellis-growth-hacker|Dave McClure, Sean Ellis]]). Converges with [[source-rahul-vohra-superhuman|Rahul Vohra's]] obsession with the "very disappointed" customer segment and [[source-casey-winters|Casey Winters]] on activation-first thinking, but uniquely pushes JTBD into marketing/messaging and stresses win-back sequences as retention lever. SparkToro case (doubled trial-to-paid without touching product) is a corrective to [[source-brian-balfour|Balfour]]/[[source-elena-verna|Verna]] bias toward product-led loops.
