# Source: Failure Stories Compilation ft. Multiple Guests

**Guest**: Multiple (Katie Dill, Paul Adams, Tom Conrad, Sri Batchu, Jiaona Zhang, Gina Gotthilf, Maggie Crowley)
**Published**: 2023-12-13
**Duration**: ~1h 1m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9euy9gC48lc

## Executive summary

A compilation episode pulling the most insightful failure stories from across Lenny's Podcast archive. The central thesis: failure is an undervalued source of wisdom, and the difference between productive and wasted failure lies in whether you design for conclusive learning. The stories range from freezing on stage at Cannes to building two of the most famous product disasters in tech history, all converging on the message that resilience and self-awareness matter far more than a spotless track record.

## Key ideas

1. **Earn trust before driving change (Katie Dill, Stripe/Airbnb)** — When Katie joined Airbnb as head of design, she "came in swinging" with changes based on interview research. A month later, five designers staged a formal intervention with written complaints. The root cause: she had not earned trust. She shifted to listening first, and within months the team had the best engagement scores in the company. Lesson: you can inflict change on people, but if you want to do it with them, trust is the prerequisite.

2. **Fear-driven products fail (Paul Adams, Intercom/Google)** — Google Buzz and Google+ were built from competitive fear of Facebook, not from genuine desire to solve user problems. Meanwhile, Google Maps and Android -- built from authentic product ambition -- thrived in the same company at the same time. Adams also froze on stage at Cannes in front of thousands, walked off cursing on a hot mic, then recovered. Lesson: the worst-case scenario is survivable.

3. **The equation matters more than execution (Tom Conrad, Quibi/Pets.com)** — Quibi bet that $2B could create a sufficiently large bespoke content library for a subscription mobile video service. It could not -- the real number was $6-10B. No amount of product craft could overcome a fundamentally broken business equation. Pets.com similarly failed due to over-funding enabling an irrational arms race, not because online pet retail was inherently unworkable (Chewy proved otherwise at $9B). Lesson: validate the math of the business model, not just the product experience.

4. **Fail conclusively (Sri Batchu, Ramp)** — Most experiments are repeated over and over because each attempt fails inconclusively, and every new executive re-runs the same test. To break the cycle, maximize the treatment effect: throw every tactic you can at the hypothesis. If it still fails, the hypothesis is dead. If it succeeds, then optimize costs. The failure to learn is the real failure, not the failure to grow.

5. **Don't be solution-first (JZ, Airbnb Plus)** — Airbnb Plus was born from competitive fear of managed marketplaces, leading to a solution-first approach (send physical inspectors to verify listings) instead of a problem-first approach (people want to know what they are getting into). The unit economics never worked because Airbnb lacked operational muscle. Better solutions existed: leverage reviews, send lockboxes, partner with local cleaners. Lesson: validate unit economics at day one, not at scale.

6. **Everyone has a B side (Gina Gotthilf, Duolingo)** — Behind every polished career narrative is a trail of firings, lost visas, depression, and failed experiments. Gina was fired and laid off multiple times before Duolingo. At Duolingo, over 50% of A/B tests failed. A badge experiment was delayed 6 months, then tested with a lame MVP that taught nothing. Only after adopting dogfooding and running a proper experiment did badges become one of the biggest growth levers. Lesson: careers are long; resilience compounds.

7. **Never rewrite (Maggie Crowley, Toast)** — A core product rewrite estimated at 6 months took 2.5 years, never reached feature parity, and was a total waste. Root causes: arrogance, skipped discovery, no one-pager, no technical research into actual requirements. Her best interview question: "What's the worst product you've ever shipped?" If a PM cannot answer honestly, they lack the reps.

## Concepts that deserve their own wiki page

- **[[Fail conclusively]]** — Sri Batchu's principle: design experiments to maximize treatment effect so failure kills the hypothesis permanently rather than leaving it ambiguous for future re-testing
- **[[A side / B side career]]** — Gina Gotthilf's framework: behind every impressive career highlight reel is a hidden trail of failures, rejections, and uncertainty that is rarely shared publicly
- **[[Trust before change]]** — Leadership principle: new leaders must earn trust through listening before driving organizational change; change inflicted without trust produces resistance

## Notable quote

> "At Facebook you can design the product, but at Intercom you can design the company." -- Owen (Intercom co-founder), as recalled by Paul Adams
