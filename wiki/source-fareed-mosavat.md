# How to build trust and grow as a product leader | Fareed Mosavat (Reforge, Slack, Instacart, Pixar)

**Guest**: Fareed Mosavat  
**Published**: 2022-10-23  
**Duration**: 1:05:10

## Executive summary

Fareed Mosavat, Chief Development Officer at Reforge, draws on his experience at Pixar, Zynga, Instacart, and Slack to explain why product management is uniquely difficult to master: there is no pre-training that guarantees success, and the only real path to growth is doing the work repeatedly. He introduces a PM learning loop of execute, generalize, communicate, and scale opportunities, arguing that each cycle builds trust and earns bigger responsibilities. Fareed details the 'manager death spiral' where new leaders hoard the best projects instead of delegating, and presents the 'product leader canyon' framework for crossing from IC to manager. He also outlines four types of product work (feature, growth, PMF expansion, and scaling) and explains why leaders must develop a portfolio across all four. The conversation closes with a discussion of the emerging trend of senior operators becoming fractional advisors rather than full-time executives.

## Key ideas

1. **The PM Learning Loop (Execute → Generalize → Communicate → Scale)**: Product managers grow by executing real work, generalizing those lessons into transferable frameworks, communicating insights to build organizational trust, and then earning larger opportunities with more unknowns. This loop never stops — even senior leaders are cycling through it at higher altitudes.

2. **Sponsorship Over Mentorship**: Career breakthroughs come less from day-to-day coaching and more from sponsors — senior leaders who deeply trust you and hand you outsized opportunities. Fareed credits Nabeel Hyatt, Merci Grace, and April Underwood as sponsors who gave him the chance to prove himself at progressively larger scopes.

3. **The Manager Death Spiral**: New managers often keep the most interesting, high-leverage projects for themselves while delegating grunt work to their reports. This blocks team growth, burns out the manager, and prevents them from becoming a leveraged leader. The fix is shifting from 'doer' to 'editor' and thinking in terms of minimum input for maximum quality.

4. **Four Types of Product Work**: All product leadership involves a portfolio of feature work (new capabilities for existing users), growth work (connecting more customers to existing value), product-market fit expansion (new audiences or new products for the same audience), and scaling work (trust, safety, and technical infrastructure). Leaders who only know one type will over-apply that lens to everything.

5. **Two Stack Levels Up, Two Down, and Sideways**: Great PMs cultivate deep curiosity about their boss's and boss's boss's priorities (up), the technical and implementation details beneath their work (down), and what adjacent teams are doing (sideways). This 360-degree awareness creates trust, enables better communication, and positions you as a connective node in the organization.

## Concepts covered

- **PM Learning Loop** — A cyclical framework for PM career development: execute on real problems, generalize learnings into transferable insights, communicate those insights to build trust, and scale into larger opportunities.
- **Manager Death Spiral** — The failure pattern where new managers hoard high-leverage projects, overwork themselves, block their reports' growth, and never develop true leadership leverage.
- **Four Types of Product Work** — A portfolio framework categorizing all product work into feature work, growth work, product-market fit expansion, and scaling work — leaders must develop fluency across all four.
- **Sponsorship vs. Mentorship** — The distinction between day-to-day coaching (mentorship) and the career-defining act of someone trusting you with outsized opportunities (sponsorship).
- **Two Stack Levels Up and Down** — A curiosity framework urging PMs to understand their boss's boss's priorities, the technical details two layers below their work, and what adjacent teams are doing.
- **Fractional Product Leadership** — The emerging trend of senior operators working across a portfolio of companies as advisors and fractional executives rather than committing full-time to a single role.
