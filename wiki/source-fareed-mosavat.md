# How to build trust and grow as a product leader | Fareed Mosavat

**Guest**: Fareed Mosavat
**Published**: 2022-10-23
**Duration**: 1:05:10
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oo0jSep7pzc](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oo0jSep7pzc)

## Executive summary

Fareed Mosavat (Chief Development Officer at Reforge; ex-Slack growth, Instacart, Pixar) maps the hidden middle of a PM career: the "Product Leader Canyon" between senior PM and VP/CPO where high performers stall because the job changes shape. He reframes management as a multi-loop learning game (the "PM Learning Loop"), distinguishes sponsorship from mentorship (the former is rarer and more career-defining), and offers a taxonomy of four kinds of product work that helps leaders staff teams without burning them out. The unifying thread: trust is built by what you do in weeks 1–90, and spent over years.

## Key ideas

1. **The Product Leader Canyon**: Between IC excellence and executive altitude is a canyon where the skills that got you here (shipping, analysis, judgement on features) stop being the job. The new job is managing leverage — systems, hiring, narrative — and most PMs fail because they keep doing the old job louder.
2. **PM Learning Loop slows as you climb**: An IC PM's loop (ship → measure → learn) runs in days. A director's loop (hire → develop → org outcome) runs in quarters. A VP's loop runs in years. Seniors who can't tolerate the longer loop regress to IC work and plateau.
3. **Sponsorship ≠ mentorship**: A mentor gives advice in a coffee chat. A sponsor spends their political capital to put your name in rooms you aren't in. Careers are made by sponsors; find them, and become one.
4. **Four types of product work**: (1) Feature work, (2) Growth work, (3) Scaling/infrastructure work, (4) Product-market-fit/0→1 work. Each requires different PMs, metrics, and timelines. Mixing them on one team without labels is the Manager Death Spiral.
5. **Trust compounds on a 90-day clock**: In a new role, the first 90 days set the trust trajectory. Over-communicate intent, ship something small but visible, and name the thing you'll be measured on before your boss names it for you.
