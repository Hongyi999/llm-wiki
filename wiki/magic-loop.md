# The Magic Loop

**The Magic Loop** is [[source-ethan-evans|Ethan Evans's]] five-step career-growth algorithm for ICs and middle managers. The bottom-up counterpart to founder-level leadership advice.

## The five steps

1. **Do your current job well** — Earn the right to more.
2. **Ask your manager how you can help** — "What do you need that's not getting done?" Genuine, open-ended.
3. **Do whatever they ask** — Even if it's unglamorous. Deliver.
4. **Link help to your own goals** — "Is there something you need that would also help me learn X?" Over time, the manager becomes a partner in your growth.
5. **Repeat** — Iteratively.

## Why it works

- **Managers help those who help them** — Human nature. Passive waiting for recognition is unreliable.
- **"The job before you have the job"** — Demonstrating next-level skills *while* in the current role is what makes promotion feel low-risk to decision-makers. When layoffs consolidate teams, the person already doing the next-level work is the one who gets more reports instead of fired.
- **Managers' own needs are accessible leverage** — They rarely say it spontaneously.
- **It's in your control** — "What your manager should do and $4 will get you a cup of coffee at Starbucks."

## Common failure modes

- **Opening with a demand** — "I want a promotion" before you've earned credits with the manager.
- **Transactional execution** — Doing help *only* to cash it in; managers detect this.
- **Stuck at senior manager without practicing next-level skills** — Expecting promotion without demonstrating strategy, inventiveness, influence.
