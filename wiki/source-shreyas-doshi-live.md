# The art of product management (Live) | Shreyas Doshi (Stripe, Twitter, Google, Yahoo)

**Guest**: Shreyas Doshi  
**Published**: 2022-08-25

## Executive summary

Recorded live at the Lenny and Friends Summit in San Francisco, this is Shreyas Doshi's second appearance on the podcast. Rather than a traditional interview, Shreyas shares three questions (plus a bonus) he wishes he had asked himself sooner in his PM career. He argues that PMs are chronically busy not because of poor time management but because of expanding scope and poor product decisions, that most PMs lack good 'taste' in the beliefs they adopt, and that frustration comes from operating misaligned with your superpowers. The episode closes with a provocative call to examine whether you are truly listening at a deep level.

## Key ideas

1. **Why am I so busy? Strategy eliminates planning overhead**: PMs drown in busyness not from poor habits but from unchecked scope growth. Shreyas found that having a real, aligned product strategy at Stripe reduced his annual planning from six weeks to three days, because strategy pre-answers most prioritization and resource questions.

2. **Most two-way doors are actually one-way doors for PMs**: Borrowing Bezos's metaphor gets teams into trouble because once a feature ships, QBR dynamics and stakeholder politics make it nearly impossible to kill. Pausing for two minutes, two days, or two weeks before committing prevents the accumulation of feature debt that makes PMs perpetually busy.

3. **Do I actually have good taste? Evaluate beliefs, not just pixels**: Good taste is not just about UI aesthetics but about the intellectual inputs a PM absorbs. Shreyas argues that PMs get seduced by catchy metaphors, alliterations like 'fail fast,' authority bias, and complicated charts, and that developing real taste means evaluating ideas independent of their social proof.

4. **Why does my job feel so frustrating? Align with your superpowers**: Product work operates at three levels -- impact, execution, and optics. Frustration comes from spending most of your time at a level that doesn't match your natural strengths. Shreyas chose to step back from managing 50+ people at Stripe because the optics-heavy work conflicted with his impact-level orientation.

5. **Am I really listening? Deep listening as leadership superpower**: Most PMs think they listen well because they make eye contact and recap, but Shreyas points to an entirely deeper level of listening, citing Rick Rubin and Peter Drucker, that unlocks world-class leadership and enables better product decisions.

## Concepts covered

- **Product Strategy as Planning Shortcut** — A real, aligned product strategy pre-answers most prioritization and annual planning questions, collapsing weeks of planning into days.
- **One-Way Door Illusion** — Most decisions that appear reversible (two-way doors) are effectively irreversible for PMs due to QBR dynamics, stakeholder politics, and accumulated feature debt.
- **Taste in Beliefs** — Good PM taste extends beyond product aesthetics to the intellectual frameworks, metaphors, and authorities a leader chooses to internalize.
- **Three Levels of Product Work** — All product work falls into impact, execution, or optics levels; frustration arises when PMs are forced to operate mostly outside their natural default level.
- **Deep Listening** — An advanced leadership skill beyond active listening techniques, involving genuine presence and receptivity that enables world-class decision-making.
