## Executive summary

This live-event episode flips Shreyas's usual format: instead of giving answers, he shares the three (plus a bonus) questions he wishes he'd asked himself earlier in his career. The questions force a different kind of self-reflection than tactical PM frameworks — they interrogate the relationship between ambition, craft, and suffering. Core themes: why product leaders feel so busy all the time, why taste is central and trainable, and why most PMs overestimate their listening skills.

## Key ideas

1. **"What am I actually optimizing for?"**: PMs default-optimize for visible progress (tickets closed, launches shipped) rather than learning velocity or long-term leverage. The question is a forcing function to restate the actual objective.
2. **"What would excellent look like here?"**: Before starting work, imagine the version of the output a PM you admire would produce. Most PMs ship the first acceptable version; this question anchors quality from the start.
3. **"Am I actually listening, or waiting to talk?"**: In meetings, most PMs are forming their response. Real listening — registering what's said, why it was said that way, and what wasn't said — is rare and taught by intentional practice, not by assumption.
4. **Bonus: "Whose permission am I waiting for?"**: High-agency PMs act. Low-agency PMs wait for invitations that often never come. The question names the invisible permission-wait and dissolves it.
5. **Taste is the fundamental hidden skill**: Every other PM skill (prioritization, storytelling, execution) routes through taste. Without taste, you optimize the wrong things more efficiently.
