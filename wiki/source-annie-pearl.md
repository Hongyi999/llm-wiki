# Behind the scenes of Calendly's rapid growth | Annie Pearl (CPO)

**Guest**: Annie Pearl  
**Published**: 2023-02-26  
**Duration**: 1:01:49  
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-tUIGpgmsZw](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-tUIGpgmsZw)

## Executive summary

Annie Pearl, CPO of Calendly, reveals how Calendly grew from a bootstrapped solo-user scheduling tool to a team-focused enterprise platform generating hundreds of millions in revenue. She describes how 70% of signups come through Calendly's viral loop (someone receives a scheduling link, tries the product, and signs up), and how the company layered a sales-led growth motion on top of its PLG foundation, growing SLG to 20% of ARR as its fastest-growing segment. Annie shares a practical product strategy framework from 'Playing to Win,' explains Calendly's four-phase product development lifecycle (discovery, solutioning, build, launch/measure/iterate), and provides detailed advice on transitioning into product management through four distinct paths.

## Key ideas

1. **Viral loop as growth engine**: 70% of Calendly's signups come through its inherent viral loop -- every time someone sends a Calendly link, the recipient experiences the product and often converts to a user themselves. This PLG foundation created organic growth that required zero marketing spend for the first users.

2. **Layering SLG onto PLG**: Calendly transitioned from 99% PLG revenue to building a sales-led growth motion that now represents 20% of ARR and is the fastest-growing segment. This required hiring inbound-oriented sales reps (not outbound hunters) who match the PLG-to-sales buyer profile of department heads rather than CIOs.

3. **Playing to Win strategy framework**: Annie uses the framework from the book 'Playing to Win' to define product strategy as an integrated set of choices: winning aspiration, where to play (markets, segments, personas), and how to win. She allocated resources across three horizons (70/30, then 50/50, then 30/60/10) as Calendly matured.

4. **Four-phase product development lifecycle**: Calendly structures development as discovery, solutioning, build, and launch/measure/iterate. Teams only commit to dates for the phase immediately in front of them, avoiding premature estimation on projects that haven't completed discovery and design.

5. **Four paths into product management**: Annie outlines formal APM programs, internal job board applications, shadowing/partnering with PMs (including SME programs), and joining early-stage startups as the four main routes into PM, with internal transfers being the most successful path she has personally facilitated.

## Concepts covered

- **Product-Led Growth (PLG)** — A go-to-market strategy where the product itself drives user acquisition, expansion, and conversion, exemplified by Calendly's viral scheduling link loop.
- **Sales-Led Growth (SLG)** — A go-to-market motion using direct sales teams to convert and expand accounts, which Calendly layered on top of its PLG foundation to move upmarket.
- **Playing to Win Framework** — A strategy framework from the book by A.G. Lafley and Roger Martin defining strategy as choices about winning aspiration, where to play, and how to win.
- **Product Qualified Lead (PQL)** — A lead identified through product usage data rather than traditional marketing signals, indicating a user or team is ready for a sales conversation.
- **Horizon Planning** — A resource allocation approach where investment is divided across near-term (horizon 1), medium-term (horizon 2), and long-term (horizon 3) product bets.
- **Competitive War Gaming** — Calendly's practice of assigning cross-functional groups to deeply study a competitor over a quarter, then present SWOT analyses and strategic implications.
