# Source: How to build a high-performing growth team | Adam Fishman

**Guest**: Adam Fishman (ex-Lyft, Patreon, Imperfect Foods; now [[source-fareed-mosavat|Reforge]] EIR)
**Published**: 2022-10-13
**Duration**: ~1h 5m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wP8YyWH524A

## Executive summary

First-principles competency model for hiring and developing growth talent. Deep case for onboarding as the single highest-leverage growth surface. "PMF for candidates" framework (People, Mission, Financials) for choosing where to work. Throughline: onboarding is the only experience 100% of users touch, the brand is the promise and the product is the delivery.

## Key ideas

1. **Growth Competency Model (four quadrants)** — Growth execution, customer knowledge, growth strategy, communication & influence; each with three sub-skills. "The goal is not to find a unicorn... it's to create a well-rounded team so you don't have any gaps in your portfolio."
2. **Onboarding is the ultimate lever** — "Onboarding is the only part of your product experience that 100% of people are ever going to touch." At Patreon, routing high-potential creators to humans then productizing those interventions lifted month-1/2 creator revenue by 25%.
3. **Opinionated defaults** — Make it easy to do the right thing, hard to do the wrong thing, without removing choice (Patreon's 3-5 tiers with $3-5 entry points). Mirrors Airbnb's "smart defaults" for host calendar setup.
4. **Onboarding is retention, not just conversion** — Done well, onboarding may slightly *decrease* signup conversion because it weeds out poor-fit users; retention gains more than compensate. Use proxy metrics (velocity to first-$100) instead of waiting 90 days.
5. **PMF for candidates (People, Mission, Financials)** — Evaluate jobs on all three; shortcutting any one has burned him. Do back-channel references, sit in on offsites/product reviews, ask "tell me about a recent strategic disagreement and how you resolved it."
