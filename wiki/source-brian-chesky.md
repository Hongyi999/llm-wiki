# Source: Brian Chesky's new playbook

**Guest**: Brian Chesky (co-founder/CEO Airbnb)
**Published**: 2023-11-12
**Duration**: ~1h 13m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4ef0juAMqoE

## Executive summary

Chesky walks Lenny through the operating-model rebuild he led at Airbnb after the pandemic — a deliberate move away from delegated, divisional, metrics-driven, AB-tested product management toward a founder-led, functional, launch-driven model inspired by Steve Jobs-era Apple. Core provocations: traditional product management has been merged into product marketing; there is one shared roadmap across the company; engineering/design don't report to product; the CEO reviews every project on a cadence. Being deeply "in the details" paradoxically gave him *more* time, not less, once the culture caught up.

## Key ideas

1. **Founder back in the details, and functional not divisional** — Traditional "delegate and empower" led to divisions, dependencies, advocacy, politics, bureaucracy, complacency. The fix: CEO as de facto CPO; functional org (design, engineering, product-marketing, marketing, comms, sales, ops); every exec a domain expert; as few layers as possible.
2. **One shared roadmap, two launches a year, metrics subordinate to calendar** — Rolling 2-year product roadmap updated monthly; releases every May and November; every project on the roadmap gets CEO-reviewed on a 1/2/4/8/12-week cadence. "Launches" force storytelling discipline and prevent incrementalism.
3. **Product management merged with product marketing** — "You can't build a product unless you know how to talk about the product." Program management offloaded to actual program managers; PMs drive neither design nor engineering but manage by influence. PMs are fewer and more senior.
4. **Performance marketing = laser; brand = chandelier; product marketing = education** — Airbnb cut $1B/year of paid-growth dependence and bet that a better product + better storytelling would grow organically. AB testing only with hypotheses and a clear treatment vs. control — never blue-vs-green — because you can't AB-test a whole house.
5. **Add a zero and set the pace** — "What would it take to 10x this?" forces first-principles thinking. Leadership pace is set by decisiveness, not hours: "If you want to improve speed, make faster decisions. Have a bias for action… let's check in an hour."
6. **Leaders-in-the-details ≠ micromanagement** — "If you don't know the details, how do you know people are doing a good job?" First 1-2 years brutally hands-on; after that, culture absorbed it, surprises flipped from 90% bad to 90% good, and he has *more* time now.

## Notable quote

> "Way too many founders apologize for how they want to run the company. They find some midpoint between how they want to run a company and how the people they lead want to run the company. That's a good way to make everyone miserable. What everyone really wants is clarity."

## B6 angle (hiring, management, leadership Part 2)

Chesky is the single most load-bearing B6 entry: his rebuild is a direct concrete counter-narrative to the "empower and delegate" orthodoxy that most B5 voices ([[source-ben-horowitz|Horowitz]], [[source-alisa-cohn|Cohn]], [[source-camille-fournier|Fournier]]) partially embody. He supplies the founder-mode operating manual — shared roadmap, launches, PM-as-product-marketing, CEO-as-CPO — that later sparked the "founder mode" discourse. Reads especially well against [[source-jason-fried|Jason Fried]] (same date range): both reject metrics-driven delegation, but Chesky keeps scale while Fried rejects scale itself.
