# Product-Market Fit

The state in which a product satisfies a real market demand strongly enough that growth becomes self-sustaining. Despite its name, PMF is **not** a single binary state — it is a spectrum with multiple dimensions, and it takes most successful companies 4–6 years to reach the top of that spectrum.

This is the root concept page. Specific measurement frameworks live on their own pages.

## Measurement approaches

- **[[sean-ellis-test|Sean Ellis Test]]**: single-question survey. ≥40% "very disappointed" is the benchmark. See also the extension in [[superhuman-pmf-engine|Superhuman PMF Engine]].
- **[[four-levels-of-pmf|Todd Jackson's Four Levels of PMF]]**: nascent, developing, strong, extreme — measured across **three dimensions**: satisfaction, demand, efficiency.
- **[[marketplace-liquidity|Marketplace liquidity]]**: for marketplaces, measured via fill rate, match rate, and market-health metrics like ETA.

For a side-by-side, see [[comparison-pmf-measurement-approaches]].

## Required clarity: the Four Ps

Todd Jackson's [[four-ps-framework|Four Ps]] argue that any PMF claim requires explicit answers to:
- **Persona** — who exactly?
- **Problem** — which burning pain?
- **Promise** — what outcome do you deliver?
- **Product** — how does the product embody the promise?

If any P is fuzzy, the founder is pre-PMF regardless of revenue.

## How founders discover PMF

Three disciplines show up repeatedly across sources:

1. **[[founder-led-sales|Founder-led sales]]** ([[jen-abel|Jen Abel]]): sales is research, not revenue. The founder must personally run the motion to learn what to build.
2. **[[dollar-driven-discovery|Dollar-driven customer discovery]]** ([[todd-jackson|Todd Jackson]]): trust dollars committed, not words. Words flatter; dollars qualify.
3. **Primary-benefit drill-down** ([[sean-ellis|Sean Ellis]]): ask "very disappointed" users what the main benefit is, and reposition around their answer. See the [[lookout|Lookout]] case study.

## Signals of real PMF

- **"Wow statements"** — users spontaneously use superlatives: "I'd cry if this went away."
- **Pull** — customers start scheduling their colleagues onto the next call unprompted.
- **Organic word of mouth** — referrals happen without referral programs.
- **Growing, widening problem** — the pain is getting worse in the world, not smaller.
- **Marginal customers look like ideal customers** — your 10th-most-recent customer, not your 1st.

## Anti-signals ("friend zone")

- "This is cool, keep me posted." (Rick Song, Persona — via Todd Jackson.)
- "What keeps you up at night?" questions answered differently each day.
- Long sales cycles that stall at "I'll email you."
- One strong dimension (e.g., satisfaction) masking two weak ones (demand, efficiency).

## Common traps

- **Conflating one dimension for PMF**: 60–70% of startups get stuck at Levels 1–2 in Todd Jackson's framework because they read one strong metric as PMF.
- **Happy ears**: founders hear what they want. Counteract with written notes and real commitments.
- **Pivoting on 10% signal**: wait for a 200% signal before abandoning (Jack Altman via Todd Jackson).
- **Skipping positioning**: you can't price or sell a product whose persona and promise aren't written down.

## Timeline

Todd Jackson is emphatic: even successful companies take **4–6 years** to reach "Extreme PMF." Impatience — not lack of talent — is the dominant failure mode.
