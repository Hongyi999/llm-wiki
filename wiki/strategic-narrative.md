# Strategic Narrative

A single story about a large undeniable shift in the world that a company is uniquely positioned to help customers navigate. Unlike a positioning statement or pitch deck, the strategic narrative is a **leadership alignment tool** that powers sales, marketing, product roadmap, recruiting, and fundraising simultaneously.

## Andy Raskin's framework

1. **Name the shift** — Identify an undeniable change from "old game" to "new game" (e.g., Zuora: transactions → subscriptions). This is not about your product; it's about the customer's world changing.
2. **Show winners and losers** — Companies already adapting are winning; those clinging to the old game are falling behind. Create existential stakes.
3. **Tease the promised land** — Describe the world the customer will inhabit before showing how your product gets them there.
4. **Introduce obstacles** — Frame problems as monsters preventing success in the new game, not as pain points your product fixes.
5. **Present evidence** — Named customer stories of reaching the promised land. Specific > generic.

## Dunford's critique

[[source-april-dunford|April Dunford]] explicitly critiques "trend-first" pitches (Raskin-style) as overused and often ungrounded. Her counter: start with competitive alternatives and differentiated value, not with a world-changing shift. The two approaches are complementary at different funnel stages — narrative for top-of-funnel/CEO alignment, positioning for mid-funnel sales execution.
