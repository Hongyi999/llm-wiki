# Source: A step-by-step guide to crafting a sales pitch that wins | April Dunford

**Guest**: April Dunford (positioning authority; author of *Obviously Awesome*; new book *Sales Pitch*)
**Published**: 2023-10-22
**Duration**: ~1h 31m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-VqmFI9vY7w

## Executive summary

Two-part sales pitch structure built on top of positioning: **Setup** (insight → alternatives' pros/cons → perfect-world criteria) then **Follow-through** (introduction → differentiated value → proof → objection-handling → ask). 40-60% of B2B deals die to indecision (not status quo), so the pitch's job is partly to *teach the customer how to buy* — reduce the champion's career risk. Heavy cross-reference to [[source-matt-dixon|Matt Dixon's]] JOLT Effect. Substantive rebuttal of Lochhead-style category-creation maximalism and Andy-Raskin-style trend-first pitches.

## Key ideas

1. **Two-part pitch architecture** — Setup is about the market, not you: state your insight, lay out alternative approaches with honest pros/cons, get agreement on "perfect world" criteria. Follow-through: introduce your category, walk differentiated value + how you deliver it, proof, objection handling, ask.
2. **Sales pitches are typically "glorified product tours"** — Feature dumps with no positioning. Fix: value-driven narrative tied to differentiated capabilities.
3. **Buying is harder than selling** — Champions have never bought your type of software. Teach them: give them a market map, purchase criteria, cover for IT/procurement/finance objections.
4. **Differentiated value = unique capability → repeatedly ask "so what?"** — Help Scout: built for digital businesses → CS as growth driver, not cost center.
5. **Arm the champion** — 5-7 person buying committees; pitch to the champion, pre-load them with answers for IT (security), finance (ROI calculators), end users (training).
6. **De-risking** — Money-back guarantees, phased rollouts, pro services as insurance — echoes Dixon's JOLT T step.
7. **Category creation skepticism** — Most legendary companies (Google, Facebook, Salesforce, Snowflake, Qualtrics) did NOT create their categories at launch; they did bowling-pin (Moore) niche-within-existing-category, then expanded once dominant.
8. **Test process** — Positioning in 1 week → involve sales + product → train ONE best rep → they iterate pitch live → when they'll never go back, it's done. End-to-end ~1 month. Reported: doubled first-call-to-opp conversion.
