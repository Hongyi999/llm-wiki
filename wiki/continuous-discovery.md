# Continuous discovery

**Continuous discovery** is the practice — popularized by [[source-teresa-torres|Teresa Torres]] — of making product discovery a weekly habit rather than a project phase. The minimum viable version: interview at least one customer every week.

## The weekly cadence

The cadence is the point. A team that interviews one customer weekly builds an intuitive model of their users that no amount of intensive quarterly research can match. Consistency beats intensity.

- **Weekly interview** — At least one.
- **Automated recruiting** — Interview recruiting should be set-and-forget (intercept at signup, calendar link, recurring outreach). Manual recruiting collapses the habit within weeks.
- **Trio attendance** — [[opportunity-solution-tree|The Product Trio]] attends together when possible.

## Story-based interviewing

The critical shift in technique: stop asking for opinions, start asking for stories.

- **Bad**: "Would you use a feature that X?"
- **Bad**: "What do you think of this idea?"
- **Bad**: "How often do you do Y?"
- **Good**: "Tell me about the last time you did Y."
- **Good**: "Walk me through what happened yesterday when you…"

Opinions are fiction. Stories are evidence. Past behavior is the only reliable signal of future behavior.

## The product-trio structure

Discovery is owned by the **Product Trio** — PM, designer, engineer — not by the PM alone. The three perspectives catch different assumption risks:

- **Viability** (PM)
- **Desirability + Usability** (designer)
- **Feasibility** (engineer)

Solo-PM discovery is the common anti-pattern. It produces half-built interview notes that the designer and engineer never read, and solutions that fail at the viability/feasibility seams.

## Relation to the Opportunity Solution Tree

Interviews feed the [[opportunity-solution-tree]]. Each interview adds or confirms opportunities (pain points), surfaces assumptions, and sometimes kills solutions. The tree is the artifact; the cadence is the habit.

## Common failure modes

- **Phase-gating discovery** — Treating it as a quarterly project kills the cadence.
- **Opinion-based interviews** — Asking users to predict their own behavior yields fiction.
- **Solo PM interviews** — Designer and engineer never build intuition.
- **Recruiting friction** — Manual scheduling collapses the habit fast.
- **No artifact** — Interviews without an updated OST are observations that don't accumulate.
