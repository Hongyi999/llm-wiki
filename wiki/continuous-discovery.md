# Continuous Discovery

Continuous discovery is a practice framework developed by [[source-teresa-torres|Teresa Torres]] in her book *Continuous Discovery Habits*. The core thesis is that product teams should have **weekly touchpoints with customers** — not occasional research sprints — to maintain a steady flow of insights that inform product decisions. Torres introduces the **Opportunity Solution Tree** as the visual thinking tool that connects business outcomes to customer opportunities to potential solutions.

## Core mechanics

### Opportunity Solution Tree (OST)

The OST is a visual map that structures product thinking:

1. **Desired outcome** (top) — the business metric the team is trying to move
2. **Opportunities** (middle) — unmet customer needs, pain points, or desires discovered through interviews
3. **Solutions** (bottom) — potential product changes mapped to specific opportunities
4. **Experiments** (leaves) — assumption tests for the riskiest assumptions in each solution

The tree forces teams to explore multiple opportunities before converging on solutions, preventing the "build the first idea" anti-pattern.

### Story-based interviewing

Torres advocates for **story-based interviewing** — asking customers to walk through a specific recent experience rather than asking about preferences or hypotheticals. This shares DNA with [[jobs-to-be-done|Bob Moesta's JTBD]] approach (interview switchers, not wishers) but is operationalized as a weekly habit rather than a project-based research method.

### Weekly cadence

The defining characteristic: teams should talk to at least one customer per week, every week. This is not a research project with a start and end date — it's a habit built into the team's operating rhythm, like standup meetings or sprint reviews.

## Relationship to adjacent concepts

- **[[jobs-to-be-done]]**: JTBD provides the theory of why customers "hire" products; continuous discovery provides the operational habit of learning about customers weekly. They're complementary — Torres's story-based interviewing is influenced by Moesta's methods.
- **[[product-operating-model]]**: Continuous discovery is the "how you solve problems" pillar of Cagan's model. Empowered teams need a discovery practice, and Torres's framework is the most widely adopted.
- **[[shape-up]]**: Shape Up's shaping phase serves a similar function (understanding the problem before committing to build) but operates on a 6-week cycle rather than a weekly cadence.
