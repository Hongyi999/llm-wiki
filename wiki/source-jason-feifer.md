# Source: How to get press for your product | Jason Feifer

**Guest**: Jason Feifer (editor-in-chief of Entrepreneur magazine)
**Published**: 2023-10-12
**Duration**: ~1h 42m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LZLdoAq3R7Y

## Executive summary

Demystifies media from the receiving end: editors don't care about you, they care about serving their audience, and most PR fails because it treats journalists as service providers. Three-step approach — prep, pick the right journalist, pitch — and "press" should be pursued only with a clear business goal, the way you'd raise money with a purpose.

## Key ideas

1. **Editors serve readers, not founders** — You get coverage by fitting the publication's mission (e.g., Entrepreneur = "magazine about thinking," not a directory of entrepreneurs); read the publication to reverse-engineer its mission.
2. **Three steps: prep, target, pitch** — Prep: know what the press is for. Target: identify the right writer (often freelancers) at the right outlet. Pitch: a concise, audience-relevant story — not a press release.
3. **Know what press is for (and when not to bother)** — Press is like fundraising: have a tactical reason (awareness, fundraising signal, positioning, partnerships). Chasing Entrepreneur for a DC hot-dog truck is wasted effort — use Eater or Washingtonian.
4. **Press releases are mostly dead; relationships matter** — Wire-distributed releases landing on Yahoo Finance are vanity metrics; a good PR person's only real asset is active relationships.
5. **The Butterie airport story as archetype** — The best pitches surface a counterintuitive business decision (market research in airport gates), not a product announcement.

## B8 angle

Reframes PR as a service-to-the-reader problem, not a self-promotion problem — pitching playbook maps cleanly onto B2B positioning: understand the buyer's mission before injecting your story. "Tiny random company + counterintuitive decision" archetype is a useful narrative template. Pairs with [[source-emilie-gerber|Emilie Gerber's]] press-as-recruiting/sales-collateral thesis.
