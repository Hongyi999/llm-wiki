# The happiness and pain of product management | Noam Lovinsky (Grammarly, FB, Thumbtack, YT)

**Guest**: Noam Lovinsky  
**Published**: 2024-03-17  
**Duration**: 1:09:35  
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a_W5Rn0bJWE](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a_W5Rn0bJWE)

## Executive summary

Noam Lovinsky, CPO of Grammarly, walks through hard-won lessons from an unusually diverse product career spanning YouTube (-1 to 1), Thumbtack (turnaround from negative growth), Facebook's NPE team (0 to 1 incubation), and Grammarly (scaling a profitable B2C subscription business). A central theme is that career growth comes from deliberately seeking uncomfortable situations that stretch you, paired with the self-awareness to know when to kill your own project or ask to be layered under another leader. Lovinsky argues that growth masks all problems and that PMs who have never experienced declining growth miss critical learning. He also shares practical advice on diversifying growth channels, building startup-within-a-startup incubation teams, and why Grammarly's 15-year stealth strategy of being underestimated proved to be a major competitive advantage.

## Key ideas

1. **Kill Your Own Project if Needed**: At YouTube, Lovinsky voluntarily argued against resourcing his own acquired-startup project and even asked to be layered under another leader. In healthy organizations, advocating for what is best for the business -- even at personal cost -- leads to better outcomes and career growth.

2. **Growth Masks All Problems**: At Thumbtack, triple-digit growth hid fundamental product issues like high-friction customer experiences and over-reliance on a single channel (SEO). Only when growth turned negative did the team confront the real problems. PMs who have never lived through declining growth miss a critical dimension of product judgment.

3. **Diversify Growth Channels Before Crisis**: Thumbtack's near-death experience came from depending almost entirely on SEO. The turnaround required rebuilding the growth engine across paid, referral, and organic channels simultaneously while also shifting the monetization model from pay-to-quote to instant booking.

4. **Incubation Requires Different Incentives**: At Facebook's NPE team, Lovinsky learned that standard six-month performance reviews kill 0-to-1 teams. Successful corporate incubation needs different time horizons, different infrastructure freedom, and different success metrics -- Nike's incubation lab is cited as a model.

5. **Grammarly's Stealth Advantage**: Grammarly was profitable from day one, intentionally stayed under the radar for 15 years, and succeeded because the product delivers value with zero user effort -- just install it and it makes you better across every text box you encounter.

## Concepts covered

- **[[growth-hacking|Growth Masks All Problems]]** — High growth rates hide fundamental product and business issues that only become visible when growth slows or turns negative.
- **[[growth-hacking|Smile Graph]]** — A growth curve that dips significantly before recovering, forming a U-shape -- indicating a successful turnaround.
- **Corporate Incubation** — Creating startup-like teams within large organizations with different incentives, infrastructure freedom, and evaluation time horizons.
- **Request-to-Book vs Instant Booking** — Marketplace transition from high-friction booking flows to instant transactions, a pattern seen at both Thumbtack and Airbnb.
- **Energy Audit for Career Decisions** — Prioritizing career moves that stretch you and cause growth, using personal energy and happiness as the primary decision filter.
