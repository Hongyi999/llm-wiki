# Lessons from one of the world's top executive recruiters | Lauren Ipsen

**Guest**: Lauren Ipsen

## Executive summary

Lauren Ipsen (ex-Daversa Partners, now at General Catalyst) translates 85+ executive placements into tactical advice on three fronts: how founders should hire senior product leaders, how PMs should manage their careers to maximize opportunity, and what recruiters get wrong. The through-line is "play the long game": stop chasing shiny logos, define the 90-day mandate before the search, and invest in breadth of experience over depth. Provided references are near-useless signal; back-channel references ("would you report to this person?") are the only reliable truth.

## Key ideas

1. **Don't chase shiny objects when hiring**: The CPO of a FAANG is often far from the work. Early-stage startups need someone closer to execution who can both operate as a PM and build a team. Hire for where the company is today.
2. **Define the mandate before the search**: Work backwards from the 90-day plan, one-year outcome, and the specific "flavor" of product leader needed (platform, core/consumer, or specialist/growth).
3. **Breadth of experience builds product leaders**: Seek diverse experience — platform, consumer, internal tools, different company stages. Specialists are valuable; breadth is the path to senior leadership.
4. **Back-channel references reveal truth**: Only 2 of Lauren's candidates had negative provided references. The real signal: ask back-channel references "Would you report to this person? Would you hire them again?"
5. **Play the long game**: The best hires often come from seven months of courtship — advising, casual conversations, building rapport — not transactional interview processes.
