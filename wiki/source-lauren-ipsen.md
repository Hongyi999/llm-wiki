# Lessons from one of the world's top executive recruiters | Lauren Ipsen (Daversa Partners, GC)

**Guest**: Lauren Ipsen  
**Published**: 2022-11-03  
**Duration**: 1:08:53  
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v3pofqabzhs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v3pofqabzhs)

## Executive summary

Lauren Ipsen, one of the most respected executive recruiters in tech having placed over 85 senior leaders, provides a three-sided tactical guide to product hiring. For founders, she warns against chasing 'shiny object' big-name executives who may be too far from the work, advocating instead for leaders with a chip on their shoulder who can both operate as a senior PM and build a team. For product leaders, she emphasizes breadth of experience and always keeping a pulse on the market regardless of job satisfaction. For recruiters, she argues that relationship-first, long-game approaches — remembering personal details, respecting timelines, genuinely listening — build the trust that is the true currency of executive recruiting.

## Key ideas

1. **Avoid the shiny object trap in hiring**: The biggest mistake founders make is pursuing famous executives from big companies who are too far from the work. The best hire for an early-stage company is often someone a level below the big name — closer to execution, hungrier, and able to both operate as a PM and build a team simultaneously.

2. **Define the mandate before starting a search**: Many searches are 'doomed from inception' because founders can't articulate what the hire will actually do. Founders must get granular on the front end: what is this person's specific mandate, what does crushing it look like, and what's the 90-day plan? Product leaders come in many flavors — platform, core product, and specialist (growth/monetization) — and picking the wrong archetype is a common failure.

3. **Always keep a pulse on the market**: Whether hiring or not, founders and PMs should continuously track which companies are thriving and which individuals are building great things. The best hires come from years of relationship-building — having informal coffee chats, engaging people as advisors, and being ready when timing aligns, rather than scrambling when a role opens.

4. **Breadth of experience creates future opportunity**: PMs aiming for leadership roles should diversify across platform, consumer, and internal tools work rather than specializing too narrowly. Combined with varied company stages (startup, growth, established), this breadth creates the versatility that head-of-product roles demand.

5. **Back-channel references reveal the real story**: Provided references almost always come back positive. The real signal comes from back-channel calls — the 'photos you're tagged in' versus your 'Instagram Reel.' Key questions include 'Would you report to this person?' and 'Would you hire them again?' Even a slight pause in the answer is telling.

## Concepts covered

- **Shiny object trap** — The tendency for founders to recruit famous executives from big-name companies who are too far from execution, when the actual need is for a hands-on builder who can also lead.
- **Back-channel references** — Unofficial reference checks conducted through the recruiter's network rather than candidate-provided references, analogized to 'photos you're tagged in' versus your curated Instagram.
- **Playing the long game in hiring** — Building relationships with great talent months or years before a role opens — through advisory roles, informal chats, and event attendance — so outreach feels natural rather than transactional.
- **Product leader archetypes** — Three common flavors of product leadership: platform/infrastructure, core/consumer product, and specialists in growth or monetization — each requiring different skill spikes.
- **Stating intent in hiring** — Ipsen's advice that recruiters must explicitly listen, remember personal details, and respect timelines to build trust — the same principle as PMs stating their intent when pushing bold ideas.
