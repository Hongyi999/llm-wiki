# Building a meaningful career | Jason Shah

**Guest**: Jason Shah
**Published**: 2022-09-18
**Duration**: 1:10:14
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nMsA5VeLoOM](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nMsA5VeLoOM)

## Executive summary

Jason Shah (product lead at Alchemy; ex-Amazon, Airbnb, Microsoft) shares a career philosophy organized around two axes — taste and trust — and a concrete set of tactics for accelerating both. His distinction between "ladder" careers (climbing an existing structure) and "map" careers (navigating terrain where no ladder exists) is the clearest frame in B4 for thinking about tech career decisions. He also gives one of the most usable accounts of Amazon's writing culture from an operator's perspective: writing isn't a chore, it's the highest-leverage thinking tool a PM has.

## Key ideas

1. **Ladder careers vs map careers**: A ladder career has a known next rung (senior → staff → principal). A map career has no prescribed rung — you're navigating terrain. Most PMs mix both; you should know which you're in for each decision.
2. **Taste is trainable**: Taste is the ability to know good from bad before anyone can prove it. You train it by exposure (reading, using great products) and by reps (making, shipping, critiquing). Work adjacent to people whose taste exceeds yours.
3. **Writing is thinking compressed**: Amazon's writing culture works because the act of writing a crisp document forces clarity of thought. Weak writers get away with vague thinking in verbal orgs; they don't at Amazon.
4. **Pushback is alignment**: Pushback isn't a threat — it's the surfaces alignment happens on. The worst meetings have no pushback because everyone has opted out. Make it safe to push back and debates become faster, not slower.
5. **Hiring is marketing + sales + product**: Top PMs get hired like top products get adopted — through clear positioning (your "brand"), a trusted funnel (relationships), and evidence of product quality (work artifacts). Don't just apply — build.
