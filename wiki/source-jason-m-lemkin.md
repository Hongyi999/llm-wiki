## Executive summary

Jason Lemkin, founder of SaaStr and veteran SaaS investor who's deployed $200M+ into B2B startups, tackles the black box of building a sales team for B2B SaaS companies. He challenges the founder assumption that sales is 'icky' and should be avoided in favor of product-led growth. Instead, he argues you must follow your first 10-15 unaffiliated customers—they reveal your true go-to-market motion. The episode covers when to hire your first salesperson (not too early, not too late), why you should hire two salespeople not one, comp structures, VP of sales timing, and critically, how to keep sales and product healthy. His core thesis: if early customers need sales effort and you deny it, you'll fail spectacularly.

## Key ideas

1. Your first 10-15-50 unaffiliated customers reveal your true go-to-market model, not your founding assumptions. If those customers need a sales motion (security questions, onboarding requirements, competition evaluation), you must embrace it—firing your sales team to chase product-led growth leads to catastrophic failure.
2. One founder he invested in had $5M ARR with 100%+ growth via sales. When things got hard, they fired the entire sales team because the founders found sales 'icky.' They collapsed to under $1M ARR with beloved customer logos. Many similar failures happen because founders choose ideology over data.
3. Hire your first salesperson only after you've personally talked to 10-15 customers and can describe their needs, objections, and decision process in detail. Going too early wastes capital; going too late means you miss market signals and growth.
4. Hire two salespeople, not one, from the start. A single salesperson can burn out and hit a ceiling. Two salespeople create culture, competition, and collaboration—and you can actually test repeatability of the sales motion.
5. As founder, your first 50 customers define whether you're a PLG product or a sales-driven one. Be honest with yourself. Ignoring this is the most common SaaS failure pattern.

## Concepts & frameworks

- **Product-Led Growth (PLG) vs Sales-Led** — First 10-15 customers reveal your true motion; forcing PLG when customers need sales creates cascading failure.
- **Founder Sales Bias** — Founders ideologically reject sales because they view it as 'icky,' causing them to ignore customer signals and misallocate GTM resources.
- **Repeatable Sales Motion** — You can only prove a sales motion works with 2+ salespeople; one person is an anomaly, not a pattern.
- **Timing First Sales Hire** — Hire after you've personally validated 10-15 customers and understand their buying cycle; too early wastes money, too late misses growth signals.
- **Comp and Hiring Structure** — Two sales hires from the start create culture and validate repeatability better than a single hire; compensation must reflect quota and base salary balance.
