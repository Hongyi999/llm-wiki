# Jobs-to-be-Done

Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD) is a framework for understanding why customers buy products by studying the causal story behind switching behavior. Rather than segmenting customers by demographics or asking what features they want, JTBD asks: "What progress was this person trying to make in their life when they hired this product?" The framework was developed over 27 years by [[source-bob-moesta|Bob Moesta]] and Clayton Christensen at Harvard Business School.

## Core mechanics

### Context and outcome, not pain and gain

Moesta's most forceful correction is that JTBD is **not** about pain points and gains — it is about the full **context** a person is in combined with the **outcome** they seek. A seemingly irrational purchase decision becomes rational when you understand the whole story. The Snickers vs. Milky Way example: same category, same shelf, completely different jobs (Snickers replaces a meal; Milky Way is a reward).

### Four forces of progress

Every purchase decision is governed by four forces:

1. **F1 — Push of the current situation**: Dissatisfaction with the status quo
2. **F2 — Pull of a new solution**: Attraction to something better
3. **F3 — Anxiety of the new**: Fear of change, switching costs, uncertainty
4. **F4 — Habit of the present**: Comfort with the familiar, inertia

If push + pull do not exceed anxiety + habit, the customer will not move. This means reducing friction (F3/F4) can be more effective than adding features (F2).

### Interview the switchers, not the wishers

Moesta insists on only interviewing people who have already made a change — bought a product, switched a service, quit a job. "Bitchin' ain't switchin'" — complaints don't predict action. The method uses interrogation-style techniques (per Chris Voss) with small samples (10-12 interviews) to uncover repeating causal patterns, typically revealing 3-5 distinct jobs.

## B3 appearances

- [[source-bob-moesta|Bob Moesta]] — The definitive JTBD episode. Moesta reframes the framework around context-and-outcome, demonstrates the four forces model, and shows how struggling moments (not features) seed the product roadmap.
- [[source-brian-tolkin|Brian Tolkin]] — Applied JTBD at [[opendoor|Opendoor]] to discover that home sellers hired the platform for **certainty** (knowing exactly when and for how much they'd sell), not speed — a finding that redirected the product strategy.

## Relationship to adjacent concepts

- **[[product-market-fit]]**: JTBD research identifies the job; PMF confirms you're doing it well enough. The [[superhuman-pmf-engine]] implicitly uses job-finding logic in its "main benefit" identification step.
- **[[founder-led-sales]]**: Jen Abel's manual-30 approach shares JTBD's insistence on talking to real switchers, not hypothesizing in conference rooms.
- **[[shape-up]]**: Ryan Singer (a Moesta collaborator) built Shape Up's "appetite" concept on JTBD's insight that scope should be shaped by the struggling moment, not by feature lists.
