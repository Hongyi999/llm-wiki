## Executive summary

Chip Conley, a hospitality veteran who joined [[airbnb|Airbnb]] at age 52 as Brian Chesky's in-house mentor, shares what it takes to thrive across generations in a tech company. He frames midlife as a chrysalis rather than a crisis, introduces the Peak Model (a Maslow-style hierarchy applied to employees, customers, and investors), and teaches how younger "fluid intelligence" combines with older "crystallized intelligence" to produce pattern-matching wisdom. After leaving Airbnb he founded the Modern Elder Academy (MEA) — the world's first midlife wisdom school.

## Key ideas

1. **Intergenerational collaboration as competitive advantage**: Younger brains excel at fluid intelligence (fast, focused, linear); older brains excel at crystallized intelligence (holistic, pattern-matching). Airbnb intentionally paired the two to produce better decisions than either could alone.
2. **Peak Model (Maslow for business)**: Employees need compensation (base), recognition (middle), meaning (top). Customers need expectations met, desires met, and unrecognized needs met. The "unrecognized needs" layer produced Airbnb's "Belong Anywhere" positioning.
3. **Invisible productivity of senior operators**: Older workers often make everyone around them better through mentorship, pattern recognition, and emotional regulation — a form of output that doesn't show up in individual velocity metrics.
4. **Working with founders in founder mode**: Align on goals and success criteria at the start of every meeting. Build credibility by getting close to customers. Keep slide decks short with combustible founders.
5. **Midlife chrysalis**: Midlife is a metamorphosis, not a crisis. Shifting mindset on aging from negative to positive adds 7.5 years of life (Becca Levy, Yale). The U-curve of happiness bottoms in the 40s and rises thereafter.
