# Lessons from working for a 20-something founder and intergenerational collaboration | Chip Conley (Airbnb, Modern Elder Academy)

**Guest**: Chip Conley  
**Published**: 2023-11-05  
**Duration**: 1:22:34  
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4LjddcccYIo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4LjddcccYIo)

## Executive summary

Chip Conley shares his extraordinary journey from founding the second-largest boutique hotel chain in the US (Joie de Vivre) to joining Airbnb at age 52 as a mentor to Brian Chesky, to launching the Modern Elder Academy. The episode explores what it means to be an older worker in tech, how to thrive through intergenerational collaboration, and the real talk on working for a founder in founder mode. Conley argues that older professionals bring crystallized intelligence -- connecting dots, pattern recognition, and emotional intelligence -- that complements younger workers' fluid intelligence. He also discusses his near-death experience, the U-curve of happiness, and why midlife should be seen as a chrysalis rather than a crisis.

## Key ideas

1. **Intergenerational collaboration unlocks compounding value**: Older brains excel at crystallized intelligence (connecting dots, holistic thinking, emotional intelligence) while younger brains have fluid intelligence (speed, focus, problem-solving). When combined on teams, this creates 'invisible productivity' where older members elevate everyone's performance without solving problems directly.

2. **Working for founders in founder mode requires strategic alignment**: To survive working under an intense founder like Brian Chesky, Conley recommends setting alignment on goals at the start of every meeting, building credibility through deep customer knowledge, and minimizing reliance on slide decks when the founder may take conversations off-script.

3. **Culture is what happens when the boss is not around**: The more distributed or remote a company is, the more important culture becomes. Conley distinguishes between 'culture fit' (which can exclude diverse candidates) and 'culture add' (which values what new members bring), and recommends asking interviewers for three to five adjectives that define the culture.

4. **The Maslow hierarchy applied to employees: compensation, recognition, meaning**: Using his Peak framework, Conley maps employee motivation as a pyramid with money at the base, recognition in the middle, and meaning at the top. Differentiation in retaining talent usually comes from the upper tiers, not just compensation.

5. **Midlife is a chrysalis, not a crisis**: Drawing on the U-curve of happiness research and Becca Levy's longevity studies, Conley argues that shifting your mindset from anti-aging to pro-aging can add 7.5 years to your life. Emotional intelligence, wisdom, and wholeness all improve with age, and anticipated regret is a powerful catalyst for action.

## Concepts covered

- **Intergenerational Collaboration** — The practice of pairing older and younger workers to leverage complementary brain capabilities -- crystallized intelligence (pattern recognition) and fluid intelligence (speed and focus).
- **Founder Mode** — A leadership style where founders maintain intense hands-on control and set aggressive expectations, popularized by Brian Chesky and Paul Graham.
- **Culture Add vs. Culture Fit** — The distinction between hiring people who simply fit existing culture versus those who bring new diversity and perspectives that enrich the culture.
- **Maslow's Hierarchy for Employees** — Conley's Peak framework mapping employee needs as a pyramid: compensation at the base, recognition in the middle, and meaning at the top.
- **Midlife Chrysalis** — Conley's reframing of midlife crisis as a transformative cocoon period where identity and purpose undergo metamorphosis.
- **Emotional Equations** — Conley's framework for expressing emotions as algebraic equations (e.g., Despair = Suffering - Meaning; Anxiety = Uncertainty x Powerlessness).
