# Making Meta | Andrew 'Boz' Bosworth (CTO)

**Guest**: Andrew "Boz" Bosworth
**Published**: 2024-03-03
**Duration**: 1:42:21
**YouTube**: [Lenny's Podcast — Boz](https://www.lennyspodcast.com/)

## Executive summary

Andrew "Boz" Bosworth — CTO of Meta and head of Reality Labs (AR/VR, Quest, Ray-Ban Meta), joined Facebook in 2006 as an early engineer, built News Feed, mobile ads, Bootcamp — argues that communication *is* the job of a leader: every artifact, silence, org chart, and tone choice is communication, and leaders take extreme ownership of miscommunication. Paired with this is a practical manager-leverage argument: employees consistently under-ask their managers for help, leaving huge value on the table. The conversation also covers Meta's transparency culture, Zuckerberg's strong-opinions-loosely-held decision loop, and career advice about variety, passion, and working on either the most important thing or holding up a critical dam.

## Key ideas

1. **Leverage your leaders more than you think you should**: The most common advice Boz gives is asking managers directly for help; a five-sentence "no response required" email lets managers clear obstacles, correct misaligned briefs, and invest political capital on your behalf. Silence costs months.
2. **Communication is the job**: Impact on the world only happens through artifacts and verbalizations. If a brief is misunderstood, the leader owns it — extreme ownership from the Marines. Org charts are communication tools; silence is communication; you cannot not communicate.
3. **Transparency has a tax but fuels leverage**: Meta's radical internal information-sharing unlocks talented people to act on wider context, but leaks violate team trust and signal-vs-noise management becomes a survival skill for newcomers.
4. **Career strategy: be in the Eye of Sauron or holding the dam**: The two highest-ROI places are on the company's most important project (smaller piece, high visibility) or carrying water on a critical but unattended area. Optimize for learning; move every six months until you find a role that keeps teaching you for years.
5. **Kindness beats "just being honest"**: Boz's biggest professional regrets were using honesty as a get-out-of-jail-free card. Feedback must be delivered with empathy for where the audience starts; being kind is not being nice — it's making the feedback productive.
