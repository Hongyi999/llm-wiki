# Source: The art of building legendary brands | Arielle Jackson

**Guest**: Arielle Jackson (9 years at Google on Gmail; early Square marketer who launched the Square Stand; now marketer-in-residence at First Round Capital, advised 100+ startups)
**Published**: 2022-08-18
**Duration**: ~1h 22m 34s
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mJEWns2hyDs

## Executive summary

Deeply structured playbook on naming startups, a 3-P brand framework (Purpose, Positioning, Personality), and PR. Has named 30+ companies (Seesaw, Maven, Eero) and codifies her process: naming briefs, seven criteria, the classic "for [target] who [need], [product] is a [category] that [benefit]" template, and the "bar test" for plain-language copy.

## Key ideas

1. **Name spectrum and preference for suggestive/evocative** — Descriptive (Internet Explorer) → suggestive (Chrome) → evocative (Seesaw, Maven) → empty vessel (Yahoo, Google, Eero). Empty-vessel names require more marketing dollars.
2. **Seven naming criteria** — Trademark, domain, distinctiveness, timelessness (avoid trends like "-ly"), reflect-your-positioning, sound/pronunciation, appearance and length.
3. **Naming process** — Positioning first, then naming brief, 1-hour brainstorm with 5-7 people (including linguists/writers), two-part structure, narrow 25 → 3-5, then trademark/domain. Use a ridiculous code name so you don't get attached.
4. **3-P brand framework** — Purpose ("we exist to ___", 10-year frame, zeitgeist-linked), Positioning (who it's for / their problem / current solution / what you make / 18-month frame), Personality (5 brand-attribute dimensions, "we are X but not Y").
5. **Classic positioning template** — "For [target] who [need], [product] is a [category] that [benefit]; unlike [alternative], we [differentiator]." Test: if 10 people at the company describe it 10 different ways, you have a problem.
6. **The bar test** — Read the positioning aloud as if telling a friend at a bar. If it includes "leverages" or "empowers," it fails. H1 on homepage should be what a customer would say ("Turn your iPad into a point of sale").
7. **Concentric-circle audience model** — TAM → target audience → named model persona (Eero's "tech-savvy dad" in suburban St. Louis).
8. **Personality uses 5 dimensions** (Jennifer Aaker): sincerity, excitement, competence, sophistication, ruggedness. Strong brands spike in two.
