# How to find work you love | Bob Moesta (Jobs-to-be-Done co-creator, author of "Job Moves")

**Guest**: Bob Moesta  
**Published**: 2025-02-23

## Executive summary

Bob Moesta, co-creator of the Jobs-to-be-Done framework alongside Clay Christensen, delivers a masterclass on understanding why customers really buy products. He reframes the framework away from pain-and-gain toward context-and-outcome, arguing that struggling moments — not features — are the seed of all innovation. Moesta introduces the four forces model (push, pull, anxiety of the new, habit of the present) that governs every purchase decision, and shares concrete examples from Snickers vs. Milky Way, Southern New Hampshire University, Intercom, and his own homebuilding business. He advocates for small-batch qualitative interviews (10-12 people) using interrogation-style techniques to uncover the irrational contradictions that reveal true demand, and warns that hypothesizing jobs in a conference room without talking to real people is the most common failure mode.

## Key ideas

1. **Context and outcome, not pain and gain**: The biggest misconception about JTBD is framing it as pain-and-gain. The real driver is the full context a person is in combined with the outcome they seek — a vector of progress that makes seemingly irrational decisions rational when you understand the whole story.

2. **Four forces of progress**: Every purchase decision is governed by four forces — push of the current situation (F1), pull of a new solution (F2), anxiety of the new (F3), and habit of the present (F4). If push and pull do not exceed anxiety and habit, the customer will not move. Reducing friction (F3/F4) can be more effective than adding features (F2).

3. **Interview the switchers, not the wishers**: Moesta insists on only interviewing people who have already made a change — bought a product, switched a service, left a job. 'Bitchin' ain't switchin'' — just because people complain does not mean they will act. The method uses interrogation techniques (per Chris Voss) to uncover the real causal story beneath surface-level explanations.

4. **Small samples, clustered not segmented**: Ten to twelve interviews typically reveal repeating causal patterns. Rather than segmenting customers by demographics, Moesta clusters them by sets of pushes, pulls, anxieties, and habits that work together as pathways — usually revealing 3-5 distinct jobs a product is hired to do.

5. **Struggling moments as the roadmap**: Instead of building a feature roadmap, teams should define the struggling moments they want to address. Features address the first 90-120 days; after that, struggling moments become the enduring seed for innovation because the context persists even as technology evolves.

## Concepts covered

- **[[jobs-to-be-done|Jobs-to-be-Done]]** — A framework that studies why people 'hire' products to make progress in their lives, focusing on the causal story behind switching behavior.
- **[[jobs-to-be-done|Four forces of progress]]** — Push, pull, anxiety of the new, and habit of the present — the four forces that together determine whether a customer will change behavior.
- **[[jobs-to-be-done|Struggling moment]]** — A context-specific moment where a person recognizes they must do something different, serving as the seed for all demand and innovation.
- **[[jobs-to-be-done|Demand-side sales]]** — Moesta's approach to designing the sales process around how customers buy rather than how companies want to sell.
- **[[jobs-to-be-done|Layers of language]]** — Interview technique recognizing three layers: pablum (surface pleasantries), fantasy/nightmare (exaggerated stories), and the actual causal story underneath.
- **[[jobs-to-be-done|Hire and fire criteria]]** — The specific trade-offs customers make when adopting or abandoning a product, revealing the true competitive set from the demand side.
