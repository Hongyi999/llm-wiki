# Source: Lessons from working with 600+ YC startups | Gustaf Alströmer

**Guest**: Gustaf Alströmer ([[y-combinator]] Group Partner; ex-Airbnb growth founder)
**Published**: 2023-03-02
**Duration**: ~1h 25m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZoKLofsp8u0

## Executive summary

Six years and 600+ companies of pattern-matching distilled: startups fail because they don't talk to customers and therefore don't find PMF — nothing else matters. Growth team thinking is actively harmful at the earliest stages (you can't "growth-team" your way to PMF). YC's job is mostly teaching founders which common failure modes to avoid.

## Key ideas

1. **"Make things people want" is still the whole game** — Overwhelming cause of YC startup failure is not talking to customers. Founders have external validators (investors, TechCrunch, accelerator acceptance) they confuse with PMF.
2. **The 10% early-adopter law of cold outreach** — 90% of any target customer group are NOT early adopters. You must reach 10 to find 1. Technical founders fear this rejection and hide in code instead of doing sales.
3. **Office Hours pattern: "What's slowing you down?"** — Single question in 1:1 Office Hours. Updates and strategy banned. In Group Office Hours: "What were your goals last 2 weeks, did you hit them, what got in the way?"
4. **You can't predict outlier winners, only failure modes** — YC's edge isn't picking winners; it's reducing preventable failure. Best signal mid-batch: each office hour brings genuinely new progress. Revenue graph at ~10% weekly growth.
5. **Technical co-founder is non-negotiable** — 90/10 equity split between "idea person" and engineer signals founder undervalues engineering. "I have an idea for a song, I just need a musician" is wrong mental model.
6. **Don't use growth-team thinking at pre-PMF** — Growth-team frameworks assume something is working. At day zero do unscalable things (Airbnb's NYC apartment visits). Growth mindset only becomes useful with thousands of users signing up weekly.
7. **Climate tech is a trillion-dollar capital reallocation** — IRA, corporate decarbonization mandates, software-transferable skills make climate the biggest opportunity.

## B7 angle

Deliberate contrarian: growth-team thinking is premature optimization at the pre-PMF stage where most readers actually live. Direct philosophical tension with [[source-brian-balfour|Balfour]] / [[source-casey-winters|Casey Winters]] (who assume traction to optimize) and convergence with [[source-rahul-vohra-superhuman|Vohra's]] obsession with qualitative feedback. "Watch people use the thing, don't ask them" beats [[source-sean-ellis-growth-hacker|Sean Ellis's]] survey-based PMF for very-early-stage.
