# Source: Why most public speaking advice is wrong—and how to finally overcome anxiety | Tristan de Montebello

**Guest**: Tristan de Montebello (co-founder Ultraspeaking; fastest-ever finalist in the World Championship of Public Speaking)
**Published**: 2024-10-13
**Duration**: ~1h 56m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BQM3Yq93nVc

## Executive summary

Reframes speaking as a *meta-skill* and subconscious flow process, not a conscious-framework skill. Most advice targets symptoms (filler words, monotone) rather than root causes (inability to pause, fear of taking space). His method: short rep-based games that create turbulence, plus two prep methods — the Accordion Method (speak from 3m → 30s → back up to 3m instead of writing/memorizing) and the Bow and Arrow (one sentence you want remembered, supported by story/data weight).

## Key ideas

1. **Speaking is a meta-skill with magical built-in hardware** — Babies learn to speak without instruction; adults have accumulated "bugs" (anxiety, hedging). Work on debugging, not adding frameworks. "If you don't enjoy speaking, you're doing it wrong."
2. **Three tactics: Think-up, End-strong, Stay-in-character** — Looking up while thinking looks thoughtful; end-strong counteracts the freestyle-rapper fMRI finding (flow collapses at the finish line); stay-in-character means don't leak insecurities — nobody sees them unless you announce them.
3. **Energy leads, emotions follow, words fill the gap (Conductor game)** — Tap into a "7" or a "2" state and the content auto-assembles. Different energy levels unlock different memory folders.
4. **Accordion Method** — Instead of writing → memorizing: deliver the talk at 3m, 2m, 1m, 30s, then back up 30s → 1m → 2m → 3m. Compresses to essence ("couch + lamp"), then re-expands intentionally. Talk becomes *internalized*, not memorized.
5. **Bow and Arrow — one sentence people will remember** — Shift from "what do I want to say" to "what do I want them to remember." The arrow = the single sentence; the bow = anecdotes/data adding weight. Every slide should pass the "one thing" test or shouldn't exist.
