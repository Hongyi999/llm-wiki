# Source: Rethinking SEO in the age of AI | Eli Schwartz (SEO advisor, author)

**Guest**: Eli Schwartz
**Published**: 2024-09-19
**Duration**: 1:55:10
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z71yGshPTwk

## Executive summary

Eli Schwartz, author of Product-Led SEO and advisor to companies like Zapier, Tinder, and Coinbase, argues that Google's rollout of AI Overviews is fundamentally reshaping SEO by swallowing top-of-funnel discovery queries while leaving mid-funnel intent-driven searches largely intact. He contends that this is actually good for both users and smart businesses because it forces a long-overdue shift from ranking-obsessed content factories toward genuine product-led SEO where PMs think about what users are actually searching for and build product experiences to match. Drawing on the Google antitrust verdict, he demonstrates that Google's 98% mobile market share and distribution agreements make it virtually impossible for ChatGPT or Perplexity to unseat Search, meaning SEO will evolve but not die.

## Key ideas

1. **Top-of-funnel SEO is being eaten, mid-funnel is the new battleground**: AI Overviews and LLM chatbots will absorb discovery-phase queries ("best beach vacations near me") by providing direct answers. But once users have direction, mid-funnel searches with purchase intent ("Cancun hotel reviews") remain search-engine territory where SEO can drive real conversions.
2. **SEO is a product discipline, not a marketing one**: Product managers should own SEO thinking because it requires understanding the user's self-discovery journey and building product experiences that serve search intent. The Zapier and Tinder case studies show that the best SEO strategies emerge from product thinking about user needs, not from marketing teams writing blog posts.
3. **Google's monopoly is unbreakable by quality alone**: The antitrust verdict revealed that even Neva, a demonstrably better search engine founded by a senior Google exec, failed because it lacked distribution agreements. DuckDuckGo grew from 1% to just 2% over 12 years despite massive investment. LLM startups face the same structural barrier.
4. **Programmatic SEO beats editorial when there's scale and user intent**: TripAdvisor and Zillow succeeded by combining data sources into programmatic pages that directly serve search queries. Editorial content (long-form blog posts) is exactly what AI Overviews will consume, while well-built programmatic pages that serve specific user needs remain valuable.
5. **Brand building is the new link building**: In an era where Google can read mentions without HTML links, building a genuine brand through product quality and PR is more effective than traditional link-building tactics. The distinction between structured and unstructured data is key — Google is turning unstructured content into structured answers, eliminating the middleman.

## Concepts & frameworks

- **Product-Led SEO** — The approach of treating SEO as a product discipline where you build experiences that serve search intent rather than writing content to chase rankings.
- **AI Overviews (AIO)** — Google's integration of LLM-generated answers directly in search results, consuming top-of-funnel queries and pushing organic results further down the page.
- **Programmatic vs Editorial SEO** — The distinction between data-driven pages at scale (Zapier integration pages) and hand-written long-form content, with programmatic being more resilient to AI disruption.
- **Search funnel disruption** — The shift where top-of-funnel discovery is absorbed by AI answers while mid-funnel intent-driven searches remain valuable for SEO.
- **Structured vs unstructured data in search** — Google's ability to convert unstructured content into structured AI-generated answers, disrupting businesses that monetized unstructured information.
