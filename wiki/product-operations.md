# Product Operations

**Product operations** (product ops) is a discipline and role that emerged around 2019 to handle the operational load PMs shouldn't own — voice-of-customer synthesis, tooling, cross-functional alignment, launch readiness — so PMs can stay focused on customers and engineers.

## Two definitions at once

- **As a *thing*** — Any system that helps a product team thrive.
- **As a *person*** — A PM-partner and strategic advisor to the head of product.

Most confusion about product ops comes from conflating the two. [[source-christine-itwaru|Christine Itwaru]] of Pendo is the canonical voice.

## Origin: Summer 2019

A perfect storm:
- Voice-of-customer sprawl (sales, success, research, NPS, support all capturing customer signal separately).
- Revenue-team misalignment with product.
- PLG adoption creating new data needs.
- Pandemic-era growth stretching every process.

CPOs moved from "deliver, deliver, deliver" to "hit business metrics," which required org infrastructure that PMs couldn't carry alone.

## What product ops actually does

1. **Voice-of-customer synthesis** — Across sales, success, research, NPS, support tickets.
2. **Tool stack for PMs** — Integrations (Pendo ↔ Salesforce ↔ Looker).
3. **Content/education strategy** — Definition of done includes in-app guides.
4. **Planning-process facilitation** — The agile-looking piece.
5. **Launch readiness** — The pattern that birthed product ops at Pendo: the gap between "they knew it was coming" and "they knew what to do with it."

## Product ops vs. product marketing

- **PMM** helps revenue teams *sell* / positions externally.
- **Product ops** helps revenue teams *understand* the product and their role in delivering value.
- "We don't teach them to sell. We don't teach them to position."

## Ops as growth, not Band-Aid

Christine's position: product ops is a sign of scaling maturity, not inefficiency. A counter to [[source-casey-winters_|Casey Winters's]] "ops as Band-Aid" take. But: the job of a good product ops person is to stand up the system, then automate or hand off and move to more strategic work.

## What PMs should never offload

> "Spending time with customers watching their pain. Then I'm going to say it — don't be in product."
