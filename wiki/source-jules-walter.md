# Source: Leveraging mentors to uplevel your career | Jules Walter (YouTube, Slack)

**Guest**: Jules Walter (YouTube Primetime Channels; first growth PM at Slack; co-founder Black Product Managers Network, CodePath)
**Published**: 2023-01-05
**Duration**: ~1h 10m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zn2JNbZwf00

## Executive summary

Walter argues PM progression is a long, deliberate practice of building specific skills one at a time, and mentors are the single highest-leverage accelerant — but only if you approach them with small, specific asks and close the loop after taking their advice.

## Key ideas

1. **IQ early, EQ later** — Early-career PMs win on execution, product sense, strategy, and interview skills. Senior-career progression stalls on EQ: communication, leadership, management, self-awareness. "EQ stuff is things I've been working on for years... it's like lifting weights."
2. **Interview like a skill, not a test** — "How many mock interviews have you done?" Answer is almost always zero. Walter interviewed at Google repeatedly over a decade before getting in; companies don't give feedback. Extra burden for underrepresented candidates walking into panels where "nobody looks like me."
3. **Work backwards from a learning outcome** — Pick a target outcome as forcing function ("ship experiments that lift activation X% in 6 months"), generate questions, read just enough to refine, take refined questions to the world's best experts. How he learned growth at Slack from Bangaly Kaba.
4. **The mentor-ask ladder** — Smallest possible first ask (two-minute email question), close the loop by reporting what worked, then escalate. "That's the thing nobody does." Never open with "Will you be my mentor?"
5. **Double down on strengths; watch their shadow** — Litmus: "What do people keep saying you're good at, but you think it's not a big deal?" "A fish doesn't think swimming is special." Every strength has a shadow in a different context.
6. **Reverse-engineer artifacts** — Save great exec emails, strategy docs, presentations; crash PM exec-review meetings. Underrated perk of big-company work is access to real artifacts IC PMs elsewhere never see.
7. **Make feedback radically safe** — "Enthusiastically grateful" externally. Ask in specific, falsifiable form ("to what extent did I show executive presence?") and pre-criticize yourself so they can agree.

## B6 angle (hiring, management, leadership Part 2)

Walter adds a *lateral* dimension B5 mostly lacks: mentorship-as-system and equity-of-access to leadership craft. Where [[source-ben-horowitz|Horowitz]]/[[source-alisa-cohn|Cohn]]/[[source-elizabeth-stone|Stone]] speak from the CEO chair, Walter speaks as an IC/manager who *built his own curriculum*. Uniquely contributes (a) specific tactics for underrepresented operators, (b) the smallest-possible-ask framework for building a mentor network from scratch, (c) the feedback-vulnerability loop that surfaces truth the calibration-room version usually won't.
