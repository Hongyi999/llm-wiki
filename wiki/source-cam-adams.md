# Source: Inside Canva: Coaches not managers, giving away your Legos, and embracing AI | Cameron Adams

**Guest**: Cameron Adams

## Executive summary

Cameron Adams, co-founder and CPO of Canva, reveals the product philosophy and cultural practices behind one of tech's most impressive growth stories: $2.3B ARR, profitable for seven years, growing 60% YoY, and used by 95% of the Fortune 500. He describes how Canva replaced traditional management with a coaching system of 800+ internal coaches, embraced "giving away your Legos" as a cultural pillar for scaling, and took a deliberately anti-Lean-Startup approach by spending a full year building an MVP that would genuinely delight users. On AI, Adams outlines a three-pillar strategy — build proprietary AI where you have data advantages, partner with best-in-class providers like OpenAI for commodities, and open an app ecosystem for third-party AI developers.

## Key ideas

1. **Coaches not managers — 800 internal coaches at scale**: Canva replaced traditional managers with specialty-matched coaches who focus exclusively on individual growth, skill development, and career pivoting. Performance feedback comes from 360 reviews by collaborators, separating the growth function from the evaluation function in a way that scales to 4,500 employees.
2. **Giving away your Legos as a scaling principle**: Borrowed from Molly Graham's framework, Canva institutionalized the practice of constantly handing off responsibilities to grow into higher-leverage work. Adams personally presents this in every cultural onboarding session, framing it as essential for the transition from individual contribution to team building and systemic impact.
3. **Delight-first MVP over Lean Startup speed**: Despite pressure from investors during the Lean Startup era, Canva spent a full year building before launch to ensure the product sparked genuine joy. The insight was that organic word-of-mouth — their primary growth driver for years — required users to be excited enough to tell others, which a minimum-viable product wouldn't achieve.
4. **Three-pillar AI integration strategy**: Build proprietary AI models where you have unique data advantages (design and image models), partner with world-class providers for commodity capabilities (OpenAI for LLMs, RunwayML for video), and open an app ecosystem so third-party AI developers can reach Canva's 170 million monthly users.
5. **Product-led SEO through end-to-end user journey design**: Canva's legendary SEO strategy mapped jobs-to-be-done to search queries, then designed the complete flow from Google query through landing page to onboarding to magic moment. The key was that SEO and product experience were inseparable — you can't SEO a bad experience.

## Concepts & frameworks

- **Giving Away Your Legos** — The practice of handing off responsibilities you enjoy to make room for higher-leverage work as a company scales, even when it means losing personal identity tied to that work.
- [[ai-native-organization|Coaching culture at scale]] — Canva's system of replacing traditional management with specialty-matched internal coaches who focus on personal growth, separate from performance evaluation.
- **Visual thinking culture** — Canva's organizational principle that ideas must be communicated visually through mockups and prototypes rather than through discussion or documentation alone.
- **Blank page fear in onboarding** — The insight that creative tool onboarding must overcome users' fear of a blank canvas by guiding them through tiny delightful steps that build into surprising accomplishments.
- **Internationalization as growth multiplier** — Canva's strategy of localizing into 100+ languages within two years of starting, which unlocked massive growth in markets like Brazil, India, and Indonesia.
