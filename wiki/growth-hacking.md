# Growth Hacking

The discipline of running growth as an experimental, engineering-style function rather than traditional marketing. The term was coined by [[sean-ellis|Sean Ellis]] when he was trying to hire someone for a role that didn't exist yet.

## Origin

Sean needed to hire people who would think about growth the way engineers think about code: as a system of hypotheses, experiments, and iterations. Traditional "marketing" candidates optimized for brand, awareness, and campaigns. Sean wanted candidates who would run A/B tests on signup flows, instrument funnels, and reason about unit economics. He couldn't find the role title, so he invented one: **growth hacker**.

## Principles

- **Growth is an engineering discipline.** It is a sequence of testable hypotheses with measurable outcomes.
- **Every lever in the funnel is in scope.** Acquisition, activation, retention, referral, revenue — the whole AARRR pirate funnel.
- **PMF gates growth.** Growth hacking after PMF is fuel; growth hacking before PMF is a leaky bucket. This is why [[sean-ellis-test|the Sean Ellis Test]] is prerequisite.
- **ICE prioritization**: rank experiments by **Impact × Confidence × Ease**.

## Related practices from the interview

### North Star Metric
Pick one number that captures **value delivered to the user**, not just activity. Everyone in the company optimizes toward it.

### Activation as the hidden bottleneck
[[logmein|LogMeIn]] had healthy satisfaction but only 5% activation. Fixing onboarding took activation to 50% and unlocked growth. Lesson: never skip activation diagnosis.

### Double-sided referral (the [[dropbox|Dropbox]] template)
Reward both sides in the **product's own unit of value** (e.g., storage). Cheap to run, tightly aligned with the value the user already cares about.

### Primary-benefit drill-down
Ask "very disappointed" users for the **main benefit** they receive, then reposition around that benefit. This is how [[lookout|Lookout]] moved from 7% to 40%+ by shifting from "antivirus" to "find my phone."

## The backlash

The term "growth hacking" was sometimes misread as **spam tactics** or **dark patterns**. Sean's original framing has nothing to do with that — it's about rigor, measurement, and experiment discipline.
