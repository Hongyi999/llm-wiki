# A framework for PM skill development | Vikrama Dhiman (Gojek)

**Guest**: Vikrama Dhiman  
**Published**: 2024-05-12  
**Duration**: 1:12:19  
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ImSvm11GR0Y](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ImSvm11GR0Y)

## Executive summary

Vikrama Dhiman, head of product at Gojek, presents a comprehensive career growth framework for product managers built around three dimensions: what you produce (outputs → outcomes → direction), what you bring to the table (quality of artifacts across data, design, tech, and strategy), and what your operating model is (communication, collaboration, organizational and community skills). He argues that early-career PMs should focus on tangible outputs rather than chasing strategic impact, and that mid-career stalls often stem from shifting focus to things outside one's control, slowing one's rate of change, or clinging to self-limiting identity narratives. The conversation also covers practical advice on transitioning into PM roles, the importance of pairing complementary skill development, and three operating tenets for working with stakeholders.

## Key ideas

1. **Three W's career growth framework**: PM career growth depends on what you produce (outputs before outcomes before direction), what you bring to the table (quality of artifacts proving impact on impact), and what your operating model is (communication, collaboration, organizational skills, community). The strongest PMs excel on at least two of three; rising PMs perform well on all three.

2. **Outputs before outcomes**: Early-career PMs should obsess over tangible outputs — shipping products, running experiments, preparing briefs — rather than trying to define strategy. Even senior PMs must never abandon their IC roots; continuing to produce high-quality artifacts earns credibility across the organization.

3. **Three operating tenets for PMs**: Raise difficult issues without being difficult to work with. Bring out important topics without drawing importance to yourself. Be in charge of getting decisions made without making all the decisions yourself. These are easy to articulate but require a career of practice.

4. **Self-limiting identity stories**: PMs often adopt labels like 'high-agency PM' or 'collaborative PM' that serve as both signals and anti-signals, excusing weaknesses. Recognizing and correcting these self-narratives — e.g., shifting from 'high agency' to 'mindful agency' — can unblock stalled growth.

5. **Complementary skill pairing for transitions**: When developing PMs, pair their existing strength with its complement: design/research-background PMs should develop data/tech skills, and vice versa. This creates maximum leverage and has proven successful in Gojek's PM development pipeline.

## Concepts covered

- **Three W's Framework** — A PM career growth model organized around what you produce, what you bring to the table, and what your operating model is.
- **Output-Outcome-Direction Ladder** — The progression from tangible outputs (shipping) to outcomes (goals) to direction (strategy), with the imperative to never abandon earlier rungs.
- **Impact on Impact** — Demonstrating personal contribution through high-quality artifacts (PRDs, product notes, experiment analyses) rather than riding a product area's success.
- **Rate of Change as Growth Driver** — Career growth is a function of how fast you are improving your skills; mid-career stalls come from slowing your rate of change rather than from lack of opportunity.
- **Self-Limiting Identity Narratives** — Labels PMs adopt (e.g., 'high-agency') that simultaneously signal strengths and excuse weaknesses, creating invisible growth ceilings.
