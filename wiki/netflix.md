# Netflix

**Netflix** is the streaming platform famous in the management world for its unusually explicit and extreme culture document — the "Netflix Culture Memo" co-authored by Reed Hastings — which later inspired a generation of talent-density and candor-based operating models.

## Culture stack (as described by [[source-elizabeth-stone]])

Three nested layers, each enabling the next:

1. **High talent density** — Hire additive people who upgrade the team on arrival; pay *personal top-of-market* so comp isn't golden handcuffs.
2. **Candor** — Continuous, direct, specific feedback (privately when corrective); "last 5%" pursuit of excellence is a daily practice.
3. **Freedom and responsibility** — Minimal process; people are trusted with expensive decisions because the first two layers are in place.

Remove any layer and the others collapse.

## Signature practices

- **[[keeper-test|Keeper test]]** — "Would I fight to keep this person if they tried to leave?" Operates continuously in lieu of traditional performance reviews.
- **No performance reviews** — Replaced with continuous feedback and the keeper test; 360 cycles used for structured input.
- **Context, not control** — Leaders share meeting notes and strategic context broadly; teams make local calls.
- **Centralized data + consumer insights** — Data engineers, data scientists, analytics, and research sit in one org rather than embedded in business lines; preserves objectivity and career mobility.
- **Personal top-of-market comp** — Paying the individual's market price, not a role's percentile.
- **Additive hiring** — Every hire must upgrade some dimension of the team.
