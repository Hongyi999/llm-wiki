# Zero-Interest-Rate Phenomenon PM

A diagnostic coined by [[source-casey-winters_|Casey Winters]] for a generation of product managers trained during the ZIRP (zero-interest-rate phenomenon) era of 2012-2021. They learned PM in FANG-style abundance — copious research, design, and analyst support — and now freeze under uncertainty.

## The diagnostic

> "I can't even begin to come up with solutions until I see all the data and talk to customers."

A ZIRP-PM takes this as a responsible principle. Casey argues it's a sign of atrophy. The real job is making decisions under uncertainty and shipping to learn.

## What ZIRP-trained PMs get wrong

- **Treat frameworks as coloring books**, not toolkits. "Tools in a toolkit, not a coloring book to stay inside the lines of."
- **Over-use research**. Research is a scarce resource; burn it only on big, ill-defined problems. Redesigning a login page? Copy FANG.
- **Confuse interview performance with the job**. "They want to be criminals more than they want to commit crime" — candidates better at interviewing than at the job.
- **Demand cross-functional support before shipping**. Slack with a full team, Figma with a designer, a research partner, a data partner — when the job often requires going first alone.

## Why this matters now

Post-ZIRP, companies can't afford extensive support structures. The PMs who thrive are those who can:
- Decide under uncertainty.
- Ship small tests to learn.
- Use frameworks as starting points, not deliverables.

Skip practiced answers. Throw real scenarios. Watch them decide under uncertainty in the room.
