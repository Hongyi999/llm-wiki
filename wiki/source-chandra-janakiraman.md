# An operator's guide to product strategy | Chandra Janakiraman (CPO at VRChat, ex-Meta, Headspace)

**Guest**: Chandra Janakiraman  
**Published**: 2025-01-26  
**Duration**: 1:47:22

## Executive summary

Chandra Janakiraman, CPO at VRChat and former product leader at Meta, Headspace, Zynga, and Amazon, presents a battle-tested five-phase playbook for developing product strategy that he has refined across five to six implementations. The process takes 8-12 weeks and moves through preparation (gathering behavioral insights, UXR, leadership interviews, competitive analysis), a strategy sprint (generating problems, clustering into opportunities, ranking on four criteria to select three strategic pillars), a design sprint (creating illustrative concepts), document writing, and rollout. Janakiraman distinguishes between 'small-S' strategy (present-forward, problem-focused, 2-year horizon) and 'big-S' strategy (future-back, aspirational, 5-10 year horizon led by design). He illustrates both with examples from Zynga's three strategic pillars and Meta's Reality Labs, showing that strategy has no intrinsic value — only execution reveals whether it works.

## Key ideas

1. **Five-phase strategy process**: Preparation (4 weeks of parallel data gathering), strategy sprint (3-5 days of problem generation, clustering, and pillar selection), design sprint (1 week of illustrative concepts), document writing (1-2 weeks), and rollout (2-3 weeks of gatekeeper alignment and team roadshows). The total 8-12 week investment pays back over a 2-year strategy horizon.

2. **Strategy working group, not solo PM work**: Form a cross-functional group of engineering, product, design, and data leads who collaboratively build the strategy. The PM facilitates but does not author alone. This builds alignment through the process itself — something that 'comes from you feels more familiar and easy to accept.'

3. **Four criteria for selecting strategic pillars**: Rank opportunity clusters on expected impact, certainty of impact (evidence quality), clarity of levers (do you know how to solve it?), and differentiation (are you uniquely positioned to build it?). Down-select from 10-15 opportunity areas to exactly three pillars plus an explicit list of what you will not do.

4. **The fruit story — interview leaders before building strategy**: Engage senior leaders early with questions about what success and failure look like, their pet ideas, and key principles. This prevents the painful cycle of presenting a 'mango' only to learn the reviewer hates mangoes. Leaders welcome these conversations because they are more creative than their typical meetings.

5. **Big-S strategy is future-back, led by design**: While small-S strategy solves present problems, big-S strategy generates three distinct visions of the future (5-10 year horizon), prototypes them like concept cars, tests with users, and pushes winning elements into the live product. At VRChat, both streams run in parallel and merge into one roadmap.

## Concepts covered

- **Strategic pillars** — The three (ideally) focus areas selected through ranked opportunity clustering that form the backbone of a product strategy.
- **Strategy working group** — A cross-functional team of engineering, product, design, and data leads that collaboratively builds the strategy rather than leaving it to the PM alone.
- **Resonance (strategy analogy)** — Selecting the right strategic frequency to achieve disproportionate impact between the product and market, analogous to resonance in physics.
- **Small-S vs. big-S strategy** — Small-S is present-forward and problem-focused (2-year horizon); big-S is future-back and aspirational (5-10 year horizon), typically led by design.
- **[[strategy-choice-cascade|Winning aspiration]]** — A newspaper-headline exercise where each team member imagines press coverage of the strategy's success in two years, then blends them into one statement.
- **How might we** — A generative question format used to bridge from strategic pillars to the design sprint, opening the mind to solution possibilities.
