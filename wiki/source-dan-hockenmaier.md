# Source: Developing a growth model + marketplace growth strategy | Dan Hockenmaier

**Guest**: Dan Hockenmaier (ex-Thumbtack, Basis One founder, now Head of Strategy & Analytics at Faire)
**Published**: 2022-10-09
**Duration**: ~1h 4m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AlTQ6O2qooI

## Executive summary

Two topics: how to build a growth model as analytical representation of your business, and the unique dynamics of marketplaces. 50% of a growth model's value is the painful process of building it; marketplace operators are "gardeners, not construction workers"; demand aggregation — not supply — is the true north-star for marketplace strategy.

## Key ideas

1. **Growth model = analytical representation of the business** — Three building blocks for SaaS (acquisition, retention, monetization), layered with AOV for transactional, + supply-side for marketplaces. "50% of the value you get from it is simply building the model."
2. **Retention is the most under-appreciated lever** — Models show growth is "exceptionally sensitive" to retention because it compounds into virality, content generation, payback period. At Thumbtack, realizing repeat rate drove LTV shifted resources from top-of-funnel to lifecycle.
3. **Marketplaces are gardens, not construction sites** — Every marketplace decision has second- and third-order consequences; pricing in particular doesn't follow a simple tangent curve. "Tread lightly... particularly if you've got something that's working."
4. **Demand aggregation is the only thing that matters** — Founders over-rotate on supply because it *is* the product early on, but "if you are successful at aggregating demand, you will have the winning marketplace." Dual-sided ROI equations replace naive "balance" ratios.
5. **Marketplace evolution → 100% commission = not a marketplace** — Trajectory: Marketplace 1.0 (lead-gen, ~5-10% commission) → managed ([[source-brian-chesky|Airbnb]], Etsy, trust) → heavily managed ([[doordash]] logistics, Faire underwriting). Creative supply (Etsy, Faire, Steam) stays; commoditized demand (ride-sharing + AVs) consolidates out.
6. **Unbundling is overhyped** — Vertical slices of horizontal marketplaces rarely win because LTV breadth and network effects favor scale. Exceptions require high order value (Airbnb vs. Craigslist) or self-contained network.

> "If you think about running a marketplace, you're basically a gardener. You have to have a very light touch. If you're building a SaaS business, you're a construction worker... For a marketplace, you're messing with this ecosystem that you don't actually really understand how it works."
