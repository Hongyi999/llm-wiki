# Rituals of great teams

**Rituals of great teams** — articulated by [[source-shishir-mehrotra|Shishir Mehrotra]] (Coda, ex-YouTube) — is the idea that team culture is produced by the weekly practices you repeat, not the strategy decks you present. Strategy happens once a year; rituals happen every week. Whatever your rituals reward is what your culture becomes.

## Core claim

> "You can write a strategy doc once a year, but rituals happen every week. Whatever your rituals reward is what your culture becomes — whether you designed it or not."

Most orgs don't design their rituals. They inherit standups, one-on-ones, all-hands, and retrospectives — and end up with a culture that is the accidental byproduct of those rituals. High-performing teams design their rituals explicitly to reward the behaviors they want.

## Ritual design dimensions

A ritual design specifies:

- **Cadence** — Weekly, biweekly, monthly, quarterly.
- **Attendees** — Who must be in the room.
- **Artifact** — What document/deck/metric gets produced.
- **Owner (DRI)** — Who runs it.
- **Success signal** — What outcome means it's working.

## The rituals Shishir highlights

### DRI (Directly Responsible Individual)
Every meaningful decision gets exactly one DRI. Accountability without a name is accountability by no one. DRIs make decisions fast; committees make them slow.

### Math-spotters
A dedicated role whose job is to double-check the arithmetic in every important memo. Strategic decisions die on quiet math errors because no one else is incentivized to catch them.

### The grid company
An org grows healthy when hires are distributed across a grid of function × seniority, not clustered. Clusters create tribal knowledge pockets; grids create cross-pollination.

### Three hiring signals
Smart, gets things done, easy to work with. Weight all three. Orgs that under-weight "easy to work with" accumulate toxic seniors whose work compounds as org damage.

## Common failure modes

- **Ritual drift** — Standups that become status theater; retros that produce no action.
- **Accumulated ritual debt** — Adding rituals without removing old ones until the calendar is full and no one knows why.
- **Ritual without DRI** — A recurring meeting with no owner rots fast.
- **Measurement without escalation** — Rituals that surface problems but don't change behavior are reporting, not operating.
