# Source: I've run 75+ businesses. Here's why you're probably chasing the wrong idea. | Andrew Wilkinson

**Guest**: Andrew Wilkinson (co-founder Tiny, the bootstrapped Berkshire-Hathaway-of-the-internet holding company; ~$300M revenue across 40+ businesses)
**Published**: 2025-07-03
**Duration**: ~1h 28m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VxAwUb86MUE

## Executive summary

Wilkinson argues most first-time founders chase sexy, crowded ideas (AI startups, cafes, PM SaaS) when they should hunt boring, niche, low-competition markets where a first win builds the confidence flywheel. Folds in a strong AI-agent operational philosophy (Lindy, Replit), a Buffett/Munger moat-and-mental-models investing frame, and raw personal disclosure on ADHD, SSRIs, and why money did not make him happier.

## Key ideas

1. **Fish where the fish are** — Munger's metaphor: walk past the crowded pond and find the quiet fishing hole. "Competition equals lower margin." Form-filling software making $30M/yr beats "the next great AI company."
2. **Don't try to deadlift 300 pounds on day one** — First-time founders should pick something where they can get an early easy win (Metalab). Flashy ambition kills because feedback loops are too long.
3. **Unfair-advantage Venn diagram** — Every business he's bought (Letterboxd, AeroPress, Serato) connected to a personal passion where he already understood the industry. "Luck comes to the prepared mind."
4. **Lazy leadership + "there are no problems, only people problems"** — Hire fully-formed talent (not potential). If you ever ask "should I fire this person?" once, fire them. 20% of his time is spent filtering to avoid narcissists.
5. **AI agents as reliable $200/month employees** — Lindy workflows (email triage, calendar emoji-tagging, pre-meeting Perplexity briefings), Replit for vibe-coding, Limitless pendant for meeting recall. "We're in the Palm Treo phase of agents."

## B8 angle

Contribution is the *category-selection* move that precedes positioning: "boring business / underserved niche" is effectively Dunford's "who are the best-fit customers" pushed up to "which market even deserves the positioning exercise." Converges with B7 growth-voices' founder-market fit and earned channels (passionate niches auto-generate WOM). Where Godin/Raskin frame narrative as the unlock, Wilkinson frames *market structure* as the unlock.
