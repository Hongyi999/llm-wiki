# Inside TikTok's product culture and advertising playbook | Ray Cao (TikTok)

**Guest**: Ray Cao  
**Published**: 2024-03-10  
**Duration**: 1:25:15  
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9N4ZgNaWvI0](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9N4ZgNaWvI0)

## Executive summary

Ray Cao, Global Head of Monetization Product Strategy & Operations at TikTok, provides a rare inside look at how one of the world's most successful companies operates. He reveals TikTok's core cultural principle of 'context, no control' -- giving employees full business context and encouraging proactive thinking while keeping execution collaborative. Cao explains how TikTok stands on the shoulders of giants, borrowing Amazon's memo culture and always-day-one mentality alongside Google's OKR system. The episode also offers highly tactical advertising advice: advertisers should test at least 10 creatives per week, use broader targeting than they would on Meta or Google, embrace TikTok as a content graph (not a people graph or intent graph), and give campaigns at least a month to learn. Cao candidly discusses his own failures, including compromising hiring standards under pressure and initially resisting the context-no-control philosophy.

## Key ideas

1. **Context, No Control**: TikTok's foundational principle encourages every employee to think like a business owner by providing full context rather than narrow task assignments. This breaks down silos and enables proactive problem-solving, though it requires disciplined collaboration to avoid chaos.

2. **The Content Graph vs People Graph vs Intent Graph**: TikTok's advertising platform operates fundamentally differently from Meta (people graph) and Google (intent graph). TikTok is a content graph, requiring advertisers to test more creatives, use broader targeting, and embrace discovery-based rather than intent-based marketing.

3. **Always Day One + Global-First Thinking**: TikTok combines Amazon's day-one mentality with a genuinely global product development approach -- launching products first in markets like Southeast Asia rather than defaulting to the US. Understanding local culture is essential because algorithms alone cannot substitute for on-the-ground market knowledge.

4. **Cross-Functional Integration Is the Secret Sauce**: TikTok holds 180-person reading-format meetings where engineering, product, sales, and data science teams all read a single document together. PMs and engineers regularly join client immersion trips to 'feel the heat' of real customer problems.

5. **Quality Over Speed in Hiring**: Cao candidly shares that his biggest mistake was compromising hiring standards when scaling from 2 to 100 people in six months. Wrong hires don't just fail to speed you up -- they actively slow you down. The cost of poor hiring quality cascades through management layers.

## Concepts covered

- **Context, No Control** — TikTok's management principle of providing employees full business context and autonomy rather than narrow task-based instructions.
- **Content Graph** — TikTok's algorithmic model that distributes content based on content quality and engagement rather than social connections or search intent.
- **Always Day One** — Cultural value borrowed from Amazon emphasizing startup hunger and avoiding complacency regardless of company size.
- **Reading-Format Meeting** — Large cross-functional meetings where all participants silently read a shared document, then discuss decisions, blockers, and celebrations.
- **Feel the Heat** — TikTok practice of bringing PMs and engineers on client immersion trips to directly experience customer pain points.
