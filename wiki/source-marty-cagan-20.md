# The disease of process people | Marty Cagan

**Guest**: Marty Cagan  
**Published**: 2023-02-06  
**Duration**: 1:24:48  
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gXDg88rSG8U](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gXDg88rSG8U)

## Executive summary

Marty Cagan delivers a forceful critique of the current state of product management, arguing that most PMs are operating as project managers on feature teams and providing little real value. He introduces the concept of 'product management theater' — where people carry the PM title but lack the skills for genuine value and viability work — and warns that a convergence of overhiring, rising interest rates, remote work challenges, and generative AI will create a reckoning for these roles. Cagan presents his 'product operating model' (from his book TRANSFORMED) as the alternative: 20 principles distilled from consistently innovative companies, emphasizing empowered teams solving problems rather than shipping features. The episode is a wake-up call for PMs to uplevel their skills from backlog administration to genuine product discovery and business ownership.

## Key ideas

1. **Product management theater**: Many companies have people with PM titles who are really doing project management — administering backlogs, facilitating meetings, and herding cats. They lack the skills for genuine value and viability work, and their companies don't actually need them in that role, which is why anti-PM sentiment is growing.

2. **Feature teams vs. empowered product teams**: On feature teams, PMs are given roadmaps of output (features to ship); on empowered teams, they're given problems to solve. The critical difference is outcomes vs. output — strong product companies understand it's 'time to money, not time to market.' Only empowered teams truly need product managers.

3. **The four competencies of the product model**: Real product organizations need four roles done well — a serious product manager (value and viability), a real product designer (service, interaction, visual design, research), a real tech lead, and a real product leader who coaches and drives strategy. Most companies have people with these titles but not these actual capabilities.

4. **90% of product advice is wrong**: Most online content, certifications, community answers, and conference talks propagate feature-team thinking because that's what most people experience. PMs must develop critical thinking skills and actively seek out resources from genuinely strong product companies rather than accepting the majority view.

5. **AI will compound the PM reckoning**: Backlog administration and feature-team project management are highly vulnerable to AI displacement. Empowered PMs focused on viability — navigating legal, ethical, compliance, and business constraints of probabilistic software — will become more important than ever.

## Concepts covered

- **[[product-operating-model|Product Management Theater]]** — The phenomenon of people carrying PM titles while performing project management work, lacking genuine product skills in value discovery and business viability.
- **[[product-operating-model|Product Operating Model]]** — A set of 20 principles used consistently by the best product companies, covering how to decide what to work on, how to solve problems through discovery, and how to build/test/deploy products.
- **Empowered Product Team** — A cross-functional team given problems to solve (not features to ship), measured on outcomes rather than output, with a PM responsible for value and viability.
- **Feature Team** — A team given a roadmap of features to design, build, and deploy — essentially an output-focused delivery team where the PM role reduces to project management.
- **Value and Viability** — The two responsibilities that define a real product manager: ensuring solutions are valuable to customers (value) and workable for the business across legal, financial, and operational dimensions (viability).
- **Transformation** — The process of moving a company from feature-team/delivery-team ways of working to the product operating model, the central subject of Cagan's book TRANSFORMED.
