# The disease of process people | Marty Cagan

**Guest**: Marty Cagan (second appearance)

## Executive summary

Marty Cagan delivers a provocative critique of the current state of product management — calling out that most PMs are operating as glorified project managers on feature teams, dramatically overpaid for the value they produce, and about to be disrupted by AI automating the administrative aspects of the role. This episode sets up the [[product-operating-model|product operating model]] he later formalized in *TRANSFORMED*. It's the sharper, angrier precursor to his [[source-marty-cagan|2024 "Product Management Theater" episode]].

## Key ideas

1. **Feature teams vs. empowered product teams**: Feature teams deliver output (roadmaps of features with dates). Empowered teams are given problems to solve, measured on outcomes. The PM role is fundamentally different in each context.
2. **Process disease**: Organizations addicted to process (SAFe, Scrum ceremonies, backlog rituals) use process as a substitute for skill. Process can't replace customer understanding, data fluency, or business judgment.
3. **PM as creator, not facilitator**: On empowered teams, PMs are side-by-side creators with design and engineering — not meeting coordinators or backlog administrators.
4. **Time to money, not time to market**: Shipping a feature is not the goal. The goal is generating value — for customers and the business — as fast as possible.
5. **A reckoning is coming**: Overhiring, GenAI, bloated process, and remote-work fragility are converging on feature-team PMs. The response: unilaterally raise your skills — customer expertise, data, viability knowledge, discovery — rather than waiting for organizational transformation.
