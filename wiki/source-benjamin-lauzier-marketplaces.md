# Source: Benjamin Lauzier — How Marketplaces Win: Liquidity, Growth Levers, Quality

Lenny's Podcast interview with [[benjamin-lauzier|Benjamin Lauzier]], ex-[[lyft|Lyft]] and ex-[[thumbtack|Thumbtack]] marketplace PM, now founding Nurra Health. Published 2024-09-29. Benjamin teaches the Reforge marketplace course. This episode is the marketplace complement to the other PMF-focused sources.

## Marketplaces have two PMFs

A marketplace is not one product — it is two products glued together by [[marketplace-liquidity|liquidity]]. You must reach PMF **per side**:
- **Supply side PMF**: providers have enough demand to make it worth being on the platform.
- **Demand side PMF**: buyers reliably get matched with good supply.

You rarely hit both at the same time, which is why so many marketplaces die in the middle.

## Pick the hard side (usually supply)

- The "hard side" is whichever side is harder to acquire or retain. For rides it was drivers; for Thumbtack it was quality pros.
- Your entire early strategy should revolve around solving the hard side, because the easy side will follow once supply is solved.
- **Single-player mode**: give the hard side value even without the other side. Lyft's early driver tools, Thumbtack's CRM-like features for pros — things that would be useful even if zero customers existed.

## Jumpstarting liquidity

- **Craigslist jumpstart**: both Airbnb and Thumbtack seeded supply from Craigslist. Ethically gray, but it worked.
- **Concierge supply**: personally recruit the first providers. Do it manually, do it unscaled.
- **Geographic focus**: start with one city, one zip code, one neighborhood. Liquidity is a local phenomenon — a national marketplace with thin local density is worse than a dense single-city one.

## Measuring liquidity

Benjamin defines marketplace liquidity as the Venn diagram overlap: **what fraction of demand finds supply it's happy with, and vice versa**. Two operational proxies:
- **Market Health Metric** (Lyft): ETA. Below a threshold (~2 minutes at mature Lyft density) the market feels magical; above it, customers churn.
- **Fill rate**: % of requests that result in a match. For Thumbtack, this was the north star.

See [[marketplace-liquidity]] for more.

## Signs of a good marketplace idea

1. **Fragmentation on both sides** — neither buyers nor sellers have market power. Restaurants, home services, freelancers.
2. **Uniform needs** — buyers want roughly the same thing, so matching is computable.
3. **High matchmaking barrier without the platform** — it's genuinely hard to find the right counterpart outside the marketplace.

If the two sides could easily transact directly (or already do), the marketplace is doomed to disintermediation.

## War stories

- **Sidecar's mistake**: over-controlled pricing and matching in ways that killed supply. Lesson: marketplaces need to give participants agency.
- **Thumbtack smoke machine**: a pro quoting on a smoke-machine rental job had to answer 15+ questions before getting a lead. Cognitive load killed supply response rate. Fix: reduce questions to the minimum necessary.
- **Lyft's 4th-largest fleet**: through a GM partnership, Lyft became one of the largest rental fleets in the US — used to get drivers on the road who couldn't afford a car. Supply unlock.
- **Mentor/ambassador program**: Lyft paid experienced drivers **$35/session + $20/recruit** to onboard new drivers. Cheap, effective, and peer-to-peer.
- **Toptal**: built a moat through **quality curation** — only the top 3% of applicants. This is supply-side scarcity as a feature.
- **Lyft vs. Uber post-COVID**: Uber's diversification (Eats) insulated it; Lyft was pure rides and suffered more. Platform diversification as risk hedge.

## PMF and marketplaces together

Benjamin is explicit that marketplace mechanics only matter **after** single-sided PMF exists. His advice for his own Reforge course: "wait until you have product-market fit before worrying about marketplace dynamics." See [[product-market-fit]].
