# Source: How to foster innovation and big thinking | Eeke de Milliano (Retool, Stripe)

**Guest**: Eeke de Milliano (Head of Product at Retool; ex-Stripe, 6 years, joined as first AE, became early PM on Connect/Radar/Checkout)
**Published**: 2023-02-02
**Duration**: ~1h 2m 56s
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2ezz4KJe7kA

## Executive summary

De Milliano distills what makes Stripe's culture a compounding decision-making machine and how Retool launched three major products (Workflows, Mobile, Database) in one year. Throughline: process is variance-reducing — it brings low performers up but drags high performers down — so the question is how to fund big thinking without bureaucratizing it.

## Key ideas

1. **Stripe's edge: lots of good decisions, not a few pivotal ones** — Eeke can't point to one or two bet-the-company moments; Stripe just consistently made good calls. Cultural enablers: "think rigorously" as an operating principle, deep writing culture, first-principles questioning.
2. **Trapdoor vs. two-way-door decisions** — Pricing feels like a trapdoor but usually isn't (grandfather existing users). Titles genuinely *are* trapdoors — which is why Stripe famously avoided them.
3. **Operating principles > values** — "Values, you can't really tell people what to value. But you can tell people here's how we operate." Stripe's: "think rigorously," "move with urgency and focus," "users first," "micro-pessimists, macro-optimists."
4. **Process is variance-reducing** — Process brings average performers up but caps top performers. "The folks you're bringing down are your highest performers, your most creative thinkers." Right amount depends on stage.
5. **Why teams aren't innovative (three diagnoses)** — (a) Fear of failure + leaders not absorbing downside; fix by renaming postmortems to retrospectives, small at-bats. (b) Urgent work crowding out important; exit the incident-treadmill. (c) Thinking big is hard; teams need *permission to think*.
6. **The "Crazy Ideas" doc** — Every January, David (Retool CEO) sends a blank doc titled Crazy Ideas: "90% chance these shouldn't happen; 10% chance they're 10-100x." They ship 3-8 items/year. Two of 2022's three product launches originated there.
7. **"Think Bigger" section in every team charter** — "With 20% more time, what would you do that isn't on this list?" Forces planning past existing headcount.
8. **Launched 3 products by treating them as startups** — 1 engineer + 1 PM/designer for first 6 months, kept deliberately separate from main org. Retool Mobile ended up with a different target customer — only realized mid-build *because* team was isolated.
9. **Why Stripe resisted PMs for ~100 employees** — When the customer *is* a developer, engineers are the customer. PMs become necessary when customer base diversifies and org is matrixed enough that cross-team storytelling matters.

> "Process, by definition, is variance reducing... while you are reducing the standard and bringing folks up to the average, you're also bringing other folks down to the average. And oftentimes, the folks you're bringing down are your highest performers."

## B6 angle (hiring, management, leadership Part 2)

Eeke contributes the *innovation management* angle B5 leadership voices mostly skip. [[source-ben-horowitz|Horowitz]] and [[source-tobi-lutke|Lütke]] talk about steering the company; [[source-jerry-colonna|Colonna]] talks about coaching leaders; [[source-molly-graham|Graham]] talks about founder mode — but Eeke talks about the middle question: once you've hired great people, how do you organize them so the best ones don't regress to the mean and urgent doesn't crush important? Her "treat new products as internal startups" model pairs directly with [[source-melanie-perkins|Perkins]]/[[source-boz|Boz]] on autonomy, and her "Crazy Ideas" ritual is a concrete artifact that complements [[source-elizabeth-stone|Stone's]] culture-writing and [[source-dharmesh-shah|Shah's]] bottom-up product discovery.
