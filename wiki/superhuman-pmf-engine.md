# Superhuman PMF Engine

A four-step process created by [[rahul-vohra|Rahul Vohra]] at [[superhuman|Superhuman]] that extends the [[sean-ellis-test|Sean Ellis Test]] from a scoreboard into a roadmap engine. Took Superhuman from **22% → 58%** "very disappointed" in about a year.

## The four steps

### 1. Survey — with three options

Use the [[sean-ellis-test|Sean Ellis question]] but make the answer set:
- Very disappointed
- Somewhat disappointed
- Not disappointed

Three options (not two) is the key. The **"somewhat disappointed"** bucket is what you'll operate on.

### 2. Segment — define the high-expectation customer (HXC)

Look *only* at users who answered "very disappointed" and ask them:
1. What type of person would most benefit from this product?
2. What is the main benefit you receive?
3. How can we improve it for you?

The intersection of their answers defines the **HXC** — a sharp persona-plus-promise. This is who the product is really for.

### 3. Analyze — why don't "somewhat disappointed" users love it yet?

Tag the responses from the "somewhat disappointed" segment. The top tags are your **roadmap**. These are users who get enough value to feel something, but not enough to feel dependency. Closing that gap is the highest-leverage work you can do.

### 4. Double down — deepen for the lovers, widen for the almost-lovers

Split the roadmap roughly 50/50 between:
- **Solution deepening** — make the HXC love it more.
- **Market widening** — fix the top blockers for "somewhat disappointed" users.

Early on, bias toward deepening until the core is unambiguously locked. See [[solution-deepening-vs-market-widening]].

## Why it works

- The Sean Ellis Test alone gives a score but no roadmap.
- The Superhuman Engine uses the score as one signal and the qualitative segmentation as the actual product direction.
- It enforces **starting from the user who already loves you**, preventing the common trap of building for the unsatisfied middle at the cost of alienating the core.

## Cadence

Superhuman ran the loop on a regular cadence (roughly quarterly) rather than as a one-time exercise. PMF is a moving target — the HXC evolves as the product changes and the market changes around it.
