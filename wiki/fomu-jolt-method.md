# FOMU & JOLT Method

**FOMU** (Fear of Messing Up) is the primary driver of 40–60% of qualified B2B pipeline closing as "no decision." **JOLT** is the four-step method for overcoming buyer indecision without increasing fear.

## The problem: FOMU > FOMO

From 2.5M analyzed Zoom-era sales calls (Matt Dixon):

- 40–60% of qualified deals end in "no decision"
- 56% of those losses are **buyer indecision** (FOMU), not status-quo preference
- Dialing up FOMO (urgency, scarcity) backfires **87% of the time** on indecisive buyers

Three drivers of FOMU:
1. Did I configure/spec it right?
2. Will I learn something post-signature that looks bad?
3. Will we actually hit projected ROI?

## JOLT method

| Step | Action | Analogy |
|---|---|---|
| **J**udge | Ping-and-echo to surface whether buyer is indecisive vs. disinterested | Sonar |
| **O**ffer recommendation | Make a concrete recommendation instead of giving more options | Waiter who recommends vs. recites the menu |
| **L**imit exploration | Establish trust so buyer stops endless research | "You've done enough homework" |
| **T**ake risk off table | Under-promise, over-deliver + safety nets (pro services, phased rollout, guarantees) | Insurance policy |

## Key insight: reset expectations early

Great reps talk buyers **down** from best-case ROI to a believable number, creating over-delivery headroom. This feels counterintuitive but dramatically reduces post-purchase regret risk.
