# Source: Working through fear, hard feedback, and hiring for fit | Merci Grace (Slack, Panobi)

**Guest**: Merci Grace (ex-game-designer; first growth / new-user-experience PM at Slack; later Slack head of growth; then VC; now founder of Panobi, co-founder Women in Product)
**Published**: 2022-11-10
**Duration**: ~1h 11m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bCel0X2Ta7U

## Executive summary

Grace traces how Slack's game-design sensibility produced one of the original product-led-growth playbooks. She lays out when to go product-led vs. sales-led, why storytelling beats numbers in interviews and pitches, and a controversial but effective hiring stance: make candidates do real work.

## Key ideas

1. **Slack is a work tool, not a social product** — An internal-but-foundational decision that "made thousands of small decisions instant and obvious," including refusing to add blocking (reframed as a tool harassers could weaponize).
2. **Product-led growth needs day-zero value and individual adoption** — "It's product-led in one direction, also product-led back out." Tools that pay off only in three months can't sustain PLG without sales. PLG is chosen before you write a line of code.
3. **Onboarding is designed from day one, not bolted on** — Game-designer heritage: design the game from onboarding backwards. "Plug-and-play" onboarding templates that promise "replicate Slack's onboarding" are actively harmful. Carousels are "panes built to be dismissed."
4. **Activation metric needs real rigor** — Slack's: 3 real humans, 50 real messages. "Three is the lowest number at which things start to break." Found via regression + hypothesis testing.
5. **Invite early and often, trust the connectors** — "It's a lot easier to get someone to send more invites than it is to get someone who's a little shy to even send one." Refuse dark patterns.
6. **Storytelling beats fundamentals** — "Slack was a company where the number always won — no, the *story* always won." Start every pitch in the middle of the action (Mission Impossible opening); lead with founder insight, not market.
7. **Hire by having people do real work** — Controversial: she gives ICPM candidates an actual project. "If you don't want to even do three hours of free work for a company, you probably don't want to work at that company." Direct correlation between candidates who "kicked ass" and those who later ran orgs at Slack.
8. **Diversity is a flywheel, not a filter** — Interview more women; don't blame the pipeline. Going from one woman to two changes the tone of the entire team.

## B6 angle (hiring, management, leadership Part 2)

Grace occupies a rare intersection — founder, senior operator, VC, founder again — and uses it to unpack *interpersonal* dynamics around hiring and deal-making that B5 voices like [[source-ben-horowitz|Horowitz]] and [[source-melanie-perkins|Perkins]] touch only obliquely. Uniquely contributes (a) concrete and controversial ICPM hiring method (real-work projects) with observed outcomes at Slack, (b) the pipeline-is-not-an-excuse framing of diversity hiring, (c) the "two is different from one" observation about hiring flywheels.
