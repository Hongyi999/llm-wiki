# Adjacent User Theory

A growth framework from [[source-bangaly-kaba|Bangaly Kaba]] (Instagram Head of Growth 440M → 1B users). Central claim: your next 100M users aren't like your current ones, and cohort curves dipping over time isn't a regression — it's *adjacent users* adopting your product and needing different things.

## The core move

1. **Draw your current user persona explicitly** — Who uses the product well today?
2. **Identify the adjacent user** — Who has tried but didn't stick? Who sits one step further from your core?
3. **Literally create a new account and use the product as them** — First-hand friction surfaces fast.
4. **Fix the specific friction that blocks adjacent user adoption** — Often a small feature, default, or onboarding change.
5. **Repeat** — The adjacent user of today becomes the core user of tomorrow, and a new adjacent user emerges.

## Canonical examples

- **Instacart 7-8 click reorder** — 90% of orders were repeats but reordering took 7-8 clicks. The adjacent user (not the power user) was blocked.
- **Instagram login omnibox** — 10-12M users/year were churning because they couldn't get back in. Adjacent users couldn't navigate the login flow.
- **Instagram 2017 connection pivot** — New users were posting into celebrity-recommendation echo chambers with no response; adjacent-user analysis showed they needed friend-friend connections first.

## Why it matters

Most growth analytics show aggregate retention curves or cohort curves and interpret declines as product regressions. The adjacent-user lens reframes decline as **success**: you're acquiring users one step further from your early adopters, which is mathematically certain to depress average metrics. The fix isn't to "fix the product" — it's to fix what the *specific new cohort* needs.
