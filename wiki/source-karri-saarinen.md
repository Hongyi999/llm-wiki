# Source: Inside Linear: Building with taste, craft, and focus | Karri Saarinen

**Guest**: Karri Saarinen (Linear co-founder/CEO; ex-principal designer at Airbnb; ex-founding designer at Coinbase)
**Published**: 2023-10-08
**Duration**: ~1h 39m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4muxFVZ4XfM

## Executive summary

Linear has become the most-loved issue tracker by inverting modern growth orthodoxy: no A/B tests, no per-feature metric goals, one PM across ~50 employees, paid work trials for every hire, year-long invite-only private beta. Profitable for two years and net-negative lifetime burn. Throughline: taste, opinionated product, and extreme focus on "the main quest."

## Key ideas

1. **Craft + speed via early-in-production shipping** — Contrary to perception, Linear doesn't polish offline. Design rough → ship behind flag → test with 1-10 opt-in customers (Vercel for roadmap) → polish before general release. "Perfectionism is the enemy of shipping."
2. **One PM at 50 people is deliberate** — Engineers and designers own projects end-to-end (project lead is an assignment, not a title). Forces product sensibility into every hire.
3. **Design as differentiator, but never the *reason* for success** — The more mature a category, the more design matters. But design is leverage on top of real product/tech edge.
4. **No A/B tests and no per-feature metric goals** — "Magic and science" balance. Talk to customers obsessively (shared Slack channels, weekly calls), then use judgment. "Sometimes people use data a lot because they're afraid of making the wrong choice."
5. **Opinionated software beats flexible software** — For productivity tools, flexibility costs users time. Linear's defaults (cycles vs. sprints, triage) are the value prop. "Design something for *someone*," not everyone.
6. **Main quest vs. side quests** — Ignore SOC 2 until asked; skip podcasts until timing is right; refuse fancy brand work. YC framing: talk to customers, build product, exercise.
7. **Paid work trial over interviews** — Every hire does multi-day paid contractor stint: vague problem, real codebase, real meetings. Both sides evaluate.
8. **Private beta + cohort-based invites + pricing slider** — 4K waitlist from Twitter launch. Invited ~10 users/week in cohorts so feedback wasn't redundant. Pricing was a literal slider ($1-$20/seat, user chooses) to test willingness to pay.
9. **PMF as a spectrum by segment** — Not "do you have it or not?" but "how strong is it in each segment?" Linear targeted startup PMF for years 1-2, then enterprise PMF for years 3-4.
