# Comparison: B5 Leadership Schools

The B5 batch surfaces at least **seven distinct schools of leadership thought**. Each is internally coherent and advocated by credible operators. Most disagree sharply on core questions.

## The seven schools

| School | Canonical voice | Core move |
|--------|-----------------|-----------|
| **Heat-injection / founder-mode** | [[source-tobi-lutke]], [[source-marc-benioff]] | Leader is exothermic; inject energy; kill sunk cost fast. |
| **Run-toward-fear / decision-velocity** | [[source-ben-horowitz]] | Hesitation is the worst sin; make the hard call. |
| **Inner-work / complicity** | [[source-jerry-colonna]] | Team dysfunction mirrors the leader's unexamined self. |
| **Script-work / behavior-design** | [[source-alisa-cohn]], [[source-rachel-lockett]] | Scripts and rituals make leadership repeatable. |
| **Talent-density / keeper-test** | [[source-elizabeth-stone]] | Candor + freedom only work on top of additive hiring. |
| **Give-away-your-Legos / scaling-self** | [[source-molly-graham]] | Repeatedly hand off what you mastered. |
| **Mission-architecture / celebrate** | [[source-melanie-perkins]] | Crazy Big Goals + mission pillars + staged celebrations. |

## Six disagreements

### 1. Should the leader be "nice"?

- **Cohn**: "Your job isn't to make people happy." But delivery matters — use scripts and "hope for the future" framing.
- **Lütke**: Direct feedback is a gift; the rarest thing is courage.
- **Boz**: Kindness > "just being honest"; feedback must be delivered with empathy for where audience starts.
- **Horowitz**: "The only value you ever add is when you make a decision that most people don't like."

**Rough consensus**: Be direct on substance, be kind on delivery. Mild disagreement on whether kindness is decoration or craft.

### 2. How do you fix a team problem?

- **Colonna**: Look in the mirror. The team is reenacting the leader's unexamined patterns.
- **Graham (Waterline Model)**: 80% of team problems are structural (unclear goals, roles). Diagnose at the surface first.
- **Lockett**: Run GROW; let reports solve it; level-3 listening.
- **Stone**: If talent density is broken, no amount of process fixes it.

**Real disagreement**: is the root cause the leader's psyche (Colonna), structure (Graham), conversation quality (Lockett), or hiring bar (Stone)? All can be true, but the first-move matters.

### 3. When to fire?

- **Levine**: 30 days after hire, re-ask "would I hire this person today?" If no, fire immediately.
- **Stone (keeper test)**: Continuous: "would I fight to keep them?" If no, have the conversation.
- **Cohn**: 30-day "deal breaker" pre-firing conversation; firing should never surprise.
- **Lockett (enthusiastic rehire)**: "Would I enthusiastically rehire this person?" If no, act.
- **Horowitz**: Invest in strength. Don't judge someone by their worst moment; coach flaws unless the strength isn't world-class.

**Convergence**: All reject the "hope they improve on their own" anti-pattern. Disagreement is on speed and forgiveness for flaws.

### 4. Is management the founder's job?

- **Dharmesh Shah**: No. Zero direct reports in 18 years. Design the company around your actual strengths.
- **Benioff**: Yes. Orchestra leader, not clarinet player.
- **Lütke**: Yes, but the job is heat-injection and standards-setting, not process-management.
- **Fournier**: Yes, and it takes ~10 years of IC mastery first.

**Real disagreement**: Whether founders should scale themselves into managers or structurally avoid management.

### 5. Where does culture come from?

- **Shah (HubSpot)**: Culture Code — 128-page versioned artifact.
- **Shishir Mehrotra** (B4): Rituals, not strategy decks — weekly behaviors produce culture.
- **Perkins (Canva)**: Mission pillars + Crazy Big Goals + staged celebrations.
- **Stone (Netflix)**: Talent density + candor; culture as emergent property.
- **Beykpour (Twitter retrospective)**: Sacred cows as roadmap; storytelling told repeatedly.

**Shared insight**: Culture is maintained, not proclaimed. Real disagreement on whether the maintenance device is a written artifact, weekly ritual, hiring bar, or repeated narrative.

### 6. Is inner work necessary, or is it a substitute for action?

- **Colonna, Hudson, Miller**: Inner work is the leverage point; process fixes fail without it.
- **Horowitz, Lütke**: Confidence and direct action matter more. Too much introspection = hesitation = failure.
- **Cohn, Lockett**: Pragmatic middle — scripts and rituals make the behavior change tractable even if inner work is incomplete.

**Real disagreement**: Whether emotional/psychological fluency is prerequisite or ornament.

## Synthesis

These schools aren't mutually exclusive — they correspond to different failure modes of leadership. A leader who is already directive but doesn't self-examine needs Colonna. A leader who is already reflective but hesitates needs Horowitz. A leader who has talent but no rituals needs Cohn. The diagnostic question is: *which failure mode is currently binding for me?*
