# A founder's guide to crisis management | Uri Levine (Waze co-founder, serial entrepreneur)

**Guest**: Uri Levine
**Published**: 2025-02-16
**Duration**: 1:23:46
**YouTube**: [Lenny's Podcast — Uri Levine](https://www.lennyspodcast.com/)

## Executive summary

Uri Levine — co-founder of Waze (sold to Google for $1.3B), Moovit, and Pontera, serial founder of ten companies, author of *Fall in Love with the Problem, Not the Solution* — distills the entrepreneurship journey into a single thesis: value creation is the goal, and the simplest path is to fall obsessively in love with a real problem. That mindset serves as North Star through a roller-coaster, failure-filled journey to product-market fit. His most provocative hiring claim: firing is harder than hiring, and the 30-day test ("knowing what I know today, would I hire this person?") is the pivotal leadership discipline.

## Key ideas

1. **Fall in love with the problem, not the solution**: The problem becomes your North Star — making pivots easier and your story emotionally compelling to users and investors alike. Start every pitch with the problem or value, not "our AI does X."
2. **The 30-day hire-or-fire test**: The day you hire anyone, set a 30-day calendar reminder and ask: "Knowing what I know now, would I hire this person?" If yes, give more equity and buy their loyalty; if no, fire immediately — tolerating the wrong person damages the team and drives top performers out.
3. **Phases require shifting gears**: Idea → PMF → growth or business model → scale, each needs different focus. PMF's only metric is retention. Companies fail because founders can't "find the clutch" to shift out of product mode into go-to-market.
4. **Fundraising is storytelling under 1% conversion**: Early-stage investors decide "before you sit down" based on "I like the CEO, I like the story." The CEO goes alone, leads with the strongest point on the title slide (the longest-displayed slide), ends with another strong point on the thank-you slide, and expects ~100 nos per yes.
5. **Reference checks with one question**: For any hire, ask the reference: "Would you hire him?" If the answer takes more than "Take the guy fast," it's a no. Simplicity is what separates tools that get adopted from tools that don't.
