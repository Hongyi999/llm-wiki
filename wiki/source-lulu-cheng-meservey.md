# Source: Gain attention as an underdog with this framework | Lulu Cheng Meservey

**Guest**: Lulu Cheng Meservey (ex-Substack head of comms; now EVP corporate affairs at Activision Blizzard; author of *Flack*)
**Published**: 2023-03-27
**Duration**: ~2m 44s (short clip excerpt from a full episode)
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qKXIBE7KRGo

## Executive summary

Underdogs win by shaping messages to fit the audience's pre-existing passions — their "cultural erogenous zones" — rather than trying to create new passions. Layers on a concentric-circle audience model, a pressure = force/area tactical formula, and strong advocacy for founders going direct rather than laundering through traditional media.

## Key ideas

1. **Cultural erogenous zones** — You can't change what people care about; shape your message into their existing worldview. Kamala Harris linking K-12 reading standards to army recruitment (national-security concern) is the canonical example.
2. **Concentric circles of influence** — Spread messages in order: employees → power users → writers/influencers → investors → mainstream. Skipping a circle undermines credibility.
3. **Pressure = Force / Area** — With limited resources, decrease surface area (narrow audience + sharp message) to apply more pressure; hyper-target diehards over broad reach.
4. **Make mistakes of commission, not omission** — The status quo is the startup's real enemy; sitting-in-cash conservatism loses by default.
5. **Go direct + pick one channel** — Founders should speak as themselves (not as a C-corp), pick the medium matching their native voice (Musk: Twitter; Armstrong/Best: blog posts), build primary distribution before they need it. LinkedIn is underutilized.

## Notable quote

> "It's a huge lift to try to change someone's worldview or their passions. It's a light lift to take the thing you want to talk about and just shape it into their worldview or their passions."

## B8 angle

Toolkit is tailor-made for B8-style underdog positioning: pressure/area tradeoff, erogenous-zones lens, and "go direct" mandate all point the same direction — narrow the audience, sharpen the message, speak as a human, own your distribution before you need it. Especially useful for companies choosing between broad-PR theatrics and focused founder-led comms.
