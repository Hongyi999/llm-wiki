# Zigging vs. zagging: How HubSpot built a $30B company | Dharmesh Shah

**Guest**: Dharmesh Shah
**Published**: 2024-04-04
**Duration**: 1:41:44
**YouTube**: [Lenny's Podcast — Dharmesh Shah](https://www.lennyspodcast.com/)

## Executive summary

Dharmesh Shah — co-founder/CTO of HubSpot (~$30B market cap), keeper of its Culture Code — runs a radically self-designed operation: build the company, the role, and the craft around your actual strengths and enjoyment rather than defaulting to what "founders are supposed to do." He has had zero direct reports in 18 years, refuses to do management (a skill he'd only become "passively okay" at), and invests calories in what he enjoys and excels at — coding, writing, culture, humor. Signature frameworks: "zig when others zag" (HubSpot's anti-focus, multi-product bet was a deliberate zig), the Culture Code as a living 128-page document, and his engineer's decomposition of public speaking into a measurable craft (LPM — laughs per minute, tracked in custom software). Throughout, he frames founder work as fighting the second law of thermodynamics.

## Key ideas

1. **Lean into strengths, design around weaknesses**: Shah rejected the CEO role and all direct reports at founding. "I don't want to spend years of my life becoming passively okay at something" — instead, stay energized doing what compounds.
2. **Zig vs. zag**: The best startup advice says focus on one thing; HubSpot's early zig was the opposite (multi-product). Identify where conventional wisdom locks everyone else in and deliberately go the other way.
3. **Culture Code as living product**: The 128-page Culture Code treats culture as a product spec with versioning, authored by the co-founder who claimed to be the worst possible person for the job. Transparency is a default ("all information equally shared" except illegal-to-share or others' salaries).
4. **LPM — laughs per minute**: High-stakes public speaking decomposed into sub-skills; he records every talk, transcribes it, and uses custom SoloWare to measure LPM. Two levers: more laughs or fewer words between them. Stand-up comedians hit high LPM; good business talks > 1.
5. **Entropy is the enemy; SoloWare is the hack**: Founders and product teams fight the second law of thermodynamics — order decays unless you push against it. Shah's answer: relentless building of SoloWare (tools for exactly one user: himself) to compound personal leverage.

## B5 angle

A founder consciously scaling a 7,000-person public company by refusing to scale himself into a manager, and by treating culture as an engineered, transparent artifact. Scaling people through structural choices (Culture Code, transparency defaults, no-direct-reports rule) rather than personal management heroics — a strong counterpoint to leadership narratives that assume the founder must become a great people manager.
