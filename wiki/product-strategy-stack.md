# Product Strategy Stack

The **Product Strategy Stack** — articulated by [[source-ravi-mehta|Ravi Mehta]] — is a hierarchical model for connecting abstract company ambition to weekly execution. It prevents the common failure of teams working hard on features that don't ladder to strategy.

## The five layers

```
Mission           (why the company exists — decades)
  ↓
Vision            (what the world looks like if we win — ~10 years)
  ↓
Strategy          (how we win — 2–5 years)
  ↓
Themes / Pillars  (where we invest this year — 12 months)
  ↓
Initiatives       (what we ship — quarters)
```

Each layer answers a different question and operates at a different time horizon. Each layer should trace to the layer above: initiatives → themes → strategy → vision → mission.

## Why the stack matters

Most orgs have pieces of the stack but not a connected stack:

- **Mission without initiatives** — Inspiring posters, disconnected daily work.
- **Initiatives without strategy** — Busy teams, random feature mix, no compounding.
- **Strategy without themes** — Strategy decks that never translate to quarterly plans.
- **Themes without traceable mission** — Quarterly goal-setting that ignores the long arc.

A connected stack lets any team member, asked "why are you building this?", trace their work up to the mission.

## Velocity vs latency

Ravi's second contribution is the distinction between **velocity** and **latency** as two different metrics for product teams:

- **Velocity**: How much ships per unit time. Linked to throughput.
- **Latency**: How long from idea to customer. Linked to learning speed.

Most orgs measure velocity and ignore latency. High-velocity / high-latency teams ship a lot but learn slowly. Low-velocity / low-latency teams learn fast from small bets. The right balance depends on the stage.

## Common failure modes

- **Skip layers** — Jumping from mission to initiatives skips the hard choices.
- **Layer inflation** — Calling a theme a "strategy" dilutes language.
- **No revision cadence** — Strategies rot; review annually at minimum.
- **Copy-pasted mission** — Vague missions ("delight customers") can't anchor choice.
