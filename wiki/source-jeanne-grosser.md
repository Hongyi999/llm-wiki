# Source: What world-class GTM looks like in 2026 | Jeanne DeWitt Grosser

**Guest**: Jeanne DeWitt Grosser (COO at Vercel; ex-Stripe CRO; ex-Google)
**Published**: 2025-11-30
**Duration**: ~1h 26m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RmnWHz8HD74

## Executive summary

World-class GTM in 2026 = an integrated lifecycle spanning marketing, sales, CS, support, and partnerships, rewired by the rise of the "GTM engineer" who turns sales workflows into agents. Vercel concrete playbooks: a lead agent collapsed 10 inbound SDRs down to 1 QA'ing a $1K/year agent; a "deal-bot" revealed that a top loss was actually failure to reach the economic buyer (not price). Segment on X=size / Y=growth-potential plus workload; treat GTM itself as a product.

## Key ideas

1. **GTM engineer is the new wedge role** — A former sales engineer shadows the best human performer, writes the workflow, builds an agent on top. 6 weeks, 1 person → kept lead-to-opp conversion flat while freeing 9 SDRs for outbound.
2. **Build-vs-buy is tilting toward build** — Agents are cheap ($1K/year lead agent), context is esoteric, off-the-shelf AI tools pull from same databases; alpha is internal content and workflow.
3. **GTM is a product** — Map customer journey from "first heard of us" to 5-year wall-to-wall account and design experiences at every touch. Stripe's first meeting was a whiteboarding session, not a discovery quiz.
4. **Segment on two axes beyond size** — Stripe: size × growth-potential × business-model (B2B/B2C/platform/marketplace). Vercel: size × growth × Crux traffic rank × workload type.
5. **80% of customers buy to avoid pain or reduce risk** — Only 1 in 5 buys on upside; founders habitually sell vision, enterprise buyers want career-risk reduction.
