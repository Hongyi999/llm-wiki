# Source: Jason Fried challenges your thinking on fundraising, goals, growth, and more

**Guest**: Jason Fried (co-founder/CEO of 37signals; maker of Basecamp, HEY, ONCE)
**Published**: 2023-12-17
**Duration**: ~1h 50m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dAnF0tk0di8

## Executive summary

Fried makes the full case for the bootstrapped "stay-up" as a legitimate — and vastly more common — alternative to the VC unicorn path. With ~75 employees, 100k+ paying customers, double-digit millions in annual profit, and no investors/board, 37signals runs on constraints: two-person feature teams, six-week appetites (not estimates), no long-term plans, decisions made by gut and "how does it feel?" His meta-claim: Silicon Valley made the most profitable style of business (software) the least profitable by removing the constraint of having to make money.

## Key ideas

1. **Stay-ups over startups; bootstrapping as a real alternative** — 24 profitable years, 100k+ customers, double-digit millions profit/year with ~75 employees vs. competitors with 1,500-3,000. "Silicon Valley has found a way to make the most profitable style of business the least profitable."
2. **Appetites, not estimates ([[source-ryan-singer|Shape Up]])** — Every feature capped at 6 weeks, 2 people (1 designer + 1 engineer). If work hasn't crested the "hill" by the deadline, it usually dies. Two-week cool-downs follow each cycle; "sprints" are rejected because you can't sprint continuously.
3. **Small teams are a destination, not a stepping stone** — "If I had 1,000 people, I wouldn't know what to do with them." Smallness keeps products simple, forces one price, avoids the enterprise tax.
4. **Gut, feeling, and intuition as decision inputs** — 37signals has no OKRs, no KPIs, no revenue or growth targets. Internal vocabulary is "What do you think? How does this feel?" not "Prove it / justify it." "Unless you're letting a machine make the decision, it's a judgment call."
5. **Promises, long-term plans, and VC capital all remove useful constraints** — "Promises are the downfall of every business." No annual plan, no multi-year roadmap. VC money removes the have-to-make-money constraint. Peter Rahal's dad: "Go sell 1,000 fucking bars and come back." Independence is the root principle.
6. **Work isn't war** — Reject military metaphors ("target customers," "make a killing," "Navy SEALs"). Frame work through creation, pride, and alternatives rather than conquest.

## Notable quote

> "Starting a business is actually way easier than staying in business… Staying is harder than starting. So I'm here to celebrate stay-ups."

## B6 angle (hiring, management, leadership Part 2)

Fried is the strongest anti-VC counter-voice in the B-series — a direct rebuttal to the growth-at-all-costs premise underlying most B5 voices ([[source-ben-horowitz|Horowitz]], [[source-melanie-perkins|Perkins]], [[source-brian-chesky|Chesky]]). For leadership, he contributes an under-represented stance: leadership by taste and gut rather than by metrics, with constraint-driven management (two people, six weeks, no promises) as its own coherent system. Pairs especially well with [[source-tobi-lutke|Lütke's]] discipline and Chesky's "be in the details" while staking out the opposite pole from enterprise-scale operators.
