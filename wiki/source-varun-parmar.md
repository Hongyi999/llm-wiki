# Source: An inside look at how Miro builds product | Varun Parmar (CPO of Miro)

**Guest**: Varun Parmar (CPO at Miro, ex-CPO Box, ex-Adobe Document Cloud)
**Published**: 2023-04-20
**Duration**: ~1h 25m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=furNg4njlsg

## Executive summary

Parmar opens the hood on Miro's unusually structured product org. Miro runs an "AMPED" cross-functional model (Analytics, Marketing, Product, Engineering, Design), organizes 450+ PMs into persona-focused streams, uses a rolling six-month roadmap (80% commit / 50% commit), tracks cycle times at every stage (P-strat → P0 → P1 → P2), and operates on one motto: "deliver customer value faster with high quality." Parmar is unusually direct about the role of competition.

## Key ideas

1. **AMPED product org** — Every stream is cross-functional with Analytics, Marketing, Product, Engineering, Design all embedded. Product marketing sits *with* the product team to enforce positioning/packaging thinking early, not downstream.
2. **Products always get better or worse — never stay the same** — Every release is a chess move vs. competitors in the customer's mind. Counters the "ignore the competition" orthodoxy.
3. **Be the first to hit the brick wall** — Speed is the single biggest determinant in an innovation-centric market. Learn faster, fail faster, correct course faster. (Inspired by Slootman's *Amp It Up*.)
4. **Quality = binary classification, not long documents** — Miro's design leadership classifies every shipped feature monthly as high-quality or not, with reasons. Calibrates shared taste faster than writing definitions (like showing pink vs. red instead of describing them).
5. **Rolling 6-month roadmap, 80/50 resolution** — First 3 months at 80% commitment, next 3 at 50%. Annual strategy white paper sets the bets; roadmaps update every quarter.
6. **Miro Connect ritual** — Every other Friday, product teams set up "pitch booths" in each hub. One story: an engineer recognized a problem another team faced and replaced 3 months of planned work with 3 hours of code that night.
7. **70/20/10 horizon allocation** — 70% current business, 20% adjacent (12-36 months), 10% longer-term bets. 20-40% separately earmarked for tech debt.

## B6 angle (hiring, management, leadership Part 2)

Parmar's contribution is on *scaling* leadership. He codifies what many canonical voices describe anecdotally: how to run 450 PMs without bureaucratic sclerosis. His "drive accountability as a product leader / drive improvement as a stream leader" duality, plus the offsite "feedback to Varun" session where eight leaders critique him openly, is a direct operational counterpart to [[source-ben-horowitz|Horowitz's]] management teachings and [[source-melanie-perkins|Perkins']] craft-of-leadership posts. The competition-as-primary-variable stance is a rare counterweight to [[source-molly-graham|Graham]]/PG-style "ignore competitors" advice in B5.
