# Synthesis: B6 Leadership Patterns

Across 24 B6 transcripts on hiring, management, and leadership (older episodes, 2022-01 through 2024-01), **eight convergent patterns** emerge — points where credible operators from different companies independently arrive at the same move. Sharpens and extends the B5 canon. The disagreements are covered in [[comparison-b6-leadership-approaches]].

## 1. Interviewing is a different skill from the job

Nearly every B6 voice independently critiques traditional interview loops:

- **[[source-merci-grace|Grace]]**: Real-work projects (3 hours; free) — strongest signal at Slack.
- **[[source-melissa-tan|Tan]]**: Presentation + prep call with feedback; watch incorporation.
- **[[source-casey-winters_|Winters]]**: Skip practiced answers; throw real scenarios.
- **[[source-ethan-evans|Evans]]**: Enthusiasm + impact stories beat activity recitation.
- **[[source-keith-yandell|Yandell]]**: Interview for culture fit ("Are you an asshole?").

**Shared insight**: Traditional interviews test interview skill. Scalable hiring approximates the job itself. Extends B5's [[source-farhan-thawar|Thawar]] job-trials and [[source-jonathan-lowenhar|Lowenhar]] Who-method consensus.

## 2. First PM hire should come from inside

- **[[source-gokul-rajaram|Rajaram]]**: Always from inside — existing engineer/designer/analyst.
- **[[source-eeke-de-milliano|de Milliano]]**: Stripe resisted PMs for ~100 employees.
- **[[source-josh-miller|Miller]]**: Browser Company has no PMs; PM is a role on a project, not an org.
- **[[source-melissa-tan|Tan]]**: First *growth* hire = portfolio-manager generalist, not specialist.

**Shared insight**: External PMs with unfamiliar processes get "rejected by the body." The first PM role is almost always grown into by an existing employee who has founder and team trust.

## 3. Founder-mode is a real pattern, not just a slogan

- **[[source-brian-chesky|Chesky]]**: CEO as CPO; functional org; one roadmap; 2 launches/year. See [[founder-mode]].
- **[[source-tobi-lutke|Lütke]]** (B5): Exothermic leadership; inject heat; reject OKRs.
- **[[source-jason-fried|Fried]]**: Same instinct, radically smaller — 75 people, no OKRs, no KPIs.
- **[[source-josh-miller|Miller]]**: Company is the product; founder curates the crew.

**Shared insight**: Founders have intuition no hire can match. How to preserve and scale that intuition (keep small, stay in details, or bake into rituals) is where voices differ — but all reject "delegate and empower" as an automatic default.

## 4. Product ops / operating infrastructure is emerging as its own discipline

- **[[source-christine-itwaru|Itwaru]]**: Canonical treatment of [[product-operations|product operations]].
- **[[source-varun-parmar|Parmar]]**: AMPED org + cycle-time tracking.
- **[[source-eeke-de-milliano|de Milliano]]**: Operating principles vs. values; process as variance-reducer.
- **[[source-alex-hardimen|Hardiman]]**: Functions × missions org with embedded editors.
- **[[source-jason-fried|Fried]]**: The anti-pole — no ops at all.

**Shared insight**: Once you pass ~100 PMs, org infrastructure between teams becomes its own job. Who keeps revenue teams aligned with product? Who builds the tool stack? Who enforces launch readiness?

## 5. Career leverage is bottom-up, not ladder-climb

- **[[source-ethan-evans|Evans]]**: [[magic-loop|The Magic Loop]] — 5-step algorithm for ICs and middle managers.
- **[[source-jules-walter|Walter]]**: Mentor-ask ladder; reverse-engineer artifacts; working backwards from learning outcome.
- **[[source-sriram-and-aarthi|Sriram & Aarthi]]**: Build personal brand; network; publish.
- **[[source-paige-costello|Costello]]**: Three Es — Experience, Exposure, Education.
- **[[source-will-larson|Larson]]**: Writing is the leverage multiplier; 1,000 posts over 16 years.

**Shared insight**: Don't wait for your manager to advance your career. All five prescribe explicit, repeatable self-directed moves. Extends B5's [[source-molly-graham|Graham]] "give away your Legos" and [[source-fareed-mosavat|Mosavat]] "Product Leader Canyon" with the IC-layer mechanics.

## 6. Coaching as script, not just reflection

- **[[source-matt-mochary|Mochary]]**: Literal Google Docs scripting firing, fear-naming, layoffs. See [[mochary-method]].
- **[[source-alisa-cohn|Cohn]]** (B5): Scripted observable-facts + hope-for-future.
- **[[source-rachel-lockett|Lockett]]** (B5): "Help me understand" + [[grow-model|GROW]].
- **[[source-ken-norton|Norton]]**: [[creative-vs-reactive-leadership|Creative vs. reactive]] diagnostic.
- **[[source-paige-costello|Costello]]**: Conscious Leadership + SBI feedback.

**Shared insight**: The hardest parts of leadership (firing, feedback, decisions in fear) are **tractable with scripts**. Script-work CEO coaching is a distinct and reproducible tradition.

## 7. Non-traditional org structures are common enough to be a pattern

- **[[source-josh-miller|Miller]]** (Browser Company): Membership team; storytelling team; no PMs.
- **[[source-eeke-de-milliano|de Milliano]]** (Stripe/Retool): No PMs until ~100; internal-startup model.
- **[[source-alex-hardimen|Hardiman]]** (NYT): Functions × missions; embedded editors.
- **[[source-varun-parmar|Parmar]]** (Miro): AMPED cross-functional streams.
- **[[source-jason-fried|Fried]]** (37signals): Two-person feature teams, no roadmap past 6 weeks.

**Shared insight**: The "standard" tech product org (PM + design + eng triad, scrum/sprints, quarterly planning) is one pattern among many. The operative question is: what's the *unit of decision-making* in your company, and who is in it?

## 8. Metrics are an input to leadership, not the output

- **[[source-josh-miller|Miller]]**: Optimize for feelings; metrics as fidelity check.
- **[[source-itamar-gilad|Gilad]]**: Metrics tree + confidence meter; evidence-guided.
- **[[source-brian-chesky|Chesky]]**: Launch calendar trumps metrics; no A/B-ing a whole house.
- **[[source-jason-fried|Fried]]**: No OKRs, KPIs, revenue, or growth targets.
- **[[source-patrick-campbell|Campbell]]**: Tactical retention (18-30 seconds on cancel flow) vs. strategic.
- **[[source-crystal-w|Widjaja]]**: "Please don't track your KPIs. Please track your user journeys."
- **[[source-kristen-berman|Berman]]**: "Uncomfortably specific behavior" — not "sign up" but "two 10-min workouts with two different instructors."

**Shared insight**: Pure metrics-driven PM is a failure mode in B6 as much as B5. Leaders choose *how much* metric discipline to apply and *where*. B6 adds a darker note: Berman's LendingClub confession — she became a "predatory lender" under ill-specified conversion incentives. "We are what we measure."

## Meta-pattern: older episodes are more contrarian

The B6 batch is dominated by operators willing to reject the consensus: Fried rejects scale, Miller rejects metrics, Chesky rejects delegation, Mochary rejects intuition, Rajaram rejects external hiring. This isn't a coincidence — Lenny's earliest invitees were more established operators with less to lose, and less algorithmic filter on what "PM best practice" looks like.
