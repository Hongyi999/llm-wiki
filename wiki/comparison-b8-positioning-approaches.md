# Comparison: B8 Positioning Approaches

The B8 batch surfaces **seven distinct schools** of positioning/narrative thought. Each sits at a different *altitude* of the same stack — some are about market/category selection, others about the sentence on the homepage, others about delivery craft.

## Seven schools

| School | Canonical voice | Core move |
|--------|-----------------|-----------|
| **Market selection** | [[source-andrew-wilkinson]] | Fish where the fish are; pick boring niches. |
| **Hidden-vision positioning** | [[source-ivan-zhao]] | Sugar-coated broccoli; hide vision inside familiar form factor. |
| **Category creation** | [[source-barbra-gago]] | Budget + language + competition; dual-track. |
| **Lifecycle-aware positioning** | [[source-geoffrey-moore]] | Crossing the chasm; four GTM playbooks. |
| **Brand-as-promise** | [[source-seth-godin]] | Four strategic choices; smallest viable audience. |
| **Structured brand methodology** | [[source-arielle-jackson]] | 3-P framework; seven naming criteria; bar test. |
| **Narrative + storytelling craft** | [[source-matthew-dicks]], [[source-nancy-duarte]], [[source-tristan-de-montebello]] | Five-second moment; what-is/what-could-be; Bow and Arrow. |

## Five key disagreements

### 1. Create a category, or fit into one?

- **[[source-barbra-gago|Gago]]**: Both. Dual-track: sell into existing category, build thought leadership for new.
- **[[source-jag-duggal|Duggal]]**: Fundamentally different. Don't be incrementally better.
- **[[source-ivan-zhao|Zhao]]**: Hide your new category inside an existing form factor (docs).
- **[[source-geoffrey-moore|Moore]]**: Fit into the pragmatist's existing pain; stage-aware.

**Real disagreement**: Whether to lead with newness (Duggal) or familiarity (Zhao, Moore).

### 2. Brand = promise or brand = methodology?

- **[[source-seth-godin|Godin]]**: Brand is a promise. If the promise is remarkable and kept, brand compounds.
- **[[source-arielle-jackson|Jackson]]**: Brand requires a structured method (3-P framework, naming criteria, bar test). Without rigor, brand decays.
- **[[source-ami-vora|Vora]]**: Brand is also *internal* metaphor infrastructure (Dolores Park, hill-climb).

**Convergence**: All three agree brand is more than logo. Disagreement on whether structure or promise is the primary lever.

### 3. Who is the hero of the story?

- **[[source-nancy-duarte|Duarte]]**: Audience is the hero; presenter is Obi-Wan.
- **[[source-matthew-dicks|Dicks]]**: Must be *your* story; vulnerability is the connection.
- **[[source-donna-lichaw|Lichaw]]**: Leader must be the hero of their *own* story first — inside-out.
- **[[source-seth-godin|Godin]]**: Customer is the hero of their life; your promise enables theirs.

**Real disagreement**: Whether the narrator disappears into the audience's story (Duarte, Godin) or provides a template by being vulnerable (Dicks, Lichaw).

### 4. How narrow should you start?

- **[[source-andrew-wilkinson|Wilkinson]]**: As narrow as necessary to find a first win — then repeat.
- **[[source-geoffrey-moore|Moore]]**: Beachhead = 30-50% of a small, coherent segment within 2 years.
- **[[source-seth-godin|Godin]]**: Smallest viable audience — 400 users.
- **[[source-ivan-zhao|Zhao]]**: Horizontal beachhead (everyone using notes) is a legitimate alternative.

**Convergence**: All four reject "we're for everyone." Disagreement on whether narrowness is a constraint (Moore, Godin, Wilkinson) or a trap (Zhao's horizontal bet).

### 5. Synthetic virality or earned word-of-mouth?

- **[[source-seth-godin|Godin]]**: Purple Cow — design the product so the person's life gets better *if* they tell friends. Earned + engineered.
- **[[source-jag-duggal|Duggal]]**: Customer fanaticism produces organic word-of-mouth; no referral mechanics needed.
- **[[source-lulu-cheng-meservey|Meservey]]**: Cultural erogenous zones + concentric circles; go direct.
- **[[source-jason-feifer|Feifer]]**: Press serves readers; pitch a counterintuitive business decision.

**Real disagreement**: Whether the mechanism is product-design (Godin, Duggal), audience-shaping (Meservey), or media-fitting (Feifer).

## Synthesis

B8 is a *stack problem*. Each school correctly addresses its altitude but becomes harmful when applied at the wrong altitude. Naming and brand methodology (Jackson) won't save a company that hasn't selected the right market (Wilkinson) or category (Gago). Story craft (Dicks, Duarte) won't save a speaker with no narrative arc (Raskin, Godin).
