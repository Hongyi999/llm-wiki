# Positioning Canon

A landscape page cataloging the positioning/narrative frameworks across B8 and the B-series. Each framework sits at a different altitude of the same problem: telling the right people about your product in a way that moves them.

## Altitude layers (top to bottom)

### 1. Market selection (before positioning)

- **[[source-andrew-wilkinson|Andrew Wilkinson]]** — Fish where the fish are. Pick boring/niche markets.
- **[[source-ivan-zhao|Ivan Zhao]] (Notion)** — Sugar-coated broccoli: hide your vision inside a form factor people already want.

### 2. Category (before positioning)

- **[[source-barbra-gago|Barbra Gago]]** — Category validation via budget/language/competition; dual-track strategy (sell in old, build new).
- **[[source-jag-duggal|Jag Duggal]]** — Fundamentally different, not incrementally better. Category-design sibling to Dunford.

### 3. Lifecycle stage (positioning changes by stage)

- **[[source-geoffrey-moore|Geoffrey Moore]]** — Crossing the Chasm; four playbooks for four inflection points. "We believe what you believe" (early market) → "We need what you have" (post-chasm).

### 4. Positioning framework (the sentence)

- **[[source-arielle-jackson|Arielle Jackson]]** — 3-P framework (Purpose, Positioning, Personality); classic template: "For [target] who [need], [product] is a [category] that [benefit]; unlike [alternative], we [differentiator]."
- **[[source-seth-godin|Seth Godin]]** — Four strategic choices: customer, competition, validation, distribution. Smallest viable audience.

### 5. Brand (living the positioning)

- **[[source-arielle-jackson|Arielle Jackson]]** — Aaker's 5 personality dimensions + "we are X but not Y."
- **[[source-seth-godin|Seth Godin]]** — Brand = promise, not logo.

### 6. Narrative (the story machine)

- **Andy Raskin** (external canonical, not yet ingested) — Strategic narrative: name the shift → promise the future → show the obstacles → produce the evidence.
- **[[source-nancy-duarte|Nancy Duarte]]** — "What is / what could be / new bliss" rise-and-fall structure. Audience is the hero.
- **[[source-lulu-cheng-meservey|Lulu Cheng Meservey]]** — Cultural erogenous zones; concentric circles; go direct.

### 7. Storytelling craft (the individual story)

- **[[source-matthew-dicks|Matthew Dicks]]** — Every story is one five-second moment; start close to the end; dinner test.
- **[[source-tristan-de-montebello|Tristan de Montebello]]** — Bow and Arrow: one sentence you want remembered.

### 8. Delivery (the human speaking)

- **[[source-matt-abrahams|Matt Abrahams]]** — Dare to be dull; anxiety-as-excitement; double-exhale breath.
- **[[source-tristan-de-montebello|Tristan de Montebello]]** — Accordion Method; stay-in-character.

### 9. Org-internal narrative

- **[[source-ami-vora|Ami Vora]]** — Metaphor-as-alignment-infrastructure (face-to-face, Dolores Park, hill-climb).
- **[[source-donna-lichaw|Donna Lichaw]]** — Leader's self-story as upstream input.

### 10. Press & distribution

- **[[source-jason-feifer|Jason Feifer]]** — Editors serve readers, not founders. Publication mission mapping.
- **[[source-emilie-gerber|Emilie Gerber]]** — Press as recruiting/sales collateral.
- **[[source-luc-levesque|Luc Levesque]]** — Growth advisor playbook; AI-search disruption.

## The shared insight

Positioning is a *stack*, not a slogan. At each altitude, a different framework is needed; most positioning failures come from applying the wrong-altitude framework (e.g., writing a tagline when the problem is market selection; doing narrative when the product doesn't yet have a category).
