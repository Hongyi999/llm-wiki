# Source: Be fundamentally different, not incrementally better | Jag Duggal (Nubank)

**Guest**: Jag Duggal (Nubank CPO; ex-Facebook, ex-Google, ex-Quantcast)
**Published**: 2024-05-16
**Duration**: ~1h 35m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8A7wXK2MncQ

## Executive summary

Category winners are built by being fundamentally different, not incrementally better, and word-of-mouth growth (80-90% of Nubank's) follows from obsessive fanaticism about the customer. Operationalized with a culture that makes "we want our customers to love us fanatically" the #1 value, [[source-sean-ellis-growth-hacker|Sean Ellis]] PMF scoring (50% bar, not 40%), Amazon-style mock press releases, and bullseye-cohort analysis over aggregate metrics.

## Key ideas

1. **Fundamentally different > incrementally better** — Only a fundamentally different experience creates the emotional pull that drives word-of-mouth; incremental improvements don't.
2. **Sean Ellis score as scaling gate** — Don't scale until ~50% of users say they'd be "very disappointed" if the product went away; hunt for the bullseye cohort where the score is highest.
3. **Strategy = coherent, concentrated bets** — Borrowing from [[source-richard-rumelt|Rumelt]]: strategy is a specific diagnosis + leveraged response, not a vision. Concentration builds wealth; diversification preserves it.
4. **Culture makes the standard real** — Nubank's 5 values (esp. "customers love us fanatically," "owners not renters") are memorized and used in decision-making.
5. **Hypothesis-led, anecdote-trusted discovery** — Form a crisp hypothesis before research, then play devil's advocate. "Pick up the phone and call 10 customers" beats formal research.

## B8 angle

"Fundamentally different, not incrementally better" is a category-design sibling to [[source-arielle-jackson|April Dunford]]-style positioning — where Dunford starts from competitive alternatives, Duggal starts from emotional customer love as measured by Sean Ellis. Operational rigor (50% PMF threshold, bullseye cohort hunting, mock press releases) makes this a more instrumented playbook than [[source-geoffrey-moore|Moore's]] frameworks.
