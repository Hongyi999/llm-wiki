# Lessons from Airtable's unconventional growth strategy | Zoelle Egner

**Guest**: Zoelle Egner
**Published**: 2023-01-29
**Duration**: 1:13:52
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0P8LMyeYl1U](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0P8LMyeYl1U)

## Executive summary

Zoelle Egner — Airtable's 11th employee — pulls back the curtain on how Airtable grew from 15 employees closing six-figure deals to an $11B company without the usual playbook. Her core insight: small companies can "punch above their weight" via brand trust signals (polished emails, relevant templates, fixed broken links, billboards), then activate champions inside customer organizations rather than chasing IT buyers. She also shares lessons from co-founding VaccinateCA, which started as "phones, Discord, and a spreadsheet" and became the US's most comprehensive vaccine database.

## Key ideas

1. **Punching above your weight through brand trust**: Small details — polished emails, industry-relevant samples, fixing broken links — signal that a tiny company operates at a much higher level, building disproportionate trust.
2. **Champions vs. buyers**: At Airtable, the person building bases on Friday nights with wine was not the IT buyer. Investing in champions first, then leveraging their adoption to sell to IT, was the growth engine.
3. **Elevate a profession, not create a category**: Helping to define and elevate a profession (as Gainsight did for Customer Success) is more durable than creating a Gartner category. A job is identity; a category is budget.
4. **Customer success before sales**: Airtable had customer success before it had a sales team. Over-investing in early users created self-replicating evangelists (intra-company virality).
5. **Laughably small MVPs**: VaccinateCA was phones + Discord + spreadsheet and still had massive impact. Starting with the minimum is possible even in high-stakes environments.

- **Champions vs. buyers** — Prioritizing user-champions over economic buyers.
- **Intra-company virality** — User-to-user spread within customer organizations.

## B4 angle

Essential for PMs thinking about go-to-market and horizontal-product growth. The champion-first strategy (tracking how many users got promoted for using your product as an unofficial KPI) is a radical reframe of customer success metrics.
