# Source: The engineering mindset | Will Larson (Carta, Stripe, Uber, Calm, Digg)

**Guest**: Will Larson (Carta CTO; formerly Stripe, Uber, Calm, Digg; author of *An Elegant Puzzle*, *Staff Engineer*, *The Engineering Executive's Primer*)
**Published**: 2024-01-07
**Duration**: ~1h 16m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z9ftpRhRiJE

## Executive summary

Larson argues the post-ZIRP moment is actually good for engineers because it forces companies to stop coddling them and hold them accountable as peers. He walks through systems thinking, writing-as-career, why every company has a strategy (just usually unwritten), and why EM/PM pairs should share performance ratings. Throughout, the throughline is: boring, honest, reversible policies beat fashionable ones.

## Key ideas

1. **Stop coddling engineers — it hurts them** — The ZIRP era optimized middle management on retention, so orgs avoided giving engineers hard problems or holding them accountable. "We can actually hold them accountable, which means we can put them in senior roles." Precondition for a real Staff-Engineer track.
2. **Systems thinking = stocks + flows; but reality is never wrong** — Model any problem (hiring pipeline, incident response) as stocks and flows, find where the model disagrees with reality, fix the model. Caution: at Stripe his team got stuck "prioritizing measurement instead of improvement." You must cut at some point.
3. **Every company has a strategy; writing it down is the unlock** — "There's always a product strategy. Sometimes it's bad." Good strategies are often boring (Uber ran its own data centers, Stripe was a Ruby monolith, Carta has a standard kit) and use constraints to focus energy. [[source-richard-rumelt|Rumelt]]'s three parts: diagnosis, guiding policies, actions.
4. **EM/PM pairs should get the same performance rating** — Larson and Carta's CPO calibrate together. Shared perf ratings force shared incentives, which dissolves most EM/PM conflict. Most conflicts are not villainy — they're failure to understand the other side's true needs.
5. **Write what energizes you; publishing > polishing** — ~1,000 posts over 16 years by refusing to write for deadlines, money, or audience. "The biggest risk to content creation is quitting." For career-advancement writing, write 2-3 great artifacts, not a newsletter. Pair writing topics to your current job to multiply leverage.
6. **Good values are honest, applicable, and reversible** — "Integrity" is an identity value (no company would claim the opposite). Useful values are reversible (Stripe's "optimize globally" vs. Uber's implicit "do what's good for your team") and screen out real hires.
