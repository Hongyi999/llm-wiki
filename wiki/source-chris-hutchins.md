# Launching and growing a podcast | Chris Hutchins (All the Hacks, Wealthfront, Google)

**Guest**: Chris Hutchins  
**Published**: 2022-12-18  
**Duration**: 1:17:35  
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kc1XhB4k0Eg](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kc1XhB4k0Eg)

## Executive summary

Chris Hutchins, former Head of New Product Strategy at Wealthfront and host of the All the Hacks podcast, shares hard-won lessons on both building big product bets inside companies and launching an independent podcast. On the product side, he details how Andy Rachleff's product-market-fit philosophy shaped the Self-Driving Money initiative at Wealthfront, emphasizing the gap between prototype enthusiasm and real adoption. On the creator side, Hutchins provides a comprehensive tactical guide to podcasting — from picking a topic and launching strategy to growth levers like cross-promotion, short-form video clips, and community engagement — all framed through the lens of treating content creation like product development.

## Key ideas

1. **Repeat the mission relentlessly**: Drawing from his founder experience, Hutchins argues that PMs must restate the product vision and mission constantly — every all-hands, every meeting — because people absorb it on different timelines. The same principle applies to podcasting: he opens every episode with the same positioning line so listeners can immediately explain the show to others.

2. **Prototype enthusiasm ≠ product-market fit**: Wealthfront's Self-Driving Money (Autopilot) product generated strong excitement in prototype testing — one participant wanted to fetch their spouse — but never became a top-of-funnel growth channel. Hutchins learned that Andy Rachleff's definition of PMF (exponential organic growth) is the true bar, not interview enthusiasm.

3. **State your intent to build internal buy-in**: Andy Rachleff taught Hutchins that when pushing bold ideas, explicitly stating your intent ('all I care about is company success, not ownership') disarms colleagues' suspicion of self-interest. This is essential for PMs driving big bets inside established companies.

4. **Content is product-market fit for creators**: Hutchins frames podcast success identically to product success — content quality is your PMF. He advises creators to be someone's favorite podcast rather than everyone's mediocre one, echoing the 1000 True Fans concept, and to commit to a season of eight episodes before deciding to continue.

5. **Leverage platforms with built-in distribution**: Since podcasting lacks algorithmic distribution (unlike TikTok, YouTube Shorts, or Instagram Reels), Hutchins recommends creating clips for these platforms to build brand awareness and credibility, even if direct download attribution is hard to measure.

## Concepts covered

- **Self-Driving Money** — Wealthfront's vision for automating a user's entire financial life — monitoring checking accounts and automatically sweeping excess into savings, Roth IRAs, 529s, and brokerage accounts.
- **Product-market fit as exponential organic growth** — Andy Rachleff's definition that true PMF manifests as exponential organic growth, not just positive user feedback in testing.
- **Slugging average over batting average** — Andy Rachleff's philosophy that infrequent home runs (big product bets) are more valuable than consistent base hits (incremental improvements).
- **1000 True Fans** — Kevin Kelly's concept that creators need a dedicated core audience of passionate advocates rather than mass mediocre reach, applied here to podcast strategy.
- **Cross-promotion and distribution leverage** — Growth tactic of appearing on other podcasts and creating short-form video clips for platforms with algorithmic distribution to drive brand awareness.
