# Launching and growing a podcast | Chris Hutchins (All the Hacks, Wealthfront, Google)

**Guest**: Chris Hutchins
**Published**: 2022-12-18
**Duration**: 1:17:35
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kc1XhB4k0Eg](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kc1XhB4k0Eg)

## Executive summary

This episode splits into two halves: Chris's PM experience taking big product bets at Wealthfront (building Self-Driving Money / Autopilot), and a tactical guide to launching and growing a podcast. On the product side, he shares lessons on vision repetition, stating intent before advocacy, and the gap between interview enthusiasm and real adoption. The podcasting half applies PM principles — [[product-market-fit|PMF]], 1000 true fans, slugging average over batting average — to creator work.

## Key ideas

1. **Repeat your vision relentlessly**: Borrowed from founder discipline — if an engineer in the hallway can't recite the goal, you haven't communicated enough. Context evaporates faster than PMs realize.
2. **State intent before advocacy**: When pushing bold or controversial ideas, declare intent first ("all I care about is the company winning"). This disarms the assumption of self-interest.
3. **Content is PMF**: No amount of growth hacking compensates for bad content. The podcast equivalent of PMF is "be someone's favorite."
4. **Product vision vs. feature set**: Distinguish what the product does from how the user should feel. Vision should start with "imagine a world where someone can feel this way."
5. **Slugging average over batting average**: From Andy Rachleff — optimize for magnitude of wins, not frequency. Big bets require leadership buy-in that failures are acceptable given transformative upside.

- **Stating intent** — Disarming self-interest assumptions before advocacy.

## Notable quote

## B4 angle

The Wealthfront Autopilot story is a cautionary tale about the gap between customer-interview enthusiasm and real adoption. The lesson about stating intent before pushing bold bets is immediately actionable for any PM managing up.
