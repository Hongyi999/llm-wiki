# Four Ps Framework

[[todd-jackson|Todd Jackson]]'s clarity checklist for any PMF claim. Before measuring PMF, the founder must write down concrete answers to all four. If any P is fuzzy, the founder is pre-PMF regardless of revenue.

> Not to be confused with the classic marketing 4 Ps (product, price, place, promotion). This is a PMF framework, not a marketing mix.

## The four Ps

### Persona
Exactly **who** is this for? Not "SMBs" or "developers" — something sharp enough that you could name 20 real people who fit.
- Role, context, budget, urgency.
- Test: can your go-to-market team identify a prospect as in-persona or out-of-persona in 30 seconds?

### Problem
What **burning pain** are you solving for that persona?
- Must be growing, not static — a pain that's getting worse in the world.
- Must already be on their priority list (not a "nice to have").
- Test: if you took the product away, would they rebuild it in spreadsheets? (See [[vanta|Vanta]]'s actual origin.)

### Promise
What **specific outcome** will you deliver?
- Not feature language. Not "we help you do X." A measurable before/after: "your SOC 2 audit goes from 6 months to 6 weeks."
- The promise is what sets pricing power. See [[rahul-vohra|Rahul Vohra]]'s positioning-before-pricing rule at [[superhuman]].

### Product
How does the **product** embody the promise?
- Not a feature list. A mental model the user can hold in their head.
- Test: can a user describe the product to a colleague in one sentence?

## Why all four, not just one

Founders typically nail one or two Ps and get stuck at [[four-levels-of-pmf|PMF Level 1–2]]:
- **Persona + problem but no promise** → users nod along but don't buy.
- **Promise + product but no persona** → building in circles looking for someone who cares.
- **Everything but product** → consulting business masquerading as SaaS.
- **Everything but problem** → a solution in search of a problem. Classic.

## Using the Four Ps diagnostically

When a product is stalled, Todd recommends writing the four Ps on a whiteboard and asking the team to attack each independently:
1. Is this persona **findable** and **growing**?
2. Is this problem **painful** and **on the priority list**?
3. Is this promise **specific** and **different**?
4. Is this product the **cleanest possible embodiment** of the promise?

If three are strong and one is weak, the weak one is almost always what's holding back PMF.
