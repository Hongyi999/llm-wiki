# Source: Lessons in product leadership and AI strategy from Glean, Google, Amazon, and Slack | Tamar Yehoshua

**Guest**: Tamar Yehoshua
**Published**: 2024-09-26

## Executive summary

Tamar Yehoshua, president of product and technology at Glean and former CPO of Slack, shares hard-won lessons from leadership roles at Google, Amazon, and Slack on career growth, cross-functional alignment, and navigating the AI transformation. She argues that companies don't need to be well-run to win — product-market fit is what matters — and that career plans are overrated; instead, follow great people where you'll learn the most. Her most actionable insights concern building strong product-engineering partnerships through shared OKR reviews and explicit role division. On AI, she warns that PMs who dismiss new tools risk being left behind, while creative, strategic PMs will thrive as routine execution gets automated.

## Key ideas

1. **Companies don't need to be well-run to win**: Product-market fit trumps operational excellence at the growth stage. Tamar has seen chaotic companies with high executive turnover and unhappy employees post amazing numbers, while well-oiled companies flatline. Operational discipline matters more at scale (5,000+ employees).
2. **Follow people, not plans**: Instead of five-year career plans, follow the best practitioners — people who are exceptional at their craft. The skills you learn are permanent even if the company fails, and the network you build from working alongside great people compounds throughout your career.
3. **Async OKR reviews at scale**: At Slack, Tamar and CTO Cal Henderson replaced live OKR review meetings (which consumed 300+ person-hours) with async Slack video clips per team, then watched them together and only scheduled follow-up meetings for red-flagged projects — dramatically reducing meeting overhead while maintaining alignment.
4. **AI will separate creative PMs from execution PMs**: Routine PM work (updating Jira, creating launch calendars, status summaries) will be automated. PMs who are primarily executors face displacement, while strategic, creative PMs who can identify what to build and differentiate will thrive. The lines between PM, design, and engineering roles will blur.
5. **Build for LLM improvement, not around LLM limitations**: When building AI products, don't let your differentiator be compensating for current LLM weaknesses — those gaps will close. Ensure your product architecture gets better as foundation models improve, so advancement lifts your product rather than eroding your value.

## Concepts & frameworks

- **Beginner's Mind** — Marc Benioff's principle of approaching problems assuming you know nothing, listening to customers rather than projecting assumptions.
- **Non-deterministic AI products** — The challenge of building enterprise products on LLMs whose outputs vary unpredictably, requiring guardrails and user education about what AI can and cannot do.
- **Prototyping mindset** — Stewart Butterfield's approach of building throwaway code prototypes to feel and test ideas rather than relying on mockups or specs.
- **Vocal minority trap** — The tendency for PMs to over-index on unhappy users of existing features rather than designing for the larger future user base.
