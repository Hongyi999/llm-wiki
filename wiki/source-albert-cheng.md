# Finding hidden growth opportunities in your product | Albert Cheng (Duolingo, Grammarly, Chess.com)

**Guest**: Albert Cheng  
**Published**: 2025-10-05  
**Duration**: 1:25:25  
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2BKmNmnEj9w](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2BKmNmnEj9w)

## Executive summary

Albert Cheng, who has led growth at Duolingo, Grammarly, and Chess.com, shares his playbook for finding and scaling growth opportunities in consumer subscription products. His core framework is explore-and-exploit: discover a counterintuitive insight through experimentation, then systematically apply it across the product. He details how flipping the emotional framing of Chess.com's game review from negative to positive grew reviews by 25% and subscriptions by 20%, and how Grammarly nearly doubled upgrade rates by sampling premium suggestions for free users. Cheng also advocates for aggressive experimentation targets (1,000 per year), explains why user retention is the single most important metric for consumer subscription businesses, and shares how AI is accelerating the growth workflow.

## Key ideas

1. **Explore and Exploit Framework**: When in exploratory mode, find the right mountain to climb by testing diverse hypotheses. When in exploitation mode, take a winning insight and systematically apply it across adjacent product surfaces. Oscillate between the two based on experiment win rates -- declining significance signals it is time to explore again.

2. **Grammarly's Freemium Breakthrough**: The biggest monetization win at Grammarly came from interspersing paid suggestion types among free corrections, creating an in-context reverse trial. This shifted user perception from spelling checker to powerful writing tool and nearly doubled subscription upgrade rates.

3. **Positivity-First UX Drives Retention**: At Chess.com, 80% of game reviews happened after wins, contradicting the assumption users wanted to analyze losses. Reframing the post-loss experience around brilliant moves and encouragement grew game reviews by 25%, subscriptions by 20%, and retention significantly.

4. **Retention Is Gold for Consumer Subscription**: D1 retention of 30-40% is a solid benchmark for consumer apps, but existing user retention (not new user retention) is the metric that most compounds over time. Resurrected users become a surprisingly large component of DAU at mature companies.

5. **1,000 Experiments Per Year as Cultural North Star**: Setting an ambitious experiment volume target forces conversations about tooling, no-code infrastructure, cross-functional experimentation (lifecycle marketing, ASO, copy), and culture change -- even if hitting the exact number matters less than the structural improvements it catalyzes.

## Concepts covered

- **[[growth-hacking|Explore and Exploit]]** — A growth framework for oscillating between divergent hypothesis testing and convergent scaling of proven insights.
- **[[growth-hacking|Freemium Reverse Trial]]** — Sampling premium features inline with free usage to shift user perception and drive upgrades without time-gated trials.
- **[[growth-hacking|Current User Retention]]** — The stickiness metric for habitual users that compounds most over time, distinct from new-user D1/D7 retention.
- **Resurrected User Experience** — A dedicated product surface for dormant users returning to the product, which becomes a major DAU driver at scale.
- **Screenshot Tracking for Virality** — Instrumenting screenshot hotspots to identify organic sharing moments, then investing design resources to amplify them.
- **Text-to-SQL for Data Democratization** — Using AI-powered Slack bots to answer ad-hoc data questions instantly, removing bottlenecks and increasing data-informed culture.
