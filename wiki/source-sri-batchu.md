# Source: Lessons from scaling Ramp | Sri Batchu (Ramp, Instacart, Opendoor)

**Guest**: Sri Batchu (Head of Growth at Ramp; ex-Instacart growth, ex-VP Ops at Opendoor)
**Published**: 2023-06-25
**Duration**: ~1h 17m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RcYCU5UAZOk

## Executive summary

How Ramp's growth engine looks unremarkable on a channel-mix pie chart but wins through relentless technology + data investment in every channel, especially a dedicated growth-engineering team that supports sales. Core thesis: *design* of a growth team matters less than its culture, cadence, MVP mentality, and north-star metric framework.

## Key ideas

1. **Cap table as a growth strategy** — Ramp's founders (already successful exit) seeded the cap table with early-stage founders whose companies became initial customers. Customers were influenced more by customer references than by VC recommendations. "Cap table as GTM."
2. **Growth-engineering as distinct function** — Dedicated eng team goaled on sales pipeline and payback period (not product metrics). Builds AI-driven workflow automation, third-party data enrichment, prospect prioritization, drafted responses — well before "AI" was a buzzword. Sales is Ramp's most efficient channel.
3. **The natural sequence of B2B channels** — Founder-led sales → first salespeople → low-cost targeted marketing (content, community, events) → PR → paid + SEO together. "Channels get more expensive as you scale but more effective as you understand your customer better."
4. **Two-metric north star framework** — One volume metric (Instacart's Monthly Active Orderers, Ramp's SQL pipeline dollars), one efficiency metric. Every team's local metric has a *translation factor* to the company north star, updated every 6 months.
5. **Activation = magic-number engagement events** — Facebook's "10 friends in 7 days", Instacart's "3 orders in first month", Ramp's "4 events in first 30 days". These "escape velocity" thresholds anchor the activation team.
