# Nickey Skarstad (Airbnb, Etsy, Shopify, Duolingo) on translating vision into goals, operationalizing product quality, second-order decisions, brainstorming, influence, and much more

**Guest**: Nickey Skarstad  
**Published**: 2022-07-18  
**Duration**: 1:00:54  

## Executive summary

Nickey Skarstad, a director of product management at Duolingo with deep experience at Etsy, Airbnb, Shopify, and The Wing, shares a practitioner's guide to translating high-level vision into executable goals. She walks through the vision-mission-strategy-objectives pyramid, explains how Airbnb's obsession with review rates operationalized product quality for Experiences, and introduces the calendar color-coding technique for identifying personal energy patterns. The episode emphasizes second-order thinking as a core PM skill, advocates for cross-functional product reviews at key gates, and makes a strong case for knowing what gives you energy before choosing your next role.

## Key ideas

1. **Operationalizing product quality through balancing metrics**: At Airbnb Experiences, the team used review rate as their top-line metric instead of pure growth, ensuring every booked experience delivered genuine quality. At Etsy, adding friction to seller onboarding paradoxically improved first-sale-in-seven-days rates, proving that quality metrics can be powerful counterweights to growth metrics.

2. **Vision-mission-strategy-objectives pyramid**: Nickey uses a simple top-down pyramid framework where vision (10-year horizon) cascades into mission, then strategy, then quarterly OKRs. The critical first principle is involving the team early -- strategies crafted in a vacuum fail because people who weren't part of the journey won't buy in.

3. **Calendar color-coding for career energy diagnosis**: To decide if a role is right, Nickey color-codes every meeting as red (draining), yellow (neutral), or green (energizing) after it ends. When her Shopify calendar was almost all red and yellow, she knew platform work wasn't her thing and pivoted to Duolingo's zero-to-one challenge.

4. **Second-order thinking as product infrastructure**: Every product decision cascades through the system -- changing an Airbnb listing title length affects hosts, design layouts, and downstream features. PMs should embed second-order impact analysis into spec templates and align on first principles before design begins, using frameworks from Donella Meadows' Thinking in Systems.

5. **Three-gate product review process**: Nickey advocates for three cross-functional checkpoints: (1) first principles alignment on what problem to solve, (2) approach review including technical architecture, and (3) ship-readiness review. Siloed functional reviews (design-only, engineering-only) miss critical feedback and create downstream surprises.

## Concepts covered

- **Balancing Metrics** — Quality metrics (like review rates or first-sale-in-seven-days) that counterweight pure growth metrics, preventing teams from optimizing volume at the expense of user experience.
- **Second-Order Thinking** — The discipline of tracing how today's product decisions cascade through the system to affect future decisions, requiring first-principles alignment and systems-level awareness.
- **Vision-Mission-Strategy-Objectives Pyramid** — A top-down planning framework that cascades from a 10-year vision down through mission, strategy, and quarterly OKRs to connect long-term direction with near-term execution.
- **Calendar Color-Coding** — A personal energy diagnostic where you mark every meeting red, yellow, or green after it happens to reveal whether your current role aligns with what gives you energy.
- **One-Way vs. Two-Way Door Decisions** — A decision classification framework where irreversible decisions deserve deliberation and stakeholder buy-in, while reversible decisions should be delegated for speed.
- **GM vs. Functional Org Structure** — Two models for product orgs: functional (reporting up through a VP of product) works for established products needing skill development; GM (business unit) works for nascent products needing autonomy.
