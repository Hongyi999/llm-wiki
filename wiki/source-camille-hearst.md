# Source: Monetizing passions, scaling marketplaces, stories from a creator economy vet | Camille Hearst

**Guest**: Camille Hearst (Head of Fan Monetization at Spotify; ex-Patreon, ex-YouTube, ex-Apple iTunes; founder of Kit, acquired by Patreon)
**Published**: 2023-08-20
**Duration**: ~1h 3m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oB-BjEjxHNw

## Executive summary

A career spent exclusively on the supply side of creator marketplaces. Core thesis: creator platforms will keep winning because aggregation economics are brutal, but fundamental creator problems — consistency, monetization, audience growth, curation — are far from solved. Musicians in particular suffer from a "starving artist ethos" that actively resists monetization.

## Key ideas

1. **Supply-side is where marketplaces are won or lost** — After Hailo pain ("doesn't matter how nice the UX is if no cars are available"), she rejects chicken-and-egg framing: almost always start by solving supply pain. "If the supplier doesn't want to actually make money…"
2. **The hamster wheel of content creation** — Once fans buy annual subscriptions, creators can never stop. Platforms should smooth revenue (Patreon/Substack model), offer creator financing, or automate some content.
3. **Three ingredients for creator success** — Consistency/predictability (10,000-hour rule applied to posting cadence), collaboration/cross-pollination, and associating with trusted curators acting like a record label.
4. **Musicians resist monetization more than other creators** — "Starving artist ethos" where they want to give fans everything free. Spotify's fan monetization work (merch, listening parties) has to fight this cultural headwind.
5. **Sell a company like you fundraise** — Start relationships with potential acquirers from day one so acquisition talks start on fifth meeting, not first. Never say "want to buy us?" — say "want a strategic partnership?"

## B7 angle

Useful counterweight: Hearst argues on creator platforms, "demand-side" growth tactics are downstream of supply-side quality and retention — a convergence with [[source-casey-winters|Casey Winters's]] marketplace writing but with a sharper point on supplier psychology (musicians actively sabotaging their own monetization). "Consistency + collaboration + curators" triad for creator success maps onto [[source-brian-balfour|Balfour's]] channel-product fit at the individual creator level.
