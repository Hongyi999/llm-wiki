# Lessons from Atlassian | Megan Cook (Head of Product, Jira)

**Guest**: Megan Cook  
**Published**: 2024-02-04  
**Duration**: 1:21:08  
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MQQ1wohqArk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MQQ1wohqArk)

## Executive summary

Megan Cook, Head of Product for Jira at Atlassian (11-year tenure), shares how one of the world's most ubiquitous software products stays ahead despite constant competitive pressure. She reveals Atlassian's stage-gated innovation process (wonder, explore, make, impact, scale) that has enabled the company to launch 15 successful product lines. Cook emphasizes the role of psychological safety and play in driving innovation, describing how noticing fear replacing play on her team led to concrete interventions like peer feedback groups and 'Fight Club' -- a weekly 30-minute session dedicated to resolving conflicts early. The episode covers practical remote work strategies for PMs, a detailed playbook for getting executive buy-in through early stakeholder partnership rather than polished presentations, and how investing in CSAT improvements (the unglamorous 'good fight') can unlock both customer satisfaction and revenue growth.

## Key ideas

1. **The Opposite of Play Is Fear, Not Work**: After noticing incremental thinking and over-polished presentations on her team, Cook learned from performance coach Ben Crowe that fear -- not work -- is the opposite of play. She implemented peer feedback groups and failure-sharing onsites to rebuild psychological safety and unlock bolder ideas.

2. **Fight Club for Leadership Triads**: Cook's leadership triad (PM, engineering, design leads) holds a weekly 30-minute 'Fight Club' dedicated to surfacing and resolving conflicts early. By normalizing disagreement through a recurring slot, difficult conversations happen before they grow into bigger problems.

3. **Stage-Gated Innovation: Wonder, Explore, Make, Impact, Scale**: Atlassian's process for launching new products starts with a single person with an idea (wonder), adds 2-3 people for prototyping (explore), then a full team of ~12 to build (make), before proving self-sufficient revenue (impact) and scaling. Jira Product Discovery emerged from this process via an internal innovation pitch.

4. **Getting Buy-In Is a Journey, Not a Meeting**: The biggest mistake people make is treating buy-in as a single presentation. Instead, loop in affected stakeholders early with rough drafts, be explicit about hypotheses versus facts, create advocates before the decision meeting, and clearly state what you need from the room (decision, feedback, or help testing an assumption).

5. **Fighting the Good Fight: CSAT Investment**: Cook led a cross-company effort to improve Jira's customer satisfaction scores by focusing on usability -- the unglamorous foundation that determines whether customers can access the value of new features. She secured 40-person investment by starting small, showing quick wins, using 'show don't tell' with customer videos, and finding low-cost shepherd partnerships with dependent teams.

## Concepts covered

- **Play vs Fear** — The insight that the opposite of play is not work but fear, and that psychological safety is required for innovative, non-incremental thinking.
- **Fight Club** — A weekly 30-minute meeting for leadership triads explicitly dedicated to surfacing and resolving disagreements before they escalate.
- **Wonder-Explore-Make-Impact-Scale** — Atlassian's five-stage gated process for incubating new products with progressively increasing investment and validation.
- **Show Don't Tell** — Design leadership principle of using customer videos, prototypes, and visual before-after comparisons to build emotional buy-in.
- **CSAT-Driven Product Investment** — Using customer satisfaction data to justify investment in unglamorous usability improvements that unlock revenue through better acquisition and expansion.
