# Source: Knowing When to Leave Your Job ft. Ada Chen Rekhi

**Guest**: Ada Chen Rekhi (Executive coach to founders; Co-founder of Notejoy; former SVP Marketing at SurveyMonkey; former growth marketing lead at LinkedIn)
**Published**: 2023-04-21
**Duration**: ~3m 50s (clip); full episode ~1h 15m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l-T8sNRcWQk

## Executive summary

Ada Chen Rekhi shares frameworks for making better career and life decisions, centered on a personal values exercise, "curiosity loops" for gathering structured advice, and the explore-exploit model for career strategy. She argues that most people do not need a coach, but that values alignment -- not resume optimization -- is the key to avoiding the trap of a successful-looking but unfulfilling career.

## Key ideas

1. **Curiosity Loops** — A structured method for gathering contextual advice: (1) craft a specific, unbiased question, (2) curate 5-10 respondents who either know the domain or know you well, (3) make the ask lightweight (pick 2 of 9 options + why), (4) process the data and close the loop by thanking people. Works for professional and personal decisions alike. Based on the marketing concept of customer advisory councils, applied to life.

2. **Values exercise as decision compass** — A 10-15 minute exercise where you select from a word list, filter into groups, then stack-rank into 3-5 value statements. The output is an "inner scorecard" (Warren Buffett's term) that grounds decisions against what actually matters to you rather than the "outer scorecard" of status, titles, and logos. Values should be revisited and updated over time.

3. **Explore and exploit for career strategy** — Early career should be exploration mode with a thesis: take roles to test hypotheses about what you like (startup vs. corporate, marketing vs. product, etc.). Once you find a rich vein, shift to exploit mode -- but exploit deliberately by choosing what to learn, not just chasing the biggest title. Ada used LinkedIn explicitly to learn growth and subscriptions before founding her next company.

4. **Don't be the frog** — The boiling frog metaphor applied to careers: small incremental discomforts (bad manager, no learning, misaligned values) accumulate without triggering action. The key question is not "am I comfortable right now?" but "which way is the temperature trending?" Inertia is the default; agency requires actively monitoring your environment.

5. **Most people don't need a coach** — Before hiring a coach, exhaust alternatives: curiosity loops for advice, courses (like Reforge) for skills, building a personal support tribe for emotional resilience. Coaches are most valuable for founders in hyper-growth (chaos + imposter syndrome + time pressure) and for sensitive interpersonal situations. If you do hire one, talk to 2-3 coaches before committing -- half of people go with the first coach they meet.

6. **Eating your vegetables** — Research shows kids need 10-12 exposures to develop appreciation for vegetables. The same applies to career skills: networking, content creation, public speaking all feel terrible at first. Distinguish "I'm bad at this because I'm new" from "I genuinely don't like this." Ada forced herself to attend events, hand out 10 business cards, and touch the back wall before leaving -- those relationships became foundational.

7. **Women in leadership: the unspoken rules** — Career advancement has implicit rules that no one tells you. Feedback on presentation, physical appearance, and communication style is almost never given to women because the social risk is too high for the feedback-giver. Ada argues for finding safe channels (coaches, trusted mentors) to surface these blind spots rather than remaining powerless.
