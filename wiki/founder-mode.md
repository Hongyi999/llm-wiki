# Founder Mode

**Founder mode** is the leadership thesis that founders should stay deeply in the details of their company rather than delegate to a layer of professional managers. Popularized by [[source-brian-chesky|Brian Chesky]] and Paul Graham; represents a pushback against the "delegate and empower" orthodoxy that dominated Silicon Valley leadership discourse in the 2010s.

## Core claim

"If you don't know the details, how do you know people are doing a good job?"

Founders have accumulated intuition and context no hire can match. The "delegate and empower" model fragments that context across professional managers who compete rather than cohere, producing: divisions, dependencies, advocacy, politics, bureaucracy, complacency.

## Chesky's concrete operating manual (Airbnb 2020+)

1. **CEO as de facto CPO** — Directly reviews every project on a 1/2/4/8/12-week cadence.
2. **Functional, not divisional** — Design, engineering, product-marketing, marketing, comms, sales, ops each report to the CEO.
3. **One shared roadmap** — Rolling 2-year product roadmap updated monthly, shared across the company.
4. **Two launches a year** — Every May and November. Launches force storytelling discipline and prevent incrementalism.
5. **PM merged with product marketing** — "You can't build a product unless you know how to talk about the product." PMs fewer and more senior.
6. **Every exec a domain expert** — Not a people manager.
7. **As few layers as possible**.

## Related voices

- [[source-tobi-lutke|Tobi Lütke]] (Shopify) — Parallel stance: founder injects heat, sets standards; rejects OKRs and process-management aesthetics. Heat-injection / exothermic leadership.
- [[source-jason-fried|Jason Fried]] (37signals) — Same instinct at a radically different scale — leadership by taste and gut, but via staying small rather than scaling founder intimacy.
- [[source-marc-benioff|Marc Benioff]] — Orchestra-leader CEO model (partial overlap).

## Tensions and disagreements

- **Founder mode vs. professional management** — [[source-camille-fournier|Camille Fournier]], [[source-ben-horowitz|Ben Horowitz]], [[source-alisa-cohn|Alisa Cohn]] argue the scaling path *is* learning to delegate well; [[source-dharmesh-shah|Dharmesh Shah]] argues founders should structurally design around their weaknesses (zero direct reports), not into management.
- **Founder mode vs. feature-factory risk** — [[source-gokul-rajaram|Gokul Rajaram]] warns that founders "becoming too tactical and dictating features" is a top product-development pitfall.
- **Founder mode at scale** — Critics worry it doesn't scale past a single founder's attention. Chesky's response: "First 1-2 years were brutally hands-on; after that, culture absorbed it and surprises flipped from 90% bad to 90% good."

## Canonical quote

> "Way too many founders apologize for how they want to run the company. They find some midpoint between how they want to run a company and how the people they lead want to run the company. That's a good way to make everyone miserable. What everyone really wants is clarity." — Brian Chesky
