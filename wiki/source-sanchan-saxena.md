## Executive summary

Sanchan Saxena distills lessons from serving under Kevin Systrom, Brian Chesky, and Brian Armstrong into a framework for product leadership under extreme uncertainty. His thesis: great products come from *intentionality* (vision-first, tell the GPS where to go) plus *rapid recovery* (iterate fast after the punch lands) — not from measure-twice-cut-once data worship. He walks through Airbnb's COVID survival, Instagram Stories' vision-led launch, and Coinbase's DRI/RAPID decision culture as case studies, and closes with hiring advice rooted in "content" (what to do when) over process.

## Key ideas

1. **Intentionality over A/B testing**: "You don't ask GPS where to go — you tell it." Set product vision first (e.g., Instagram Stories as real-time news → refused to allow old photos); use A/B tests to find the fastest route *to* that vision, not to discover it. Gut is "data that isn't statistically significant yet."
2. **15-out-of-10 design, unconstrained**: Learned from Chesky — first design the ideal end-state product without resource constraints, then build the lovable minimum in *one* location (e.g., a single best-in-class Airbnb Plus lounge) rather than an MVP across 120 countries. Scale what worked.
3. **Rapid recovery beats perfect planning (Airbnb COVID)**: Revenue dropped to single digits; 1,900 layoffs. Survival came from two-week planning cycles, dissolving sub-teams into "#Airbnb, one team," pivoting from feature/revenue obsession to "belief obsession," and leader-led storytelling when data said "you're dying."
4. **DRI / RAPID decision-making (Coinbase)**: Every project has a Directly Responsible Individual who collects written input across functions and makes the call; others "disagree and champion." Kills designing-by-committee and the pattern where decision speed scales with relationship strength rather than with role.
5. **Hire for content, not process; little bets over five-year plans**: What correlates with startup success is "someone knew what to do when," not adherence to scrum or process. Careers work the same way — take little bets, build rapid recovery, pivot fast; the best PMs learn the art (instinct, community) alongside the science (data, experimentation).

## Notable quote

> Sanchan Saxena: "The real genius isn't to dot every i, to cross every t before you get started. The real genius is, what do you do when shit goes wrong? Rapid recovery is the key to success."

## B5 angle

Sanchan's DRI/RAPID pattern is a decisions-at-scale playbook (clear ownership, written input, disagree-and-champion) directly applicable to B5 hiring and operating structure. His crisis-leadership lessons (one team, belief obsession, calm under fire) are a template for how leaders retain and motivate talent when the market tells them they're dying.
