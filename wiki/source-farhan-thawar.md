# Why Shopify's high-intensity, hard-path culture works | Farhan Thawar (VP and Head of Engineering)

**Guest**: Farhan Thawar
**Published**: 2025-03-13
**Duration**: ~1:30:00
**YouTube**: [Lenny's Podcast — Farhan Thawar](https://www.lennyspodcast.com/)

## Executive summary

Farhan Thawar — VP and Head of Engineering at [[shopify|Shopify]], previously co-founder/CTO of XtremeLabs (acquired by Pivotal), ex-Microsoft, ex-Trilogy — argues that most companies default to the "easy path" (lots of meetings, solo coding, comfort) and that the harder path — fewer meetings, constant pair programming, shipping daily, blunt feedback — is both more productive and more humane long-term. Shopify deliberately runs hot: a "Meetingageddon" purge, a GSD ("get shit done") prize, 6-week performance reviews, job trials instead of interviews. Intensity is framed as a gift to talented people who want to do their best work — A-players stay, pretenders self-select out, the org compounds.

## Key ideas

1. **Hard path vs. easy path**: The easy path (meetings, async Slack, solo coding) feels productive but hides low throughput; the hard path (pair programming, live decisions, same-day shipping) is uncomfortable but dramatically faster. Leaders should name the hard path explicitly and make it the default.
2. **Meetingageddon and calendar hygiene**: Shopify literally deleted all recurring meetings and forced people to rebuild their calendar from zero. Most meetings never came back, freeing makers to make; a standing GSD prize rewards shipping over coordinating.
3. **Pair/mob programming as the unit of work**: Farhan treats solo coding as a bug. Pairing doubles learning speed, removes knowledge silos, creates real-time mentorship, and is how he onboards senior hires and rotates talent — including the 1,000 interns in 2025 plan.
4. **Hire via job trials, not interviews**: Interviews select for interview skill; paid trials (a few days to a couple weeks doing real work with the team) select for the actual job. Combine with the "trust battery" — trust starts at 50% and charges/drains based on delivery.
5. **Six-week performance cycles + Delete Code Club**: Annual reviews are too slow to change behavior; 6-week cycles keep feedback tight. Celebrating deleted code (not lines written) reorients engineers toward leverage and simplicity.
