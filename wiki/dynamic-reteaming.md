# Dynamic Reteaming

**Dynamic reteaming** is Heidi Helfand's reframe of "reorgs" — not rare top-down events but a continuous, inevitable set of patterns in any scaling organization. Named in her book *Dynamic Reteaming*.

## The five patterns

1. **One-by-one** — Joining or leaving the company. The smallest unit of team change.
2. **Grow-and-split** — A team gets too big; split it along a natural seam.
3. **Merging** — Two teams combine; needs a "story of our team" timeline exercise.
4. **Isolation** — Beneficial silo for innovation or emergency response; physically separate, protect from disturbance.
5. **Switching** — People move between teams within the company for fulfillment or learning.

Calling a change by the right pattern name changes how you manage it.

## Practices

- **Whiteboard reteaming** — Draft org designs on whiteboards with team names, missions, open slots; invite feedback; time-box tightly.
- **Self-selection reteaming** — Redgate Software style: people choose their team from a menu of missions.
- **Pairing + switching rotations** — Build knowledge redundancy so no single person owns a system.
- **Hack days / ShipIt Days** — Cross-team bonding and serendipitous collaborations.

## Decision framework

**RIDE** (credited to Pat Wadors at Procore/UKG):
- **R**equest — who asked for the change?
- **I**nput — who gives input?
- **D**ecide — who decides?
- **E**xecute — who implements?

Clarifying RIDE up front prevents the chaos that makes reorgs feel arbitrary.

## Anti-patterns

- **Percentage allocation fallacy** — Splitting people across 10%/20%/30% of many projects; nobody ships anything.
- **"Poof they're gone"** — Surprise reassignments without communication.
- **Spreading high performers to "seed" other teams** — Jon Walker's AppFolio experiment; kills the original magic without transferring it.
