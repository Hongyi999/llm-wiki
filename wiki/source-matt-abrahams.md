# Source: How to speak more confidently and persuasively | Matt Abrahams

**Guest**: Matt Abrahams (Stanford GSB lecturer; author of *Think Faster, Talk Smarter*; host of *Think Fast Talk Smart* podcast)
**Published**: 2024-03-31
**Duration**: ~1h 12m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LpbBzmXrzEY

## Executive summary

Communication anxiety is innate and manageable, and most of the speaking we do is spontaneous (Q&A, feedback, toasts, small talk), which requires paradoxical preparation. Core techniques: desensitizing (visualization), reframing physiological arousal as excitement, "daring to be dull" to strive for connection over perfection, and embodied techniques like the double-exhale breath.

## Key ideas

1. **Prepare to be spontaneous** — Like jazz musicians or athletes, on-the-spot speaking is a trained skill; build reusable structures (problem-solution-benefit, what/so what/now what) ahead of time.
2. **Dare to be dull** — Strive for connection over perfection; the pressure to be brilliant taxes cognitive bandwidth. "Just answer the question; you'll end up saying something better."
3. **Reframe anxiety as excitement** — Arousal has one physiological signature; the label is under your control. Alison Wood Brooks's research: saying "I'm excited" measurably improves perceived performance.
4. **Visualization as desensitization** — Running a mental dress rehearsal reduces novelty and anxiety, like a pilot's flight simulator.
5. **Embodied cognition / double-exhale breath** — Exhale twice as long as inhale to trigger the relaxation response; body states drive mental states (80% body-to-brain signaling).
