# How to be the best coach to product people | Petra Wille (Strong Product People)

**Guest**: Petra Wille  
**Published**: 2022-11-27  
**Duration**: 1:12:17  
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4n3ybRqU5mU](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4n3ybRqU5mU)

## Executive summary

Petra Wille, an independent product leadership coach and author of Strong Product People, distills a decade of coaching over 130 product leaders into actionable frameworks for developing PMs. She presents five ingredients for being the best PM coach: defining what good looks like, assessing where PMs are today, sharing a growth vision, co-creating a development plan, and following up consistently. She then pivots to storytelling as a career-critical skill, arguing that PMs chronically underinvest in story preparation time, and closes with a research-backed case for product communities of practice as a cost-effective alternative to expensive training programs.

## Key ideas

1. **Five ingredients for coaching PMs**: Define your compass (what a good PM looks like in your context), assess where each PM is today, share a vision for their growth, co-create a development plan they own, and follow up with regular nudges. Start with the development plan even if you haven't built the full compass yet.

2. **The PMwheel assessment framework**: Wille's eight-dimension assessment for PMs covers understanding problems, finding solutions, planning, getting it done, listening and learning, team dynamics, personal growth, and agile fundamentals. Each bucket has 15+ framing questions to create a well-rounded competency picture.

3. **Storytelling requires serious time investment**: PMs chronically underestimate how much time great storytellers invest. For a quarterly strategy narrative, Wille recommends two weeks of work (1-2 hours daily). Stories should use natural language that triggers emotional responses, avoiding jargon and three-letter abbreviations.

4. **Prepare stories in three lengths and three formats**: Have your narrative ready as a 75-second elevator pitch, a 6-minute pre-planning version, and an 18-minute full presentation. Each should exist in spoken, written, and visual formats to handle any communication context.

5. **Communities of practice scale PM development cheaply**: Internal communities of practice reduce development burden on product leads, improve retention (people stay where they learn), and cost less than training budgets or conference tickets. Success is measured by signal-to-noise ratio, not raw engagement.

## Concepts covered

- **PMwheel** — Wille's eight-dimensional PM competency assessment covering problem understanding, solution finding, planning, delivery, listening/learning, team dynamics, personal growth, and agile foundations.
- **Next bigger challenge** — A quarterly exercise where product leads identify the next stretch assignment for each direct report that would accelerate their growth, then watch for the opportunity to assign it.
- **Community of practice** — An internal or external group of practitioners who share learnings, reduce individual development burden, and improve retention by creating an environment of continuous peer-to-peer learning.
- **Hero's journey for product narratives** — Applying the classic hero's journey story structure to product narratives by placing either the team or the user at the center and framing obstacles as demons to overcome en route to a brighter future.
- **Personality traits vs. coachable skills** — Wille's distinction that traits like curiosity and empathy should be hired for because they're nearly impossible to develop, while skills like prioritization and discovery techniques can be coached.
