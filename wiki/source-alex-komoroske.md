# The magic of LLMs, the adjacent possible, and how to be a gardener-leader | Alex Komoroske

**Guest**: Alex Komoroske
**Published**: 2024-12-12
**Duration**: ~1:40:00
**YouTube**: [Lenny's Podcast — Alex Komoroske](https://www.lennyspodcast.com/)

## Executive summary

Alex Komoroske — co-founder of Common Tools / The Compendium, formerly Head of Corp Strategy at Stripe and 13 years at Google on Chrome and Search — blends complexity theory with product craft: organizations are slime molds, not machines; leaders should be gardeners, not builders; and LLMs are "magical duct tape" that collapse the cost of prototyping, making taste the scarce resource. He argues that the highest-leverage leadership act is planting acorns — tiny, cheap bets in the adjacent possible — and protecting them from the org's immune system. Execution alone is no longer the moat.

## Key ideas

1. **LLMs as magical duct tape; taste as the moat**: Because LLMs reduce the cost of producing a working prototype toward zero, execution stops being a differentiator. The scarce input becomes taste — knowing *what* to build and *when* to kill it. Hire for taste; develop it in others by exposing them to reps.
2. **Gardener vs. builder leadership**: Builders assume deterministic systems: specify, build, ship. Gardeners assume emergent systems: plant many seeds, prune ruthlessly, water what's working. Large orgs over-index on builders; gardener moves are what compound.
3. **Slime mold organizations**: Coordination cost scales roughly with the square of the org size, which is why big companies can't ship small things. The fix isn't more process; it's smaller, loosely-coupled units and norms that make bottom-up signal legible to the top.
4. **Adjacent possible and acorns**: Strategy is a walk through the adjacent possible, not a plan. Leaders should sponsor many acorns (tiny, cheap, asymmetric bets), accept that most die, and resist picking winners early. Kayfabe — the fiction that leaders know — kills acorns.
5. **Strategy salons, nerd clubs, and 70/30**: Alex runs small invite-only gatherings (strategy salons, nerd clubs) to surface weak signals cheaply. Personal rule: 70% executing today's work, 30% planting seeds for what's next; enforced with always-rules (non-negotiable) vs. sometimes-rules (negotiable).
