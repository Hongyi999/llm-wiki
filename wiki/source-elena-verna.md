# Source: 10 growth tactics that never work | Elena Verna

**Guest**: Elena Verna (third-time guest; growth advisor at Amplitude, Miro, Dropbox, SurveyMonkey; Reforge faculty)
**Published**: 2025-01-19
**Duration**: ~1h 35m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IHwS2By9UKM

## Executive summary

A contrarian catalog of ten growth anti-patterns Verna repeatedly sees waste resources at startups and mature companies alike. Thesis: growth is a young field flooded with shortcut-chasing newcomers, and most "growth hacks" are pattern-illiterate. Real leverage comes from earned channels, overlaying new growth models every 18 months, and not confusing scientific precision for progress.

## Key ideas

1. **Hire head of growth too early = destined to fail** — Growth teams can't manufacture PMF; founder-led growth must get you to $1-10M ARR (sometimes $100-200M) first. Equally, hiring a growth exec to reverse a declining business is a category error — growth can lift 10-15% but can't fix a broken core product.
2. **Rebrand/homepage redesign never drives growth** — "Never ever once have I seen a rebrand or redesign... produce good performance results." Best case: net neutral + 3-6 months of clawback.
3. **Own earned channels, not rented ones** — Organic/paid search and social make Google/Meta rich; algorithms giveth and taketh away. Virality, UGC, word-of-mouth, sharing loops are yours forever. Dropbox's sharing loop drives 50%+ of acquisition after 17 years.
4. **Overlay new growth models every 18 months; new channel every ~5 years** — Andrew Chen's Law of Shitty Clickthroughs: over-optimizing one loop has diminishing returns. Budget 20-25% of annual growth-team time to exploratory loops that won't have metrics for 12-18 months (Miroverse was unmetered for ~18 months before becoming an acquisition engine).
5. **Stop experimenting on everything** — "If every single one of your initiatives is an experiment, that's a problem... a paralyzing disease." Rule: if you can't reach sample size within a month, don't A/B test — do pre/post. Reserve rigor for high-traffic surfaces.
