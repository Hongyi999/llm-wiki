# Working Backwards

Working Backwards is Amazon's product development methodology where teams start by writing the press release and FAQ for the finished product *before* writing any code. The process forces clarity about who the customer is, what problem is being solved, and why it matters — before the team invests in building. Two B4 guests with direct Amazon experience provide authoritative accounts.

## Core mechanics

### The PR/FAQ

The central artifact is a 1-page mock press release plus a 5-page FAQ:

- **Press release** (1 page): Written as if the product has launched. Must include the customer, the problem, the solution, and a quote from a customer and a company leader. If you can't write a compelling press release, the product isn't worth building.
- **FAQ** (5 pages): Anticipated questions from customers (external FAQ) and from the business (internal FAQ — cost, timeline, risks, dependencies).

### The six-pager

For strategic decisions beyond individual products, Amazon uses narrative six-page memos read silently at the start of meetings. [[source-ian-mcallister|Ian McAllister]] describes Jeff Bezos's specific review style: reading the entire document, then starting questions from the end (where the weakest thinking usually hides).

### Key principles

- **No PowerPoint**: Narrative writing forces rigorous thinking that bullet points hide.
- **Customer-first framing**: The press release is written from the customer's perspective, not the company's.
- **Iterative refinement**: PR/FAQs go through multiple drafts. McAllister describes rewriting documents 5-7 times before leadership review.
