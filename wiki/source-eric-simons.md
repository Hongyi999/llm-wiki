# Source: Inside Bolt: From near-death to one of the fastest-growing products in history | Eric Simons

**Guest**: Eric Simons

## Executive summary

Eric Simons tells the story of Bolt, StackBlitz's AI coding product that went from zero to $40M ARR in five months after the company was on the verge of shutting down. The key enabler was WebContainer, a browser-based operating system seven years in the making that runs entirely on the user's CPU, giving Bolt zero-latency execution, near-infinite scalability, and a generous free tier that cloud-VM competitors cannot match. Simons credits Claude Sonnet as the specific model breakthrough that made AI-generated code reliable enough to cross the production-quality threshold, calling it a deterministic zero-to-one moment for AI coding. He argues that PMs, designers, and non-technical entrepreneurs are the primary beneficiaries of this shift, with 67% of Bolt's users being non-developers, and predicts that org charts across the software industry will fundamentally restructure as a result.

## Key ideas

1. **WebContainer as the deep-tech moat**: Bolt's core advantage is a WebAssembly-based operating system that runs inside the browser using client-side compute, eliminating cloud VM costs and latency. This took seven years to build and is analogous to how Figma built a browser-based rendering engine before anyone thought it possible -- a deep technology bet that enabled a radically different product experience.
2. **Claude Sonnet as the zero-to-one moment for AI coding**: Simons identifies Anthropic's Claude Sonnet (released June 2024) as the specific model that crossed the threshold for producing beautiful, functional, production-grade code. Software is uniquely suited for LLM improvement because code is deterministic -- it either runs or it doesn't -- enabling massive reinforcement learning at scale.
3. **The PM as the ideal AI power user**: With 67% of Bolt's users being non-developers, Simons argues that product managers are the best-positioned role for the AI coding era because their existing skills -- scoping, specifying requirements, communicating with developers -- translate directly to prompting AI agents effectively.
4. **Overnight success, seven years in the making**: StackBlitz survived by maintaining an extremely low burn rate, a stable core team of 5-7 people over 5+ years, and taking many shots on goal. The lesson is that deep technology bets require capital discipline and founder conviction through periods where the consensus view says you're wrong.
5. **Usage-based pricing over Netflix models**: Bolt discovered that users willingly pay more for additional AI inference because the ROI is tangible -- a $300 Bolt project replacing a $30K agency quote. This token-based pricing became the industry standard, replacing the flat-rate subscription model that prior AI tools used.
