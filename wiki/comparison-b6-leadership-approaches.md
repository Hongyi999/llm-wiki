# Comparison: B6 Leadership Approaches

Where [[comparison-b5-leadership-schools]] mapped **seven schools** of B5 leadership thought, the B6 batch surfaces five additional poles and sharpens the contrasts already visible. B6 is the "older episodes" batch on hiring/management/leadership (2022-01 through 2024-01) — less recent than B5 but often more contrarian.

## Five B6-distinctive poles

| Pole | Canonical voice | Core move |
|------|-----------------|-----------|
| **[[founder-mode|Founder-mode / leaders-in-the-details]]** | [[source-brian-chesky|Chesky]] | CEO as CPO; functional org; one shared roadmap; 2 launches/year. |
| **Calm-company / constraints-first** | [[source-jason-fried|Fried]] | Appetites, not estimates; no promises; small is a destination. |
| **Feelings-first / anti-operator** | [[source-josh-miller|Miller]] | Optimize for surprise/joy/airiness over metrics; company *is* the product. |
| **AMPED / cycle-time-maximalist** | [[source-varun-parmar|Parmar]] | A-M-P-E-D cross-functional teams; "be first to the brick wall." |
| **IC / middle-manager bottom-up** | [[source-ethan-evans|Evans]] | [[magic-loop\|The Magic Loop]]; the job before you have the job. |

Plus two coaching-canon additions:
- **Script-work (CEO coaching)** — [[source-matt-mochary|Mochary]]; [[mochary-method]]: literal scripts for firing, fear-naming, layoffs.
- **Creative vs. reactive diagnostic** — [[source-ken-norton|Norton]]; [[creative-vs-reactive-leadership]].

## Six disagreements sharpened by B6

### 1. Does the founder scale via mode or via small?

- **[[source-brian-chesky|Chesky]]**: Scale founder intimacy — CEO-as-CPO across 5,000+ people.
- **[[source-jason-fried|Fried]]**: Reject scaling; 75 employees is a destination. Founder intimacy stays intact because the org never outgrows it.
- **[[source-varun-parmar|Parmar]]**: Scale via mechanism — AMPED org, cycle-time metrics, rolling roadmap. Founder is a node, not the engine.

**Real disagreement**: Whether founder-mode is a *structural* choice (stay small) or an *operational* choice (stay central as you grow).

### 2. Metrics vs. feelings as product input

- **[[source-josh-miller|Miller]]**: "Optimize for feelings." Metrics are a fidelity check.
- **[[source-itamar-gilad|Gilad]]**: Evidence-guided; confidence meter; metrics tree.
- **[[source-brian-chesky|Chesky]]**: Metrics subordinate to the launch calendar, but don't A/B-test a whole house.

**Shared insight**: Pure-metrics PM is a failure mode. **Real disagreement** is whether the primary input is taste/feeling (Miller), evidence (Gilad), or storytelling (Chesky).

### 3. Interviewing vs. real-work hiring

- **[[source-merci-grace|Grace]]**: Make candidates do 3-hour real-work projects — strongest signal at Slack.
- **[[source-melissa-tan|Melissa Tan]]**: 1-week-prep presentation + prep call with real feedback; watch what they do with the feedback.
- **[[source-casey-winters_|Casey Winters (ep2)]]**: Skip practiced answers; throw real scenarios; watch them decide.
- **[[source-ethan-evans|Evans]]**: Enthusiasm + impact stories; "people want to work with people who want to work with them."

**Convergence**: Traditional interview loops over-select for interview skill. **Real disagreement**: How much real work is fair to ask for free.

### 4. First PM hire: inside vs. outside

- **[[source-gokul-rajaram|Rajaram]]**: Always from inside — existing engineer/designer/analyst who has founder and team trust.
- **[[source-eeke-de-milliano|de Milliano]]**: Stripe resisted PMs for ~100 employees because engineers were customers.
- **[[source-melissa-tan|Tan]]**: First *growth* hire: analytical portfolio manager, not a specialist.

**Convergence**: Don't rush to an external PM profile. First PM is almost always a role an existing employee grows into.

### 5. Are ops/process a Band-Aid or growth?

- **[[source-christine-itwaru|Itwaru]]**: Product ops is a sign of growth; scales the PM function without overloading.
- **[[source-casey-winters_|Casey Winters]]**: Ops as Band-Aid.
- **[[source-eeke-de-milliano|de Milliano]]**: "Process is variance-reducing" — brings low performers up, caps high performers.
- **[[source-jason-fried|Fried]]**: No OKRs, KPIs, or process at all. Gut-driven.

**Real disagreement**: Whether the cure for underperformance (process) is worse than the disease.

### 6. Where does career growth come from?

- **[[source-ethan-evans|Evans]]**: The [[magic-loop|Magic Loop]] — a repeatable 5-step algorithm.
- **[[source-jules-walter|Walter]]**: Mentorship ladder (smallest-possible-ask) + reverse-engineering artifacts + working backwards from a learning outcome.
- **[[source-ken-norton|Norton]]**: Inner work — the shift from reactive to creative leadership.
- **[[source-molly-graham|Graham]]** (B5): Give away your Legos; J-curve jumps.
- **[[source-sriram-and-aarthi|Sriram]]**: Build a personal brand; network; publish.

**Meta-pattern**: Five distinct career-leverage strategies, all valid for different failure modes. An operator who won't self-promote needs Sriram; one who won't self-reflect needs Norton; one who waits to be told what to do needs Evans.

## Synthesis

The B6 batch is where the **oldest, most contrarian** Lenny episodes live. Five themes emerge that sharpen but don't replace the B5 canon:

1. **Founder-mode is an older, more contrarian stance** than the "founder mode" Silicon Valley discourse of 2024 — [[source-tobi-lutke|Lütke]] and [[source-brian-chesky|Chesky]] were both arguing it in 2023.
2. **Bootstrapping and stay-up culture** (Fried, Campbell) are a distinct leadership tradition the B5 canon barely touches.
3. **Script-work CEO coaching** (Mochary) is the most operational leadership material anywhere in the corpus.
4. **Non-traditional product orgs** (Browser Company without PMs, Stripe without PMs until ~100 employees, NYT with embedded editors) are common enough to constitute a pattern.
5. **IC/mid-manager career leverage** (Evans, Walter, Sriram) is under-served by B5's CEO-heavy canon.
