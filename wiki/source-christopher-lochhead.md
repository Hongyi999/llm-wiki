# Source: How to become a category pirate | Christopher Lochhead

**Guest**: Christopher Lochhead (3x public-company CMO; author of *Play Bigger*, *Niche Down*, *Category Pirates*, *Snow Leopard*)
**Published**: 2023-09-17
**Duration**: ~1h 48m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mS4B541m9xg

## Executive summary

Maximalist case that category design — not product, brand, or positioning — is the primary driver of legendary outcomes in tech. HBR-published data: in tech categories, one company captures ~76% of total market cap; if you're "competing" you've already lost by accepting ~24%. Dense with polemic (Threads as failed "better Twitter," Amazon Fire Phone) and frameworks (languaging, framing-naming-claiming, dam-the-demand, backcasting, lightning strikes).

## Key ideas

1. **Category design thesis** — Category makes the product, not the other way. One company captures ~76% of tech category market cap. If you compete, you're fighting for 24%.
2. **Frame / name / claim a problem** — Legendary companies reframe the problem so a new solution is needed. Purell/Gojo (liquid soap, then hand sanitizer), Otis ("vertical railway"), Starbucks (grande/venti languaging to charge $3), Peloton.
3. **Languaging = strategic use of new language to change thinking** — OpenAI with "large language models" and "training data"; Starbucks with grande/venti.
4. **"Better Trap"** — Threads had the greatest distribution advantage in software history and still failed; attacked a known problem with a known solution framing. Amazon Fire Phone, Microsoft Stores, Red Bull Cola — all "better existing thing" bets that cratered.
5. **Dam the demand, don't compete** — Spinning reframed: why risk your life biking? Peloton: why drive to the gym?
6. **Backcasting (Mike Maples)** — Forecasting drags the past forward; backcasting starts from an imagined different future and reasons backward.
7. **Product-market fit is dangerous** — "Fit my product into a market that already exists." Correct: design a market for your product.
8. **Execution**: Magic triangle (product + company + category), lightning strikes (1-3x/year Hollywood launches), super consumers (~8-10% drive most profit), word-of-mouth as primary marketing.
9. **"Positioning is for losers"** — If you're positioning against competitors, you've accepted fighting for the 24%.

## Notable quote

> "Categories make products, not the other way around. Threads achieved product-market fit faster than any product in the history of the world. What you want is to design a market category for your product, not fit your product into a market category."

## B9 angle

High-value but polarizing; directly contradicts [[source-april-dunford|April Dunford]] on category creation being "the only way." Great raw material for comparison: **Lochhead (category design maximalism) vs. Dunford (positioning + bowling pin + category creation as one path)**. Tactical: (a) languaging as pricing lever, (b) dam-the-demand as alternative to head-to-head positioning, (c) super-consumer targeting, (d) lightning-strike launch cadence.
