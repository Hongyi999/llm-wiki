# A guide to difficult conversations, building high-trust teams, and designing a life you love | Rachel Lockett

**Guest**: Rachel Lockett
**Published**: 2025-11-23
**Duration**: 1:45:19
**YouTube**: [Lenny's Podcast — Rachel Lockett](https://www.lennyspodcast.com/)

## Executive summary

Rachel Lockett — executive coach (ex-Stripe, ex-Pinterest HR; now Lockett Coaching) — argues that the "human side" of company building is more tractable than most leaders think, and that technical founders under-invest in it because they climbed by having answers. Her antidote: concrete, structured micro-skills for coaching, listening, conflict, and co-founder hygiene. She demonstrates several in real time on the episode, turning soft skills into reusable tools.

## Key ideas

1. **Coach vs. advise (GROW model)**: Advise only when you know the right answer, it's urgent, or the person lacks the skill. Otherwise, use GROW (Goal, Reality, Options, Way forward) to let reports solve their own problems. People are more bought in to answers they generate themselves.
2. **Three levels of listening**: Level 1 is internal (thinking about yourself). Level 2 is focused (hearing words). Level 3 is global (body language, tone, context). Great leaders operate at Level 3 and reflect back both content and emotion — often in under a minute.
3. **Reframe conflict as mutual understanding, not winning**: Most leaders enter hard conversations to prove a point. Rachel's goal is mutual understanding. "Professionals have feelings" — acknowledging the emotional layer is what makes hard conversations productive rather than explosive.
4. **Burnout is a leak; gifts are the fuel**: Track five energizing and five depleting activities daily for two weeks. Then aggressively redesign your role toward your gifts (target 80%). Navigating your own career toward your gifts is your job, not your manager's.
5. **Co-founder vows + "enthusiastically rehire" test**: 65% of startups fail due to co-founder conflict. Co-founders need structured recommitments (quarterly check-ins, co-founder dinners) like marriages need date nights. For every team member ask: "Would I enthusiastically rehire this person?" If no, act.
