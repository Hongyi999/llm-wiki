# Source: Building high-performing teams | Melissa Tan (Webflow, Dropbox, Canva)

**Guest**: Melissa Tan (ex-Dropbox growth, ex-Webflow Head of Growth, advisor to [[canva]]/Grammarly/Miro/Ro)
**Published**: 2023-06-18
**Duration**: ~1h 14m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DoEfXj1b_ko

## Executive summary

Tan distills what she's seen across six high-performing teams: hire first-principles thinkers with growth mindsets, set crystal-clear results goals, wrap them in a team-first and ownership culture, develop people through their full lifecycle, and be extremely deliberate about your first growth hire (usually an analytical "portfolio manager," not a specialist). She delivers a frank retrospective on what Dropbox did right and wrong.

## Key ideas

1. **Dropbox's four lessons** — Right: selective hiring of humble first-principles thinkers; recruiting infused into culture; relentless execution. Wrong: too late to start sales/enterprise; treated growth as a "layer on top"; "growth was a dirty word" until self-serve slowed.
2. **Four ingredients of high-performing teams** — (1) Results/impact culture with very clear metrics, (2) Team-first collaboration (one + one = three), (3) Ownership mentality (carve scope people want to own; leader takes responsibility for failures), (4) Fun.
3. **Care deeply + challenge directly** — [[source-kim-scott|Radical Candor]] workshopped at Dropbox while Melissa was there. Direct feedback *is* how you show you care; state intention ("I'm saying this because I believe in you") and offer support.
4. **Hiring PMs: presentation + prep call that delivers feedback** — Melissa schedules a 1-week-prep presentation interview plus a prep call where she gives real feedback on the draft. How candidates incorporate (or don't) the feedback is the strongest signal of what working together will feel like.
5. **First growth hire = analytical portfolio manager, not specialist** — Don't hire a paid-acquisition expert early; hire an analytical first-principles thinker who can test channels like a portfolio. Product-growth PMs hire *later*, not first.
6. **Flying formation (DACI + operating rhythms)** — How growth works with marketing, core product, sales. Explicit doc: Driver/Accountable/Contributor/Informed, plus weekly metric review and quarterly planning cadences. Solves "growth as a layer on top" failure mode.

## B6 angle (hiring, management, leadership Part 2)

Tan is the canonical voice on *growth team* leadership specifically. Her emphasis on hiring untrained first-principles thinkers (Dropbox hiring smart people with no sales background, getting innovation as a result) is a direct echo of [[source-elizabeth-stone|Stone]]/[[source-joe-hudson|Hudson]]-era hiring philosophies but applied to a PLG era. Her "follow-me-from-company-to-company" retention as a manager, combined with her insistence that results-orientation and deep care coexist, adds a PLG-native counterpart to the more enterprise-leaning voices ([[source-ben-horowitz|Horowitz]], [[source-tobi-lutke|Lütke]]) in B5.
