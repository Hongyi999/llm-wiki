# Source: When to invest in new acquisition channels | Adam Grenier (Uber, MasterClass)

**Guest**: Adam Grenier (ex-Uber Head of Growth Marketing & Innovation; ex-Lambda; ex-MasterClass VP Marketing)
**Published**: 2022-09-15
**Duration**: ~1h 9m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-PDsvl2WCZU

## Executive summary

Three-ingredient framework for evaluating emerging acquisition channels (channel-customer-company fit, channel DNA, company DNA). Champions the "Growth CMO" as a successor to traditional CMO. Closes with candid real-talk on burnout vs. depression in tech. About 5% of truly-new channel bets pay off, but the ones that do (like early mobile install ads on Facebook) are game-changing.

## Key ideas

1. **Three-ingredient framework for emerging channels** — (a) Overlap between channel strength, customer need, business goal (Clubhouse fit Spotify; Paparazzi didn't); (b) Channel DNA — trajectory stage, monetization alignment (how Hotel Tonight got into Facebook's mobile-install alpha); (c) Company DNA — risk appetite, staffing, channel mix. Don't chase new channels before Google/Facebook basics work.
2. **Time-box channel experiments** — Don't let a test bleed past a quarter; a month of "fishing" gives signal. Match investment to content cost. Look for qualitative momentum, not statsig, on early channels.
3. **Growth CMO vs. traditional CMO** — Traditional CMOs treat brand as campaigns; Growth CMOs treat every investment as iterative product-cycle. Data-driven across full funnel (including brand), married to product, comfortable with agile sprints.
4. **Assume you no longer have PMF in a regime change** — "You had product-market fit in a different market. It's a different market now." Don't solve a demand-contraction problem by launching a new channel.
5. **Burnout vs. depression — adaptability is the tell** — Depression collapses motivation across life; exhaustion is contained to work. Leading signal of burnout: adaptability drops — someone stops being curious about new experiments.
6. **Improv as a growth skill** — "Yes, and..." and "the gift of details" translate directly into cross-functional collaboration and marketing-positioning craft.
