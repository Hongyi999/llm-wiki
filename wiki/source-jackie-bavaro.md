# Source: The art of product strategy and PM career growth | Jackie Bavaro (Asana, Google, Microsoft)

**Guest**: Jackie Bavaro
**Published**: 2024-04-07
**Duration**: 1:27:45
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aXGo1o_baBo

## Executive summary

Jackie Bavaro, author of Cracking the PM Interview and Cracking the PM Career and former head of product management at Asana, delivers the most tactically detailed breakdown of product strategy in this batch. She defines strategy as three components — vision (inspiring future picture), strategic framework (market, success criteria, big bets), and roadmap (feasibility check against the vision timeline) — and explains how most teams confuse a revenue target with a strategy. Drawing on eight years at Asana where she grew from first PM to head of PM, she shares how learning strategy is fundamentally experiential and collaborative, not something you can study in isolation. Her career advice centers on a powerful template for promotion conversations that reframes the request as future-oriented and brings your manager onto your side.

## Key ideas

1. **Three pillars of product strategy**: Vision (inspiring picture of the future), strategic framework (market definition, success criteria, and big bets on what it takes to win), and roadmap (backward-looking feasibility check that reveals whether your plan is a 5-year vision or a 30-year fantasy). Most teams are missing at least one pillar, leading to incomplete strategies.
2. **Good strategy is connecting the dots**: A CEO saying "increase revenue 50%" is not a strategy — it's an endpoint. Strategy is the chain of reasoning from business goal through market analysis, user insights, and prioritization down to specific product work. The test of a good strategy is whether people are confused — confusion reveals missing assumptions you need to fill in.
3. **The promotion conversation template**: Tell your manager: "I would really like at some point in the future to grow into [goal]. What do you suggest I work on now so I'll be ready when the opportunity comes up?" This is non-threatening (future-framed), brings your manager onto your side (collaborative), and focuses feedback on what actually matters for advancement.
4. **Strategy skill comes from experiential accumulation**: You cannot learn strategy from books alone. It builds through seeing experiments play out, iterating on the same product for years, and being in rooms where leaders debate strategic choices. Cross-applying strategic frameworks from other domains (e.g., consistency vs. comprehensiveness from Google Shopping applied to YouTube) accelerates learning.
5. **Repeated disagreements signal missing strategy**: When team members have recurring, value-level disagreements about features, it's a sign of an underlying strategic misalignment. Address the problem at the strategy level by writing down principles and frameworks rather than fighting over individual decisions.

## Concepts & frameworks

- **Three pillars of strategy** — Vision (inspiring future), strategic framework (market + success criteria + big bets), and roadmap (backward-looking feasibility sanity check).
- **Connecting the dots** — The core job of strategy is building the chain of reasoning from a high-level business goal down to specific feature work, filling in every missing assumption.
- **PEARL interview framework** — Problem, Epiphany, Action, Result, Learning — Jackie's alternative to STAR for PM interviews that emphasizes insight and growth alongside impact.
- **Eigenquestions** — Shishir Mehrotra's framework for identifying the one question whose answer determines all downstream decisions, referenced for strategic clarity.
- **Two-way door decisions in career** — Treating the move into management as reversible rather than permanent, allowing PMs to try it and return to IC if it doesn't fit.
