# Source: How to unlock your product leadership skills | Ken Norton, Ex-Google

**Guest**: Ken Norton (14 years at Google — Docs, Calendar, Maps, GV; now full-time executive coach to senior product leaders)
**Published**: 2022-07-24
**Duration**: ~1h 18m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6P0Es02vIF4

## Executive summary

Norton argues that advancing as a product leader is less about new frameworks and more about a mindset shift from "reactive" (fear, wanting to be liked/right/in control) to "creative" (purpose, curiosity, vision). The hardest parts of senior PM leadership are people-stuff, and the work is inner work.

## Key ideas

1. **Creative vs. reactive leadership** — ~75% of leaders operate reactively (fear-driven, protecting, defending); creative leadership (openness, purpose, curiosity) is measurably more effective per Anderson/Adams research. Also framed as above/below the line, daring/armored, playing-to-win vs. playing-not-to-lose.
2. **Three reactive postures** — Complying (wanting to be liked, move toward), Protecting (needing to be right, move away), Controlling (my way or the highway, move against). Goal isn't to kill the underlying trait (e.g., empathy) but redirect it creatively — Ken's unlock: "admiration over time" instead of "liking in the moment."
3. **Advice is cotton candy** — [[coaching|Coaching]] (partnership, questions, client sets the agenda) beats giving advice because advice rarely confronts the real problem. "What worked for us at Google may not have worked anywhere else — it may not even have worked at Google."
4. **The people side is the job** — Senior PM/CPO work is all people: persuasion, difficult conversations, vision. Blind spot: treating communication/storytelling/difficult-conversation skills as "soft" optional rather than investing like technical skills. "If something seems hard and squishy, chances are it's really going to matter."
5. **Self-complexity and the driving-lesson analogy** — Leadership growth requires rebuilding your "internal operating system," not just learning new tactics. Like teaching a 16-year-old to drive — rules haven't changed, but your relationship to the world has. Robert Kegan's adult-development work underlies this.

## B6 angle (hiring, management, leadership Part 2)

Norton is the most explicitly inner-work-focused voice — closer to [[source-jerry-colonna|Colonna's]] therapeutic lens than [[source-ben-horowitz|Horowitz's]] war-room frame, but specifically built for product leaders. His "creative vs. reactive" taxonomy and three reactive postures give B5's leadership canon a concrete diagnostic that Colonna gestures at but doesn't systematize. Ken's point that PMs practice leadership-without-authority "from day one" reframes [[source-rachel-lockett|Lockett]]/[[source-kayvon-beykpour|Beykpour]]-style management advice: the craft of influence is the senior-leadership craft, just earlier. Pairs especially well with [[source-molly-graham|Graham]] and Colonna on self-knowledge.
