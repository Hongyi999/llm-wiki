# Source: Becoming more strategic, navigating difficult colleagues, founder mode | Anneka Gupta

**Guest**: Anneka Gupta (CPO at Rubrik; formerly President/GM at LiveRamp; Stanford lecturer)
**Published**: 2024-10-17
**Duration**: ~1h 9m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E3dUveqt9Bw

## Executive summary

What makes a product leader "strategic," how to survive/thrive under a founder-mode CEO, tactical tools for decision-making, feedback, and navigating abrasive colleagues. Signature frame: strategic = (compelling, simple "why") + (change-agent courage on hard initiatives that take a long time). Rich tactical texture — summarization as strategy, being a "historian" of prior decisions, managing energy not time, reframing difficulty as fun.

## Key ideas

1. **Strategic = simple "why" + change-agent on big things** — Lacking either side makes you look unstrategic.
2. **Founder mode — both sides** — When working for a founder in founder mode, frame them as a lever; when YOU are the product leader in founder mode, go deep on details but intervene via Socratic questioning and planted hypotheses rather than dictates.
3. **Summarization is strategy** — Simply summarizing other people's points in meetings (verbally, whiteboard, Zoom chat) is perceived as strategic thinking and moves decisions forward.
4. **"Make the decision, not the right decision"** — Commit on ~70% information; you only learn post-commitment. Reward learning over outcomes by articulating the hypothesis underlying every bet.
5. **Be a historian** — Joining Rubrik, she systematically studied past failed products/decisions — gives context, reveals organizational baggage, improves future bets.
6. **Navigating difficult personalities** — Understand what drives them, reframe frustration into gratitude/curiosity, find what you can learn from them.

## Notable quote

> "When people say, 'I want someone that's strategic,' what they're really saying is, 'I want someone that can come up with and articulate a compelling and simple why behind the decisions and the direction of the company and product' and 'I want someone that's going to champion and be a change agent to do things that may be hard but actually best for the long-term interest.'"

## B9 angle

Modest direct GTM content, but one sharp aside: she repeatedly shipped products for new personas only to discover the org couldn't sell them — her rule now is **"know how you're going to sell something and who's going to do the selling before you actually go build out the product."** That's a sales-first product-strategy heuristic.
