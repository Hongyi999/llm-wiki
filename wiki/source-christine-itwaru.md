# Source: Understanding the role of product ops | Christine Itwaru (Pendo)

**Guest**: Christine Itwaru (longtime Pendo product ops leader, now Principal Strategist)
**Published**: 2023-02-16
**Duration**: ~1h 6m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tGS-NhxrN_Q

## Executive summary

Itwaru defines a role that barely existed five years ago. Product ops is two things: a *system* that lets a product team thrive, and a *person* who partners with PMs and advises the CPO on customer data and org alignment. Product ops emerged from the "summer of 2019" to handle the operational load PMs shouldn't own (voice-of-customer synthesis, tooling, cross-functional alignment, launch readiness), letting PMs stay focused on customers and engineers.

## Key ideas

1. **Product ops = a thing AND a person** — As a thing: any system that helps a product team thrive. As a person: a PM-partner / strategic advisor to the head of product.
2. **Summer 2019 was the "birth of product ops"** — A perfect storm: voice-of-customer sprawl, revenue-team misalignment, PLG adoption, pandemic-era growth. CPOs moved from "deliver, deliver, deliver" to "hit business metrics," which required org infrastructure PMs couldn't carry alone.
3. **What product ops actually does** — (a) Voice-of-customer synthesis across sales, success, research, NPS, support tickets; (b) tool stack for PMs (Pendo ↔ Salesforce ↔ Looker integrations); (c) content/education strategy woven into product release (definition of done includes in-app guides); (d) planning-process facilitation.
4. **The bad launch that birthed product ops at Pendo** — Christine's 5th week in: their biggest launch flopped on readiness — sales didn't know what to do with it. That gap between "they knew it was coming" and "they knew what to do with it" became the wedge.
5. **Ops is a sign of growth, not inefficiency** — Counter to [[source-casey-winters_|Casey Winters']] "ops as Band-Aid" take. The job of a good product ops person is to stand up the system, automate/hand off, move to more strategic work.
6. **Product ops vs. product marketing** — PMM helps revenue teams sell / positions externally. Product ops helps revenue teams *understand* the product. "We don't teach them to sell. We don't teach them to position."
7. **The sacred core of PM no one should offload** — Time with customers watching their pain. "Then I'm going to say it — don't be in product."

## B6 angle (hiring, management, leadership Part 2)

Itwaru contributes something B5's canonical voices don't touch: the organizational design question of how to scale a product function past ~100 PMs without overloading them. Where [[source-ben-horowitz|Horowitz]]/[[source-camille-fournier|Fournier]]/[[source-rachel-lockett|Lockett]] write about *management*, Itwaru writes about the *infrastructure between teams* — the glue role. She also provides a test for when a company has outgrown heroic PMs: when revenue teams are stealing PM customer-research time faster than PMs can replenish it. Complements [[source-marty-cagan|Cagan's]] empowered-team thesis with an answer to "and who keeps the machine aligned?"
