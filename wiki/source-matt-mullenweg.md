# Open source, WordPress, and the future of the web | Matt Mullenweg (Automattic)

**Guest**: Matt Mullenweg  
**Published**: 2025-08-14  
**Duration**: 1:32:09  
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ts9ZvlkeWGs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ts9ZvlkeWGs)

## Executive summary

Matt Mullenweg, co-creator of WordPress and CEO of Automattic, discusses his 20+ year mission to advance open source software, the WP Engine trademark dispute that has consumed the WordPress community, and his views on AI and open source. He explains how WordPress grew to power 40% of the web through a flywheel of open source community contribution, and why Meta's Llama is 'fake open source' due to its usage cap restrictions. Mullenweg defends his actions against WP Engine -- a Silver Lake-backed company he accuses of trademark abuse, feature degradation, and duplicitous negotiation -- while acknowledging that 4-5% of the community now views him negatively. He also shares perspectives on community building, the importance of AI being trained on open source code, and his philosophy of building movements rather than just products.

## Key ideas

1. **Open Source as the Most Important Idea of Our Generation**: Mullenweg argues that as more of life is controlled by software, fundamental freedoms (use, inspect, modify, redistribute) must attach to that software. The GPL's viral provision ensures these freedoms propagate, making it superior to permissive licenses for preserving liberty.

2. **Llama Is Not True Open Source**: Meta's Llama requires a license above 750 million MAU, violating the freedom to use for any purpose. Mullenweg suspects the 'open source' branding is driven by EU regulatory advantages rather than genuine confusion, since Meta is otherwise a major open source contributor (React, PHP improvements).

3. **Build a Movement, Not Just a Product**: WordPress succeeded by giving people something to believe in -- a philosophy, a worldview, 'code is poetry.' Community events, contributor pathways for non-developers, and naming releases after jazz musicians create emotional bonds that transcend utility.

4. **True Platforms Let Ecosystems Earn More Than Core**: WordPress's 60,000 plugins and themes constitute an irreplicable moat because the open source license prevents rug-pulling. Unlike Facebook or Shopify, successful third-party developers on WordPress cannot have their access revoked or be strong-armed into giving up equity.

5. **The WP Engine Dispute Is About Protecting Open Source**: Mullenweg frames the conflict as defending the WordPress trademark and user experience against a private equity-owned company (Silver Lake) that disabled features (revisions), confused users about official association, and negotiated in bad faith while preparing a lawsuit.

## Entities discussed

- **Matt Mullenweg** — Co-creator of WordPress (powering 40% of the web), CEO of Automattic ($7B+ valuation), and lifelong open source advocate.
- **Automattic** — 1,700-person fully distributed company across 90 countries, owning WordPress.com, WooCommerce, Tumblr, and more.
- **WordPress** — Open source CMS powering over 40% of all websites, 10x the market share of the next competitor.
- **WP Engine** — WordPress hosting company acquired by Silver Lake in 2019, at the center of a trademark and contribution dispute with Mullenweg.
- **Silver Lake** — Private equity firm that acquired WP Engine in 2019, accused by Mullenweg of hollowing out the company's community contributions.
- **Meta (Llama)** — Creator of the Llama AI model which Mullenweg argues is misleadingly branded as open source due to its MAU usage cap.

## Concepts covered

- **GPL and Four Freedoms** — The open source license granting freedom to use, inspect, modify, and redistribute software, with a viral clause requiring downstream recipients receive the same freedoms.
- **Open Source Washing** — The practice of labeling software as open source when it does not meet the OSI definition, potentially for regulatory or marketing advantage.
- **Platform Moat via Open Source** — An ecosystem of 60,000+ plugins and themes that constitutes an unreplicable competitive advantage because the open license prevents rug-pulling.
- **Build a Movement Not a Product** — Mullenweg's principle that lasting success comes from giving people a philosophy and community to believe in, not just utility.
- **Technical Debt** — Accumulated maintenance burden from past development decisions that Mullenweg cites as a hidden liability for many high-market-cap companies.
- **Bundling and Unbundling Cycles** — The recurring pattern in software where value shifts between integrated suites and point solutions, with timing determining winners.
