# Source: How to Build a Powerful Marketing Machine ft. Emily Kramer

**Guest**: Emily Kramer (Co-founder, MKT1; former Head of Marketing at Asana, Carta)
**Published**: 2022-09-11
**Duration**: ~1h 11m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gzs61z9Fekw

## Executive summary

Emily Kramer presents a comprehensive framework for how startups should think about building their marketing function. The central insight is to strip away confusing marketing sub-functions and instead think in terms of "fuel" (the content and messaging you create) and "engine" (how you distribute it and track results). She introduces the concept of the "Pi-shaped marketer" — someone with deep expertise in two of three core marketing areas — as the ideal first marketing hire, and provides concrete guidance on when to hire, what to look for, and how product and marketing teams should collaborate.

## Key ideas

1. **Fuel and engine framework** — Forget the alphabet soup of marketing sub-functions. "Fuel" is everything you create: copy, blog posts, templates, positioning, videos. "Engine" is how you get it out: distribution channels, email automation, segmentation, ops, paid ads. Some activities (like community) are both fuel and engine. Before hiring, diagnose which side is your bottleneck — many teams build an engine with no fuel, or produce great content with no distribution.

2. **Three marketing archetypes** — The core sub-functions are (1) content/community marketing (fuel-heavy), (2) growth/demand gen marketing (engine-heavy), and (3) product marketing (straddles both). Product marketers understand the product, the audience, and the market, then figure out how to communicate the right things at the right time. They are the most common right first hire because they bridge fuel and engine.

3. **The Pi-shaped marketer** — For the first marketing hire, look for someone who is expert in one of the three archetypes, proficient in a second, and has working knowledge of the third. The symbol Pi has two vertical lines, representing two spikes of depth rather than just one (T-shaped). Note: content + growth Pi-shapes are rare because they require opposite sides of the brain. The common combinations are product marketing + growth, or product marketing + content.

4. **Hiring mistakes to avoid** — Do not hire someone from only large/public companies (Google, Salesforce) as your first marketer — they have never built the foundation. Do not over-index on industry experience over business-model experience. The business model (top-down sales vs. PLG vs. freemium) dictates what marketing actually does far more than the industry vertical.

5. **Product-led growth is a misnomer** — Emily's hot take: PLG really means "not as much sales," which translates to product + marketing. In PLG companies, you may need a bigger marketing team earlier because sales is not doing the communication work. The marketing-to-product handoff (signup flow to in-product experience) is as critical as the traditional marketing-to-sales handoff.

6. **Marketing-product collaboration at Asana** — Key practices: (1) AORs (Areas of Responsibility) — a shared list of who owns what, distinct from job titles. (2) Roadmap Week — cross-functional open meetings before each quarter for shared planning. (3) The GACCS brief — Goals, Audience, Creative, Channels, Stakeholders — shared before any major marketing project to get early buy-in and prevent wasted work.

7. **Impact focus vs. "splatergy"** — The best marketing teams can articulate their core work (linear growth), their big bets (step-change growth), and their foundational gaps. Red flags: activity goals ("write 10 blog posts") instead of impact goals ("drive X signups at Y conversion rate"), no conversion tracking across the funnel, and inability to name their big bets.

## B10 angle

Emily's fuel-and-engine framework provides the marketing counterpart to [[product-led growth]] concepts discussed in other B10 sources. Her emphasis on business-model fit over industry experience when hiring echoes the general startup hiring philosophy. The GACCS brief and AOR practices from [[Asana]] offer concrete collaboration templates that any product-marketing team could adopt. Her "PLG is a misnomer" hot take directly challenges assumptions in sources like [[source-naomi-ionita]] that treat PLG as primarily a product discipline.
