# Creative vs. Reactive Leadership

A taxonomy from Bob Anderson and Bill Adams's *Mastering Leadership* (Leadership Circle), operationalized for product leaders by [[source-ken-norton|Ken Norton]]. Distinguishes two qualitatively different ways a leader can operate.

## Reactive leadership (fear-driven)

About **75% of leaders** default here. Reactive leadership is protective — organized around avoiding threats to status, approval, or control.

### Three reactive postures

| Posture | Want | Movement | Shadow |
|---------|------|----------|--------|
| **Complying** | To be liked | Move toward | Avoidance of hard feedback |
| **Protecting** | To be right | Move away | Cynicism, detachment |
| **Controlling** | My way | Move against | Micromanagement |

Most people primarily inhabit one. The goal isn't to kill the underlying trait (e.g., empathy) but to redirect it creatively. Ken's unlock: "admiration over time" instead of "liking in the moment."

## Creative leadership

Organized around **purpose, curiosity, vision**. Measurably more effective on every dimension per the Leadership Circle's research.

Overlapping frames:
- **Above / below the line** — Conscious Leadership Group.
- **Daring / armored** — Brené Brown.
- **Playing to win / playing not to lose** — [[source-roger-martin|Roger Martin]].

## Why it matters for product leaders

- **PMs are leaders from day one** without formal authority. The craft of influence *is* the senior-leadership craft, just earlier.
- **The people side is the job** at senior levels. Treating communication/storytelling/difficult-conversation skills as "soft" is a blind spot.
- **Self-complexity** — Leadership growth requires rebuilding your "internal operating system," not just learning tactics (Robert Kegan's adult-development work).
