# Source: The science of product, big bets, and how AI is impacting the future of music | Gustav Söderström

**Guest**: Gustav Söderström
**Published**: 2023-05-21
**Duration**: 1:24:30
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QtJoYFyrdPI

## Executive summary

Gustav Söderström, Co-President and CPO/CTO of Spotify, maps a sweeping framework for how the internet has evolved through three eras — curation, recommendation, and generation — and argues that generative AI will force product teams to rethink user experiences as fundamentally as the recommendation era did. He shares the inside story of Spotify's AI DJ, the first Spotify product that could not have existed without generative AI, and distills the critical principle of 'fault-tolerant user interfaces' where product design must match the actual performance of underlying ML models. The episode also provides a candid post-mortem of Spotify's homepage redesign failure, illustrating how shifting from 90% recall to 90% discovery alienated existing users, and the discipline required to separate 'change aversion' from genuine product regression. Gustav rounds out with insights on organizational design, contrasting Amazon's decentralized model with Apple's centralized one, and explains why Spotify moved autonomy from individual squads to the VP level.

## Key ideas

1. **Curation → Recommendation → Generation**: Gustav frames internet product evolution in three eras. Curation (users organize content), recommendation (algorithms organize content), and generation (AI creates content). Each era requires fundamentally rethinking products, not just incremental improvement, and Spotify is at the threshold of the generative era.
2. **Fault-tolerant user interfaces**: Product designers must understand the hit rate of their ML models and design UIs accordingly. A single play button demands 100% accuracy; a grid of four options works with 25% accuracy. The AI DJ and Midjourney's four-image grid both exemplify designing around actual model performance rather than aspirational performance.
3. **The recall vs. discovery tension**: Spotify's homepage redesign failed because it shifted from 90% recall / 10% discovery to the inverse. Users primarily visit the homepage to resume known sessions, not explore new content. Discovery features worked well when voluntary (in subfeeds) but failed when they displaced the recall function on the homepage.
4. **Autonomy at the VP level**: Spotify abandoned its famous squad model because extreme autonomy at the leaf level produced 'heat' — 100 squads running 100 strategies. Conversely, centralizing at the top creates bottlenecks. The VP level offers the optimal balance: enough people thinking independently (tens to hundreds) with enough seniority and pattern recognition.
5. **Strong opinions loosely held, operationalized**: Gustav advocates believing 100% in a hypothesis until data says no, then believing 100% in the next hypothesis. He acknowledges this is psychologically hard because people distrust those who change their minds, but insists it is the only scientific approach to product development.

## Concepts & frameworks

- **Curation → Recommendation → Generation** — A three-era framework for internet product evolution where each transition requires fundamental rethinking of user experience, business models, and product design.
- **Fault-Tolerant User Interfaces** — The principle that product UI must be designed around the actual performance of underlying ML models, with escape hatches for when predictions are wrong.
- **Magic Trick Products** — Gustav's observation that truly great products must pull some 'magic trick' that feels impossible to users, which correlates strongly with virality and adoption.
- **Recall vs. Discovery** — The tension between users wanting to resume known sessions (recall) versus explore new content (discovery), requiring different UI paradigms for each mode.
- **10% Planning Time** — The principle that planning should consume roughly 10% of execution time — one week per quarter or two weeks per six-month increment.
- [[product-velocity-ai-era|Think It, Build It, Ship It, Tweak It]] — Spotify's four-phase product development framework where the 'think it' phase should be cheap (risk reduction) before the expensive 'build it' phase begins.
