# Source: Career frameworks, A/B testing, and onboarding tips ft. Laura Schaffer

**Guest**: Laura Schaffer (VP of Growth, Amplitude)
**Published**: 2023-03-09
**Duration**: ~1h 21m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UMAmj1bciww

## Executive summary

Laura Schaffer shares a career growth framework centered on proactively building customer insight expertise rather than waiting for managers to define your path. She also delivers a masterclass on experimentation philosophy -- arguing that teams should lower their statistical confidence thresholds, embrace the 80% experiment failure rate, and validate hypotheses faster through painted doors and mocks rather than expensive A/B tests.

## Key ideas

1. **Carve your own career path** -- Instead of relying on your manager to advocate for you, build your brand by becoming the person who deeply understands customers. Share voice-of-the-customer insights broadly; executives always lack direct customer access and will value anyone who fills that gap.

2. **Good friction vs. bad friction in onboarding** -- Adding signup questions at Twilio (coding language, use case, product interest) actually *improved* conversion by 5%. New users feel anxious and uncertain; questions that confirm "you're in the right place" are psychologically comforting and reduce the fear of the unknown.

3. **The "pilling the hot dog" technique** -- Embed scary or unfamiliar steps inside comfortable, familiar contexts. At Twilio, moving the intimidating "get a phone number" step out of the console and into a docs page surrounded by code samples dramatically improved onboarding conversion.

4. **80% of hypotheses are wrong** -- Research from Microsoft and Netflix consistently shows that 80-90% of product hypotheses fail. The implication: iterative, cheap validation beats big redesigns every time. "If it's not embarrassing, you've gone too far."

5. **Lower your confidence intervals** -- The 95% statistical significance threshold belongs in pharmaceuticals, not SaaS conversion optimization. Accepting lower confidence (supplemented with qualitative corroboration) can double the number of experiments a team ships per year, producing better net outcomes.

6. **Failure as compass, not wall** -- Growth teams should be measured on longer time horizons (annual, not weekly). Short-term performance pressure leads to vanity metrics and data massaging. Given enough time, a well-functioning growth team will find real wins.

7. **Always set hypotheses before running experiments** -- Decide your success criteria and confidence threshold *before* collecting data. Post-hoc rationalization ("making the data fit the hypothesis") is the most common and dangerous failure mode for growth teams.
