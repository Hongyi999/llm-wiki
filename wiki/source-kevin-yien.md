# Unorthodox PM tips: Automating user insights, unselling candidates, decision logs, more | Kevin Yien

**Guest**: Kevin Yien  
**Published**: 2024-08-18  
**Duration**: 1:28:41  
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xOTO98MXG9o](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xOTO98MXG9o)

## Executive summary

Kevin Yien, who leads product for merchant experiences at Stripe (previously at Square and Mutiny), shares a battery of unorthodox but highly practical PM tactics. He reframes product management as converting a team's potential energy into realized customer value, argues PMs should start their careers in engineering, design, or sales before transitioning, and insists great PMs must be great writers because writing is 'clarity at scale.' The conversation dives deep into specific tools: decision logs for building product sense through deliberate practice, the 'unsell email' for filtering hiring candidates by frontloading hard truths, silent doc reads for faster feedback cycles, and automated customer research pipelines using Gong and Zapier. Kevin's story of being laid off while his wife was nine months pregnant and rebuilding his identity at Square offers a deeply personal coda on resilience and environment-fit.

## Key ideas

1. **PM as potential-to-kinetic energy converter**: Product management's purpose is to convert a team's potential into maximum realized customer value with minimum loss. Not every team needs a PM person, but every team needs PM activities—and when builders are also the customers (like at Stripe or Figma), a dedicated PM may be unnecessary.

2. **Decision logs as deliberate practice for product sense**: Product sense is the ability to make good decisions with insufficient data. PMs should keep a decision log—documenting rationale, predictions about other companies' moves, and reviewing outcomes months later—to accumulate reps the way pianists practice scales.

3. **The unsell email for hiring**: At offer stage, send candidates up to 8 bullet points of the hardest truths about the role and company to surface misalignment early. Initially lost 30% of candidates at offer stage but dramatically improved retention and hire quality.

4. **Draw the perimeter, obsess over the deliverable**: PMs should define constraints (customer segment, jobs to be done, platform, speed vs. consistency tradeoffs) to let engineers and designers be maximally creative within bounds—but also care deeply about the final 'last mile' details like animation timing.

5. **Automate customer research pipelines**: Use tools like Gong alerts, Zapier workflows, and Calendly to automatically identify relevant sales call mentions and schedule user research interviews, removing the 'I don't have time' excuse from talking to customers.

## Concepts covered

- **Decision Log** — A personal practice of documenting decisions, rationale, and predictions—then reviewing outcomes later—to deliberately build product sense.
- **Unsell Email** — A hiring tactic where the hiring manager sends candidates an honest email of the hardest truths about the role at offer stage to filter for genuine fit.
- **Tuning Fork Feedback** — The principle that the best way to get feedback is to put something concrete in front of people and have them react, rather than asking open-ended questions.
- **Potential vs. Kinetic Energy (PM)** — A metaphor for PM work as converting a team's latent potential into realized customer value with minimum loss.
- **Silent Doc Read** — A meeting format where attendees silently read and comment on a document in real time, enabling rapid feedback cycles.
