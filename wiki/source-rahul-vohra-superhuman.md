# Source: Rahul Vohra — Superhuman's Secret to Success

Lenny's Podcast interview with [[rahul-vohra|Rahul Vohra]], founder and CEO of [[superhuman|Superhuman]]. Published 2025-03-23. Rahul walks through how Superhuman turned the [[sean-ellis-test|Sean Ellis Test]] into a repeatable engine for reaching PMF, and the craft decisions (positioning, pricing, onboarding, design) that followed.

## The PMF crisis

Superhuman had shipped to 100+ beta users but was stuck. Rahul asked the [[sean-ellis-test|Sean Ellis Test]] question and got **22%** — below the 40% threshold. Instead of shipping more features blindly, he built a system to move the number deliberately. This system is the [[superhuman-pmf-engine|Superhuman PMF Engine]].

## The PMF Engine (four steps)

1. **Survey** with the Sean Ellis question, but use **three** response options (very disappointed / somewhat disappointed / not disappointed). This gives a "somewhat disappointed" bucket that is the key lever.
2. **Segment to find high-expectation customers**: look only at "very disappointed" users and ask them who benefits most, what the main benefit is, and how to improve. This reveals a sharp persona and promise.
3. **Analyze why "somewhat disappointed" users don't love it yet.** Tag reasons. The top tags are your roadmap.
4. **Double down on what they love; fix what's holding others back.** Split the roadmap 50/50 between [[solution-deepening-vs-market-widening|solution deepening and market widening]] — but bias toward deepening for the core persona first.

Running this loop, Superhuman went from 22% → 58% "very disappointed" in about a year.

## Solution deepening vs. market widening

- **Deepening** = making the product more loved by people who already love it. Safe. Compounds.
- **Widening** = reaching people who don't love it yet. Risky — can dilute the core experience.
- Rahul's rule: deepen first, widen only when the core segment is locked in.

## Other craft decisions

- **Positioning around speed**: "the fastest email experience ever made." Everything else (design, pricing, onboarding) cascades from that single promise. Rahul borrowed from April Dunford's *Obviously Awesome*.
- **Pricing**: Van Westendorp Price Sensitivity Meter landed at **$30/month**. Rahul argues positioning precedes pricing — you can't price a product you haven't positioned.
- **Manual concierge onboarding**: every new user got a 1:1 session with one of ~20 people. Expensive, un-scalable, and precisely why it worked — they learned what to automate.
- **Adelle Sans** as the custom typeface. Design as product, not decoration.
- **Game design, not gamification**: Superhuman borrowed mechanics (goals, feedback, progression) from real games rather than slapping on badges.
- **Switch Log**: Rahul's personal time-tracking discipline — log every context switch — that he credits for his leverage.
- **Hiring a president** to go from 8 direct reports down to 2. Unblocked him to focus on product.
- **Single Decisive Reason (SDR)** from Reid Hoffman: for any hard decision, identify the one reason that would make it right or wrong. If you can't, don't decide yet.
- **TM meditation** twice daily. Rahul is explicit that it underwrites his decision quality.

## Virality ≤ word of mouth

Rahul quotes Elliot Shmukler: **virality is a subset of word of mouth**. If people don't organically recommend your product, engineered virality won't save you. Superhuman's growth was almost entirely WOM driven from the start.

## Enterprise and AI expansion

- Expanded from Gmail-only to **Outlook** to unlock enterprise.
- AI features: write with AI, auto summarize, instant reply, ask AI, workflows. Rahul frames AI as a new layer of speed, staying true to the positioning.
