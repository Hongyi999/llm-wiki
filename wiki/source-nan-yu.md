# Source: Linear's secret to building beloved B2B products | Nan Yu

**Guest**: Nan Yu (Head of Product at [[linear|Linear]])
**Published**: 2025-01-30
**Duration**: ~1h 21m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nTr21kgCFF4

## Executive summary

Why Linear is the one B2B tool people desperately want IT to approve. Core tenets: no trade-off between speed and quality (experts go fast), always ship a workable V1 inside the first 10% of the budget, prioritize ICs over middle-manager reporting requests, and hunt for emotional valence — "my goal is to feel bad in the same way the customer feels bad" — behind every feature request rather than taking it literally.

## Key ideas

1. **Speed and quality are complementary, not tradeoffs** — Sloppy ≠ fast. Experts at the pinnacle of their craft (chefs, Magnus Carlsen, Stripe engineers) ship faster AND better. Target a workable V1 by week one of a month-long project.
2. **Prioritize ICs over middle managers — no debate** — Custom fields and reporting requests from managers corrupt the IC workflow. Linear won't take a single step down that path, even under buyer pressure; they convince buyers the premise is wrong.
3. **Tie features back to specific named humans** — Not persona Alice/Bob; "Bob at Company X with this email." Customer Requests came from spotting that 40% of custom-field asks were really "I need to tag this by customer."
4. **Hunt the emotion, not the feature request** — Keep digging until you understand the exact moment the user felt bad (a marketing blow-up over a Dec 30 ship date led to flexible target dates at any granularity).
5. **Systematized creativity via extremes** — Build the most-extreme version along each axis (fastest-possible draft save vs. safest-possible auto-save), ship both, triangulate.

## B9 angle

Linear's B2B success is a product-led counterpoint to [[source-jen-abel-20|Jen Abel's]] founder-led enterprise playbook: crossing-the-chasm into the enterprise happens because ICs desperately pull the tool upward, not because AEs push it down. Complements [[source-april-dunford|Dunford]] — Linear's positioning is "for ICs who want their company to act like a startup" — and offers a concrete refusal model (no reporting custom fields, ever) founders can borrow when saying no to enterprise RFP checklists.
