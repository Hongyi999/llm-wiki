# 37signals

Bootstrapped software company behind Basecamp, HEY, and ONCE. Founded 1999 by Jason Fried; CTO David Heinemeier Hansson (DHH) joined soon after. Canonical "stay-up" (vs. startup) — 24+ profitable years, ~75 employees, 100k+ paying customers, double-digit millions in profit per year, no investors, no board.

## Operating model

- **Appetites, not estimates** — Every feature capped at 6 weeks, 2 people (1 designer + 1 engineer). If work hasn't crested the "hill" by the deadline, it usually dies.
- **2-week cool-downs** — Every 6-week cycle is followed by 2 weeks with no fixed project. "Sprints" are rejected because you can't sprint continuously.
- **No OKRs, KPIs, revenue, or growth targets** — Decisions made by gut: "What do you think? How does this feel?"
- **No long-term plans** — "Promises are the downfall of every business." Just the next 6 weeks.
- **Small is a destination, not a stepping stone** — "If I had 1,000 people, I wouldn't know what to do with them."

## Canonical principles

- **Independence** — Root value; precondition for all others.
- **Bootstrapping as thesis** — Silicon Valley made the most profitable kind of business (software) the least profitable by removing the have-to-make-money constraint.
- **Work isn't war** — Reject "target customers," "make a killing," "Navy SEALs." Frame work through creation, pride, and alternatives.

## Products

- **Basecamp** — Project management; flagship.
- **HEY** — Email.
- **ONCE** — One-time-purchase desktop software.
