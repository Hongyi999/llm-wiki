# Source: How to build a team that can "take a punch" | Hilary Gridley (Head of Core Product, Whoop)

## Executive summary

Hilary Gridley, Head of Core Product at WHOOP, presents a deeply personal and practical framework for product leadership grounded in cognitive behavioral therapy, behavioral activation, and habit formation. Her central thesis is that modern product leaders must teach their teams to 'take a punch'—to deal with setbacks, criticism, and fear of failure—by focusing on counter-programming negative narratives through action rather than litigating past events. She introduces tools like 'magic questions' (statements ending in 'do you agree?') for building mental models of stakeholders, emphasizes understanding how leaders think rather than just what they think, and advocates for using AI not just as a productivity tool but as a radical accelerator for learning and skill development. The episode also covers her controversial view that CPOs should understand their role as operationalizing the CEO's vision rather than imposing their own, and her habit-formation approach to driving AI adoption on teams.

## Key ideas

1. **Counter-program the narrative, don't litigate the past**: When you feel punched—criticized, misunderstood, or embarrassed at work—don't try to correct the record. Instead, take one small action that demonstrates the opposite of what you fear someone thinks about you. This breaks negative spirals and builds agency.
2. **Behavioral activation over waiting to feel better**: Borrowed from CBT, the principle is 'act and then feel better' rather than 'feel better and then act.' Gridley keeps a personal list of behavioral activations and teaches her team to identify specific small actions that reliably lift their mood and break downward spirals.
3. **Build mental models of how people think, not just what they think**: The most effective way to operate autonomously is to understand the reasoning frameworks, values, and priorities of key stakeholders. Gridley uses 'magic questions'—declarative statements ending with 'is that right?' or 'do you agree?'—to tease out mental models without asking open-ended questions.
4. **Habit formation over education for AI adoption**: Driving AI tool adoption should follow behavioral psychology, not classroom education: consistency (daily use), reduced friction (start with fun non-work tasks), and powerful immediate emotional reward loops. Her '30 days of GPT' program builds habits through daily micro-tasks.
5. **AI as a learning accelerator, not a replacement**: Custom GPTs can compress years of professional development into weeks by providing on-demand feedback loops. Her 'Aristotle' GPT creates LSAT-style product management scenarios, and another simulates engineering scope estimation—enabling infinite reps at skills that normally develop slowly through on-the-job experience.

## Concepts & frameworks

- **Taking a punch** — A management framework for handling professional setbacks by counter-programming negative narratives through small, demonstrative actions rather than defensive explanations.
- **Behavioral activation** — A CBT technique where you take action to improve your mood rather than waiting to feel better first—applied to both personal wellbeing and professional leadership.
- **Magic questions** — A technique for understanding how someone thinks by presenting declarative statements and asking 'do you agree?' rather than asking open-ended questions.
- **Reward loop design** — Designing habits around powerful, immediate, and emotional rewards—exemplified by WHOOP's red/green recovery scores that change behavior more effectively than information alone.
- **Counter-programming** — Taking a specific action to demonstrate the opposite of what you fear someone thinks of you, rather than trying to verbally correct their impression.
- [[taste-as-differentiator|Negative capability]] — John Keats' concept of remaining in uncertainties, mysteries, and doubts without irritable reaching after fact and reason—applied to the ambiguity inherent in product leadership.
