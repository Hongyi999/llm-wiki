# Conscious leadership: Unlocking vision, strategy and purpose | JM Nickels (Uber, Waymo, DoorDash)

**Guest**: John Mark Nickels  
**Published**: 2024-10-06  
**Duration**: 1:18:06  
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W7o40qcYHlM](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W7o40qcYHlM)

## Executive summary

JM Nickels, a Senior Director of Product Management at Uber who previously led product at Waymo and DoorDash, shares his philosophy of 'conscious leadership'—becoming more self-aware and taking responsibility for one's influence. He argues that accepting emotions rather than suppressing them leads to better decision-making and leadership, drawing from Buddhist mindfulness practices and stoic philosophy. The conversation covers how to develop better strategy and vision through deep domain immersion, first-principles thinking, and quiet contemplation rather than back-to-back meetings. JM also contrasts the cultural DNA of Uber (rider-centric, like Amazon) versus DoorDash (merchant-centric, like Shopify), and reflects on lessons from commercializing autonomous vehicles at Waymo. His core message is that dropping ego-driven focus on optics and instead connecting to a larger purpose paradoxically leads to better career outcomes.

## Key ideas

1. **Conscious leadership through self-awareness**: Leadership is about having influence, and consciousness means becoming aware of your internal world—biases, inherited belief systems, and emotional patterns. Accepting emotions like fear and sadness rather than resisting them breaks negative cognitive-emotive loops and leads to clearer thinking and better outcomes.

2. **Vision through deep immersion and first-principles thinking**: Great strategy comes from spending years deeply immersed in a domain, then closing your eyes and imagining the future 5-10 years out. Question why things are the way they are (e.g., why does a 4,000-pound vehicle move one person three miles?) to find doorways to innovation. Schedule dedicated contemplation time away from back-to-back meetings.

3. **Purpose over optics**: Paradoxically, dropping focus on presentations, career optics, and approval-seeking in favor of connecting to a larger purpose (like transforming transportation) leads to better work and faster career advancement. The finger pointing at the moon (the OKR, the deck) is not the moon itself (the product impact).

4. **Balance vision and execution dynamically**: Going too far into theory land (Uber's early pricing PhD sessions) or too far into execution (DoorDash's 'ready, fire, aim') are both failure modes. The balance should shift dynamically—lean into strategy when direction is unclear, pedal to the metal on execution when strategy is baked.

5. **Get clear on your life's objective function**: Most people operate on an implied objective function inherited from parents, culture, or work. By being mindful that life will end and asking what future-you will care about, you can reprioritize toward relationships and meaningful work rather than optimizing Sunday night presentations.

## Concepts covered

- **Conscious Leadership** — A leadership philosophy centered on self-awareness, taking responsibility for one's influence, and allowing emotions rather than suppressing them.
- **Cognitive-Emotive Loop** — A negative feedback cycle where stressful thoughts trigger anxious feelings, which generate more stressful thoughts.
- **First-Principles Thinking** — Questioning fundamental assumptions about why things are the way they are to uncover innovation opportunities.
- **Objective Function** — The implicit or explicit optimization target guiding your life and career decisions, which most people inherit rather than consciously choose.
- **Induced Demand** — The economic phenomenon where increasing supply (e.g., cheaper rides, more highway lanes) generates proportionally more demand, negating the intended benefit.
