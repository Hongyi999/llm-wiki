# Source: Sean Ellis — The Original Growth Hacker Reveals His Secrets

Lenny's Podcast interview with [[sean-ellis|Sean Ellis]], coiner of the term "growth hacking" and creator of the [[sean-ellis-test|Sean Ellis Test]] for measuring [[product-market-fit|product-market fit]]. Published 2024-09-05.

## Core thesis

You can measure PMF quantitatively by asking users "How would you feel if you could no longer use this product?" If **≥40% answer "very disappointed,"** you have product-market fit strong enough to fuel sustainable growth. Below that, growth efforts will leak out the bottom of the funnel faster than you fill the top.

## Origin of the Sean Ellis Test

- Born at [[xobni|Xobni]] out of frustration with ambiguous PMF debates. Sean wanted a single number to settle the question.
- The 40% threshold was empirical — it emerged from observing which products in his portfolio could scale profitably and which couldn't.
- PMFsurvey.com was launched to give founders a free, standardized instrument.

## Case studies from the interview

- **[[lookout|Lookout]]**: Started at 7% "very disappointed." After re-positioning around lost-phone recovery (away from antivirus), the number climbed past 40% and the company scaled.
- **[[logmein|LogMeIn]]**: The product survey was healthy but activation was broken — only ~5% of signups became active users. By fixing onboarding, activation jumped to ~50% and PMF signal translated into growth.
- **[[dropbox|Dropbox]]**: Sean helped design the double-sided referral program that became the canonical growth loop — give space, get space.
- **Nubank**: Hit ~50% "very disappointed" — an example of PMF so strong that growth was almost mechanical.
- **[[superhuman|Superhuman]]** extended the test into the [[superhuman-pmf-engine|Superhuman PMF Engine]] (see [[rahul-vohra|Rahul Vohra]]'s version).

## Primary benefit drill-down

After the main question, Sean recommends asking:
1. What type of people would most benefit from this product?
2. What is the main benefit you receive?
3. How can we improve this product for you?

The "very disappointed" segment's answers to #2 reveal the **real value prop** — often different from what founders think they're selling. Focus product and marketing on that benefit, for that persona.

## Other takeaways

- **Growth hacking** was coined because Sean couldn't find marketing hires who cared about growth the way engineers cared about code. He wanted the discipline to feel like engineering.
- **ICE** prioritization (Impact, Confidence, Ease) for ranking growth experiments.
- **North Star Metric**: pick one number the whole company optimizes. Must capture value delivered, not just activity.
- **Activation unlocks matter more than top-of-funnel**: he repeatedly found companies with hidden activation gates that, once removed, 10x'd growth.
- TikTok anecdote: their PMF was so strong early that Sean basically said there was nothing to fix — just pour fuel on the fire.
