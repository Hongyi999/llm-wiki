# Source: Building Wiz: the fastest-growing startup in history | Raaz Herzberg

**Guest**: Raaz Herzberg (Wiz's first PM; now CMO and VP Product Strategy)
**Published**: 2024-11-17
**Duration**: ~1h 5m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vnW3zIkrOvY

## Executive summary

How Wiz went from a failing network-security pitch to $100M ARR in 18 months (fastest software company in history) by learning to distinguish polite "interesting" from real pull. Why most CMOs fail (trust + product + market depth are all hard to import). Tactical moves — Wizard-of-Oz booths, bright/pink/optimistic brand, the "dummy explanation" — to make noise in a category where buyers had never heard of Wiz.

## Key ideas

1. **The pivot signal was "pull" not "interesting"** — Customers asking "how much?" / "when can we POV?" / "let me connect you" signals real PMF; polite enthusiasm is deceptive. Make prospects fill out hard questionnaires to test commitment.
2. **Founder-led sales to ~$2M ARR, even when rushed** — If the founding team can't do a function end-to-end once, no hire will save you. Wiz closed several million before hiring any salesperson.
3. **Follow the heat** — Heat moves from product → engineering → sales → marketing as the company scales; leaders should follow where the blocker is.
4. **The dummy explanation** — Every message must be understandable without insider context (don't say "CNAPP" when customers Google "cloud security"); remember you live in a bubble.
5. **Embrace failure as default** — Raaz took the CMO role assuming she'd fail at it. "Imposter syndrome" is real, embrace it and act anyway.
