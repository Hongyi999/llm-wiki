## Executive summary

Archaeology of a modern AI-oriented startup: every engineer ships a marketable feature every week; they maintain a "secret roadmap" separate from the public/user-requested one; strategic technical debt is treated like financial leverage. Also a rare inside look at Snap's design-led, no-PM product org under Evan Spiegel.

## Key ideas

1. **"Every engineer ships a marketable feature every week"** — A marketable feature is one a user would sign up or pay for on its own. Scope-cut ruthlessly until the 1-week MVP is "useless if we cut any more" — then ship.
2. **Public roadmap vs. Secret roadmap** — Public roadmap = things users (and competitors) ask for; can't be game-changer. Secret roadmap = things nobody asked for but that change behavior once experienced. Captions' "eye contact" feature (shift gaze to camera) came from the secret roadmap.
3. **Strategic technical debt as leverage** — "A startup's job is to take on technical debt because that's how you operate faster than a bigger company." Every piece costs ~1-2% team time as interest. Too much debt before value-generation = out of "technical debt runway." Q4 is dedicated infrastructure quarter.
4. **Snap's secret sauce: designer-PMs under a product-CEO** — For thousands of employees Snap had only ~10-12 IC designers (no reports) who functioned as PMs; everything UI-impacting went through Evan. One person with granular control over the entire user-facing product.
5. **Internal virality creates alignment** — At scale, prototype via small design-engineering team, share internally, let it go viral among engineers → managers → VPs → Evan. By the time it reaches leadership, the org is aligned.
6. **AI video marketing is the final adoption frontier** — AI-generated video ads hit performance inflection ~1 year ago; localization by AI-translating winners performs nearly as well as original-language creative.
7. **PMs should own marketing** — "Search marketing is just placing a button to your product in Google." The funnel starts when the user clicks, not when they land on onboarding.
