# Comparison: B10 — Design, craft & founder-led 0→1 approaches

21 unique voices spanning design philosophy, product craft, founder evaluation, growth, and personal resilience. Unlike B9's tight sales/GTM focus, B10 is thematically diverse — but several deep threads connect them.

## School 1: Design as organizational philosophy

| Voice | Core thesis | Key mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| [[source-bob-baxley\|Baxley]] | Software is a medium; great product is a moral obligation | Design tenets (decision tools, not platitudes) |
| [[source-stewart-butterfield\|Butterfield]] | Comprehension > friction; taste as competitive advantage | Utility curves with quality thresholds |
| [[source-katie-dill\|Dill]] | Quality IS growth | Walk the Store + Performance = Potential - Interference |
| [[source-david-singleton\|Singleton]] | Meticulous craft compounds | Friction logging + engineer-cations |

**Convergence**: All four reject the speed-vs-quality tradeoff. Design is not a cost center but a growth engine.

## School 2: Founder-led detail obsession

| Voice | Core thesis | Key mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| [[source-dmitry-zlokazov\|Zlokazov]] | Local CEO model; 99% done ≈ 0% | Founder review of 100% of screens; deep-dive on 7–10 of 100 projects |
| [[source-stewart-butterfield\|Butterfield]] | Divine discontent | Called Slack "a giant piece of shit" in 2014 |
| [[source-katie-dill\|Dill]] | Reach for the stars, land on the moon | PQRs with CTO and VP Design quarterly |
| [[source-david-singleton\|Singleton]] | Co-create with early users | Shared Slack channels with Figma/Slack during Billing alpha |

**Convergence**: Founders/leaders who review details at scale create higher quality floors than those who delegate and audit.

## School 3: 0→1 product creation

| Voice | Core thesis | Key mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| [[source-mike-maples-jr\|Maples]] | Inflections + insights + founder-future fit | Pattern Breakers framework; force a choice, not a comparison |
| [[source-jessica-livingston\|Livingston]] | Social radar for founder evaluation | Defensiveness detection; earnestness > charisma; Airbnb cereal box story |
| [[source-mihika-kapoor\|Kapoor]] | Hype is fuel for internal 0→1 survival | Keeper of the flame; conviction calibration; hackathons as launchpads |
| [[source-eric-ries\|Ries]] | MVP = learning efficiency, not low quality | Pivot = change strategy, keep vision; zombie companies worse than failure |
| [[source-jeremy-henrickson\|Henrickson]] | Design for the most complex case first | Compound startup model; small teams (4 people) found new products |

**Key disagreement**: Ries's MVP philosophy vs. Henrickson's "design for the hardest case first" and Butterfield's "99% done ≈ 0%" — all three value learning but disagree on acceptable quality thresholds during iteration.

## Key disagreements across B10

1. **MVP quality bar**: Ries (MVP = minimum learning vehicle, can be ugly) vs. Butterfield/Zlokazov (99% done ≈ 0%, never compromise quality) vs. Henrickson (design for the most complex case first, skip MVP)
2. **Prototyping speed**: Kapoor (see to believe; demo before memo) vs. Baxley (delay the primal mark; first visual collapses possibilities)
3. **Scope vs. depth**: Zlokazov (keep 100 things running, selectively deep-dive) vs. Chesky/Singleton (cut scope to maintain craft)
4. **Metrics role**: Dill (Walk the Store scores drive accountability) vs. Baxley (no reliable design metric exists; stay out of that game) vs. Antin (NPS is broken; CSAT is better)
5. **Research velocity**: Antin (research can deliver in 48 hours) vs. Baxley (stay in block-frames for weeks before high-res; rush kills quality)
6. **Founder glorification**: Conrad (fewer founders, more great operators) vs. Maples (founder-future fit is the key to breakthrough) vs. Livingston (bet on founders, not ideas)
