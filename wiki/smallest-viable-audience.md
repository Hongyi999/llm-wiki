# Smallest Viable Audience

[[source-seth-godin|Seth Godin's]] principle: pick the smallest audience you can economically serve, then delight them completely. Counterintuitively, this drives more growth than broad-targeting because:

1. **Perfect fit produces word-of-mouth** — [[source-nilan-peiris|Wise-style]] 10x product experiences only happen when the product is designed for someone specific.
2. **You can actually serve them** — Big audiences are promises you can't keep.
3. **Niche → category → larger audience** — [[source-geoffrey-moore|Moore's]] bowling alley logic: land a beachhead, radiate to adjacent segments.

## The stack

- **Godin**: Smallest viable audience = the 400 users whose taste you're actually matching.
- **Moore**: Beachhead = geography + industry + profession + compelling use case, small enough you can be 30-50% in 2 years.
- **Jackson**: Named model persona ("tech-savvy dad in suburban St. Louis") — not TAM.
- **Wilkinson**: Fish where the fish are. Passionate niches auto-generate WOM.
- **Tavel** (B7): Find your thimble and boil the white-hot center.

## Anti-pattern: "We're for everyone"

- **[[source-ivan-zhao|Notion's]] lost years** partly stemmed from "developer tools for everyone."
- **Jaguar's re-logo** as a case of broad-positioning failure (per Godin).

## Practical filter

- Can you name 10 real people who match?
- Would they pay you more than the market price?
- Can you reach them without paid ads?

If any answer is no, your audience is too broad.
