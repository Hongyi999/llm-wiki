# Dylan Field live at Figma's Config: Intuition, simplicity, and the future of design

**Guest**: Dylan Field 2.0  
**Published**: 2024-06-30  
**Duration**: 0:48:08  
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJQYBjto41s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJQYBjto41s)

## Executive summary

Dylan Field, co-founder and CEO of Figma, shares how he kept the company focused and accelerating after the failed Adobe acquisition by communicating transparently and offering a voluntary severance program called Detach. He discusses Figma's expansion from one product to many — including FigJam, Slides, Sites, Make, Draw, and Buzz — guided by a strategy of following the user workflow rather than chasing TAM. Dylan emphasizes that design is now the key differentiator in software, making craft and taste essential competitive advantages. He also covers Figma Make's AI-powered prototyping capabilities, the importance of time to value and removing user blockers, and his belief that role boundaries between designers, PMs, and engineers will increasingly blur as AI tools mature.

## Key ideas

1. **Follow the workflow, not the TAM**: Figma expanded from one product to many by tracing the user workflow from ideation to production, rather than sorting opportunities by total addressable market. Dylan argues that market size is often unknowable in advance — Figma's own market of 250K designers was wildly underestimated.

2. **Design as the ultimate differentiator**: As developer tools and AI make code generation easier, the value in software moves up the stack. 'Good enough is not enough — it's mediocre.' Companies that invest in design craft and taste will win; those that don't will regret it.

3. **Time to value and blocker removal**: Dylan obsesses over shortening the time for new users to experience the product's core magic. Figma created a dedicated team called 'Blockers' that systematically eliminated adoption barriers, producing measurable improvements in retention and activation with each fix.

4. **Keeping startup pace at scale through clarity and curiosity**: After 13 years, Figma maintains startup energy by keeping orgs flat, questioning timeline padding from first principles, matching people to problems they are passionate about, and investing in rituals like the Make-a-Reek hackathon.

5. **Navigating the post-Adobe pivot**: After the $20B Adobe deal fell through, Dylan used frequent transparent communication and a voluntary 'Detach' severance program to reset the team. Over 4% of the company took the off-ramp, and the remaining employees accelerated to a successful IPO.

## Concepts covered

- **Time to Value** — The speed at which a new user experiences the core 'magic' of a product, a metric Dylan considers central to product success.
- **Follow the Workflow** — Figma's product expansion strategy of tracing the user journey from idea to production to identify natural new product surfaces.
- **Blocker Removal** — Systematic elimination of adoption-blocking issues, which Figma found to be as important for retention as shipping new features.
- **[[taste-as-differentiator|Design as Differentiator]]** — The thesis that as code becomes easier to produce, design craft and taste become the primary competitive advantage in software.
- **[[taste-as-differentiator|Product Taste]]** — A cultivated point of view on quality, developed through broad exposure and reflection — few can create the framework, many can match one.
