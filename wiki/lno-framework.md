# LNO framework (Leverage / Neutral / Overhead)

The **LNO framework** — popularized by [[source-shreyas-doshi|Shreyas Doshi]] — classifies every task a PM does into one of three buckets based on the shape of its return curve:

## The three buckets

### L — Leverage tasks
- Tasks where effort and quality have **10x–100x returns**.
- Examples: strategy memos, hiring decisions, major product bets, executive-facing narratives, key customer relationships.
- **Rule**: Invest disproportionate time. A+ effort matters.

### N — Neutral tasks
- Tasks where returns are **approximately linear** with effort.
- Examples: spec reviews, well-scoped feature launches, standard customer meetings.
- **Rule**: Match effort to expected return. Don't over-invest, don't under-invest.

### O — Overhead tasks
- Tasks where **"done" is the goal** and extra quality has zero marginal return.
- Examples: status updates, expense reports, ticket triage, most admin.
- **Rule**: Do them fast enough to be acceptable. Don't polish.

## The core insight

Most PMs invert the effort allocation. They put A+ effort on O tasks (polishing status docs no one reads) and B- effort on L tasks (strategy memos that will shape quarters of work). The flip — A+ on L, adequate on N, fast on O — is often the single highest-leverage mental upgrade available to a mid-career PM.

## Using LNO as a weekly ritual

- **Friday review**: List last week's hours. Classify each chunk L/N/O. Note the imbalance.
- **Monday plan**: Budget the upcoming week by L/N/O target. A typical senior PM target might be 50% L, 30% N, 20% O.
- **Meeting hygiene**: Before accepting a meeting, classify the task. O-class meetings can be declined, delegated, or replaced with async.

## Why the framework is hard to apply

- **Leverage is less visible than output**. L tasks often look like "thinking time" and produce no shipped artifact this week. Peer pressure defaults to visible-output tasks (which are often O).
- **Calibration requires exposure**. A junior PM may not yet know which strategy memos are L and which are O-dressed-as-L. Mentorship and pattern-matching help.
- **Org culture resists L time**. Orgs that reward "busyness" make L-time socially costly.
