# Crafting a compelling product vision | Ebi Atawodi (YouTube, Netflix, Uber)

**Guest**: Ebi Atawodi  
**Published**: 2023-12-03  
**Duration**: 1:39:47  
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tBPTwUmaxDs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tBPTwUmaxDs)

## Executive summary

Ebi Atawodi, Director of Product Management for the Creator Experience at YouTube (formerly Netflix and Uber), delivers the most tactical advice on product vision that Lenny says he has ever heard on the podcast. She presents a three-phase framework — Empathize, Create, Evangelize — for developing and communicating vision. For empathizing, she advocates maintaining a living 'Top 10 Things You Should Know' document of user problems updated quarterly. For creating, she offers three concrete formats: a 'Once Upon a Time' Mad Libs narrative, a future TechCrunch headline article, and visual mockups (even hand-sketched app store screenshots). For evangelizing, she describes three concentric circles — core team, stakeholders, then leadership — and stresses that a good vision should last at least three years without needing reinvention. She also defines PM craft as 'clarity and conviction' and shares rich insights on how company culture (Uber vs Netflix vs Google) shapes product outcomes.

## Key ideas

1. **Empathize-Create-Evangelize Framework for Vision**: Developing a product vision follows three phases: empathize deeply with user problems (maintain a living 'Top 10 Problems' doc), create the vision using storytelling formats, and evangelize it through three concentric circles — core team, stakeholders, then leadership.

2. **Three Formats for Communicating Vision**: A 'Once Upon a Time' Mad Libs narrative (problem → one day → as a result), a future press headline written as a TechCrunch article, and visual mockups (even hand-drawn app store screenshots). No designer required to start — just sketch it.

3. **Top 10 Things You Should Know — Living Problem Document**: Every PM should maintain a regularly updated list of the 10 most important problems facing their product area. This document aligns triads (PM, eng, design), informs strategy sessions, and ensures the PM always has clarity on why they exist.

4. **Product Management is Clarity and Conviction**: Clarity means sifting out noise to reveal the core problems and narrative (insights → strategy → big rocks). Conviction is the feeling of what you think the world should look like — manifested through the vision. Together they define PM craft.

5. **Company Culture Shapes Product Outcomes**: Uber's monolithic, autonomous culture enabled innovations like cash payments (despite CEO opposition). Netflix's 'freedom and responsibility' culture allowed evolution from SVOD-only to ads. Google's microcultures allow wildly different product approaches across divisions.

## Concepts covered

- **Product Vision (Lofty, Realistic, Limitation-Free, Problem-Grounded)** — A good vision must be lofty enough to inspire, realistic enough to be attainable, free of today's technical constraints, and grounded in a clear user problem.
- **Clarity and Conviction** — Atawodi's definition of PM craft: clarity is removing noise to reveal core problems and narrative; conviction is the feeling of what the world should look like when you solve them.
- **Once Upon a Time Framework** — A simple Mad Libs storytelling template for product vision: once upon a time (problem), one day (solution), and as a result (impact on the world).
- **Insights-Strategy-Big Rocks** — A three-part narrative structure for product planning: understand the problems (insights), decide which to focus on (strategy), and identify 3-5 deliverables (big rocks).
- **Microcultures** — At large companies like Google, individual teams develop distinct sub-cultures within a loose macro-culture, enabling different product approaches across divisions.
