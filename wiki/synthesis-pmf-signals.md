# Synthesis: Cross-source PMF Signals and Anti-signals

A distillation of the signals and anti-signals that recur across all five ingested sources. These are the things to watch for (and watch out for) when trying to answer the question **"do we have [[product-market-fit|PMF]] yet?"**

## Signals that PMF is real

### From user behavior
- **Spontaneous "wow statements"** with specifics. ("I'd cry if this went away." "This is the only tool I open in the morning.") — [[todd-jackson|Todd]], [[sean-ellis|Sean]].
- **≥40% "very disappointed"** on the [[sean-ellis-test|Sean Ellis Test]] from engaged users. — Sean, [[rahul-vohra|Rahul]].
- **Users pulling colleagues into the next call** unprompted. — [[jen-abel|Jen]], Todd.
- **Visualization behavior**: prospects start designing the product in their heads ("so could it do X?") during sales calls. — Jen.
- **Growing, widening problem**: the pain is getting worse in the world. The prospect has already tried to solve it with headcount or another tool. — Jen.
- **Organic word of mouth > engineered virality.** — Rahul (via Elliot Shmukler), Sean.
- **Marginal customers look like ideal customers** — not just your first 10. — Todd.

### From business metrics
- **Retention curves flatten.** — Todd (three dimensions).
- **Unit economics turn positive** on new cohorts. — Todd.
- **Inbound ratio rising** without increased marketing spend. — implicit across sources.
- **[[marketplace-liquidity|Fill rate / ETA thresholds]] hit the "magical" zone** for marketplaces. — [[benjamin-lauzier|Benjamin]].

### From real commitments
- **Dollars committed, not words spoken** ([[dollar-driven-discovery]]). — Todd.
- **Services-first contracts** while the technology matures — still real money, still real commitment. — Jen.
- **Prospects willing to co-author the SOW**. — Jen.

## Anti-signals (friend-zone indicators)

- **"Cool, keep me posted."** The canonical friend-zone phrase (Rick Song via Todd).
- **"What keeps you up at night?"** produces a different answer every day. — Jen.
- **"I'll email you"** after the first call. Nine times out of ten: a polite no. — Jen.
- **Every successful sales cycle requires the founder personally in the room.** — implicit: the motion isn't repeatable.
- **Healthy survey score, broken activation** ([[logmein|LogMeIn]] case). — Sean.
- **Strong demand, broken efficiency**: subsidized growth masking weak unit economics. — Todd.
- **One "very disappointed" superfan who is also an advisor/investor.** The marginal-customer lens catches this. — Todd.
- **Different prospects each say the product is great, but no two describe it the same way.** Positioning is fuzzy; persona is undefined.
- **Mid-market as a default**: straddling SMB and enterprise without deliberately choosing one. — Jen.

## The meta-signal: how hard you're pushing

A latent theme across all five sources: **if the founder is the only thing keeping the motion alive, PMF has not arrived.**

- Todd: marginal customers must be as good as first customers.
- Jen: the sales cycle should feel like momentum, not like dragging prospects.
- Rahul: word of mouth must do the heavy lifting, not ad spend.
- Benjamin: the hard side of the marketplace should start attracting participants organically.
- Sean: growth hacking before PMF is just a leaky bucket.

Phrased inversely: **if you stopped pushing for a week, what would happen?** Pre-PMF products collapse. PMF products coast.

## The five most generalizable heuristics

1. **Trust dollars, not words.** ([[dollar-driven-discovery]])
2. **Obsess over the users who already love you; ignore the middle.** ([[solution-deepening-vs-market-widening]], [[sean-ellis-test]])
3. **Measure across three dimensions — satisfaction, demand, efficiency — not one.** ([[four-levels-of-pmf]])
4. **Write down all four Ps (persona, problem, promise, product) in explicit language.** ([[four-ps-framework]])
5. **Founder-led sales is research.** ([[founder-led-sales]])

## Disagreements worth noting

- **Market-first vs. technology-first.** Jen observes that market-first companies ([[vanta|Vanta]]) reach PMF faster but may be upside-capped; technology-first companies (Figma, Airtable) are slower but uncapped. Todd would likely agree but remind everyone that 60–70% of the technology-first bets get stuck at L1–L2.
- **When to hire a salesperson.** Jen says not until Series A. Todd's case studies are mixed. The real answer seems to be: hire only after the founder's sales motion is repeatable by a stranger.
- **How much to widen early.** Rahul says 50/50 deepen vs. widen, but bias toward deepening. Todd would probably argue that before L3 Strong PMF, almost no widening is justified.

## Questions this synthesis raises

- Is there a reliable leading indicator — earlier than the [[sean-ellis-test|Sean Ellis Test]] — that predicts which pre-PMF startups will eventually reach L3? (Todd's Four Ps clarity is a candidate but hasn't been quantitatively validated.)
- How should **AI-native products** adapt these frameworks when user expectations shift every quarter?
- In what cases does the 40% threshold fail? ([[marketplace-liquidity|Marketplaces]] are one known exception; enterprise sales with tiny user counts may be another.)
