# The rituals of great teams | Shishir Mehrotra

**Guest**: Shishir Mehrotra
**Published**: 2022-08-14
**Duration**: 1:31:49
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7uSuMIJhONA](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7uSuMIJhONA)

## Executive summary

Shishir Mehrotra — co-founder/CEO of Coda, formerly head of product and engineering at YouTube — argues that great teams are distinguished not by their strategy decks but by their rituals. Rituals are the repeated, codified practices that shape behavior every week; most orgs don't design them and end up with cultures they didn't intend. The episode catalogues the rituals that made YouTube and Coda work: DRIs, math-spotters, the grid company, and the three-factor hiring signal. It's the rare operating-manual-level episode in B4.

## Key ideas

1. **Rituals > strategy for culture**: Strategy gets presented once a year; rituals happen every week. The rituals you repeat become the culture you have. Design them explicitly.
2. **DRI (Directly Responsible Individual)**: Every meaningful decision gets exactly one DRI. Accountability without a name is accountability by no one. DRIs make decisions fast; committees make them slow.
3. **Math-spotters**: Hire people whose job is to double-check the math in every important memo. Strategic decisions die on quiet arithmetic errors that no one is incentivized to catch.
4. **The grid company**: An org grows healthy when hires are distributed across a grid of function × seniority, not clustered. Clusters create tribal knowledge pockets; grids create cross-pollination.
5. **Three hiring signals**: Smart, gets things done, easy to work with. Weight all three; most orgs optimize for 1–2 and under-weight the third, usually "easy to work with." That under-weighting compounds into toxic seniors.
