# The rituals of great teams | Shishir Mehrotra, Coda, YouTube, Microsoft

**Guest**: Shishir Mehrotra  
**Published**: 2022-08-14  
**Duration**: 1:31:49  
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7uSuMIJhONA](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7uSuMIJhONA)

## Executive summary

Shishir Mehrotra, CEO of Coda and former VP of product and engineering at YouTube, delivers a wide-ranging conversation covering growth loops, team rituals, eigenquestions, and talent evaluation. He introduces Black Loops (viral document sharing) and Blue Loops (public publishing) as Coda's twin growth engines, shares insights from interviewing over 1,000 people for his book 'Rituals of Great Teams,' and explains eigenquestions -- the questions whose answers eliminate the most subsequent questions -- using the pivotal YouTube 'Modern Family' decision as the defining example. He also presents the PSHE framework (Problem, Solution, How, Execution) for evaluating talent at any level and makes a strong case for reference checks as the most reliable hiring signal.

## Key ideas

1. **Black Loops and Blue Loops as growth engines**: Coda's growth model has two distinct loops: the Black Loop (share-create viral cycle, like Microsoft Office) driven by collaboration, and the Blue Loop (publish-to-world cycle, like YouTube) driven by creators seeking exposure. Maker Billing -- charging only document creators, not editors -- removes friction from the share edge that powers the Black Loop.

2. **Eigenquestions -- the question that answers all other questions**: Derived from the mathematical concept of eigenvectors, an eigenquestion is the question whose answer eliminates the most subsequent questions. At YouTube, the eigenquestion 'Does online video favor consistency or comprehensiveness?' resolved the Modern Family link-out debate and cascaded into decisions about the iPhone app, embedded players, and content strategy.

3. **Rituals as mirrors of culture**: Drawing from Bing Gordon's three rules of golden rituals (named, known by first Friday, templated), Shishir argues that rituals are not just meetings but the visible expressions of company culture. Coda's Dory/Pulse ritual eliminates groupthink by having everyone write opinions before revealing them, and was later improved by borrowing Coinbase's RAPID role-assignment technique.

4. **PSHE framework for evaluating talent**: Product talent should be evaluated not by scope but by where they operate on the Problem-Solution-How-Execution spectrum. Junior PMs execute playbooks, mid-level PMs devise solutions, and senior PMs identify the right problems. This framework explains the 'trough of disillusion' where career advancement stops correlating with scope.

5. **Reference checks as the top hiring signal**: Shishir prioritizes reference checks above all interview signals, asking contrast questions to assess where candidates sit on the PSHE axis without revealing his own value hierarchy. The key technique is asking references 'Who on the team regularly identifies what problems to focus on?' rather than directly evaluating the candidate.

## Concepts covered

- **Black Loop / Blue Loop** — Coda's dual growth engine: Black Loop is viral sharing within teams (the Microsoft loop), Blue Loop is public publishing to attract new users (the YouTube loop).
- **Eigenquestions** — The question whose answer eliminates the most subsequent questions, enabling teams to resolve complex strategic decisions through one well-chosen framing.
- **Rituals of Great Teams** — The thesis that company culture is best understood and transmitted through named, templated rituals that every employee learns by their first Friday.
- **PSHE Framework** — A talent evaluation model ranking contributions along Problem-Solution-How-Execution, where advancement is measured by moving up the spectrum rather than growing scope.
- **Maker Billing** — Coda's pricing model that charges only document creators (not editors or viewers), deliberately removing dollar signs from the share action to maximize viral growth.
- **Consistency vs. Comprehensiveness** — YouTube's eigenquestion: whether online video would evolve to favor a consistent experience on one platform or comprehensive access across many, which drove strategic decisions for years.
