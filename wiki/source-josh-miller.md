# Source: Competing with giants: An inside look at how The Browser Company builds product | Josh Miller (CEO)

**Guest**: Josh Miller (CEO, The Browser Company, makers of Arc)
**Published**: 2023-03-19
**Duration**: ~1h 28m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AZpo5785v8A

## Executive summary

Miller articulates a self-aware reaction to Silicon Valley's metric-obsession: optimize for *feelings* (surprise, airiness, focus) rather than graphs, treat the company itself as the product, hire "mutts" with heartfelt intensity, and build in public as radical trust-building rather than marketing. The company organizes into non-traditional orgs (membership, storytelling), has no PMs, tracks a single metric (D5/D7), and writes its values as a road-trip essay rather than a corporate list.

## Key ideas

1. **Optimize for feelings, not metrics** — Feelings (surprise, joy, airiness, focus, secure) are the creative input; metrics are a fidelity check. Counter-example: Facebook obsessing over "OBPS" (original broadcast posts) while Snapchat thought about making people feel close to friends.
2. **D5/D7 as the single obsessed-over metric** — How many people use Arc 5+ days a week. Combines retention, engagement, growth into one uncheatable number. Track cohort-over-cohort, not absolute numbers.
3. **The company is the product** — Hursh and Josh started the company so they could hire whoever they wanted. Hiring announcements are product launches. Celebrate ICs publicly to counter CEO-hero-worship and create the "tumbleweed" effect (Darren from Chrome, Tara from Vimeo, Peter Vidani from Slack all joined as ICs).
4. **Values as a road-trip essay ("Notes on Road Trips")** — Rejected corporate-value tropes. Values: heartfelt intensity; assume you don't know; start by asking what could be; swarm for the crew; make them feel something.
5. **No PMs, non-traditional orgs (membership, storytelling)** — "PM" is a role on a project, not an org. Membership team owns the full human relationship (support + success + research). Storytelling team (3 people) owns PR + marketing + investor relations as unified "telling our story."
6. **Build in public = radical trust building, not transparency** — Board meetings on YouTube, reaction videos. Bet: browsers handle the most sensitive data; trust in tech is broken; the antidote is letting people feel they know the humans behind the software.
7. **"Assume you don't know"** — Everything is a prototype, including values, org structure, whether to have PMs. Explicitly predicts they'll eventually hire PMs and come back to admit it.

## B6 angle (hiring, management, leadership Part 2)

Miller is the most explicit *anti-operator* voice in B6 — a philosophical counterweight to [[source-varun-parmar|Parmar's]] AMPED machinery, [[source-itamar-gilad|Gilad's]] evidence-guided rigor, and [[source-melissa-tan|Melissa's]] results-culture. His "celebrate the IC, not the CEO" hiring philosophy and "the company *is* the product" framing echo [[source-jerry-colonna|Colonna's]] humanism and [[source-molly-graham|Graham's]] founder-as-curator stance but go further: he treats the org chart itself as a prototype. Uniquely useful as the test case of whether founder-led, feelings-first, transparency-maximal product building actually scales.
