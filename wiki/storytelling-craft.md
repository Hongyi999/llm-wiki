# Storytelling Craft

A landscape page for the storytelling techniques across B8. Distinct from **positioning** (what category you're in) and **narrative** (your strategic story); this is the tactical craft of making any single story land.

## Three canonical voices

### [[source-matthew-dicks|Matthew Dicks]] — Storyworthy

- **Every story is one five-second moment** of transformation or realization.
- **Start close to the end** (Vonnegut).
- **Stakes devices**: elephant, backpack, breadcrumb, hourglass, crystal ball.
- **Dinner test**: a public story should resemble, slightly elevated, a story you'd tell at dinner.
- **It must be your story**: vulnerability is the connection mechanism.

### [[source-nancy-duarte|Nancy Duarte]] — Presentations as sparklines

- **"What is / what could be / new bliss"** rise-and-fall structure.
- **Audience is the hero; presenter is the mentor** (Obi-Wan, not Luke).
- **Star moment** — One memorable anchor per talk.
- **Can they see what you're saying?** — Sketches, diagrams, napkin visuals.

### [[source-tristan-de-montebello|Tristan de Montebello]] — Ultraspeaking

- **Bow and Arrow** — One sentence you want remembered; bow = supporting story/data.
- **Accordion Method** — Deliver talk at 3m → 30s → back to 3m instead of memorizing.
- **Think-up, End-strong, Stay-in-character** — Three micro-tactics.
- **Games-as-practice pedagogy** — Short rep + feedback.

## The shared insight

Stories aren't about facts — they're about **emotional transformation via the listener**. All three voices converge on three requirements:

1. **One thing to remember** (Dicks's five-second moment; Tristan's arrow; Duarte's star moment).
2. **Structured contrast** (Dicks's stakes devices; Duarte's what-is/what-could-be; Tristan's accordion).
3. **Audience-centered** (Dicks's "your story" = your vulnerability for them; Duarte's audience-as-hero; Tristan's stay-in-character so insecurity doesn't leak).
