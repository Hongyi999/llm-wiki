# Source: An inside look at how the New York Times builds product | Alex Hardiman (CPO, the New York Times)

**Guest**: Alex Hardiman (CPO, The New York Times; previously CPO of The Atlantic; head of News at Facebook post-2016)
**Published**: 2022-11-13
**Duration**: ~1h 07m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y3-cwoHMwQs

## Executive summary

Hardiman describes how a 170-year-old journalism institution is being rebuilt as a connected subscription bundle — news, games, cooking, sports (The Athletic), Wirecutter, audio — targeting 15M subscribers by 2027. Doubles as a tutorial on "wartime product management," built-for-mission cross-functional teams, and integrating beloved acquisitions like Wordle without breaking them.

## Key ideas

1. **Mission shapes impact metric** — Impact is growing subscribers *and* triggering policy change with deeply reported stories. "Our impact and our business goals are in service of our mission, not the other way around." That alignment is what Facebook couldn't give her.
2. **Two-axis org: functions × missions** — Functions (product, design) own craft, career paths, community; missions are cross-functional teams (consumer, monetization, platform) led by a GM/product/eng leader. Uniquely: *editors are embedded* in consumer missions — the one structural difference from a pure tech co.
3. **Wartime product management** — Post-2016 Facebook news integrity, COVID-era NYT (she worked two weeks out of a friend's laundry closet in Vermont), Ukraine live coverage. PMs thrive here because ambiguity, competing inputs, no playbook reward PM core skills. Seek the "Eye of Sauron" rather than avoid it.
4. **Two modes for storytelling product teams** — Mode 1: shipping at the speed of news with editors. Mode 2: building content + tooling systems so magical one-offs become reusable formats.
5. **Editorial independence by org design** — Product teams help stories find their widest audience but have zero say in story selection; firewall sits in the reporting line (newsroom vs. business).
6. **Acquire + don't-break integration** — Wordle deal closed in weeks. Integration priority: preserve the core game loop while adding an NYT-account backend so stats/streaks persist across devices. The "fetus" Wordle-solution-day-after-Roe-leak incident is a live-fire case study in transparent comms during mid-migration.
7. **The bundle thesis** — 135M addressable-market analysis; news is "the sun" that gives birth to planet-products (games, cooking, audio, Wirecutter, The Athletic). Goal: essential daily subscription.

## Notable quote

> "What is true? Where do we have conviction? Where do we have questions? What are the most important problems to solve? How do you get a team rallied around a shared context and one single goal? These are actually the conditions where product managers thrive."

## B6 angle (hiring, management, leadership Part 2)

Hardiman is the rare non-FAANG product-leadership voice in the corpus — a CPO running cross-functional missions inside a mission-driven institution. Uniquely contributes (a) how to structure a product org when journalists/editors must be first-class teammates, (b) an editorial-business firewall pattern with analogues for any org with a craft-driven discipline that must *not* be governed by product metrics, (c) a lived example of wartime product leadership (election-integrity, COVID, Ukraine) that complements [[source-ben-horowitz|Horowitz's]] war-story framing with specific structural and acquisition tactics.
