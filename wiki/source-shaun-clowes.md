# Source: Why great AI products are all about the data | Shaun Clowes (CPO at Confluent)

**Guest**: Shaun Clowes
**Published**: 2024-12-29
**Duration**: 1:21:35
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yVS1gTAQYSU

## Executive summary

Shaun Clowes, CPO at Confluent and former first head of growth at Atlassian, argues that 90% of the effort in building great AI products goes into data management -- getting the right data, at the right time, in the right quality to the LLM -- not into model selection or prompt engineering. He makes a compelling case that enterprise SaaS incumbents like Salesforce and Workday are not threatened by AI-generated clones because their real value lies in years of accumulated business rules and configuration, not in their UI. Clowes also delivers a sharp critique of product management as an underdeveloped discipline, arguing most PMs spend too much time inside the building and too little time synthesizing external signals, and shows how LLMs can supercharge PMs who learn to use them for adversarial analysis of their own strategies.

## Key ideas

1. **Data is 90% of AI product quality**: Models are commoditizing and prompts are improvable, but the real differentiator in AI products is the context -- all the customer data, competitive intelligence, business rules, and real-time signals fed to the LLM. Information has a decay rate, making timely, high-quality data the decisive input.
2. **Business rules are the SaaS moat, not UI**: Enterprise SaaS apps are 'forms on databases' whose real value is years of accumulated business rules and configuration that make Salesforce into 'Lenny Inc's Salesforce.' AI cannot easily replicate this because even the companies themselves cannot articulate their rules without reading their own configuration code.
3. **LLMs as adversarial strategy tools for PMs**: The most powerful way for PMs to use LLMs is not to confirm their hypotheses but to disprove them -- feeding customer interviews and competitor positioning into ChatGPT and asking where your strategy does NOT fit, where competitors better match customer needs, and what you are missing.
4. **The 10x PM opportunity**: Product management remains an underdeveloped discipline where outcomes are random. The path to becoming a 10x PM is spending 80% of your time thinking about what's happening outside the building -- customers, markets, competitors -- and bringing back synthesis, not raw data.
5. **Data as compass, not GPS**: After years of calling himself data-driven, Clowes now argues data should disprove rather than prove -- check one click upstream, downstream, and above any data point before trusting it. Intuition built from massive data exposure is often more reliable than any single analysis.

## Concepts & frameworks

- **Data as the AI Moat** — The thesis that in AI products, the model and prompts are commoditizing while the quality, timeliness, and relevance of context data is the true differentiator.
- **Information Decay Rate** — The observation that all data loses decision-making value rapidly over time, making real-time data pipelines critical for AI product quality.
- **Business Rules as Lock-in** — Enterprise SaaS value comes not from UI or data models but from years of configured business processes that become a black box even to the company itself.
- **Feedback River** — Sachin Rekhi's concept of surrounding yourself with a constant flow of customer feedback, competitive intelligence, and usage data -- amplified by LLMs that can process it at scale.
- **Product-Led Growth (B2B)** — The approach Clowes pioneered at Atlassian of applying consumer growth techniques to B2B products, making the software sell itself.
- **Data as Compass Not GPS** — The reformed view that data should guide direction and disprove hypotheses rather than provide definitive answers, with intuition as the primary decision-making tool.

## Memorable quote

> Shaun Clowes (00:00:45): 'Well, you've got this synthesis machine, which is this LLM thing that's going to help you do synthesis, but if it hasn't got all that data to do synthesis on top of, it's got nothing.
