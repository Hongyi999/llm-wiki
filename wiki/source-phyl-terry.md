# Source: Landing your dream job with councils, candidate market fit, and negotiation ft. Phyl Terry

**Guest**: Phyl Terry (Author of *Never Search Alone*; Founder, Creative Good)
**Published**: 2024-09-12
**Duration**: ~1h 52m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OH3nzRdwYPA

## Executive summary

Phyl Terry argues that the job search is fundamentally broken because people do it alone, amplifying anxiety and leading to poor outcomes. He presents a product-thinking approach: form a Job Search Council (a free peer support group of 6-8 seekers), define your "candidate market fit" with ruthless narrowness, and then "play to win" by writing your own job mission with OKRs before negotiating — a tactic that consistently differentiates candidates and leads to better offers.

## Key ideas

1. **Job Search Councils** — Free peer groups of 6-8 job seekers who meet regularly (twice weekly for "fast seekers," biweekly for "slow seekers"). Putting anxious people together in a structured, vulnerable environment flips fear into hope, accountability, and confidence. Over 2,000 councils launched, all volunteer-run and free.

2. **Candidate market fit** — The job-search analog of product-market fit. In a down market, your "fit" may be lower than your last title. The key is radical narrowness: "Director of product at a healthcare series B startup in San Francisco" — a spear, not a net. People are expansive from a narrow statement but never reductive from a broad one. Typically 3-4 attributes (stage, industry, level/function, culture).

3. **The Mnookin two-pager and listening tour** — Before searching, write what you want and do not want (the Mnookin two-pager). Then conduct a listening tour: reverse exit interviews with former colleagues, broader network conversations using the "golden question" ("If you were in my shoes, how would you approach this?"), and recruiter conversations. This builds invested listening posts across your network.

4. **Job mission with OKRs** — During interviews, privately draft your own version of the job description with concrete objectives and key results. Share it with the hiring manager mid-process. This demonstrates initiative and accountability, and has been the difference between "silver medal" and "gold medal" candidates repeatedly. An Amazon exec who hired 2,000+ people said no one had ever done this — he would hire them on the spot.

5. **The four legs of negotiation** — After establishing OKRs, negotiate what you need to succeed (tech debt budget, training, headcount) before discussing salary. Companies love this because it shows investment in outcomes. Then ask for more comp — 87% of the time, asking yields more money. Do it live with the hiring manager, not over email.

6. **Asking for help well** — Not a sign of weakness but of confidence. 85% of senior leaders credit asking for help in reaching their role, while 85% of junior people fear it shows weakness. The key: do your homework, ask for perspective (not for someone to do it for you), and use the phrasing "What would you do if you were in my shoes?" Asking well is a giving activity — recipients feel honored, not burdened.

7. **Emotional balance sheet** — The most important thing to manage during a job search is not your resume or LinkedIn but your emotional state. Fear and imposter syndrome erode interview performance. Tools: the gratitude house exercise (list everyone who helped you get where you are), naming your inner critic, and debriefing every interview immediately with your council.
