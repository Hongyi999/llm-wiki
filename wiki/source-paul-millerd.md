# Redefining success, money, and belonging | Paul Millerd (The Pathless Path)

**Guest**: Paul Millerd  
**Published**: 2023-11-19  
**Duration**: 1:04:53  
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yFckwBtiCUM](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yFckwBtiCUM)

## Executive summary

Paul Millerd, author of the self-published bestseller The Pathless Path, explores what it means to break free from the 'default path' — the unquestioned script of college, career, salary, house, and continuous work that most people follow. He argues that a three-month sabbatical has a near-100% approval rating among the 500+ people he has counseled, yet most professionals never take one because of deeply internalized scripts about what work should look like. Millerd's practical advice ranges from micro-experiments (take three hours during a workday to walk or revisit a childhood hobby) to reframing saved money as 'a gift from your former self.' The conversation resonates deeply with Lenny Rachitsky's own journey from Airbnb PM to independent creator, with both sharing that paying close attention to what gives you energy after doing it is the most powerful compass for finding work you love.

## Key ideas

1. **The Default Path vs. The Pathless Path**: The default path is the unexamined script about what you should do with your life (get a job, work continuously, climb the ladder). The pathless path is a conscious shift toward embracing uncertainty and designing work around what brings you alive, rather than following the script.

2. **The 3-Month Sabbatical as Catalyst**: A sabbatical has a near-100% approval rating among people Millerd has counseled. It takes 6-8 weeks just to unwind from work mode. Three months out of ~500 working months is a tiny investment that nearly always leads to transformative clarity about what you actually want.

3. **Follow the Energy, Not the Plan**: The most powerful compass for finding meaningful work is paying close attention to what gives you energy after you do it versus what drains you. Millerd and Lenny both found that advising/consulting drained them while writing and creating energized them — leading to their current paths.

4. **Reframe the Financial Investment**: Treat savings spent on exploration as a 'gift from your former self' or as a 'life MBA' — no different from the $200K people happily spend on business school. Name what you are spending on and create explicit agreements with yourself and your partner.

5. **Coming Alive Over Getting Ahead**: Millerd's core motto. Extrinsic outcomes (title, salary, prestige) provide diminishing returns; extended states of feeling alive and connected to your work are more sustainable and, often, eventually more lucrative — Millerd now earns more than he ever did in consulting.

## Entities discussed

- **Tim Ferriss** — Author and podcaster whose fear-setting exercise and 'do five episodes' podcast advice directly influenced Millerd's path.
- **David Autor** — MIT economist whose research on shrinking middle-class jobs is cited to contextualize why comfortable career paths are becoming less available.

## Concepts covered

- **The Default Path** — The unexamined life script of continuous employment, career advancement, and identity-through-job-title that most people follow without conscious choice.
- **The Pathless Path** — A shift from treating uncertainty as a problem to embracing it, operating from abundance rather than scarcity, and actively searching for work you want to keep doing.
- **Fear Setting** — Tim Ferriss' exercise of writing down fears, mitigation strategies, and — crucially — the cost of inaction, which helps people see the hidden costs of staying on the default path.
- **Ship, Quit, and Learn** — Lenny's framework for exploration: ship something quickly, design it so you can quit easily, and use the shipping to learn what to do next.
- **Coming Alive Over Getting Ahead** — Millerd's core motto prioritizing personal energy, connection, and intrinsic satisfaction over extrinsic markers of career success.
