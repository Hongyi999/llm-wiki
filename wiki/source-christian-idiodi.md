# The essence of product management | Christian Idiodi (SVPG)

**Guest**: Christian Idiodi  
**Published**: 2023-12-21  
**Duration**: 1:33:14  
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SXYc5RoU3Lg](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SXYc5RoU3Lg)

## Executive summary

Christian Idiodi, a partner at Silicon Valley Product Group and self-described practitioner who still builds new products annually, delivers a masterclass on what product management actually is and why so many organizations do it poorly. He defines the PM's essence as waking up on behalf of someone else to solve their problem well enough that they give you something back — a 'certificate of appreciation' in the form of revenue, loyalty, or referrals. His single favorite discovery technique is building reference customers: immersing yourself with real people who have the problem and iterating until 6-8 (B2B) or 15-25 (B2C) are willing to put their reputation on the line to endorse your solution. He also shares powerful frameworks for coaching, building trust through 'teach me / help me' relationships with influential leaders, and argues that companies chronically promote people to incompetence by failing to let them practice leadership before giving them the title.

## Key ideas

1. **Reference Customers as the Holy Grail of Discovery**: The most reliable path to product-market fit is finding and working directly with target users until 6-8 (B2B) or 15-25 (B2C) love your solution enough to publicly recommend it. If you cannot recruit enough people with the problem, the problem may not be worth solving.

2. **The Four Risks of Product Work (Value, Usability, Feasibility, Viability)**: Product teams must address whether people will buy it (value), can use it (usability), can be built (feasibility), and works for the business (viability). Value is by far the most important and most overlooked, especially when teams are handed roadmaps instead of problems.

3. **Trust Through Competence — The 'Teach Me' Technique**: The fastest way to build trust in an organization is to ask the most influential person to teach you, thereby extending their trust to yourself. This simultaneously builds relationships, accelerates learning, and makes the teacher accountable for your growth.

4. **Practice Leadership Before the Title**: Companies routinely promote great individual contributors into management roles they have never practiced, leading to micromanagement and dysfunction. Leaders should have people 'do VP things' before becoming a VP, creating safe spaces for practice without the high stakes of the actual role.

5. **Coaching as the Day Job of Managers**: The number one reason people give bad coaching is they have never experienced good coaching themselves. The manager's job is not to solve problems directly but to get a team of people good at solving problems — like a coach on the sideline, not a player on the field.

## Concepts covered

- **[[product-market-fit|Reference Customers]]** — Users who love your product enough to put their reputation on the line by recommending it — the strongest signal of product-market fit.
- **Certificate of Appreciation** — Idiodi's term for what customers give back (revenue, engagement, loyalty, referrals) when you solve their problem well enough.
- **Value Risk** — The most critical and overlooked product risk: whether people will actually buy, choose, or use what you build.
- **Collaborative Problem Solving** — The core skill of product management — working with a team of people (PM, designer, engineer) to discover and deliver solutions.
- **Promote to Practice, Not to Learn** — The principle that people should practice leadership responsibilities before being formally promoted into a leadership role.
