# Source: Relentless curiosity, radical accountability, and HubSpot's winning growth formula | Chris Miller

**Guest**: Christopher Miller
**Published**: 2023-08-10
**Duration**: 1:31:24

## Executive summary

Chris Miller, VP of Product for Growth and AI at HubSpot, shares the inside story of how he helped transform HubSpot from a primarily sales-led business into one of the most successful product-led growth companies in B2B SaaS. Miller describes how a scrappy early growth team seized neglected self-service infrastructure, redesigned it, and created a step-function change in HubSpot's business physics. He advocates for a modular, hybrid approach to PLG rather than dogmatic self-service, arguing that the right go-to-market depends on customer segment, product complexity, and purchase consideration. The conversation also covers his dual role leading both growth and AI at HubSpot, including experiments with ChatSpot and microapps as new distribution channels, plus deeply practical advice on PM career development, hiring traits, and the importance of relentless curiosity.

## Key ideas

1. **Radical accountability as growth engine**: HubSpot's early growth team succeeded by adopting an 'every problem is our problem' mentality, seizing neglected code bases and infrastructure that no other team was actively developing. By asking forgiveness rather than permission and delivering results, they earned expanding scope and resources.
2. **PLG is modular, not monolithic**: Product-led growth doesn't mean fully self-service. The best approach is hybrid -- map each stage of the customer journey to determine where humans add value (data migration, security concerns) and where product can lead. Having a human backstop doesn't make you less product-led.
3. **Distinguish business problems from customer problems**: When framing experiments and features, explicitly separate business problems from customer problems. If you only see the business problem (revenue decline), you risk hostile solutions; tracing it to the underlying customer problem leads to decisions that serve both long-term.
4. **Give value before extracting value**: HubSpot's growth flywheel evolved from content marketing (free eBooks and whitepapers) to free software that provides genuine, sustainable value. The free tier is designed so small customers can get real value, but engaged users naturally encounter upgrade triggers.
5. **Microapps as emerging distribution channel**: HubSpot is experimenting with standalone microapps (Website Grader, Brand Kit Generator, ChatSpot) as top-of-funnel acquisition tools that provide immediate value while creating natural conversion paths to the platform.

## Concepts & frameworks

- **Product-Led Growth (Modular)** — Miller's framework that PLG is not synonymous with self-service but is a spectrum where product leads the go-to-market with humans as backstops where needed.
- **Relentless Curiosity** — Miller's #1 hiring trait: an insatiable desire to understand things combined with no fear of admitting ignorance.
- **Radical Accountability** — The growth team's operating principle of treating every business problem as their own, regardless of formal charter or scope.
- **Microapps** — Standalone free tools (Website Grader, ChatSpot) used as top-of-funnel acquisition channels that demonstrate value before requiring product adoption.
- **Sponsors vs. Mentors** — The distinction between mentors who advise and sponsors/advocates who put professional capital on the line to bet on you.
- **HubSpot Culture Code** — Publicly published cultural values (humility, empathy, adaptability, remarkability, transparency) that serve as both internal alignment and external talent attraction.
- **Customer Obsession (Long-term)** — HubSpot's principle that customer-hostile decisions only seem rational when you're not thinking long-term enough.
