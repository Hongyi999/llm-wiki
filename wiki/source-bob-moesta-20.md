# The ultimate guide to JTBD | Bob Moesta (co-creator of the framework)

**Guest**: Bob Moesta

## Executive summary

In this second appearance on the podcast, JTBD co-creator Bob Moesta pivots from product innovation to career innovation with his new book Job Moves. He argues that employees hire companies more than companies hire employees, and that most people end up in worse jobs because they optimize for features (salary, title) rather than experiences (energy, growth, alignment). Moesta introduces four career quests -- get out, take the next step, regain control, and realign -- each with distinct heuristics for what to optimize in a job search. The episode delivers a deeply tactical framework for self-assessment through energy drivers and drains, job prototyping through informational interviews, and crafting a career story using the Pixar narrative template.

## Key ideas

1. **Job features vs. job experiences**: Just as products have features and experiences, jobs have features (salary, title, location) and experiences (energy, growth, respect). Features depreciate over time while experiences determine long-term satisfaction. Money is often a surrogate for deeper needs like respect or security.

2. **Four career quests drive job changes**: Every job change maps to one of four quests: get out (escape a toxic situation via a 'jobcation'), take the next step (find a big enough leap toward your long-term goal), regain control (recover work-life balance), or realign (get back to doing what you're great at and love).

3. **Energy drivers and drains as career design requirements**: By cataloging moments that give and drain energy across your career, you create design requirements for your next role. The goal is shifting from 95% energy-draining work to at least 50/50 -- at which point work stops feeling like work.

4. **Prototype wide, then narrow**: Treat yourself as a product. Generate a broad list of potential career paths, then conduct informational interviews with people in those roles to test fit before committing. This builds interview confidence and reveals what jobs actually look like versus what descriptions promise.

5. **The Pixar story template for career narrative**: Structure your career story as: Once upon a time... Every day... One day... Because of that... Until finally... Ever since that day... This creates a compelling arc that communicates who you are, what changed you, and where you want to go.

## Concepts covered

- **[[jobs-to-be-done|Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD)]]** — A framework for understanding what motivates people to 'hire' a product or, in this case, a job -- focusing on the progress they're trying to make in a given context.
- **Four Career Quests** — The four reasons people change jobs: get out (escape), take the next step (grow), regain control (balance), and realign (return to strengths).
- **Energy Drivers and Drains** — A self-assessment method for cataloging work moments that energize or deplete you, forming design requirements for your ideal next role.
- **Jobcation** — A deliberately low-stress transitional job that allows recovery and self-rediscovery after burnout, like a sabbatical with a paycheck.
- **Career Prototyping** — Conducting informational interviews across diverse roles to test career hypotheses before committing, analogous to product prototyping.
- **Pushes and Pulls** — The JTBD forces model applied to careers: pushes are frustrations driving you away from a current job, pulls are desired outcomes attracting you to a new one.
