# The ultimate guide to JTBD | Bob Moesta (co-creator of the framework)

**Guest**: Bob Moesta (second appearance)

## Executive summary

Bob Moesta, co-creator of [[jobs-to-be-done|JTBD]] with Clay Christensen, applies the framework to careers in his book *Job Moves*. His core insight: employees hire companies far more than companies hire employees. He identifies four quests that drive job changes (Get Out, Take the Next Step, Regain Control, Realign), distinguishes job features (salary, title) from job experiences (energy drivers/drains), and lays out a systematic process for prototyping the next role. For Bob's original JTBD episode focused on products, see [[source-bob-moesta]].

## Key ideas

1. **Four quests for job change**: Get Out (escape toxicity), Take the Next Step (skills/growth), Regain Control (time/balance), Realign (return to energy). Each quest has a different optimization criterion for the next role.
2. **Job features vs. job experiences**: Salary and title are static features that depreciate over time. Experiences — what energizes or drains you — are what keep you in a role. Money is a surrogate for deeper needs (respect, security, recognition).
3. **Energy drivers and energy drains**: Systematically catalog moments that give vs. drain energy. The ideal ratio is roughly 50/50; at that point work stops feeling like work. Use StrengthsFinder's bottom 5 to surface drains.
4. **Prototype your career**: Treat yourself as a product. Prototype widely via informational interviews across industries, then narrow. Most people under-estimate how broadly their skills transfer.
5. **Jobcation**: After intense startup experiences, deliberately take a less-demanding role to recover, rebuild relationships, and rediscover who you are outside of work.

## Notable quote

## B4 angle

Extends JTBD from product discovery to career navigation. The features-vs-experiences distinction is the same structural insight that makes JTBD powerful for products: users don't buy features, they buy progress.
