# Source: Building Anchor, Selling to Spotify, and Lessons Learned ft. Maya Prohovnik

**Guest**: Maya Prohovnik (Head of Podcast Product, Spotify; Employee #1 at Anchor)
**Published**: 2023-09-28
**Duration**: ~1h 8m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1gXNOJEWajU

## Executive summary

Maya Prohovnik shares how Anchor went from a 20-person startup to powering over 75% of all new podcasts through relentless friction reduction and willingness to "kill your darlings" when product-market fit doesn't match the mission. She argues that dogfooding is non-negotiable for anyone building creator tools, and that your gut is a valid data type that should be treated as seriously as quantitative metrics.

## Key ideas

1. **Dogfooding as a leadership practice** — Maya runs four personal podcasts and insists every member of her product team create one. She finds that PMs who don't use their own product can read data and talk to users, but they don't truly internalize the emotional barriers of being a creator until they feel them firsthand.

2. **Do things that don't scale** — When Anchor needed one-click podcast distribution, they hired college interns to manually create Apple Podcast accounts and submit hundreds of thousands of shows. Users saw a magic "distribute everywhere" button; behind the curtain, it was manual labor. This unscalable hack drove massive hosting market share before they could automate it.

3. **Kill your darlings to serve the mission** — Anchor went through two major pivots (1.0 voice messaging to 2.0 creator tools, then 2.0 anti-podcast to 3.0 full podcast platform). Each time they had loyal, retained users telling them not to change, but the team recognized the existing product would never reach the mission of democratizing audio. The second pivot — adding RSS/podcast export against their philosophical resistance — triggered the hockey-stick growth curve.

4. **Your gut is a type of data** — Maya treats intuition as a data point alongside analytics and user research. The key is articulating *why* your gut says something — referencing experience, user stories, and personal usage. Just saying "trust me" doesn't work in a large org; framing gut as evidence does.

5. **The 80/20 rule for user feedback** — Build for the 80% of potential users, not just the vocal 20% who already love the product. Especially on creative platforms targeting everyone, listening only to power users digs you deeper into a niche.

6. **Post-acquisition depression is real** — Founders who exit through acquisition often experience an unexpected existential crisis. Maya describes losing the survival-mode energy that drove her at a startup, and emphasizes that there is not enough support for this transition. She credits staying aligned with a clear mission for ultimately thriving inside Spotify.

7. **The Eisenhower Matrix as a daily practice** — Maya uses the four Ds (do, defer, delegate, delete) every evening to clear her task list. She writes everything down in Todoist and refuses to end her day without triaging every outstanding item. This frees mental bandwidth for the deep thinking her role requires.

## Concepts that deserve their own wiki page

- **[[Dogfooding]]** — Using your own product as a core leadership and team practice, not just a one-off exercise
- **[[Do things that don't scale]]** — Deliberately choosing manual, unscalable solutions to deliver magical user experiences early on
- **[[Kill your darlings]]** — Willingness to abandon a working product that doesn't serve the larger mission
- **[[Friction reduction]]** — Systematically eliminating barriers to entry, especially for creator onboarding
