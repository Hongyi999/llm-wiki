# Source: Making an impact through authenticity and curiosity | Ami Vora (CPO at Faire)

**Guest**: Ami Vora (CPO at Faire; ex-WhatsApp/Facebook/Instagram, ~15 years at Meta)
**Published**: 2024-06-23
**Duration**: ~1h 23m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6UHAop9fhNU

## Executive summary

Explicit case for authentic messy leadership: no career plan, works out of her bathroom, optimizes jobs by trying on "the coat" and checking whether it feels like home. Leadership toolkit centers on *curiosity as ego-override* ("fascinating, tell me more"), metaphors as alignment infrastructure, execution-eats-strategy, and principles-not-answers product reviews.

## Key ideas

1. **"Fascinating, tell me more" as ego-sublimation** — [[source-boz|Boz's]] story: he'd respond to profoundly wrong takes with genuine curiosity. Learned because being-right-all-the-time failed: "I was letting my ego overtake my desire to get to the best outcome."
2. **Metaphor-as-alignment-infrastructure** — Hill-climb (local vs. global optimum), dinosaur-brain (execs hold three facts, so bring the recommendation), face-to-face (WhatsApp's north-star feeling), "Dolores Park on a sunny Saturday" (brand feel). Narrative lets teams decide without checking in.
3. **Execution eats strategy for breakfast** — "Customers don't care about your five-year plan." With bad strategy + perfect execution you still learn. Spend ~20% on strategy, 80% confirming it with customers.
4. **Product reviews = principles, not decisions** — Come with *one* decision but walk out with principles about decisions of that shape, so the org scales without routing everything through you.
5. **"You can have more energy or less ambition" + only-bad-decisions senior leadership** — Chamath quote. Every problem a senior leader sees is unsolvable (someone else would have solved it), so your job is choosing which suboptimal branch to sign your name on. Normalize the double-bind women's feedback and expand toolkit rather than shrink yourself.
