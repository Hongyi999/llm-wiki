# Driving alignment within teams, work-life balance, and the changing PM landscape | Nikita Miller

**Guest**: Nikita Miller
**Published**: 2023-04-06
**Duration**: 59:20
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4PhfAbRQpbI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4PhfAbRQpbI)

## Executive summary

Nikita Miller (SVP & Head of Product at The Knot Worldwide; former Head of Growth & Retention at Trello/[[atlassian|Atlassian]]) shares frameworks for cross-functional alignment. Her central move — the roles-and-responsibilities contract — forces each function to explicitly write down its role and its expectations of counterparts, then align as a group. She pushes the triad to a "quad" by embedding data science, warns against over-correcting from "output" to pure "outcomes," and offers a universal decision question: "What are you optimizing for?"

## Key ideas

1. **Roles & Responsibilities Contract**: Have each function (PM, design, engineering, data) write its own role and expectations of others, then align as a group. Revisit every 3-6 months or whenever tension arises.
2. **The Quad (not Triad)**: Embed data science into the cross-functional team alongside PM, design, engineering. Centralized data teams become blockers; embedded data scientists spot patterns sooner.
3. **Outcomes AND Output**: The industry over-corrected to "outcomes, outcomes, outcomes" and forgot that output (shipping velocity) is a leading indicator of outcomes. Teams that only discuss goals but don't ship never reach the outcomes.
4. **"What are you optimizing for?"**: Her universal decision question. If the optimization target isn't explicit, trade-offs can't be.
5. **Work-life optimization, not balance**: Don't balance everything simultaneously. Ask what you're optimizing for right now (this week, this quarter, this phase), and accept the trade-offs.

## B4 angle

The Roles & Responsibilities Contract is an immediately actionable framework for any PM leading a cross-functional team. The outcomes-vs-output tension is a direct counterpoint to [[source-marty-cagan|Cagan's]] "output teams" critique — worth reading the two together.
