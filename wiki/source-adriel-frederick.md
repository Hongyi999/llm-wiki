# Humanizing product development | Adriel Frederick

**Guest**: Adriel Frederick
**Published**: 2022-10-20
**Duration**: 1:07:26
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uMhBej6-Ey4](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uMhBej6-Ey4)

## Executive summary

Adriel Frederick (VP Product, Reddit X; ex-Facebook/Lyft) argues that product teams win by re-humanizing their process — remembering that users are people, that teams are people, and that the PM craft is fundamentally social. He pushes PMs to think about the "marginal user" (the person who will churn with one more bad experience), shares Lyft's Cannonballs-vs-Lead-Bullets framework for bet sizing, and makes a strong case that team diversity is a product advantage, not an HR talking point. The episode is a corrective to PM culture that over-indexes on frameworks and under-indexes on empathy.

## Key ideas

1. **Design for the marginal user**: The user who will churn with one more friction point, not the power user who already loves you. Feature additions that delight power users often kill marginal-user retention. Always ask "does this help the person on the edge?"
2. **Cannonballs vs Lead Bullets** (from Horowitz): Sometimes you need many small, cheap experiments (lead bullets); sometimes you need one decisive bet that only works if you go all-in (a cannonball). Most teams confuse the two and under-invest in cannonballs.
3. **Diversity is a product advantage**: A team that mirrors the user base catches edge cases earlier and builds for more of the market. Homogeneous teams build for themselves and are systematically blind to whole user segments.
4. **Humanize the process**: Product rituals (standups, reviews, retros) drift into theater. Rehumanize them by explicitly asking "what are we learning?" and "how are we feeling?" — especially under pressure.
5. **0→1 at scale is a different sport**: Reddit X incubates new products inside a mature org. The biggest failure mode is trying to apply mature-product rigor (OKRs, roadmaps, process) to 0→1 work. Early bets need air cover, not governance.

## Concepts covered

- **Marginal user design** — Building for the churning edge, not the delighted core.
- **Cannonballs vs Lead Bullets** — Bet sizing and commitment depth.
- **Diversity as product advantage** — Team composition as a product input.
- **Rehumanizing rituals** — Retros, standups, reviews that don't drift to theater.
- **0→1 inside mature orgs** — Governance kills incubation.
- **Psychological safety in product teams** — Precondition for honest learning.

## Notable quote

> Adriel Frederick: "The marginal user is the one you're about to lose. A roadmap that only serves your most engaged users is a roadmap that ships you into a smaller and smaller pond."
