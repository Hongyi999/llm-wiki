# Humanizing product development | Adriel Frederick (Reddit, Lyft, Facebook)

**Guest**: Adriel Frederick  
**Published**: 2022-10-20  
**Duration**: 1:07:26  
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uMhBej6-Ey4](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uMhBej6-Ey4)

## Executive summary

Adriel Frederick, VP of Product at Reddit X, shares hard-won lessons from being the first Black PM at Facebook, leading marketplace pricing at Lyft, and incubating new products at Reddit. His central thesis is that algorithms need human judgment to make strategic decisions — product managers must design the interface between machine intelligence and human intent rather than pursuing fully automated solutions. He illustrates this with vivid stories: driving for Lyft to understand driver pay complaints, discovering that Facebook's name field assumptions failed in India, and redesigning Lyft's pricing system after an overly complex algorithmic approach proved operationally inflexible. The episode also covers how diverse teams build better global products, how to navigate working at controversial companies, and why the 'marginal user' concept is key to unlocking growth.

## Key ideas

1. **Algorithms Need Human Judgment**: Product managers working on ML-heavy systems must decide what the algorithm should be responsible for versus what requires human strategic decisions. Algorithms optimize for given objectives but cannot understand long-term effects, user intent, or strategic constraints — PMs must design the interface that lets humans amplify their intent through machines.

2. **The Marginal User as Growth Lever**: Focus on the person just on the cusp of taking the action you want. Go to the extreme worst case to see everything that is broken, then work backwards to the marginal user to prioritize what to fix. Data tells you how bad things are; watching users tells you why.

3. **Diversity as Competitive Advantage**: Having teams that reflect your global user base makes product design dramatically faster — arguments that would take two weeks of user research can be resolved in 15 minutes of internal debate. The key is recognizing concrete business value from diversity, which drives retention of diverse talent and makes recruiting easier.

4. **Operational Control as First-Order Product Requirement**: When building products for complex real-world systems (like ride-sharing marketplaces), operational flexibility must be a first-class design requirement. Adriel's team had to rebuild Lyft's pricing algorithm from scratch because the initial version was too complex to allow human operators to make day-to-day strategic adjustments.

5. **No Silver Bullets, Many Lead Bullets (and Some Cannonballs)**: Sustainable growth comes from grinding on core actions — making products easy to find, easy to get into, and easy to use. Growth hacks provide early traction but don't substitute for solving real problems. Occasionally, massive investments like phone number sign-up or global SMS delivery produce cannonball-level impact.

## Concepts covered

- **Human-in-the-Loop Product Design** — The principle that ML/algorithmic products must be designed with explicit interfaces for human strategic judgment, not treated as autonomous black boxes.
- **Marginal User** — The person just on the cusp of taking a desired action — focusing on them reveals actionable friction points, while examining the worst-case user reveals the full inventory of problems.
- **Operational Control** — The requirement that people can make day-to-day strategic and tactical adjustments to algorithmic systems, especially in marketplaces with real-world variables.
- **R&D Team Integration** — Three principles for internal innovation teams: work must be core to the mission, success must be shared across the org, and other teams must still feel they can innovate too.
- **Lead Bullets and Cannonballs** — Growth portfolio framework: most progress comes from many small improvements (lead bullets), but occasional massive product investments (cannonballs) create step-function gains.
