**Published**: 2022-01-01
**Duration**: ~1h 2m

## Executive summary

Rajaram offers a compact playbook on picking companies, designing product development processes, hiring (especially your first PM and senior leaders), titles, and angel investing. Organizing thesis: serendipity and paying it forward, executed with time-boxed discipline, compound into outsized careers.

## Key ideas

1. **Serendipity + pay it forward as career strategy** — Gokul ended up at Google AdSense by walking past an office at 6pm and volunteering as nights-and-weekends PM. "Great careers aren't built linearly by working your job and getting promoted." Be curious, take inbounds, help others without keeping score.
2. **Hire your first PM from inside (8-10 engineers) — and pick problem-solvers over feature-shippers** — At every company he's been at, the first PM was an existing engineer/designer/analyst who already had founder and team trust. Bringing an external PM with a different process often gets "rejected by the body." Watch for ex-engineer PMs regressing to the how instead of living in the what/why.
3. **Feature factory is the biggest product-development pitfall** — Founders become too tactical and dictate features instead of presenting problems. "If a feature is shipped but doesn't change customer behavior, is it really a feature?" Empowered teams should take "increase new user acquisition" and return three prioritized approaches.
4. **Hire leaders from the lieutenants of best-in-class companies** — Not the function head, but the up-and-coming #2s and #3s at the company recognized as excellent at that function for a similar customer type. Build the target org chart on LinkedIn. Gokul watched this happen to Square post-IPO (their payments/compliance org was "raided" by fintechs).
5. **Delay titles, especially Director/VP** — Titles can only be given once; giving VP Engineering at 25 people locks you in for life. Prefer "Lead" or "Head of [narrow scope]." Jack Dorsey at Square/Block pioneered this; Gokul saw zero title-politics debates as a result.

## B6 angle (hiring, management, leadership Part 2)

Rajaram's contribution is unusually operational and specific: the "hire your first PM from inside" rule, the "lieutenant-of-the-lieutenant" leader-hiring playbook, and the anti-Director/VP title discipline are all concrete tactics B5 voices gesture at but don't systematize. His founder-authenticity-to-company-culture framing is a useful counterpoint to [[source-ben-horowitz|Horowitz's]] "right way to run a company" — closer to [[source-molly-graham|Graham's]] "live in the future" and [[source-brian-chesky|Chesky's]] authenticity-as-strategy. Unique angles vs. B5: the 2-hours-a-day hiring discipline, and the founder-intuition / serendipity career thesis that complements but doesn't duplicate [[source-jerry-colonna|Colonna's]] internal work.
