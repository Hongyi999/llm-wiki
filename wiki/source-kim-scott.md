## Executive summary

Kim Scott, author of the million-copy bestseller Radical Candor, joins Lenny to deliver a masterclass on giving and receiving feedback in the workplace. She walks through her two-by-two framework — Radical Candor, Obnoxious Aggression, Manipulative Insincerity, and Ruinous Empathy — arguing that 90% of feedback failures land in the Ruinous Empathy quadrant where people avoid hard conversations out of misplaced kindness. Scott provides highly tactical advice for soliciting, delivering, and receiving feedback using her HHIIPPP and CORE frameworks. She distinguishes Radical Candor from toxic directness (a la Bridgewater) and argues you don't have to choose between being kind and being successful, introducing her forthcoming book Radical Respect as a prequel addressing bias, prejudice, and bullying in the workplace.

## Key ideas

1. **The Ruinous Empathy Trap**: 90% of feedback mistakes fall into Ruinous Empathy — caring personally but failing to challenge directly. Scott's story of 'Bob,' whom she failed to give honest feedback for 10 months before having to fire him, illustrates how avoiding short-term discomfort creates far worse long-term outcomes for everyone involved.
2. **HHIIPPP and CORE Frameworks for Feedback**: Effective feedback should be Humble, Helpful, Immediate, In-person (synchronous), Praise in public / Criticize in private, and not about Personality. The CORE structure (Context, Observation, Result, next stEp) gives a repeatable pattern for both praise and criticism.
3. **Soliciting Feedback as the First Step**: The order of operations for Radical Candor starts with soliciting criticism, not giving it. Scott recommends crafting a personalized go-to question (e.g., 'What could I do or stop doing that would make it easier to work with me?'), then embracing the discomfort of six seconds of silence, listening to understand, and richly rewarding candor by visibly fixing problems.
4. **Radical Candor vs. Obnoxious Aggression — The Steve Jobs Distinction**: Scott pushes back on the notion that being a jerk is necessary for success. She distinguishes Steve Jobs (who built deep caring relationships with his reports) from Ray Dalio and Elon Musk, arguing the false dichotomy between competence and kindness is destructive.

## Concepts & frameworks

- **Radical Candor** — A two-by-two framework where caring personally and challenging directly simultaneously produces the best feedback and relationships.
- **Ruinous Empathy** — The most common feedback failure mode where people avoid giving honest criticism out of concern for others' feelings.
- **Obnoxious Aggression** — Challenging directly without showing personal care, which triggers fight-or-flight and is both harmful and inefficient.
- **CORE Feedback Structure** — A framework for structuring feedback: Context, Observation, Result, next stEp — applicable to both praise and criticism.
- **Soliciting Feedback** — The practice of proactively asking others for criticism using a personalized go-to question, as the essential first step of Radical Candor.
