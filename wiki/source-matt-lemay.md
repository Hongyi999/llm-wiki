# The one question that saves product careers | Matt LeMay

**Guest**: Matt LeMay  
**Published**: 2025-08-14  
**Duration**: 1:32:09  
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ts9ZvlkeWGs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ts9ZvlkeWGs)

## Executive summary

Matt LeMay, author of Product Management in Practice and Impact First Product Teams, argues that the most important thing a product team can do is align every piece of work to business-critical outcomes. He introduces the Low Impact PM Death Spiral -- a self-reinforcing cycle where teams gravitate toward safe, low-impact features that gradually make the product harder to change, leading inevitably to layoffs. LeMay offers three concrete steps to break this cycle: set team goals no more than one step away from company goals, keep impact front-and-center at every stage of product development, and connect every bit of prioritized work back to impact using the same unit of measure as the team goal. He emphasizes that commercially-minded PMs are paradoxically also the happiest, because clarity about business contribution reduces anxiety rather than increasing it.

## Key ideas

1. **The CEO Funding Question**: Ask yourself -- if you were the CEO, would you fully fund your own team? If you cannot answer confidently, you are in a risky position. This perspective shift forces teams to evaluate their work through the lens of business investment rather than feature delivery.

2. **The Low Impact PM Death Spiral**: Teams doing low-impact work create product complexity that makes high-impact work harder, which drives more low-impact work, which creates more complexity -- a vicious cycle that only breaks with layoffs or courageous individual action to pursue high-impact goals.

3. **Goals One Step Away from Company Goals**: Inspired by Christina Wodtke's Radical Focus, team goals should orbit the company goal with at most one mathematical operator (multiply, subset) between them. Multi-level cascading OKRs introduce compounding risk that disconnects daily work from business outcomes.

4. **Impact Estimation in Goal Units**: When prioritizing work (RICE, ICE, etc.), express the 'I' (impact) in the same unit of measure as your team goal. This prevents abstract scoring from masking the reality that most items on a backlog have negligible contribution to what actually matters.

5. **Options Plus a Recommendation**: When pushing back on executive requests, never say yes or no outright. Present multiple options with trade-offs and a clear recommendation, which transforms adversarial dynamics into collaborative decision-making and preserves trust.

## Concepts covered

- **Low Impact PM Death Spiral** — A self-reinforcing cycle where low-impact feature work creates product complexity that makes high-impact work harder, driving teams deeper into irrelevance.
- **Impact First Product Teams** — A methodology where every product team proactively aligns its work to business-critical outcomes, regardless of organizational structure or process.
- **Goals One Step Away** — The principle that team-level goals should connect to company goals through at most one mathematical operation, preventing cascading abstraction.
- **Work Around the Work** — Daniel Ek's characterization of supporting activities that do not directly contribute to business-critical outcomes, cited as justification for layoffs.
- **Facilitating a Value Exchange** — Melissa Perri's definition of product management as the practice of mediating between what a business needs and what customers need.
- **Options and a Recommendation** — A stakeholder management technique for pushing back without saying no -- presenting trade-offs with a recommended path forward.
