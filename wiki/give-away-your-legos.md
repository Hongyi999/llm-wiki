# Give Away Your Legos

**"Give away your Legos"** is Molly Graham's framework for growing inside a hypergrowth company. Coined from her Facebook years, where she "rehired herself every three weeks."

## The idea

In a scaling company, the Lego pile keeps getting bigger. Every quarter there's more scope than any single person can hold. Leaders who cling to the thing they just got good at get buried by the new pile that's forming behind them.

Growth comes from **repeatedly handing off** your current job (the Legos you just got good at playing with) and **picking up the next unfamiliar pile**.

## The emotional friction

The hard part isn't finding someone to hand it to — it's accepting that:

1. They will do it differently than you.
2. They may initially do it worse.
3. You will feel obsolete and scared.

Molly's frame: **the fear to listen to is financial; the fear to ignore is "I'm scared I can't do this."**

## Pairs with

- **J-curve vs. stairs careers** (also Molly Graham): Stair-step careers are predictable and ceiling-limited. J-curve careers require jumping off cliffs into roles you're unqualified for, falling for 6–9 months, and climbing to places stairs never reach. Giving away your Legos is the first move in every J-curve jump.
- **Waterline Model** — Diagnose structural problems first, then people problems.
- **The cave you fear contains the treasure you seek** — the Campbell-esque version.

## Operator implication

If you've been doing the same role for 18 months and it feels comfortable, either (a) you've stopped giving away Legos and the pile has stopped growing, or (b) the company has stopped growing. Both are signals.
