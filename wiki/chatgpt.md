# ChatGPT

[[openai|OpenAI's]] consumer AI chat product, launched November 2022, grew to ~700M weekly users by 2025. In B1 it appears both as a subject (how it was built and scaled) and as an object of competitive analysis (why it has a durable moat).

## B1 appearances

- [[source-nick-turley]] — **Nick Turley leads ChatGPT product at OpenAI.** The episode is the most detailed public telling of ChatGPT's internal culture: the original launch was a hackathon accident, every decision since has been made empirically with daily shipping cadence, and the organization explicitly values "vibe checks" over benchmarks. See [[product-velocity-ai-era]].
- [[source-brian-balfour]] — Brian uses ChatGPT as the canonical example of a "[[ai-agents|context-and-memory moat]]": the product's memory of user context compounds into switching cost that independent AI tools can't match. This is his core prediction for why ChatGPT wins the consumer AI layer.

## Key claims about ChatGPT in B1

- **Empirical over strategic.** Nick Turley says the team does not do much "strategy work" in the classical sense; they ship and measure. Product is discovered, not planned. See [[product-velocity-ai-era]].
- **Vibe beats benchmarks.** Internal evaluation leans heavily on subjective quality taste, not leaderboard metrics.
- **Memory is the moat.** Brian Balfour argues that ChatGPT's accumulating user context (history, preferences, custom instructions) is a compounding switching cost that trumps raw model capability.
- **Agentic roadmap with human oversight.** Turley's vision is toward agents that take actions on the user's behalf, always with a human-in-the-loop checkpoint.
