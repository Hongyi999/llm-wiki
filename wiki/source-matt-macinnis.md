# "I deliberately understaff every project" | Leadership lessons from Rippling's $16B journey

**Guest**: Matt MacInnis  
**Published**: 2025-12-28  
**Duration**: 1:36:17  
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O_W76LR77Vw](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O_W76LR77Vw)

## Executive summary

Matt MacInnis, CPO and former COO at Rippling, shares his operating philosophy for building high-performance product organizations. He argues that extraordinary outcomes demand extraordinary effort, and that leaders must relentlessly inject energy into their teams to fight organizational entropy. MacInnis introduces his alpha/beta framework for balancing process rigor with creative freedom, and explains how Rippling's Product Quality List (PQL) codifies lightweight quality standards. He also offers candid advice on knowing when to quit a startup, the dangers of VC incentive structures, and why product market fit is like drug-receptor binding -- either the market wants your product or it does not.

## Key ideas

1. **Deliberate Understaffing**: MacInnis argues it is always better to understaff than overstaff projects. Overstaffing breeds politics, wasted effort on low-priority work, and organizational cruft. The wisdom lies in knowing where the line is between productive constraint and destructive deprivation.

2. **Alpha/Beta Framework for Product Teams**: Borrowed from finance, alpha represents outperformance (creativity, risk-taking) while beta represents volatility. Processes exist solely to lower beta but inevitably suppress alpha. Leaders must apply process surgically -- rigid standards for mature products like payroll, but latitude for zero-to-one initiatives.

3. **Entropy Demands Relentless Energy**: Drawing from the second law of thermodynamics, MacInnis argues that every system -- codebases, teams, processes -- tends toward disorder. The only antidote is constant energy injection. Leaders must model intensity publicly, never buffer their teams from the founder's urgency, and treat every bug and escalation as a gift.

4. **Product Market Fit Is Binary**: Like drug-receptor binding in biotech, the market either latches onto your product or it does not. No amount of marketing can create demand that does not exist. Founders should view their startup as running an experiment against fate, and should be willing to quit after 4-5 years if PMF has not clearly arrived.

5. **The PQL (Product Quality List)**: Rippling created an idiosyncratic, iterative checklist for shipping quality. Named deliberately to become part of internal culture ("pickle"), the PQL is a lightweight process that lowers beta without crushing alpha, and evolves with every lesson learned from production incidents.

## Concepts covered

- **Alpha/Beta Framework** — A mental model from finance applied to team design: alpha is creative outperformance, beta is volatility, and processes trade one for the other.
- **Deliberate Understaffing** — The principle of intentionally keeping teams lean to force prioritization and prevent low-priority work from proliferating.
- **Organizational Entropy** — The tendency of teams and systems to decay toward disorder unless leaders continuously inject energy and maintain standards.
- **[[product-market-fit|Product Market Fit as Drug-Receptor Binding]]** — A metaphor framing PMF as a binary discovery -- the market's receptors either bind to your product or they do not, and marketing cannot change that.
- **Product Quality List (PQL)** — Rippling's iterative checklist for shipping standards, designed as a lightweight process to lower output volatility while preserving innovation.
- **Conway's Law** — The observation that organizations ship products reflecting their communication structure -- locally optimized teams produce locally optimized products.
