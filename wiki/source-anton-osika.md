# Source: Building Lovable: $10M ARR in 60 days with 15 people | Anton Osika (CEO and co-founder)

## Executive summary

Anton Osika, co-founder and CEO of Lovable, describes building 'the last piece of software' -- an AI engineer that lets anyone turn an English description into a fully working product. Lovable hit $4M ARR in four weeks and $10M in two months with just 15 people, making it the fastest-growing startup in European history. Osika's key technical insight was identifying and systematically eliminating the places where AI gets stuck (login, database persistence, Stripe payments) through a scaling law of painstaking debugging and quantitative tuning. He argues strongly for generalist team members who combine engineering, design, and product taste, and believes the bottleneck has permanently shifted from engineering capacity to product taste and user understanding.

## Key ideas

1. **The 'last piece of software' vision**: Lovable aims to be the final tool anyone needs to build software, where going from idea to fully working, deployed product is nearly instant. The roadmap extends beyond code generation to automated analytics, A/B testing, and growth assistance for the products people build.
2. **Scaling law for AI reliability**: Osika discovered that when you painstakingly identify the specific places where AI gets stuck (login flows, database persistence, payment integration) and systematically address each one, the product reliably improves. This debugging-focused scaling law is the core technical moat, and they are still early in exploiting it.
3. **Generalists over specialists**: For AI-era product teams, Osika recommends hiring people with the broadest possible skill set -- individuals who understand architecture, design, product taste, and user research. The bottleneck is no longer engineering capacity but having good taste and intuition about what users need.
4. **Minimum Lovable Product philosophy**: The company name itself encodes a product philosophy -- build not just a minimum viable product but a minimum lovable one, then iterate toward an 'absolutely lovable product.' This emphasis on craft and user delight drove organic word-of-mouth growth without significant marketing spend.
5. **Engineering-led product development**: For AI-native products, Osika argues against traditional PM-led prioritization because the right solution is often entangled with technical details. Engineers who also have product taste and user empathy should lead, with weekly planning cycles and rapid iteration rather than long roadmaps.

- **AI Reliability Scaling Law** — The observation that systematically identifying and fixing the specific failure modes where AI gets stuck leads to reliable, measurable product improvement.
- [[ai-coding-tools|Engineering-Led Product Development]] — An organizational approach where engineers with product taste lead prioritization because solutions are deeply entangled with technical implementation details.
- [[ai-coding-tools|The Last Piece of Software]] — Lovable's vision that their AI tool will be the final software anyone needs to build, as it can create all future applications.
