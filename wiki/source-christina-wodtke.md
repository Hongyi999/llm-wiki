# Source: The Ultimate Guide to OKRs ft. Christina Wodtke

**Guest**: Christina Wodtke (Stanford lecturer; author of *Radical Focus*; former product leader at LinkedIn, Zynga, MySpace, Yahoo)
**Published**: 2023-03-16
**Duration**: ~1h 13m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kvkL18Ue0dE

## Executive summary

Christina Wodtke, widely considered the leading practitioner-teacher of OKRs, presents OKRs not as a cure-all but as a "vitamin" that supercharges companies that already have strategy, empowered teams, and psychological safety in place. She breaks down the full stack from mission to strategy to quarterly OKRs to weekly status emails, emphasizing that the atomic unit is not the OKR document itself but the weekly question: "What am I doing this week to get closer to our goals?"

## Key ideas

1. **OKRs are a vitamin, not a medicine** — OKRs will not fix a broken company; they will only reveal what is broken. Prerequisites: clear strategy, empowered teams, and [[psychological safety]]. Companies that already have their fundamentals right see OKRs become a supercharger for focus, alignment, and learning cadence.

2. **The atomic unit: "What am I doing this week?"** — The heart of OKRs is not the document but the weekly practice of asking what you are doing right now to move toward your strategic goals. An accountability partner who hand-waves about direction but asks this question every Monday can outperform someone with meticulous tracking who burns out on precision.

3. **Mission to strategy to OKR to weekly status** — The hierarchy: mission (5-year horizon, specific enough to be meaningful), strategy (strongly held hypothesis about how to win), quarterly OKRs (one inspiring objective + ~3 key results answered via "how do we know?"), and weekly status emails (confidence level, last week, next week). The "last week" field is the most valuable because it reveals what blocked progress.

4. **How to derive key results: "How do we know?"** — Start with the objective (an inspiring vision for the quarter). Then ask "How do we know we succeeded?" Brainstorm for 10 minutes -- obvious answers come first, but weird ideas emerge later and often yield the best metrics. Aim for three key results: one hard metric, one quality/delight measure, one revenue signal.

5. **Friday celebrations matter more than you think** — CEOs who started Friday celebrations before even rolling out OKRs reported immediate cultural improvement. Asking "What was the most awesome thing that happened this week?" across teams creates belonging and momentum. Monday commitments + Friday celebrations = the weekly bookends of OKR cadence.

6. **Pilot with your best team** — Never try to fix a bad team with OKRs; it will make everyone hate OKRs. Start with a high-performing multidisciplinary team, give them the book, let them experiment for a quarter, then learn from what they discovered about OKRs in your specific culture. Expand from there.

7. **Don't over-plan; don't over-grade** — OKR planning should take a week maximum, not a month. The approval process kills velocity -- replace boss-approval with peer review (3 related teams, 24-hour turnaround). Grading should be rough (70-80% = success) with emphasis on the retrospective, not numerical precision. "What went wrong?" matters infinitely more than "Was it 0.72 or 0.78?"

8. **PMs should focus on business, not just UX** — Wodtke's contrarian take: too many PMs come from design backgrounds and over-index on user experience while neglecting business models, pricing, target markets, and growth trends. Product sense is "compressed experience" -- young PMs should learn business fundamentals rather than trusting intuition they don't yet have.

## Concepts that deserve their own wiki page

- **[[OKRs]]** — Objectives and Key Results: quarterly goal-setting framework with an inspiring objective and measurable key results, reviewed weekly
- **[[Radical Focus]]** — Wodtke's approach to OKRs emphasizing single-objective focus per quarter with weekly Monday commitments and Friday celebrations
- **[[Strategy]]** — A strongly held hypothesis about how to win in the market and fulfill a company's mission; sits between mission and OKRs in the planning hierarchy

## Notable quote

> "People do not value celebrations enough. I've had CEOs who said, 'It was the middle of the quarter, so we didn't start OKRs, but we did start Friday celebrations and oh my God, things are already changing. Things are already getting better.'"
