# She turned 100+ rejections into a $42B company | Melanie Perkins

**Guest**: Melanie Perkins
**Published**: 2025-11-02
**Duration**: 1:06:10
**YouTube**: [Lenny's Podcast — Melanie Perkins](https://www.lennyspodcast.com/)

## Executive summary

Melanie Perkins, co-founder/CEO of [[canva|Canva]] ($42B, ~$3.3B ARR, 240M+ MAUs, 8 years profitable), argues that most companies plan from available bricks ("Column A") when they should plan from the dreamed-of future ("Column B"). Canva's trajectory — from 100+ investor rejections to a generational company — came from relentlessly iterating the pitch, setting "Crazy Big Goals" paired with concrete mission pillars, and running product by listening hard to customers while protecting the long-term vision.

## Key ideas

1. **Column B thinking**: Don't plan by stacking today's bricks. Start with the wild 10-year future you want, build a ladder of small concrete steps to it. Canva's mission — empower everyone to design anything, in every language, on every device — was set as a student and still governs roadmaps today.
2. **Chaos to clarity**: Every idea starts embarrassing and amorphous. You add clarity by progressively forcing it into writing, then a deck, then designs, then a prototype. Vision decks for every project are Canva's standard operating procedure.
3. **Crazy Big Goals + mission pillars + celebrations**: Three nested layers: 10-year mission → 4–6 mission pillars → annual Crazy Big Goals under each. Each goal achievement triggers a staged ritual (plate-smashing, dove releases, La Tomatina). Celebration is a management tool, not a perk.
4. **Rejection as product feedback**: 100+ VC nos became structural input for the pitch deck — each common objection ("market too small," "too similar to X") was pre-answered with a new slide. The vision stayed constant; the articulation was iterated into the legendary 2012 deck.
5. **Hire authentic to your company, not off-the-shelf**: Executives joining from bigger companies repeatedly imported frameworks that didn't fit. Scaling requires reinventing systems authentic to your own bricks, not transplanting Google/Facebook/Salesforce playbooks.

## B5 angle

Mission-driven goal architecture (3 layers: mission → pillars → Crazy Big Goals with one owner) is both a recruiting magnet and a management tool. Melanie's authenticity-over-imported-playbooks lesson is an underweighted filter for executive hiring in scaling companies.
