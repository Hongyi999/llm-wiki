# Source: Mastering onboarding | Lauryn Isford (Head of Growth at Airtable)

**Guest**: Lauryn Isford (ex-Airtable Head of Growth; ex-Meta)
**Published**: 2023-02-12
**Duration**: ~1h 4m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dLku0AiGPVA

## Executive summary

Isford argues onboarding is the most undervalued growth lever: it sits upstream of retention, monetization, and expansion for any self-serve product. Walks through Airtable's 20% activation lift from three bets — guided onboarding wizard, segmentation by learning/building style, ongoing "Mole" education — and offers a contrarian take that growth teams over-experiment.

## Key ideas

1. **"Lower activation rates are better than higher ones"** — An activation metric where only 5-15% of users qualify is more valuable than 40% because the former has much higher correlation with long-term retention. Airtable's metric: week-4 multi-user active.
2. **Don't over-experiment** — Growth teams default to A/B testing everything. But experiments are expensive. Use experimentation primarily for risk mitigation on dramatic changes. Example: Airtable Forms copy-to-submitter feature shipped without A/B test because rigorous customer research gave conviction.
3. **Guided onboarding wizard > tooltips** — Airtable's biggest activation win was replacing tooltips with immersive wizard: user picks simple options on left, workflow visualizes on right. Drove ~20% activation lift.
4. **Segment by learning/building style, not role** — Marketing vs. PM vs. ops bucketing was less predictive than "do you need database familiarity / visual learner / team-invited collaborator."
5. **The Reverse Trial** — Offer BOTH freemium AND a free trial. Trial showcases premium value for a limited time; freemium keeps users long-term. Don't pick; do both.
6. **PLG funnel: Join → Evaluate → Upgrade → Expand** — Deliberately "Evaluate" (not Activate) because it captures user perspective. Airtable mapped acquisition/activation/monetization teams onto Join/Evaluate/Upgrade.
7. **Onboarding pitfalls** — (a) Tooltips that announce feature names ("This is automations") instead of enabling outcomes; (b) Pushing premium features in onboarding because they correlate with monetization — breaks customer trust.
