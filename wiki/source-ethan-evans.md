# Source: Taking control of your career | Ethan Evans (Amazon)

**Guest**: Ethan Evans (retired Amazon VP — Prime Video, Amazon Appstore, Prime Gaming, Twitch Commerce; now executive coach)
**Published**: 2024-01-14
**Duration**: ~1h 21m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GB0P0_nFPTA

## Executive summary

Ethan Evans offers a highly tactical playbook for taking ownership of your career. The centerpiece is **The Magic Loop** — a 5-step pattern of doing your job, asking your manager how to help, delivering, then linking their needs to your goals. He also dissects how to recover from high-stakes failure (the famous Jeff Bezos Appstore launch disaster), how to systematically invent, and why patience plus proactive next-level behavior is the cure for getting stuck at senior manager.

## Key ideas

1. **The Magic Loop** — Five-step repeatable algorithm: (1) do your current job well, (2) ask your manager how you can help, (3) do whatever they ask, (4) link help to your own goals ("Is there something you need that would also help me learn X?"), (5) repeat. "Managers help those who help them — it's human nature."
2. **The stuck-at-senior-manager problem** — Director/VP promotions depend on (a) whether the org needs that role and (b) your practicing next-level skills now (strategy, inventiveness, influence). Start "doing the job before you have the job" — then when layoffs consolidate teams, you're the one given more reports instead of fired.
3. **Recovering from high-stakes failure (the Bezos Appstore story)** — When his team's launch broke in front of Bezos, Evans (a) owned it immediately, (b) bought trust "one hour at a time" with proactive status updates, (c) sat next to Jeff in the next meeting because "flaming is easy, but being angry face-to-face is hard." Two years later he was promoted to VP — proving "you can dig out of any hole."
4. **Systematic invention = expertise + dedicated thinking time + combining two existing things** — You only need two hours a month. Evans holds 70+ patents; his drone-delivery-from-a-truck patent came from combining drone delivery with aircraft-carrier mental models. "You don't need many good ideas to be seen as tremendously inventive."
5. **Interview signal: appearance + enthusiasm + impact stories, not activity** — "People want to work with people who want to work with them." At senior levels, candidates fail by describing *what they did* rather than *why it mattered*. Evans wrote the phrase "An owner never says that's not my job" — now enshrined in Amazon's Ownership leadership principle.

## B6 angle (hiring, management, leadership Part 2)

Evans contributes the most concrete bottom-up IC/middle-manager career operating system in the B-series: where [[source-ben-horowitz|Horowitz]] and [[source-molly-graham|Graham]] write from the CEO chair and [[source-alisa-cohn|Cohn]]/[[source-jerry-colonna|Colonna]] coach from psychology, Evans gives the mid-career "how do I actually get promoted from L6 to L7" script. His failure-recovery protocol (own it, update hourly, meet in person) is a crisp counterpoint to [[source-brian-chesky|Chesky's]] "get in the details" and [[source-tobi-lutke|Lütke's]] macro-level accountability — it's the tactical analogue at the individual leader level.
