# Source: Notion's lost years, near collapse during COVID, staying small to move fast | Ivan Zhao

**Guest**: Ivan Zhao (Notion founder/CEO)
**Published**: 2025-03-06
**Duration**: ~1h 12m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IIPKMixTMfE

## Executive summary

Zhao tells the untold story of Notion's three-to-four "lost years," multiple code rewrites, a near-death COVID database-scaling crisis, and the philosophical throughline: you have to *hide your vision inside a form factor people already want*. Notion's craft-first, Lego-for-software religion produces both the joy (one billion+ users in docs/notes as B2C2B top-of-funnel) and the pain (horizontal products are slow to find PMF and always threatened by vertical point solutions).

## Key ideas

1. **Sugar-coated broccoli** — Early Notion built "developer tools for everyone" and nobody cared. They hid the Lego-for-software broccoli inside the sugar of a productivity tool people actually use every day.
2. **Resets compound through better abstractions** — Throwing away codebase isn't a loss; humans underestimate exponential catch-up. Inspired by Smalltalk/Lisp: a ~100-line kernel can catch a system that took years.
3. **Small-bus metaphor for talent density** — Smaller bus turns corners faster. Track revenue-per-employee, not headcount. Profitable for years, first salesperson at $10M ARR, first PM at 50 people.
4. **B2C2B horizontal flywheel + Lego-box vs Lego-brick** — Docs/notes is one of only three 1B-user use cases. But enterprise buyers want "solutions" (Lego boxes pre-assembled) not "Lego bricks." They got lost hard-coding sprint features that broke composability — a year-plus recovery.
5. **Craft as operating value** — Conference rooms named after timeless tools (iPhone, Toshiba rice cooker, Lamy 2000). "We shape our tools, then our tools shape us" (McLuhan).

## B8 angle

"Sugar-coated broccoli" is a direct analogue to Andy Raskin's strategic narrative and [[source-arielle-jackson|April Dunford's]] positioning-for-best-fit: if the true vision is too weird, position into an existing category people understand and let the vision emerge through use. Where [[source-geoffrey-moore|Moore's]] crossing-the-chasm says pick a beachhead vertical, Zhao argues the horizontal beachhead is a universal human use case (notes) growing into verticals via B2C2B — a disagreement with Moore.
