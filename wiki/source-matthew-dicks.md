# Source: How to tell better stories | Matthew Dicks (Storyworthy)

**Guest**: Matthew Dicks (Moth GrandSlam champion; author of *Storyworthy*)
**Published**: 2023-12-15
**Duration**: ~1h 43m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J4wguyJZI6A

## Executive summary

Every good story hinges on one five-second moment of transformation or realization — and most business communication fails because it's "round, white, and flavorless," forgettable by the time you reach your driveway. Concrete craft techniques (elephants, backpacks, breadcrumbs, hourglasses, crystal balls) for building stakes, plus rules (dinner test, start close to the end, tell your own story) that make stories land.

## Key ideas

1. **Every story is one five-second moment** — A transformation or realization; 98% of the story is context to bring that moment into clarity. Start at the end — the opposite of that moment defines your beginning.
2. **Stakes as attention machinery** — Audiences listen only while they're wondering what's next. Use elephants (opening hook), backpacks (tell them the plan), breadcrumbs, hourglasses (slow time at climax), crystal balls (predict a future) to keep them wondering.
3. **The dinner test** — A story told on stage should resemble, slightly elevated, a story told at dinner. No unattributed dialogue, no "bang the door opened" openings.
4. **It must be your story** — Telling someone else's story requires no vulnerability, which is what audiences connect to. Frame third-party events through your own transformation.
5. **Without story, you're forgettable** — Pie-charts and platitudes aren't retained; the lunchbox story beats the fluent executive. The risk of not telling stories is being forgotten.
