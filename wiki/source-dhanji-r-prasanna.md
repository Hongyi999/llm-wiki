## Executive summary

Dhanji Prasanna, CTO of Block, led the company's transformation into an AI-native large organization by writing an internal manifesto arguing Block must centralize its technology approach. He moved Block from a divisional GM structure to a functional org, reinstated hack weeks, and deployed Goose, an internal open-source AI agent that saves engineers 8-10 hours per week. Non-technical employees benefited most from AI agents, not engineers. A key counterintuitive insight: code quality has nothing to do with product success; instead, organizational structure (Conway's Law) and clear ownership determine shipping velocity. Block is a case study in how large enterprises rapidly adopt AI through structural alignment and internal tooling.

## Key ideas

1. AI agents save 8-10 hours/week for engineers and climbing: Goose is already producing pull requests based on Slack discussions; baseline productivity gains only increase as models improve.
2. Non-technical employees see highest AI productivity gains: Product, ops, and support teams see transformational gains because they lack traditional technical training.
3. Organizational structure matters more than individual talent: Moving from divisional GMs to functional organization enabled unified technology focus and faster AI implementation.
4. Code quality is uncorrelated with product success: Shipping velocity, feature delivery, and user impact drive success — over-optimization for code quality slows iteration.
5. Centralized AI strategy is table-stakes for enterprises: Block's speed came from top-down commitment, a company-wide manifesto, and organizational restructuring.
