# Building better roadmaps | Janna Bastow (Mind the Product, ProdPad)

**Guest**: Janna Bastow  
**Published**: 2022-10-16  
**Duration**: 53:40  
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W3cvqPCGcck](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W3cvqPCGcck)

## Executive summary

Janna Bastow, co-founder of Mind the Product and CEO of ProdPad, makes a case against timeline-based roadmaps and introduces the Now, Next, Later framework she invented as an alternative. Her core argument is that a roadmap should be a prototype for your strategy — not a delivery promise — because no one actually delivers roadmaps on time, and false precision with dates causes organizational dysfunction. She draws a powerful analogy to sales teams, who are never asked to predict exactly which deals will close, only to run a process that reliably produces results. The episode also covers community building lessons from growing Mind the Product to 300,000+ members, practical public speaking techniques, how retrospectives and psychological safety underpin great product teams, and advice for PMs transitioning to founders.

## Key ideas

1. **Roadmap as Strategy Prototype**: A roadmap's value is not in the document itself but in the roadmapping process — laying out assumptions about problems to solve, sharing them with stakeholders and customers, and iterating based on feedback. It is a prototype for your strategy, not a delivery plan.

2. **Now, Next, Later Framework**: Replace timeline-based roadmaps with three buckets: Now (what we are building), Next (what we plan to tackle soon), and Later (what we are considering). This embraces the cone of uncertainty — items get less detailed and less committed the further out they are — without abandoning structure or accountability.

3. **Soft Launch / Hard Launch Separation**: Decouple development completion (soft launch) from marketing launch (hard launch). Developers ship when ready; marketing then takes the working product, gets testimonials, creates videos, and plans the public launch on their own timeline. This eliminates the stress of coordinating two fundamentally different types of projects.

4. **Psychological Safety as Foundation**: The best product teams practice retrospectives, which are both a symptom and a driver of psychological safety. When teams can question what's working and what isn't, they naturally move toward better practices like discovery and outcome-oriented roadmapping.

5. **The Sales Team Analogy for Product Accountability**: Product teams should be held to the same standard as sales teams — accountable for running experiments and moving metrics, not for predicting in advance which specific initiatives will succeed. Sales teams never promise which exact deals will close; product teams shouldn't promise which exact features will ship on which dates.

## Concepts covered

- **[[shape-up|Now, Next, Later Roadmap]]** — A three-bucket roadmapping framework that replaces timeline Gantt charts, embracing the cone of uncertainty and focusing on problems rather than delivery dates.
- **Roadmap as Strategy Prototype** — The principle that a roadmap's value lies in the iterative process of laying out and testing strategic assumptions, not in the final document.
- **Soft Launch vs. Hard Launch** — Decoupling development completion from marketing launch so that each team works on its own timeline without creating artificial coordination pressure.
- **Cone of Uncertainty** — The principle that estimates become less reliable the further into the future they project — roadmaps should reflect this by being less specific for later items.
- **Culture as Calcification** — Janna's metaphor for organizational culture: built up over time like limestone deposits, it can only be changed by chipping away at it in small pockets rather than all at once.
