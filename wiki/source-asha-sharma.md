## Executive summary

Asha Sharma, VP of AI Product at Microsoft overseeing 80,000 companies building on their platform, articulates a fundamental shift from products as static artifacts to products as living organisms that improve continuously through user interactions. She unpacks the 'agentic society' thesis where marginal costs approach zero, org charts become work charts, and human skills multiply through personal AI stacks. Real examples: Dragon medical AI jumped from 30-60% to 83% acceptance rates through fine-tuning on 600K annotated physician interactions; Microsoft deployed 15,000+ customer agents (millions of instances) already on Azure.

## Key ideas

1. Post-training is now the economic focus—once models hit 30B parameters, further pre-training doesn't make financial sense; winning happens through continuous user-data feedback loops and fine-tuned outputs.
2. The shift from product artifact to organism: products that think and live and learn, tuned to specific outcomes (price, performance, quality) and improved through observability, evals, and metabolic data ingestion.
3. Seasons, not semesters: organizations must align on macro secular shifts (Season 1=prototyping, Season 2=reasoning models, Season 3=agents) rather than rigid 6-month plans, then layer loose quarterly OKRs and 4-6 week squad goals.
4. Code-native interfaces and composability replace GUIs: LLMs compose better with text streams than pixels; databases went SQL, cloud went Terraform, AI goes terminals and agents reading text, not drawing on canvas.
5. Full-stack builders and polymath roles emerge because 10 execution steps × 500+ weekly models/tools = insufficient linear organization; functions blur; the loop and observability, not the lane, become the IP.

- [[post-training-data-moat|Post-Training Economics]] — After 30B parameters, pre-training ROI collapses; competitive edge shifts to user data loops, synthetic data generation, and continuous fine-tuning.
