# How embracing your emotions will accelerate your career | Joe Hudson (Art of Accomplishment)

**Guest**: Joe Hudson
**Published**: 2024-08-08
**Duration**: 1:18:47
**YouTube**: [Lenny's Podcast — Joe Hudson](https://www.lennyspodcast.com/)

## Executive summary

Joe Hudson — executive coach to OpenAI, SpaceX, and Apple leaders, founder of Art of Accomplishment — argues that the two deepest blockers to success and enjoyment at work are (1) a critical, repetitive inner voice and (2) a kinked, disowned relationship with emotions. He reframes personal development as small daily experiments rather than self-discipline: respond to the inner voice in new ways each day, "welcome" emotions like children entering your home, treat enjoyment as the highest-leverage productivity tool. His signature move is the "fall in love with the feeling you're avoiding" reversal — whatever you're trying not to feel is the exact emotion you're inviting in.

## Key ideas

1. **The critical voice is always wrong**: The repeating critical voice inside you is not logical or useful — it's a tantruming child. Instead of silencing it, run daily experiments with new responses ("I see you're scared, I got you").
2. **Emotional fluidity / the kinked hose**: Emotions are like water in a hose; when kinked (anger becomes contempt, guilt, passive aggression) they come out sideways. Unkinking yields love-grounded expression, not loss of control.
3. **What you resist persists**: Whatever emotion you're trying to avoid — shame, abandonment, conflict — is the exact one you invite into your life in the form you most fear. The conflict-avoidant CEO creates the most tense org.
4. **Enjoyment as efficiency**: Enjoyment is not a reward, it's the highest-leverage productivity lever. Ask "how can I enjoy this 10% more right now?" — it increases quality, stamina, and contagion in teams.
5. **VIEW decision-making & 7-minute gratitude**: Good decisions come from Vulnerability, Impartiality, Empathy, Wonder. A daily 7-minute felt-sense gratitude practice (especially on areas of lack) is his highest-ROI single habit.
