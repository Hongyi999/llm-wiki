# Hard-won lessons building 0 to 1 inside Atlassian | Tanguy Crusson (Head of Jira Product Discovery)

**Guest**: Tanguy Crusson  
**Published**: 2024-06-16  
**Duration**: 1:54:03  
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cZqpqb5qR5A](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cZqpqb5qR5A)

## Executive summary

Tanguy Crusson, a 10-year veteran of Atlassian's product management team, provides a deeply candid account of building zero-to-one products inside a large company. He walks through three major efforts — HipChat (failed to Slack), Statuspage (painful acquisition integration), and Jira Product Discovery (breakout success) — extracting hard-won lessons from each. The core thesis is that big companies must artificially create startup-like scarcity and autonomy for new bets to succeed, which Atlassian eventually institutionalized through its Point A incubator program. Tanguy shares tactical frameworks including the Lighthouse Users Program (10 → 100 → 1000 customer progression), the Safety Funnel for limiting blast radius, and the incubate-iterate-integrate model for building within an existing platform.

## Key ideas

1. **Create artificial scarcity in big companies**: Startups benefit from starving; big companies must intentionally constrain resources for new bets. Tanguy positioned JPD as 'most likely to fail' to keep the org from over-investing and imposing heavyweight processes on a seven-person team.

2. **Break rules without breaking trust**: Tanguy spent accumulated social capital ('chips') to bypass engineering norms — no engineering manager, contractors instead of full-time hires, operating from France instead of Sydney — all to maintain startup-like speed while keeping leadership trust.

3. **Lighthouse Users Program (10 → 100 → 1000)**: A staged customer development approach where qualitative depth with 10 named customers precedes broader distribution, preventing premature exposure that could create unfixable churn and setting clear metrics expectations at each stage.

4. **Competitive myopia is fatal**: HipChat's team reacted to Slack feature-for-feature instead of doubling down on their own users' needs. Tanguy now recommends checking competitors quarterly at most and re-anchoring on user research videos whenever the team gets distracted by competitor announcements.

5. **Communicate velocity to protect the ugly baby**: Weekly bite-sized updates combining product demos, customer video snippets, and data created a perception of a high-speed train that nobody wanted to get in front of, buying the time needed for the product to mature through its fragile early stages.

## Concepts covered

- **Safety Funnel** — An inverted growth funnel that limits the number of users exposed to an early product to prevent unfixable churn from premature distribution.
- **Lighthouse Users Program** — A staged customer development approach (10 → 100 → 1000) where deep qualitative relationships with named users precede broader distribution.
- **Incubate-Iterate-Integrate** — A model for innovating within an existing platform by first building separately, iterating with users, then merging back into the core product.
- **Competitive Myopia** — The trap of reacting feature-for-feature to competitors instead of staying grounded in your own users' needs and research.
- **Why Now Articulation** — The critical skill of framing the urgency and perishability of an opportunity when pitching a new initiative inside a large organization.
