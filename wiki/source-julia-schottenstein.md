# Source: M&A, competition, pricing, and investing | Julia Schottenstein (dbt Labs)

**Guest**: Julia Schottenstein (Product Lead at dbt Labs; active angel investor in data/infra)
**Published**: 2023-07-13
**Duration**: ~1h 01m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y9hmrMBRPDI

## Executive summary

Practitioner-level treatment of three under-discussed PM topics: (1) how to think about M&A from the product side (acqui-hire vs. product tuck-in vs. strategic expansion — each demands a different integration playbook), (2) how to compete when a larger player copies your feature (don't panic, compete on pace + depth), and (3) pricing as product strategy (usage-based vs. seat-based, when to change, how to test). Also covers angel investing frameworks for PMs evaluating opportunities.

## Key ideas

1. **Three M&A archetypes** — Acqui-hire (team only, sunset product), product tuck-in (absorb product into existing surface area), strategic expansion (new category via acquisition). Each has a different integration speed and autonomy spectrum.
2. **Competing against a copy** — When a bigger player ships your feature, compete on depth and iteration speed. They're spreading resources across 100 features; you have 100% focus. Customers know the difference between a checkbox feature and a real product.
3. **Pricing changes are product changes** — Treat pricing changes with the same rigor as product launches: customer interviews, A/B tests on willingness to pay, rollout plan with grandfather clauses. Usage-based pricing aligns incentives but adds unpredictability.
4. **Angel investing as PM training** — Evaluating startups sharpens the same muscles PMs use: market sizing, competitive positioning, user empathy, founder-market fit assessment. "Investing makes you a better PM."
5. **dbt Labs' open-source-to-enterprise arc** — Community-first adoption via open-source dbt Core → commercial Cloud product → enterprise tier. The open-source community is the moat; pricing must never alienate it.
