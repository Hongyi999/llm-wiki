# Source: Seth Godin's best tactics for building remarkable products, strategies, brands | Seth Godin

**Guest**: Seth Godin (marketing legend; ex-PM at Spinnaker Software; author of *Purple Cow*, *Permission Marketing*, *This Is Strategy*; daily blogger 10,000+ posts)
**Published**: 2024-12-08
**Duration**: ~45m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UyWTDmDjNy4

## Executive summary

In an AI-saturated world, great strategy has *tension* (not stress) at its core. The four decisions that shape a product and a life are: the customers you pick, the competition you pick, the source of validation you choose, and the distribution channel. Brand is a promise you make and keep; AI will stop being a feature the way electricity stopped being a feature.

## Key ideas

1. **Brand = promise, not logo** — "Hyatt has a logo, Nike has a brand. If Nike opened a hotel, we'd know what it would be like." Airlines don't have brands because the only reason people stick is points (bribery). Loyalty = would you pay extra?
2. **Four strategic choices that lock in your future** — Pick customers (smallest viable audience), pick competition (don't fight Walmart), pick validation (match 400 users' taste, not your boss's), pick distribution. "If you're glossing over them it's because you're afraid."
3. **Tension ≠ stress** — Tension is the possibility the promise might be true; stress is wanting to leave but needing to stay. Every great innovation and art form runs on tension.
4. **Purple Cow / worth-making-a-remark-about** — Ship something where the person's life gets better *if they tell their friends*, and you know in advance what you want them to say. Google's two-button homepage vs. Yahoo's 183 links was a promise designed to raise the tech-status of the person recommending it.
5. **Better waves make better surfers + see the system** — Great surfers pass up mediocre waves. Systems invent culture ("the way things are around here") to protect themselves; strategic thinking is naming the system. Jaguar didn't rebrand, it re-logo'd; Cybertruck is divisiveness-as-stunt and divisiveness doesn't sell trucks.

## Notable quote

> "AI very soon is going to stop being a feature the same way electricity is not a feature. What AI companies and all companies need to do is say, what's in this for the user? What promise do I want to make — a difficult promise, a remarkable promise — and then how do I keep it?"

## B8 angle

Canonical Godin brick for the B-series. Maps onto [[source-arielle-jackson|April Dunford's]] "position against alternatives" (pick your competition) and Raskin's "name the old game" (see the system). Four choices are a more opinionated version of Dunford's positioning worksheet. Tension/promise supplies the *emotional engine* for Raskin-style narrative. Converges with B7 earned-channel/WOM voices: Purple Cow is the product-level mechanism that makes WOM work.
