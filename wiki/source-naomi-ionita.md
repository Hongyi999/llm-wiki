# Source: How to Price Your Product ft. Naomi Ionita

**Guest**: Naomi Ionita (Partner, Menlo Ventures)
**Published**: 2023-01-12
**Duration**: ~53m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xvQadImf568

## Executive summary

Naomi Ionita draws on her experience at Evernote and Invoice2go to lay out a practical pricing playbook for B2B/SaaS startups. The core thesis is that monetization is a massively underappreciated growth lever — a 1% improvement in monetization has 4X the bottom-line impact of a 1% improvement in acquisition — yet most founders wait too long, underprice, and then never revisit their pricing. She also introduces the concept of the "Modern Growth Stack," a new wave of purpose-built tools that replace the internal infrastructure growth teams historically had to build themselves.

## Key ideas

1. **Three common pricing mistakes** — (1) Waiting too long to monetize, which cheapens the product and deprives you of feedback loops. (2) Underpricing, especially by offering only a single tier instead of segmenting by willingness to pay. (3) Setting price and forgetting it — pricing should be revisited every 6-12 months alongside the product roadmap.

2. **Match price to value** — Pick a value metric (API calls, messages, storage, invoices) that creates a natural revenue escalator as usage grows. This aligns incentives with the customer and lets you target different segments. The shift from pure seat-based to usage-based (or hybrid) pricing is a major SaaS trend.

3. **Day 1 vs. Day 100 features** — Free/starter features should get users to the aha moment and build habit formation. Premium features that require scale or advanced workflows belong in higher tiers. At Invoice2go this framework doubled the upgrade rate while also raising the price of the pro plan by ~30%.

4. **Van Westendorp pricing method** — Survey users with four price-sensitivity questions (too cheap, right price, expensive-but-would-pay, prohibitively expensive). Plot the four curves to find the acceptable price range. Pair this with feature prioritization (must-have / nice-to-have / not necessary) to design packages.

5. **Single-player to multiplayer chasm** — Evernote's failure to bridge from personal tool to collaborative/enterprise product capped its growth. Companies that make this transition well see compounding benefits: acquisition rises through shared workflows, retention increases through team stickiness, and monetization scales with organizational adoption. Figma is the canonical success story.

6. **The Modern Growth Stack** — Analogous to the "Modern Data Stack" (ELT + warehouse + transformation + visualization), the Modern Growth Stack is a layer of purpose-built tools that sit on top of data infrastructure and power growth workflows: product-led sales (Endgame, Pocus), experimentation (Eppo, Amplitude), and billing/monetization infrastructure (Metronome, Orb). These tools drive hard ROI through both cost reduction and revenue generation.

7. **Experiment with pricing** — PLG companies with public pricing pages should A/B test value metrics, quota limits, price points, and promotions. Segment tests by geography (e.g., Canada or Australia before the US) to contain risk. Watch for long-term effects on churn — a year-one discount requires understanding year-two retention.

## Concepts that deserve their own wiki page

- **[[Pricing strategy]]** — The discipline of setting, packaging, and iterating on price to maximize value capture; includes Van Westendorp, value-metric selection, and good-better-best tiering
- **[[Modern Growth Stack]]** — Naomi's thesis for a new category of cloud-native tools that power product growth, monetization, and go-to-market workflows on top of the modern data stack
- **[[Product-led sales]]** — Layering a sales motion on top of PLG by using product usage data to identify upgrade-ready accounts
- **[[Single-player to multiplayer]]** — The critical product evolution from individual use to collaborative/team/enterprise adoption that unlocks compounding growth
