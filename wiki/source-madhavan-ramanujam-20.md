# Source: The art and science of pricing | Madhavan Ramanujam (Monetizing Innovation, Simon-Kucher)

**Guest**: [[source-madhavan-ramanujam|Madhavan Ramanujam]] (Managing Partner at Simon-Kucher; author of *Monetizing Innovation* and *Scaling Innovation*; 250+ companies, 30+ unicorns) — earlier Lenny appearance
**Published**: 2022-12-08
**Duration**: ~1h 39m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A6veeCbKIzw

## Executive summary

Comprehensive pricing and monetization strategy framework. Core thesis: founders must master both market share (customer acquisition) and wallet share (revenue per customer) simultaneously — single-engine strategies fail. Nine strategies across startup and scale-up phases, with special emphasis on B2B negotiation (every concession you give must be traded for something), value selling (create needs, don't just discover them), and co-created ROI models. AI companies must establish strong monetization from day one or permanently anchor customers at low prices.

## Key ideas

1. **Dual-engine growth** — Market share (acquiring customers) and wallet share (expanding revenue per customer) need equal attention. Disruptors over-discount; Moneymakers over-monetize; Community Builders give too much away. Balance both engines.
2. **Beautifully simple pricing** — Test by asking early prospects to articulate your pricing. If they can't explain it simply, it's too complex. Pricing must tell a value story.
3. **Gives and gets in negotiation** — Every concession must be traded for something valuable: value audits, commitments, references, multi-year deals. Giving without receiving signals weakness.
4. **Value selling: create needs, don't discover them** — Instead of "what problems do you have?" ask provocative questions that help customers recognize problems they didn't know existed. Create affirmation loops throughout demos.
5. **Co-create ROI models with customers** — Build business cases together from day one, aligning on assumptions and inputs. Customers won't push back on outputs if they've agreed on inputs. POCs should build business cases, not just test functionality.
6. **Show options, not single price points** — Good/better/best tiers reframe negotiations around value rather than price and give customers agency. Outcome-based pricing alternatives work for AI.
7. **AI pricing trap** — Discounting heavily early to gain adoption, then being unable to raise prices. Winners master outcome-based pricing and capture willingness-to-pay from day one.
