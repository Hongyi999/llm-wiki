# Growth Loops

A growth framework from Reforge (Brian Balfour, [[source-casey-winters|Casey Winters]], Andrew Chen, [[source-fareed-mosavat|Fareed Mosavat]]) that replaces the linear funnel with **compounding loops**: a user action at the end feeds back into the start, generating further acquisition/activation without linear spend.

## Why loops beat funnels

- **Funnels are depleting** — Every stage leaks users. Acquisition is a one-way flow of spend → users.
- **Loops compound** — Each user output (invite sent, content created, referral made) re-enters as an input to the next cohort.
- **Loops are usually earned** — [[source-elena-verna|Verna's]] thesis: virality, UGC, word-of-mouth, sharing loops are yours forever; organic/paid search and social make Google/Meta rich.

## Canonical examples

- **[[dropbox]] sharing loop** — Still drives 50%+ of acquisition after 17 years. User shares folder → recipient signs up → invites their own recipients.
- **Snyk GitHub fix-PR loop** — Scans repo → creates Snyk-branded PR → other devs in repo see it → sign up → connect their repos.
- **Miroverse UGC library** — Takes ~18 months to measure but becomes a major acquisition engine.
- **Instagram celebrity partnerships + SEO** ([[source-bangaly-kaba|Kaba]]): celebrity partnerships seed news articles → Instagram embeds → SEO compounding.

## Types of loops (Ben Williams / Snyk taxonomy)

- **Company-generated, company-distributed** — Programmatic SEO (Snyk Advisor, Canva templates, Zillow listings).
- **Company-generated, user-distributed** — Shareable artifacts (Calendly links, Superhuman signatures).
- **User-generated, company-distributed** — UGC + algorithmic distribution (Pinterest pins, TikTok videos).
- **User-generated, user-distributed** — Pure word-of-mouth and invites.

## Tipping loops ([[source-sarah-tavel|Sarah Tavel]])

Beyond growth loops: **happiness loops** act like kidneys, churning out bad suppliers via search ranking + reputation. Both must run in parallel on marketplaces.

## Law of Shitty Clickthroughs

Andrew Chen's principle: any single loop decays over time as it over-optimizes. [[source-elena-verna|Elena Verna]] operationalizes: overlay new growth models every 18 months, new channels every ~5 years. Budget 20-25% of annual growth-team time to exploratory loops that won't have metrics for 12-18 months.
