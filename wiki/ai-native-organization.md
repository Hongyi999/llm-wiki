# AI-Native Organization

An organization whose structure, culture, and tooling are designed around AI as a core teammate — not bolted on as a layer. In B1, 7+ guests describe variations of this pattern from their own companies, converging on a consistent set of design choices.

## Canonical cases

- **[[linkedin]]** — [[source-tomer-cohen|Tomer Cohen]] reoriented product teams toward AI as "the ultimate matchmaker" starting in 2016. Launched the **Full Stack Builder** program to train PMs on algorithm objectives, data strategy, and fine-tuning — not just UI specs.
- **[[block]]** — [[source-dhanji-r-prasanna|Dhanji R. Prasanna]] restructured 3,500 engineers from divisional GMs to a functional org and deployed **Goose**, an internal AI agent that saves engineers 8-10 hours/week.
- **[[source-julie-zhuo|Julie Zhuo]]** (ex-Facebook Design VP) — reframes management as **orchestrating humans + AI as one team**, with goal clarity more important than role boundaries.
- **[[source-dan-shipper]]** (Every, 15-person AI-native team) — pioneered the **Head of AI Ops** role, ships 5 products with Claude Code as primary interface. Founder comfort with experimentation is the #1 predictor of who can build this way.
- **[[source-howie-liu]]** ([[figma|Airtable-style]]) — "product refounding" via fast/slow team splits; the CEO must code daily ("IC CEO") to stay calibrated.
- **[[source-eoghan-mccabe]]** ([[source-eoghan-mccabe|Intercom]]) — bet the company on **Fin** agent, reset 40% of workforce, founder-mode decisiveness.
- **[[source-asha-sharma]]** ([[meta|Microsoft AI]]) — "**composability over canvas**" and "agentic society" reshape org charts.

## Convergent design choices

1. **Functional over divisional.** When AI is the product, [[block|Block]]-style functional org beats divisional GM structure. [[source-dhanji-r-prasanna|Dhanji]] invokes Conway's Law: structure determines architecture.
2. **AI as teammate, not tool.** [[source-julie-zhuo|Julie Zhuo]]: the manager's job is to orchestrate a team where some members are AI agents. Role boundaries between "designer," "engineer," "PM" blur into "builders."
3. **Fast-slow team split.** [[source-howie-liu|Howie Liu]]: separate teams for shipping velocity vs. deep refactors, otherwise the fast loop pollutes the slow one.
4. **Clarity over correctness.** [[source-tomer-cohen|Tomer Cohen]]: "we might be wrong but we are not confused" — decisiveness under uncertainty beats perfect analysis.
5. **Head of AI Ops as a new role.** [[source-dan-shipper|Dan Shipper]] created this role at Every to own internal AI tooling + training across departments.
6. **Founder-mode decisiveness.** [[source-eoghan-mccabe|Eoghan McCabe]]: in the AI transition, gradual change kills you; visible top-down commitment is required. Linked to **AI bet or death**.
7. **Non-technical workers benefit most.** [[source-dhanji-r-prasanna|Dhanji]]: product, ops, support see the largest productivity gains from AI agents — not engineers.
8. **Management is AI orchestration.** [[source-julie-zhuo|Julie Zhuo]]: the skill is breaking work into chunks an AI teammate can execute, not micromanaging humans.

## Anti-patterns

- **Grassroots-only AI adoption** — [[source-dhanji-r-prasanna|Dhanji]]: it doesn't scale past a few teams without top-down restructure.
- **Treating AI as a feature** — [[source-tomer-cohen|Tomer]]: "bolting AI on" fails; you must return to first principles about the objective.
- **Optimizing for code quality** — [[source-dhanji-r-prasanna|Dhanji]]: "code quality has nothing to do with product success."
- **Slow shipping cadence** — [[source-nick-turley|Nick Turley]]: [[chatgpt|ChatGPT]] wouldn't exist if the team hadn't shipped daily from day one.

## Open questions

- How do you restructure a 10,000-person org without triggering attrition? (Dhanji: you just do it and accept the losses.)
- What's the right manager-to-agent ratio? (No one has hard data yet.)
- Do AI-native orgs produce higher-quality products or just faster iteration? (The B1 guests assume both; critics point to rising technical debt.)
