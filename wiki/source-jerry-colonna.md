# How have I been complicit in creating the conditions I say I don't want? | Jerry Colonna

**Guest**: Jerry Colonna
**Published**: 2025-05-08
**Duration**: 1:22:50
**YouTube**: [Lenny's Podcast — Jerry Colonna](https://www.lennyspodcast.com/)

## Executive summary

Jerry Colonna — co-founder/CEO of Reboot, former Flatiron Partners co-founder with Fred Wilson, ex-JPMorgan Chase — argues that leadership failure is almost never a skills problem; it's an inner-work problem. His operating equation: *practical skills + radical self-inquiry + shared experiences = enhanced leadership + greater resilience*. His signature question: **"How have I been complicit in creating the conditions I say I don't want?"** The thesis: high-functioning teams are a downstream output of the most powerful person in the room doing their own psychological work — otherwise the group unconsciously reenacts the leader's unresolved family-of-origin patterns.

## Key ideas

1. **Complicity, not responsibility**: The complicity question evokes agency without blame — you may not be robbing the bank, but you might be driving the getaway car. Radical self-inquiry cuts through the self-delusions that let leaders reproduce the conditions they claim to hate (busyness, dysfunction, burnout).
2. **The leadership equation**: Practical skills alone produce technicians. Layering on *radical self-inquiry* (scary questions like "What am I not saying that I need to say?") plus *shared experiences* (peer circles that don't fix or judge) produces both greater leadership and the resilience needed to sustain it.
3. **Unsorted baggage gets paid in tears**: Borrowing from Bruce Springsteen, every leader has unprocessed childhood material that will exact a price — via self-sabotaged companies, depression, or teams that unconsciously repeat family patterns (e.g., a team that jokes away every hard conversation).
4. **Attachment fuels suffering, including at work**: When identity fuses with outcome ("See, I'm a somebody"), every metric dip feels existential. Unconditional positive regard for self, not anxiety-driven ambition, is the more durable motivator.
5. **Power carries moral responsibility for self-inquiry**: The more power you have, the greater the obligation to examine your complicity. A CEO complaining no one makes decisions without her must ask: who hired them, and how does she react when they decide against her?

## B5 angle

For hiring/managing/leading, Colonna's bedrock insight is that team dysfunction is a mirror of the leader's unexamined self — interventions like better process, strategy, or hires will fail unless the person with most power does their inner work first. Essential context for any leader diagnosing "team problems," and a counterweight to [[source-tobi-lutke]]'s heat-injection leadership and [[source-ben-horowitz]]'s confidence-restoration school.
