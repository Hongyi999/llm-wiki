# Lyft

US rideshare marketplace. In this wiki, Lyft is a case study in [[marketplace-liquidity|marketplace liquidity]] and hard-side supply management, via [[benjamin-lauzier|Benjamin Lauzier]]'s time on the marketplace team.

## Marketplace lessons from Lyft

- **Hard side = drivers.** Most early strategy aimed at getting and keeping drivers.
- **Market Health Metric**: ETA was the magical-feeling signal. Below ~2 minutes at density, the experience felt reliable; above that, riders churned.
- **Rental-car partnership with GM** turned Lyft into the 4th-largest rental fleet in the US — a structural supply unlock for drivers without their own cars.
- **Mentor/ambassador program**: paid existing drivers $35/session + $20/recruit to onboard new drivers. Peer-to-peer liquidity bootstrapping.
- **Post-COVID divergence from Uber**: Lyft's pure-rides focus was more fragile than Uber's diversified platform (rides + Eats). Concentration as risk.
