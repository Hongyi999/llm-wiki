# Source: Are your fears giving you terrible advice? | Matt Mochary

**Guest**: Matt Mochary (CEO coach to Naval Ravikant, Sam Altman, Brian Armstrong, Henrique Dubugras, Pedro Franceschi, and many others)
**Published**: 2023-01-17
**Duration**: ~7:00 (short clip; parent episode ~1h 11m)
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qOrwVtZvdaE

## Executive summary

Mochary distills ten years of coaching into a set of repeatable scripts. Throughline: fear and anger give bad advice, and the highest-leverage skills for any leader are (1) letting people go humanely, (2) making people feel genuinely heard, (3) structuring work so the whole company runs on simple, documented methodologies instead of the CEO's charisma.

## Key ideas

1. **Fear gives bad advice** — "When someone is in fear, they're gripped. They can't see reality." Fix: have a trusted outsider name it ("I perceive you to be in fear") and do the opposite of what fear counsels. "I've made this bet hundreds of times and so far I've never lost."
2. **Firing as agency, not abandonment** — Separate decision (always in service of the customer) from implementation. Become the person's agent "like Michael Ovitz"; actively place them in their next job. "Everyone I have let go is still my friend."
3. **The difficult-conversation script** — Warn ("this is going to be a difficult conversation, take a few seconds to prepare"), deliver the news, then actively invite and reflect emotions. Surprise is what triggers the amygdala; pre-warning reduces trauma dramatically.
4. **Mass layoffs humanely, cut deep once** — Give managers a dollar target (not a headcount target), let each manager choose, do all one-on-ones in a single morning, same-day all-hands, then follow-up 1:1 with every stay-teamer. "Cut deep so that we only cut once." Within 60 days companies perform better in absolute terms.
5. **Innovate inside big companies via independent C-corps** — Spin new products into a separate C corp with founder-mentality leads (recruit from YC-alumni-whose-startups-failed pool), report directly to CEO, don't touch core codebase's review process.

## Notable quote

> "The biggest marker that I've seen between a botched layoff and a successful layoff is at the moment someone hears that they no longer have a job, did they hear it from their manager in a one-on-one?"

Uniquely contributes (a) "fear gives bad advice" frame, (b) firing-as-agent reframe, (c) dollar-denominated layoff protocol absent from earlier canonical episodes.
