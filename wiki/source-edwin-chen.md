## Executive summary

Edwin Chen, founder and CEO of Surge AI, achieved the unprecedented: $1B in revenue in under 4 years with fewer than 100 employees, 100% bootstrapped, zero VC. Surge builds the data that trains frontier AI models at OpenAI, Anthropic, Google. The thesis is radical: most 'data labeling' is overhyped labeling cats and drawing bounding boxes; real value is teaching AI models what's good vs bad through complex, value-aligned data curation. Edwin's worry: labs are optimizing for engagement and 'AI slop' instead of advancing humanity. Surge positions itself to influence objective functions at frontier labs—choosing whether models optimize for user time-efficiency (stop wasting time) vs endless iteration (suck up engagement like social media). This philosophical approach to data quality is why Surge has become indispensable to frontier labs.

## Key ideas

1. You are your objective function. Training data reflects what you value; if you optimize for clicks and engagement, you get addictive slop. If you optimize for productivity and truth, you get different behaviors. Frontier labs' values shape their models—this is why Surge exists to help them articulate their north star.
2. Data quality is not data labeling; it's 'raising AI as a child.' You teach values, creativity, what's beautiful, subtle things about what makes a good model. Traditional data labeling (cat photos, bounding boxes) is vestigial; real frontier work requires deep understanding of model behavior and desired outputs.
3. Model differentiation will come from values and objective functions, not just capability. Claude and ChatGPT and Gemini will behave differently because Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google have different principles—this is what Edwin noticed over the past year as models matured.
4. Edwin's warning: instead of building AI to cure cancer and solve poverty, we're optimizing for AI slop and dopamine-chasing—like building models for people who read tabloids at grocery stores. Data companies should resist this and push toward meaningful objective functions.
5. Surge's bootstrapped approach proves you don't need VC hype, constant tweets, or quarterly metrics. Build something so good it cuts through noise. This unlocked Edwin to stay in the weeds with data and research instead of becoming a traditional CEO.
