# Product Operating Model

The product operating model is a framework for how companies organize to build products, encompassing strategy (which problems to solve), discovery (how to solve them), and delivery (how to build and ship). The term is most associated with [[source-marty-cagan|Marty Cagan]] and SVPG, who codified it as 20 principles shared by the best product companies, but the underlying ideas appear across five B3 sources under different names.

## Core thesis: empowered teams vs. feature teams

The deepest fault line in B3's product management discourse is Cagan's distinction between **feature teams** and **empowered product teams**:

- **Feature teams** are given a roadmap of output (features to ship). The PM's role is project management — facilitating, herding, managing backlogs. Cagan calls this "product management theater."
- **Empowered product teams** are given problems to solve and measured on outcomes (value delivered, viability risk navigated). The PM must have deep customer knowledge, business acumen, and technical fluency.

Cagan argues that the majority of people carrying PM titles are on feature teams, are dramatically overpaid for the value they provide, and face a reckoning from hiring corrections and GenAI automation of backlog administration.

## The Build Trap

[[source-melissa-perri|Melissa Perri]]'s complementary framing is "the build trap" — organizations that measure output (features shipped) rather than outcomes (problems solved). Her transformation playbook (baseline → train → sort) was demonstrated at Athenahealth with 365 product owners, where the first step was discovering that many "product owners" were actually project managers. This aligns precisely with Cagan's feature-team/empowered-team distinction.

## Product operations

[[source-brian-tolkin|Brian Tolkin]] and [[source-melissa-perri-denise-tilles|Melissa Perri & Denise Tilles]] extend the operating model to include **product operations** — a function that sits between product, engineering, data, and customers to enable informed decision-making at scale. Tolkin's "twin turbine" model at [[opendoor|Opendoor]] shows product-ops as the connective tissue between technology-building and real-world operations.

## Relationship to adjacent concepts

- **[[product-market-fit]]**: The product operating model determines *how* you search for PMF. Empowered teams find it faster because they own the problem, not just the backlog.
- **[[jobs-to-be-done]]**: JTBD provides the customer understanding that empowered PMs need. Cagan cites Teresa Torres's Continuous Discovery Habits (rooted in JTBD) as one of the few good PM resources.
- **[[ai-native-organization]]**: AI-native orgs from B1 (LinkedIn, Block, Airtable) restructured around AI as a capability; the product operating model restructures around empowered teams as a capability. Different catalysts, same pattern.
