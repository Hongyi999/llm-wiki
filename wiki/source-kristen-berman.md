# Source: Using behavioral science to improve your product | Kristen Berman (Irrational Labs)

**Guest**: Kristen Berman (CEO/co-founder of Irrational Labs; previously embedded in Google for three years starting their behavioral-economics team)
**Published**: 2022-10-02
**Duration**: ~56m 15s
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u53fplD_C30

## Executive summary

Berman makes the case that product teams over-trust what users *say* and under-study what users *do*. Her three-B framework — Behavior, Barriers, Benefits — plus behavioral-diagnosis journey-mapping has produced measurable wins at TikTok (-24% misinformation shares), One Medical (+20% bookings), Credit Karma (+18% recurring deposits).

## Key ideas

1. **Humans are predictably irrational** — Behavioral economics uses predictable patterns (present bias, status-quo bias, social norms) to *design* behavior change. Goals alone don't change behavior — environment does.
2. **The three Bs** — (1) **Behavior**: pick one uncomfortably specific action ("within 7 days of starting the app, they do two 10-min workouts with two different instructors"). (2) **Barriers**: logistical (form fields) + cognitive (uncertainty aversion, status-quo). (3) **Benefits**: make them *immediate* — we are all present-biased.
3. **Skepticism of requested features** — Most-requested feature in a popular FinTech was budgeting; 10k-user experiment showed zero impact on spend. Real answer is defaults (401(k)) or rules of thumb.
4. **Friction can *increase* conversion when it adds engagement** — Asking questions inserts an idea into the user's head (Apartment List's preference quiz; Trunk Club's +133% conversion). Multiple-choice only — open text kills.
5. **Slow people down to do less of something** — TikTok misinformation: pre-share "Are you sure?" friction + "unverified" label cut shares 24%. Elevator-study analogue: 16-second door delay pushed people to stairs.
6. **Right for Wrong** — Get people to do the right thing for the wrong reason (pizza at polling lines; donuts for vaccines; Peloton instructor shout-outs). Not manipulation if the end is aligned — but check the incentive structure.
7. **Incentives over ethics lectures** — Berman's own LendingClub engagement: paid only on hitting a 5-pt conversion lift, she became a "predatory lender" her legal team had to restrain. Set incentives on behavior aligned with customer outcome; extend their duration.
8. **One Medical onboarding redesign** — Ask health questions, *recommend* one provider + small set of tomorrow's virtual appointments instead of showing all options. +20% bookings.

## B6 angle (hiring, management, leadership Part 2)

Berman isn't a canonical leadership voice, but she contributes a scientific lens the B5 operator-heavy canon largely lacks: rigorous behavior change applied inward. Most directly relevant for managers running performance systems, incentives, and change programs — her LendingClub confession is a pointed lesson in how ill-specified incentives corrupt even ethically careful people ("we are what we measure"). The three-B / "uncomfortably specific behavior" language is a transferable tool for OKR-setting and team habit formation. Useful counterweight to more intuition-driven B5 voices.
