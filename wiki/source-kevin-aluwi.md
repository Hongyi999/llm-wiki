# Source: Taxi mafias, cash vaults & 100% MoM growth | Kevin Aluwi (Gojek)

**Guest**: Kevin Aluwi (co-founder and former CEO of Gojek)
**Published**: 2023-03-26
**Duration**: ~1h 3m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HVB-dqjUJks

## Executive summary

Origin story of Southeast Asia's biggest startup: motorcycle-taxi super app hit 100%+ MoM growth for 16-18 months, 2.7M drivers, 3B orders, IPO'd at ~$28B. Scrappy war stories (private security against motorcycle-taxi mafias, physical cash-vault booths, copying fraudulent third-party driver apps) and brand/doing-hard-things as underrated moats, while super apps are overrated without a unifying concept.

## Key ideas

1. **Super apps are overrated without a unifying concept** — Customers need a coherent mental model (e.g., "the Gojek driver") to cross-adopt services; without it, cross-sell benefits don't materialize (customers asking if the massage-service driver would give them a massage).
2. **Brand as survival mechanism, not afterthought** — With 1/100th the capital of competitors, Gojek survived by branding drivers with jackets/helmets so the product value proposition was visible in traffic.
3. **Doing hard things is the most durable "moat"** — All technical moats erode; the real edge is willingness to run private security, build cash-vault ATM networks, take operations-heavy detours.
4. **Ops-heavy scrappiness beats technical elegance early** — Copying fraudulent driver-app features ("if you can't beat them, join them"); founder doing every job before hiring.
5. **Build for local, not as a clone** — Gojek wasn't Uber-for-Indonesia; the motorcycle-taxi insight drove both product shape and branding (helmets/jackets only make sense for motorcycles).

## Notable quote

> "I don't believe that any moats are durable over time... one so-called moat that doesn't get talked about enough is the fact that you're able to do hard things because hard things are hard."

## B8 angle

Rare emerging-market counter-narrative: brand > features, ops > tech early, hard things > moats, and "local insight beats clone thinking." Useful as anti-template case against lazy super-app positioning.
