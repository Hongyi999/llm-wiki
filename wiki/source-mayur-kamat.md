# Unconventional product lessons from Binance, N26, Google, more | Mayur Kamat (CPO at N26)

**Guest**: Mayur Kamat  
**Published**: 2025-05-22  
**Duration**: 1:37:56  
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UVyfuSBwbNA](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UVyfuSBwbNA)

## Executive summary

Mayur Kamat, CPO at N26 and former Global Head of Product at Binance, shares unconventional career and product advice drawn from working across wildly different cultures and companies. He describes Binance's extreme operating model -- 55 direct reports per leader, daily 11 PM leadership calls, and the ability to spin up 500-person teams overnight -- and contrasts it with the experimentation-driven approach he has brought to N26. Kamat argues that strategy is overrated for most product managers, advocating instead for speed from hypothesis to data. He offers pointed career advice: optimize for learning compounding over compensation, identify your superpowers early, and decide honestly whether the C-suite path is truly fulfilling for you. His story of leading Google Hangouts to failure despite unlimited resources illustrates that company DNA can override even the best execution.

## Key ideas

1. **Daily Meeting Execution Model**: At Binance, the leadership team met every single day (including weekends) at 11 PM. No decision was blocked for more than 24 hours. Urgent problems were assigned an owner who ran daily all-hands until resolution. This extreme cadence enabled zero-to-$400B growth in five years.

2. **Strategy Is Overrated -- Speed to Data Is What Matters**: For most product managers, strategy should be reduced to 'how fast can I go from hypothesis to data.' If you can test it, you do not need elaborate strategic frameworks. Experimentation democratizes performance and gives PMs scientific authority to reject opinion-based directives.

3. **Optimize for Learning Compounding, Not Compensation**: Career growth compounds like interest -- daily compounding (fast-growing companies) vastly outperforms yearly compounding (slow-moving enterprises). Do not optimize for compensation in the first 15 years; you will make 90% of career earnings in the final five years.

4. **Company DNA Limits Product Possibilities**: Certain companies structurally cannot succeed at certain products (Google with social, Microsoft with mobile, Facebook with enterprise). The Hangouts failure -- despite thousands of engineers, Larry Page's direct support, and Chrome integration -- proved that organizational DNA can override unlimited resources.

5. **High-Leverage Problem Selection with the Moving Desk**: Inspired by White Pages CEO Alex Allgood who physically moved his desk to whichever department had the highest-leverage problem, leaders should keep calendars free and focus personally on the 10X positive or negative impact problems rather than delegating everything.

## Entities discussed

## Concepts covered

- **Running Products via Daily Meeting** — An operating model where urgent problems get an owner and daily all-hands until resolved, ensuring no decision is blocked more than 24 hours.
- **Learning Compounding** — The principle that career growth follows compound interest: fast-growing companies provide daily compounding versus slow companies' yearly compounding.
- **Company DNA** — The thesis that a company's founding culture and strengths structurally prevent it from succeeding in certain product categories regardless of resources invested.
- **Overlapping Superpowers** — Kamat's heuristic for career decisions: find roles where your personal superpowers and the company's superpowers reinforce each other for accelerated growth.
- **Experimentation as PM Science** — Building experimentation culture gives product managers a scientific discipline, enabling them to override opinion with data and democratize performance evaluation.
- **The Moving Desk** — A leadership metaphor from White Pages CEO Alex Allgood who physically relocated his desk to the department with the highest-leverage problem.
