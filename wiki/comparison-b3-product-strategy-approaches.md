# Comparison — B3 Product Strategy & PM Craft Approaches

B3 covers 30 Lenny's Podcast transcripts on product strategy and PM craft. Unlike B1/B2 (which centered on AI products), B3 spans strategy theory, product management reform, methodology, growth, leadership, and career design. The 30 guests group into 8 schools of thought.

## 8 schools of thought

### 1. Strategy theory (3 guests)

The canonical strategy thinkers. Each provides a distinct lens on sustainable competitive advantage.

| Guest | Framework | Core question | Key insight |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[source-hamilton-helmer\|Hamilton Helmer]] | [[seven-powers\|7 Powers]] | What structural power enables winning? | Power = benefit + barrier; only 4 powers matter for startups |
| [[source-roger-martin\|Roger Martin]] | [[strategy-choice-cascade\|Playing to Win]] | Where to play, how to win? | Strategy = choices competitors find uncomfortable to mirror |
| [[source-richard-rumelt\|Richard Rumelt]] | Good Strategy / Bad Strategy | What is the crux? | Strategy kernel = diagnosis + guiding policy + coherent actions |

### 2. Product operating model / PM reform (5 guests)

The strongest theme in B3: a shared critique that most product organizations are doing product management wrong, with convergent prescriptions.

| Guest | Framing | Prescription |
|---|---|---|
| [[source-marty-cagan\|Marty Cagan]] | "Product management theater" | Replace feature teams with empowered teams; 20-principle [[product-operating-model]] |
| [[source-melissa-perri\|Melissa Perri]] | "The Build Trap" | Product owner ≠ product manager; baseline → train → sort transformation |
| [[source-melissa-perri-denise-tilles\|Perri & Tilles]] | Product strategy + product ops | Strategy is a living system; product ops enables informed decisions at scale |
| [[source-christian-idiodi\|Christian Idiodi]] | SVPG in practice | Reference customers as the discovery method; promote to practice, not to learn |
| [[source-brian-tolkin\|Brian Tolkin]] | Twin turbine model | Product operations as a formal function connecting tech and real-world ops |

## Key disagreements

| Question | Position A | Position B |
|---|---|---|
| **Does strategy matter early?** | Helmer: consider power from day one; it tilts probabilities | Kamat: strategy is overrated; velocity of learning is what matters; Caldwell: just talk to users |
| **Scrum: friend or foe?** | Perri: Scrum's PO role is legitimate but different from PM | Cagan: Scrum-based feature teams are the root of PM theater; Singer: Shape Up replaces Scrum entirely |
| **Should PMs specialize or generalize?** | Cagan: PMs must master value + viability or face automation | Dhiman: generalize across the Three W's; Millerd: leave PM entirely if it doesn't fit |
| **Top-down vs. bottom-up strategy?** | Martin/Rumelt: strategy is a leadership responsibility | Cook/Crusson: innovation emerges from empowered teams with psychological safety |
| **Network effects as moat?** | Common wisdom: network effects = competitive advantage | Helmer: network effects ≠ network economies; most flywheel pitches are overstated |
| **Is operational excellence a competitive advantage?** | Cao (TikTok): daily meetings + always-day-one = execution edge | Helmer: operational excellence is essential but imitable; it's a treadmill, not a moat |
