# Twitter's ex-Head of Product on Elon, consumer products, culture, more | Kayvon Beykpour

## Executive summary

Kayvon Beykpour — longest-tenured Head of Product and GM of Consumer at Twitter (pre-Elon), co-founder of Periscope (acquired by Twitter in 2015) — tells the insider story of turning Twitter's risk-averse product org into a shipping machine (Spaces, Communities, Super Follows, Community Notes, Fleets, Twitter Blue) before being fired by Parag Agrawal the day after he returned from the hospital with his newborn, immediately before Elon signed the deal. Thesis: cultural change at a stagnant org is a political, patience-intensive craft anchored in three moves — attack the sacred cows as your roadmap, import hungry founder-operators via acquihires, and repetitively tell the story. Candid about the biggest failure: lacking the "fortitude" and org structure to move people off the wagon fast enough.

## Key ideas

1. **Sacred cows are the roadmap**: List every "we're not allowed to change this" — ranked timeline, 140 chars, reply ownership — and start there. The cultural hesitation they expose is the real work.
2. **Acquihire founders to break inertia**: Community Notes (Keith Coleman), Super Follows (Esther Crawford), Fleets (Mo Oladam), Communities (Chroma Labs). Small founder-led squads inside a large org deliver speculative bets that internal teams would smother.
3. **Refine-the-core + portfolio of bets**: Twitter's DAU revival came from optimization (ranked feed, ML, SMS verify bugs). Ambitious bets must sit alongside — not replace — disciplined core refinement.
4. **Storytelling is the unlock for culture change**: Repetitive internal storytelling plus external narrative gets people on/off the wagon and gives advertisers and users a reason to believe. You can't tell the story just once.
5. **On/off the wagon — cost of being slow to decide**: Their inability (and Jack's) to quickly remove people misaligned with the new direction was the biggest drag on cultural change; high performers leave when they see tolerated misalignment.
