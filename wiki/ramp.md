# Ramp

Corporate card + spend management; the fastest-growing SaaS company to $100M ARR. Founded by Eric Glyman (CEO) and Karim Atiyeh (CTO). Canonical case study of velocity-first operating principles.

## Operating principles

- **Velocity as cultural OS** — "Any second you spend planning is a second you don't spend doing."
- **Small single-threaded teams** — 3 engineers, 1 designer, 1 PM built competitors to Amex (3 months), Expensify (6 months), Bill.com (3 months). Teams shielded from rest of company during 0→1.
- **Context over control** — Align on goals → hypotheses → data → *then* solutions. CTO Karim was "extremely hands-off" on product decisions once goals aligned.
- **Support reports into product** — "Every support ticket is a failure of our product." 400,000+ users served by <30 agents. Bugs go directly to on-call engineer, not a backlog.
- **13 PMs to 100+ engineers** — PMs as force multipliers, not bottlenecks. No tickets, no linear, just vision/priority/spec.
- **days.ramp.com** — Cultural artifact embedding "we work in days" time horizon.

## Growth playbook

- **Cap table as growth strategy** — Founders seeded the cap table with operators whose companies became customers.
- **Growth-engineering as distinct function** — Dedicated eng team goaled on sales pipeline $ and payback period (not product metrics). AI-driven workflow automation, data enrichment, prospect prioritization — built pre-ChatGPT.
- **North star translation factors** — Every team's local metric has a translation factor to company-level metric (SQL pipeline $).
- **Activation = "4 events in first 30 days"** — Escape velocity threshold.

## Strategy docs

Goals + hypotheses + **unique right to win** + metrics + initiatives. Key differentiator: articulating *why this company* has right to win (bill-pay expansion reused existing money movement + accounting integrations + risk infra).
