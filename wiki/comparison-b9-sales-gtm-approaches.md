# Comparison: B9 — Sales, GTM & pricing approaches

19 unique episodes spanning founder-led sales, enterprise GTM, product-led sales, positioning theory, pricing strategy, category design, strategic narrative, and recession tactics. Grouped into 8 schools of thought.

## Schools of thought

### 1. Positioning & pitch architecture
**Voices**: [[source-april-dunford|Dunford (Sales Pitch)]], [[source-april-dunford-20|Dunford (Obviously Awesome)]], [[source-andy-raskin|Andy Raskin]]

The most technically developed school. Dunford provides the five-component positioning framework (competitive alternatives → unique attributes → value → best-fit customers → category) and the two-part pitch (Setup → Follow-through). Raskin provides the strategic narrative framework (old game → new game → promised land). Dunford explicitly critiques Raskin-style "trend-first" pitches; both are useful at different funnel stages.

### 2. Category design maximalism
**Voice**: [[source-christopher-lochhead|Christopher Lochhead]]

"Categories make products, not the other way around." 76% of tech market cap goes to category king. Frame-name-claim a problem. Languaging as strategic weapon. Direct opposition to Dunford's positioning-within-existing-category approach.

### 3. Founder-led & early-stage sales
**Voices**: [[source-pete-kazanjy|Kazanjy]], [[source-jen-abel-founder-led-sales|Jen Abel (1st)]], [[source-jen-abel-20|Jen Abel (2nd)]]

Kazanjy: founders must sell 20–30 customers personally; 15–25% win rate threshold before first hire; hire pioneer ICs, not VPs. Abel (2nd episode): $1M→$10M requires tier-1 logos, $75K–$150K land, vision-casting not problem-selling.

### 4. Buyer psychology & closing science
**Voice**: [[source-matt-dixon|Matt Dixon]]

Data-driven (2.5M sales calls). FOMU > FOMO. 87% of urgency-based closes backfire on indecisive buyers. JOLT method: Judge-Offer-Limit-Take-risk-off.

### 5. Product-led sales & bottom-up GTM
**Voices**: [[source-elena-verna-20|Elena Verna]], [[source-claire-butler|Claire Butler]], [[source-carilu-dietrich|Carilu Dietrich]]

Verna: product must own monetization and pipeline targets; monetization awareness is 75% of the battle. Butler: Figma's IC-first adoption → designer advocates in sales calls → enterprise. Dietrich: Atlassian's 2–3x R&D vs. sales spend proves PLG scales.

### 6. Pricing & monetization
**Voices**: [[source-madhavan-ramanujam-20|Ramanujam (earlier)]], [[source-madhavan-ramanujam|Ramanujam (later)]], [[source-julia-schottenstein|Julia Schottenstein]]

Ramanujam: dual-engine (market share + wallet share); gives-and-gets negotiation; good/better/best packaging; AI must monetize from day one. Schottenstein: open-source-to-enterprise pricing, community-as-moat.

### 7. Modern GTM operations
**Voices**: [[source-jeanne-grosser|Jeanne Grosser]], [[source-jonathan-becker|Jonathan Becker]]

Grosser: GTM engineer role; agent-ified SDRs; deal-bot diagnostics; 80% of customers buy to avoid pain. Becker: creative-as-lever post-privacy; agency + in-house hybrid; first-party data strategy.

### 8. Specialist & adjacent
**Voices**: [[source-raaz-herzberg|Raaz Herzberg]], [[source-nan-yu|Nan Yu]], [[source-jessica-hische|Jessica Hische]], [[source-anneka-gupta|Anneka Gupta]], [[source-sahil-mansuri|Sahil Mansuri]]

Herzberg: Wiz hypergrowth PLG+sales hybrid. Nan Yu: Linear's craft-is-moat, speed + quality. Hische: design craft as pricing power. Gupta: strategic thinking and navigating difficult colleagues. Mansuri: recession survival — retention-first, comp redesign.

## Key disagreements

### 1. Category creation vs. positioning within existing categories
**Lochhead**: Create a new category or accept fighting for 24% of existing market cap. "Positioning is for losers."
**Dunford**: Most legendary companies (Google, Salesforce, Snowflake) did NOT create categories. Category creation is risky and expensive; bowling-pin positioning within existing categories is the safer and more common path.

### 2. Trend-first narrative vs. value-first positioning
**Raskin**: Start with a world-changing shift (old game → new game) to create emotional urgency and CEO alignment.
**Dunford**: Trend-first pitches are overused and often ungrounded. Start with competitive alternatives and differentiated value, not a macro shift.

### 3. When to hire sales
**Kazanjy**: 15–25% win rate after 50–100 at-bats. Hire pioneer ICs.
**Abel**: Chase tier-1 logos early; mid-market doesn't exist. Hire "cosplay-the-founder" AEs.
**Butler/Dietrich**: Delay sales as long as possible; let product traction prove demand first.

### 4. FOMO vs. FOMU in closing
**Dixon**: 87% of urgency-based closes backfire. FOMU is the real enemy; reduce risk, don't increase fear.
**Abel**: Vision-casting (showing the gap/alpha, not the pain) — a Challenger-adjacent approach that creates positive urgency rather than fear-based urgency.

### 5. Product's role in revenue
**Verna**: Product must own revenue targets and PQA metrics. Non-negotiable for PLS.
**Traditional view** (implicit across Kazanjy, Abel, Dixon): Product builds; sales sells. Product accountability for revenue is uncomfortable and organizationally disruptive.

### 6. Advertising's power
**Dietrich**: Advertising amplifies but can never create demand. Organic WOM is the only path to hypergrowth.
**Becker**: Creative quality is now the #1 lever in paid growth (post-privacy). Paid acquisition works when creative is excellent.
