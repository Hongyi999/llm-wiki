# Executive Coaching

Across the B5 batch, **executive coaching** emerges as a distinct profession with a shared toolkit, even as individual coaches specialize in different layers. This page catalogs the landscape.

## The shared toolkit

Most coaches in the batch converge on five skills:

1. **Level-3 listening** — Beyond words, into body language / tone / context.
2. **[[grow-model|GROW]] or a variant** — Goal / Reality / Options / Way forward.
3. **Observable-facts feedback** — "What I observed was…" not "you are…".
4. **The emotional layer** — "Professionals have feelings" — acknowledge it to make hard conversations productive.
5. **Coach vs. advise** — Default to coaching; advise only when you know the answer, it's urgent, or the report lacks the skill.

## When to bring in a coach

Recurring themes:

- **Confidence collapse** — After a major founder mistake (see [[source-ben-horowitz]]).
- **Stuck conversations** — Same issue raised repeatedly without movement.
- **Role transition** — Founder → CEO; senior PM → VP (see [[source-jonathan-lowenhar]]).
- **Team dysfunction** — Especially when the pattern repeats across teams (see [[source-jerry-colonna]]: family-of-origin echoes).
- **Burnout approaching** — Feather-stage signals (see [[source-jonny-miller]]).

## Inner work vs. script work

A visible divide in the batch:

- **Script-work school** (Cohn, Lockett, Berger) — Reusable phrases and frameworks; soft skills as teachable tools.
- **Inner-work school** (Colonna, Hudson, Miller) — The leader's psychological/somatic state is the real leverage point.

Both are real. The script-work school makes behavior change immediately tractable; the inner-work school prevents the behaviors from regressing under pressure.
