# How Netflix builds a culture of excellence | Elizabeth Stone (CTO)

**Guest**: Elizabeth Stone
**Published**: 2024-02-22
**Duration**: 1:13:44
**YouTube**: [Lenny's Podcast — Elizabeth Stone](https://www.lennyspodcast.com/)

## Executive summary

Elizabeth Stone — CTO of [[netflix|Netflix]] (previously VP Data & Insights there), prior VP Science at Lyft, COO at Nuna, trader at Merrill Lynch, PhD economist — explains Netflix's culture as a three-layer stack: high **talent density** enables **candor**, and candor plus talent density enables **freedom and responsibility**. Remove any layer and the others collapse. She also shares her personal playbook for rising quickly across four companies: dedication-as-high-standards (not hours), translating between technical and non-technical, learning by osmosis, and a three-step feedback ritual (set expectations → give specific feedback → jump in to help). Netflix operates without traditional performance reviews, relying on the "keeper test" as a continuous mental model.

## Key ideas

1. **Talent density is the prerequisite, not the goal**: Candor, freedom, and responsibility only work if the talent bar is high. Hire for additive perspective (people who'd upgrade your team), pay personal top-of-market so comp isn't golden handcuffs, and act quickly when someone doesn't clear the keeper test.
2. **The keeper test as operating model**: Managers regularly ask, "Would I fight to keep this person if they tried to leave?" Reports are encouraged to ask it back. Replaces performance reviews and makes exit conversations less surprising because feedback has been continuous.
3. **High bar via 3-step manager loop**: Set expectations clearly up front, give specific direct feedback (privately, not on stage) when work falls short, then jump into the doc/problem to help close the gap. Modeling the bar yourself is non-negotiable.
4. **Dedication means standards, not hours**: Being responsive, on time, and follow-through-reliable — not burning weekends — is how senior leaders signal they respect their teams' time and set the excellence tone.
5. **Centralized data + user research is a competitive moat**: Netflix keeps data engineers, data scientists, analytics, and consumer insights in one org rather than embedded in business lines. Preserves objectivity (they can tell uncomfortable truths) and creates career mobility and functional excellence.
