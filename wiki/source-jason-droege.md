## Executive summary

Jason Droege, new CEO of Scale AI, explains how the frontier-model data market has evolved from generic labelers to PhD-credentialed experts (80% with bachelor's degrees, 15% with PhDs). Models now require experts to solve domain-specific judgment problems—from healthcare diagnostics catching missed drug interactions to Salesforce agents booking meetings. The shift reflects AI's evolution from 'knowing things' to 'doing things,' with enterprise deployments requiring 6-12 months to achieve production robustness, not weeks.

## Key ideas

1. Expert-as-judge, not crowd labor — 18 months ago labeling was 'is this short story better?'; now it's 'design a website like our best engineer' or 'explain rare cancer to a model.' 80% of Scale's network holds bachelor's degrees or higher
2. Generalizability determines data value — the key is finding training data generalizable across use cases; e.g., 'find meeting on my calendar' should transfer to any calendar action, not require 45 trillion scenario combinations
3. Enterprises must label their own domain expertise — off-shelf models plateau; a healthcare system must have their own doctors labeling cases because 'a document with the same words in Company A means different things in Company B'
4. AI deployment timeline: 6-12 months to robustness — POCs reach 60-70% easily, but achieving the last 30% (legal approval, policy, regulatory) requires months of human judgment loops and change management
5. Models are moving from knowing to doing — future AI isn't just about knowledge benchmarks but agent actions in sandboxes (Salesforce, healthcare, web systems); this requires reinforcement learning environments and real-time feedback
