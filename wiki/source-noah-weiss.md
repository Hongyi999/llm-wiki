# Source: The 10 traits of great PMs, AI, and Slack's approach to product | Noah Weiss (Slack, Google)

**Guest**: Noah Weiss
**Published**: 2023-07-23
**Duration**: 1:25:34
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XrRlVOWe5GE

## Executive summary

Noah Weiss, CPO of Slack, draws on 15 years of AI experience across Google, Foursquare, and Slack to articulate how enterprise products should thoughtfully integrate AI capabilities. He reveals that Slack's original 2014 product vision literally included 'then do magic AI stuff on top' as a placeholder -- and that the moment has finally arrived. Weiss shares Slack's approach of running parallel AI prototyping teams on shared ML infrastructure while keeping the steady state goal of AI becoming part of every team's roadmap. The conversation also covers how Slack turned around a plateauing self-service business by discovering a new north star metric ('successful teams' -- 5 people using Slack most of the work week), the art of working with product-minded founders through principle alignment and the U-curve of involvement, and unique processes like Complaint Storms and Customer Love Sprints that maintain product quality at scale.

## Key ideas

1. **UI promise must match data quality**: The biggest failing of current LLMs is appearing supremely confident even when hallucinating. Products must use transparency about data sources and acknowledge uncertainty to build user trust, rather than presenting AI outputs as infallible.
2. **Take bigger boulder bets**: Slack's internal principle encourages teams to look beyond incremental KPI improvements and seek the 'next hill' -- major capability leaps that open entirely new opportunity spaces. This led to products like huddles, clips, and now AI features.
3. **The U-curve of founder involvement**: When working with product-minded founders, involve them heavily at the beginning (strategic alignment, goals, anti-goals) and end (quality taste-testing in code, not mocks), but give teams space to run in the middle. Do the final review collectively as a bug bash, not a gate.
4. **Successful teams as north star metric**: Slack discovered that getting 5 people to use Slack most of the work week made a team 400% more likely to upgrade in 6 months. This activation metric rallied every product team around a shared goal earlier in the funnel than paid conversion.
5. **Complaint Storms for fresh eyes**: Start by critically examining competitors' products as a group, cataloging every pain point and confusion. This builds the muscle and calibration needed to then honestly assess your own product, unlocking creative solutions to stale problems.

## Concepts & frameworks

- **Complaint Storms** — Slack's process of critically reviewing competitors' products as a group before turning the same lens on their own, building empathy for new users' experience.
- **Customer Love Sprints** — Two-week hackathon-style sprints focused on shipping the lowest-effort, highest-impact improvements to generate customer delight, run quarterly.
- **Successful Teams Metric** — Slack's activation north star: 5 people using Slack most of the work week, predictive of 400% higher upgrade likelihood in 6 months.
- **Product Principles as Common Language** — Slack's five principles (be a great host, don't make me think, etc.) that create shared vocabulary for product quality across the organization.
- **Stacking S-Curves** — The concept that product-market fit is not a one-time achievement but a series of S-curves, each requiring new understanding of the next customer segment.
- [[taste-as-differentiator|Comprehension and Desirability]] — The two axes Slack focused on when reviving self-service growth: do new users understand what Slack is, and why should they care enough to adopt it?
- **Virtuous Training Cycles** — Designing AI products so that normal user behavior naturally generates training data that improves the underlying model.
