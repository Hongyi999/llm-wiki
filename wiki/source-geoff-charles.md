# Source: Velocity over everything: How Ramp became the fastest-growing SaaS startup ever | Geoff Charles

**Guest**: Geoff Charles (VP Product at Ramp)
**Published**: 2023-08-06
**Duration**: ~1h 17m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aNJDZ_RzTVk

## Executive summary

How Ramp operationalizes velocity as the organizing principle for everything: hiring, planning, roadmap, management, support. Counterintuitive insight: velocity actually produces higher quality (because fixes ship fast) and prevents burnout (because people feel impact), but only if underlying engineering talent is A+ and the CEO genuinely delegates product decisions downward.

## Key ideas

1. **Velocity as cultural OS** — "Any second you spend planning is a second you don't spend doing." Small (3 engineers, 1 designer, 1 PM), single-threaded teams built competitors to Amex in 3 months, Expensify in 6, Bill.com in 3. Shield teams from rest of company.
2. **Context over control** — Aligning on goals → hypotheses → data → *then* solutions. Junior leaders debate solutions; senior leaders debate upstream. CTO Karim was "extremely hands-off" once goals aligned.
3. **Strategy = goals + hypotheses + unique right to win + metrics + initiatives** — Strategy docs live at pod level. Key: articulating *why this company* has right to win (Ramp's bill-pay expansion reused existing money movement infra).
4. **Support reports into product as forcing function** — "Every support ticket is a failure of our product." 400,000+ users served by <30 agents. No bug backlog; bugs assigned directly to on-call engineer who feels the pain.
5. **PM as force multiplier, not bottleneck** — 13 PMs to 100+ engineers. PMs don't write tickets, don't run linear, focus only on vision/priority/high-level spec. Product operations team handles enablement, release, research.
