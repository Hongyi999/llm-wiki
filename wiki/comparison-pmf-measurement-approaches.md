# Comparison: PMF Measurement Approaches

Four distinct frames for measuring [[product-market-fit|PMF]] appear across the ingested sources. They are complementary more than competing — each catches a different failure mode.

## At a glance

| Approach | Key question | Threshold | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **[[sean-ellis-test\|Sean Ellis Test]]** | "How disappointed if you could no longer use this?" | ≥40% "very disappointed" | Single number, simple to run, validated across many companies | Only measures satisfaction; ignores efficiency and TAM |
| **[[superhuman-pmf-engine\|Superhuman PMF Engine]]** | Same Q + segmentation of "somewhat disappointed" | Same 40% score, but also an action plan | Turns the score into a roadmap | Assumes you already know enough users to run qualitative analysis |
| **[[four-levels-of-pmf\|Four Levels of PMF]]** (Todd Jackson) | "Where are we on satisfaction, demand, and efficiency?" | Benchmarks per level (L1–L4) | Catches founders who fake PMF via one strong dimension | Harder to run — needs real financial data and a sharp persona |
| **[[marketplace-liquidity\|Marketplace Liquidity]]** (Benjamin Lauzier) | "What fraction of demand finds supply it's happy with, fast?" | Product-specific (fill rate, ETA, match rate) | Only framework that handles two-sided PMF | Only applies to marketplaces |

## Where they agree

- **PMF is not binary**. Even the Sean Ellis Test is best used as a trend line — watching the score move matters more than hitting 40% on any single day.
- **Focus on the user who already loves you.** Sean's primary-benefit drill-down, Rahul's "high-expectation customer," Todd's "wow statements," and Benjamin's "pick the hard side" are all variants of: *ignore the indifferent middle, obsess over the intense lovers*.
- **Satisfaction alone is not enough.** All four frameworks eventually gesture at efficiency and scale. Sean's [[logmein|LogMeIn]] activation story makes the same point in miniature: a healthy survey score was useless until activation was fixed.
- **Qualitative beats quantitative for diagnosis.** Numbers tell you *whether*; the drill-down questions (Sean), the "somewhat disappointed" tags (Rahul), and the dollar-committing questions (Todd) tell you *why*.

## Where they disagree

### Single number vs. multi-dimensional

- **Sean Ellis / Rahul Vohra**: a single number (the 40% threshold) plus qualitative follow-ups is enough. The simplicity is the point — founders cannot be trusted with complex dashboards when they are searching for PMF.
- **Todd Jackson**: a single number is dangerous because it can be satisfied in isolation. A company with 45% "very disappointed" can still have broken unit economics and a non-scaling channel. He wants **three dimensions** (satisfaction, demand, efficiency) checked independently.

**Resolution**: the Sean Ellis Test is a satisfaction probe. Todd doesn't dispute the probe — he adds two other probes (demand, efficiency) alongside it. They're compatible; Todd's framework is a superset.

### Start with the lover or the non-lover

- **Rahul Vohra**: the roadmap comes from analyzing **somewhat disappointed** users — the ones who are close-but-not-there.
- **Todd Jackson / Sean Ellis**: focus mostly on **very disappointed** users and ignore the middle until the core is locked.

**Resolution**: Rahul's insight is that the *very disappointed* segment defines *who to build for*, while the *somewhat disappointed* segment defines *what to build*. Both are needed.

### Speed vs. rigor

- **Jen Abel / founder-led sales**: qualitative signal from conversations is primary; formal surveys are secondary. Founders should be in the field, not running dashboards.
- **Todd Jackson**: formal measurement is the only thing that catches self-delusion.

**Resolution**: Jen is talking about **pre-PMF discovery** (sales conversations as the signal source). Todd is talking about **PMF measurement** (formal frameworks to decide whether you've arrived). Different moments in the same journey.

## How to use them together

1. **In pre-PMF / founder-led sales phase**: use [[founder-led-sales]] conversations as the primary signal source. Look for **wow statements**, **spontaneous pulling-in of colleagues**, and **real dollar commitments** ([[dollar-driven-discovery]]).
2. **When users exist but you're unsure**: run the [[sean-ellis-test|Sean Ellis Test]] with the drill-down questions. Use the results to define your **high-expectation customer**.
3. **When the score moves into the 25–40% range**: run the [[superhuman-pmf-engine|Superhuman PMF Engine]] to turn the number into a roadmap.
4. **When satisfaction is strong**: check [[four-levels-of-pmf|Todd's three dimensions]] to make sure demand and efficiency are keeping up.
5. **If you're a marketplace**: use [[marketplace-liquidity]] proxies alongside all of the above, per side.

## The unified picture

Viewed together, the four frameworks describe the same beast from different angles:

- **Sean** measures whether the product is **loved**.
- **Rahul** translates love into a **roadmap**.
- **Todd** translates love into a **business**.
- **Benjamin** translates love into a **marketplace**.

None of them is wrong. None of them is sufficient alone.
