# What it takes to become a top 1% PM | Ian McAllister (Uber, Amazon, Airbnb)

**Guest**: Ian McAllister  
**Published**: 2022-11-20  
**Duration**: 1:04:59  
**YouTube**: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7gaPPrAd7nc](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7gaPPrAd7nc)

## Executive summary

Ian McAllister, author of the legendary 'What Separates a Top 1% PM from a Top 10% PM' post and veteran of 12 years at Amazon, distills PM excellence into two tiers of skills. For new PMs, the essentials are communicate, prioritize, and execute. For senior PMs, the critical additions are think big, earn trust, and be driven by impact rather than promotion. McAllister then provides an authoritative insider account of Amazon's working backwards process, explaining that the press release mechanism is merely a tool to enforce the real discipline: starting with the customer problem before ever considering solutions. He shares direct lessons from Jeff Bezos and Jeff Wilke, including the Weekly Business Review as a cascading accountability mechanism.

## Key ideas

1. **Communicate, prioritize, execute for new PMs**: These three skills form the irreducible core. Communication means answering the question asked (date for 'when,' number for 'how much') before explaining context. Prioritization is the single highest-leverage PM skill — given identical resources, a great prioritizer generates 5x the impact. Execution means molding work into compact, high-impact packages.

2. **Think big, earn trust, drive impact for senior PMs**: Senior PMs must expand their definition of what product ownership means beyond product and tech to anything that influences customer success. Trust is the currency of product leadership — built by repeatedly setting and meeting expectations, telling the truth, launching what you said you'd launch, and owning mistakes.

3. **Working backwards means starting with the problem**: The most common mistake teams make is having a solution in mind and retrofitting a customer problem to justify it. The telltale sign: people talk about combining existing technologies or 'ingredients in the pantry' rather than a compelling customer pain point. If there's no problem paragraph, there's probably no real problem.

4. **The Weekly Business Review as a learning mechanism**: Jeff Wilke's WBR covered the entire North American retail business in one hour, creating a cascading effect where every leader had to deeply know their metrics and be prepared to explain variances. This operational rigor taught the lesson that product leadership isn't just building new things — it's how well you run what you've already built.

5. **Bezos's three tests for new investments**: Is it a big idea? Is it something we (the company) should be doing? Is there a legitimate plan to succeed? The FAQ portion of the working backwards document serves as the early-stage test of that legitimate plan.

## Concepts covered

- **[[working-backwards|Working backwards]]** — Amazon's product development methodology that enforces starting with a customer problem, codified through an internal press release with problem paragraph, solution, customer quote, and FAQ documenting the legitimate plan to succeed.
- **Trust as PM currency** — The idea that trust — built by repeatedly setting and meeting expectations — is the primary asset that determines a product leader's ability to gain resources and grow their impact.
- **Weekly Business Review (WBR)** — Amazon's one-hour weekly meeting reviewing metrics across the entire business, creating cascading accountability where every leader must deeply know their numbers and explain variances.
- **Operator's Manual** — McAllister's compiled set of communication best practices (answer first then explain, avoid weasel words, own your problems) originally gathered as the 'Book of Kim' at Amazon.
- **Driven by impact not promotion** — The philosophy that waking up every day focused on maximizing business impact — rather than title or politics — naturally leads to career advancement as a byproduct.
