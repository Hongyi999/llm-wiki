# Source: How to be more innovative | Sam Schillace (Microsoft deputy CTO, creator of Google Docs)

**Guest**: Sam Schillace (Microsoft Deputy CTO; creator of Writely → Google Docs; now runs consumer AI under Kevin Scott and Satya Nadella)
**Published**: 2024-01-11
**Duration**: ~1h 27m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IOWDoDpQ7Xg

## Executive summary

Schillace argues that disruptive innovation is fundamentally a mindset problem, not a tech problem. The key move is replacing "why-not" questions with "what-if" questions, keeping your tools sharp so experiments are cheap, and being willing to look foolish. He frames generative AI as the next Google-Docs-sized category shift: "pixels are about to become free," and products will be features of AI, not the other way around.

## Key ideas

1. **What-if vs. why-not questions** — Disruptive ideas always trigger pessimistic "why-nots" (browser isn't ready, users won't trust the cloud). The unlock is asking "what if this works? How valuable is that world?" then working backwards. SpaceX, Tesla, Google Docs and GPT-4 all earn their disruption by clearing a compelling "what-if" bar.
2. **Love-it / hate-it bifurcation as disruption detector** — A bell curve of mild-like / mild-dislike means you built an incremental product. "If some people absolutely love it and a lot of people really hate it, that's a very good signifier of truly impactful and disruptive." Early Google Docs had Google executives telling him to shut it down even at 5M users.
3. **Convenience always wins; users are lazy** — "People are lazy. Look beyond cool to how much easier a new tool makes someone's life." Writely didn't even ask for an email address at signup. Value delivered has to exceed total adoption friction by roughly 10x.
4. **Optimism as a discipline + virtue from error** — "There's just not that much of a prize for being pessimistic and right." Keep tools sharp so experiments are cheap, be receptive to surprises, make failure into material. Personal motto: "Virtue from error."
5. **AI as the next platform** — "AI isn't a feature of your product; your product is a feature of AI." GPT-4 alone is a stochastic pure function; the real work is building state, control flow, orchestration, memory, multi-agent systems. Like the 2005 browser-as-platform moment: door open, new room, new game.

## B6 angle (hiring, management, leadership Part 2)

Schillace offers the "chief-creative-technologist" voice that complements B5's operators. Where [[source-boz|Boz]] and [[source-camille-fournier|Fournier]] focus on engineering management discipline, Schillace argues the leadership job is setting "north stars" (e.g., "write The Eye in Python") and protecting cheap experimentation. His defense of "sending Satya stupid emails" and picking leaders by energy/optimism is a useful counterweight to canonical B5 rigor — leadership of creators requires cultivating play, not just accountability.
