# Source: How to see like a designer | Jessica Hische

**Guest**: Jessica Hische (lettering artist; clients: Wes Anderson, USPS, Apple, Nike, Mailchimp, Eventbrite, Philz)
**Published**: 2024-10-20
**Duration**: ~1h 21m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tLLqE6Ia8-U

## Executive summary

Teaches non-designers how to "see" like a designer via the refresh of Lenny's own podcast brand. Contrarian view: most startups should not spend venture money on a big brand exploration early, because pivots destroy that investment. Use "good enough" to start, hire a specialist for a $25-35K refresh when scaling events, swag, or competitive pressure warrant it.

## Key ideas

1. **Good-enough branding buys time** — Bootstrap a logo; wait for PMF/scale events before investing in a refresh. A $200K brand sprint is wasted pre-pivot.
2. **Reverse-justify your intuition** — When something feels off, don't jump to "the R is wrong"; ask broad "why does this feel bad?" first, then narrow. Take your glasses off / blur your eyes to judge overall vibe.
3. **Typography patterns trigger safety judgments** — Humans are pattern absorbers; fonts carry industry stereotypes (green = FinTech, script = wedding). Pick in or against the category deliberately.
4. **Optical corrections separate pros from hobbyists** — True geometric sans-serifs aren't mathematically geometric; strokes thin where they join. Engineers who design type make everything too regular.
5. **Pricing as a buyout structure** — Price creative exploration cheaply and final ownership/buyout separately, so clients iterate widely without paying for rights to every discarded version.

## B9 angle

"Brand is often overemphasized" view is a useful counterweight inside a GTM-heavy batch: she argues product/team matter more than brand for most startups. Aligns with [[source-raaz-herzberg|Raaz's]] "make noise cheaply" thesis and pushes back on the fully-invested brand-first school. The $25-35K refresh price band and buyout-decoupled pricing model is itself a Ramanujam-style example of pricing-as-product.
