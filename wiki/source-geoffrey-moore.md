# Source: Geoffrey Moore on finding your beachhead, crossing the chasm, and dominating a market

**Guest**: Geoffrey Moore (author of *Crossing the Chasm*, 33 years after publication)
**Published**: 2024-01-25
**Duration**: ~1h 25m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RBbINB5HSHk

## Executive summary

Revisits why pragmatists buy differently than visionaries and why founders still under-narrow their beachhead. Lays out four distinct go-to-market playbooks keyed to the four inflection points of the technology adoption lifecycle — early market, bowling alley (chasm), tornado, Main Street — with different personas, messages, and economic models at each.

## Key ideas

1. **Big enough to matter, small enough to lead, fit with your crown jewels** — Beachhead formula: same geography + same industry + same profession + compelling use case, small enough you can be 30-50% in 2 years.
2. **Compelling reason to buy vs. compelling reason to sell** — Pragmatists buy on pain severe enough to override inertia, not on your vision. "Shut the damn laptop" — the conversation is about their problem.
3. **Four playbooks for four inflection points** — Early market (project model, visionary sponsor, marquee logo), Bowling Alley (solution model, adjacent-segment expansion), Tornado (product model, grab share), Main Street (service innovation on commoditized products).
4. **Bonfire/kindling analogy** — You can't light a log with a match; you need kindling (narrow segment) held in one place until it catches.
5. **Ecosystems form around market leaders** — Partners don't back #3 in a broad market; they back the big fish in a small pond. Fish-to-pond ratio is the leverage point.

## Concepts that deserve their own wiki page

- **Technology Adoption Lifecycle** — Visionary/pragmatist/conservative/laggard + the chasm.
- **Bowling alley / beachhead selection** — Geography + industry + profession + compelling use case.
- **Four GTM playbooks** — Early market / bowling alley / tornado / Main Street.
- **Compelling reason to buy (not sell)** — Pragmatist-centric sales motion.
- **Trapped value / 10x effect** — Venture-scale innovation criteria.
- **Fish-to-pond ratio** — Why narrow beachheads beat wide ones.

## Notable quote

> "The tendency when you're in the chasm is, 'I just need more customers, I should take any customer I could find.' It's like taking a match and running it back and forth under a log. You start a fire by putting a little kindling and holding the match in one place."

## B8 angle

Canonical voice for stage-aware positioning; every other B8 author (Dunford, Raskin, Moore himself) implicitly assumes his lifecycle map. The useful crystallization: "we believe what you believe" (early market) vs. "we need what you have" (post-chasm) shift — direct input to Raskin's strategic narrative (pre-chasm) vs. [[source-arielle-jackson|Dunford-style]] competitive positioning (post-chasm).
