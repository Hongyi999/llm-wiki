# Source: The Full-Stack PM ft. Anuj Rathi

**Guest**: Anuj Rathi (Chief Product and Marketing Officer, Jupiter Money; formerly SVP Revenue & Growth at Swiggy, first PM at Flipkart)
**Published**: 2023-12-07
**Duration**: ~1h 12m
**YouTube**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1sClhfuCxP0

## Executive summary

Anuj Rathi argues that the most effective product managers are "full-stack" -- they think like marketers, salespeople, and influencers, not just feature builders. Drawing on experience at Flipkart, Swiggy, and Jupiter Money, he shares concrete frameworks (the 4BB model, "show don't tell" walls, three divergent PR FAQs) for product strategy and prioritization, with special attention to the unique challenges of building products for India's diverse, price-sensitive market and managing three-sided marketplaces.

## Key ideas

1. **Users are lazy, vain, and selfish** — Framework from Scott Belsky: new users do not care about your product. They have no time (lazy), strong existing habits (vain), and only care about their own benefit (selfish). Onboarding must be designed with this lens, continuing the marketing message seamlessly into the product experience.

2. **Full-stack PM as influencer** — Product managers are in "the business of influence" -- influencing users, engineers, leadership, and cross-functional partners. The best PMs think like marketers and salespeople, not just builders. Breadth across domains (like Federer playing multiple racket sports) creates better outcomes than narrow specialization.

3. **Three divergent PR FAQs** — When using Amazon's [[Working Backwards]] process, Anuj requires teams to produce three fully thought-through press releases exploring divergent directions before recommending one. This forces creative exploration, surfaces stakeholder concerns early, and gives leadership real choices rather than rubber-stamping a single option.

4. **The 4BB framework for product strategy** — Allocate focus across four buckets: **Brilliant Basics** (tech debt/hygiene), **Bread and Butter** (incremental product improvements), **Big Bets** (cross-team strategic initiatives), and **Breaking Bad** (company-redefining pivots, e.g., Swiggy moving from food delivery to convenience). Leadership must explicitly choose the allocation rather than letting PMs implicitly trade off.

5. **"Show don't tell" product walls** — Instead of abstract personas, build detailed visual journeys of a specific person (not a persona) through the entire product experience, including emotional states, marketing touchpoints, and real-time scenarios. This aligns the whole company and reveals gaps that documents miss.

6. **OKRs fail in three-sided marketplaces** — In marketplaces like Swiggy (consumers, restaurants, delivery partners), goals for each side inherently conflict. Pulling one lever (e.g., raising delivery fees) shifts outcomes for the other two. Big Bets with holistic narratives work better than divide-and-conquer OKRs. A/B testing also breaks down due to network effects.

7. **Can't do / Won't do / Not set up to do** — When things go wrong, diagnose whether the problem is capability (can't do), motivation/alignment (won't do), or organizational setup (not set up to do). Anuj estimates 70-80% of failures are setup issues -- org design, OKR design, and ways of working that leaders failed to configure properly.

- **[[Three-sided marketplace]]** — Marketplaces with three distinct user types whose incentives inherently conflict

## Notable quote

> "There are only three reasons why things do not happen the way you want them to happen as a leader. Either that person can't do, which is a capability issue, or they won't do, which is a motivation or an alignment issue, or they were not set up to do, which is really your problem."
